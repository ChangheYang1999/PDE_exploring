## 1. Exact mathematical target

Let y belong to R^3, let r = |y| denote spherical radius, and define

    J = [[0, -1, 0],
         [1,  0, 0],
         [0,  0, 0]].

Thus J y = (-y_2, y_1, 0). Seek U and P satisfying

    (1-c_l) U + ((c_l I - omega J)y + U) · grad U
        + omega J U + grad P = 0,

    div U = 0.

The desired solution is smooth through the origin and rotation axis, genuinely non-axisymmetric, nonzero, and decays uniformly at infinity. C^2 regularity is the minimum mathematical target; the numerical representation must support every higher derivative used in its residuals and verification.

The corresponding physical ansatz is

    tau = T-t,    s = -log(tau),
    y = tau^(-c_l) exp(-omega s J)(x-x_*),
    u(x,t) = tau^(c_l-1) exp(omega s J) U(y).

The present task is to find and validate a profile, not to claim blowup from smooth finite-energy initial data. A profile discovery alone would not establish that physical construction or Navier-Stokes blowup.

Do not impose U in L^2(R^3), exponential decay, compact support, or an artificial zero-velocity boundary at a finite radius. Do not add viscosity to the final equation. Artificial viscosity may be used only as a separately labeled continuation device that is reduced to zero and independently checked afterward.

Initially impose only two-fold rotational equivariance:

    U(exp(pi J)y) = exp(pi J) U(y),
    P(exp(pi J)y) = P(y).

In spherical vector components this allows all even azimuthal modes, including m=0, ±2, ±4, ... . It does not mean a pure m=2 truncation. Do not impose axisymmetry, reflection across z=0, no swirl, vanishing velocity at the origin, or any IPM/Boussinesq vanishing condition. If this restricted search stalls, spend a documented part of the budget on fully non-axisymmetric searches admitting odd azimuthal modes.

## 2. Derive and test the equations before large runs

Define Omega = curl U and, for convenience,

    W = (c_l I - omega J)y + U.

Implement independently derived residuals

    R_u = (1-c_l)U + W · grad U + omega J U + grad P,

    R_Omega = Omega + W · grad Omega + omega J Omega
                  - (Omega · grad)U,

    R_div = div U,

    R_P = -Delta P - sum_{i,j}(partial_i U_j)(partial_j U_i).

For exactly divergence-free U, verify numerically and symbolically that

    curl R_u = R_Omega,
    div W = 3 c_l.

Use manufactured fields with known derivatives, not only fields generated by the training code. Include non-axisymmetric divergence-free polynomial tests, smooth decaying test fields, and rotation/dilation covariance tests. A manufactured field may require an artificial forcing for this test only; it must never be mistaken for an unforced solution.

Test all signs in the rotational terms. In particular, the rotation acts on both the coordinates and the vector components. Also test the Cartesian-to-spherical conversion away from, near, and directly on the axis using appropriate regular limiting formulas.

Check automatic-differentiation Jacobian-vector products against independent directional differences at multiple step sizes. Use higher precision for small test problems when double-precision subtraction becomes limiting.

Do not spend the entire campaign re-proving theory. Time-box the initial derivation/literature audit to approximately four hours, overlapping hardware and implementation tests where practical. Extend it only when a concrete contradiction could invalidate the computation.

## 3. Far-field model: unknown coefficients, not a prescribed solution

Define the exponent

    alpha = (1-c_l)/c_l,    so 1 < alpha < 3/2,

and the angular argument

    chi = phi + (omega/c_l) log r.

Here theta is polar angle and phi is azimuth. F, G, and H below denote components in the local spherical vector basis, not Cartesian components.

The primary search class has a nonzero, differentiable formal leading tail

    U_0 = r^(-alpha) F(theta, chi),
    Omega_0 = r^(-1/c_l) H(theta, chi).

This is a search assumption, not a theorem implied by U -> 0. Record the restriction explicitly. Failure within it does not exclude all decaying profiles.

The leading linear equation is

    (1-c_l)U_0 + c_l(y · grad)U_0
        + omega[J U_0 - (J y · grad)U_0] = 0.

The spiral form solves this equation. Its remaining leading incompressibility constraint is

    (2-alpha)F_r + (omega/c_l) partial_chi F_r
      + (1/sin(theta)) partial_theta[sin(theta) F_theta]
      + (1/sin(theta)) partial_chi F_phi = 0.

F must be a smooth vector field on the entire sphere, including both poles. Do not optimize three unrelated singular coordinate functions and call them smooth.

Optimize the far-field coefficients together with the inner profile. Do not freeze one selected F throughout the main campaign. Do not require U_0 itself to solve stationary nonlinear Euler: the next velocity correction balances its nonlinear residual.

### First nonlinear correction

Use the following equations as formulas to verify independently, then implement:

    U = r^(-alpha)F(theta,chi)
        + r^(-(2*alpha+1))G(theta,chi) + ...,

    P = r^(-2*alpha)Q(theta,chi) + ... .

Define N by the spherical components of

    (U_0 · grad)U_0 = r^(-(2*alpha+1)) N(theta,chi).

Explicitly,

    N_r = F_r[-alpha F_r + (omega/c_l) partial_chi F_r]
          + F_theta partial_theta F_r
          + (F_phi/sin(theta)) partial_chi F_r
          - F_theta^2 - F_phi^2,

    N_theta = F_r[-alpha F_theta + (omega/c_l) partial_chi F_theta]
          + F_theta partial_theta F_theta
          + (F_phi/sin(theta)) partial_chi F_theta
          + F_r F_theta - cot(theta) F_phi^2,

    N_phi = F_r[-alpha F_phi + (omega/c_l) partial_chi F_phi]
          + F_theta partial_theta F_phi
          + (F_phi/sin(theta)) partial_chi F_phi
          + F_r F_phi + cot(theta) F_theta F_phi.

The pressure coefficient solves

    -{[(omega/c_l) partial_chi - 2*alpha]
       [(omega/c_l) partial_chi + 1 - 2*alpha]
       + Delta_S2} Q

      = [1-2*alpha + (omega/c_l) partial_chi] N_r
        + (1/sin(theta)) partial_theta[sin(theta) N_theta]
        + (1/sin(theta)) partial_chi N_phi.

The correction is

    G_r = N_r + [-2*alpha + (omega/c_l) partial_chi]Q,
    G_theta = N_theta + partial_theta Q,
    G_phi = N_phi + partial_chi Q / sin(theta).

It must satisfy

    [1-2*alpha + (omega/c_l) partial_chi]G_r
      + (1/sin(theta)) partial_theta[sin(theta)G_theta]
      + (1/sin(theta)) partial_chi G_phi = 0.

Check the identity that the linear similarity operator applied to
r^(-(2*alpha+1))G equals -r^(-(2*alpha+1))G.

Check the pressure operator mode by mode. Its eigenvalue before the outer minus sign is

    (-2*alpha + i*m*omega/c_l)
    (1-2*alpha + i*m*omega/c_l) - ell(ell+1).

There is no zero eigenvalue for 1 < alpha < 3/2, but conditioning worsens near the endpoints. Verify this rather than assuming every higher-order operator is also invertible.

Do not assume the full expansion consists only of successive powers
r^(-alpha-n/c_l). Global pressure contributions can introduce other terms; for example an r^(-3) pressure contribution can generate an r^(-4) velocity correction. Determine necessary multipoles, moment cancellations, and any logarithmic resonances consistently with the full-space pressure. Record which coefficients are determined locally and which require inner-outer matching.

### Initial tails

Use several unrelated families, including poloidal and toroidal mixtures. One seed family, not a boundary condition to freeze, is

    F_r = 0,
    F_theta = 2 epsilon sin(theta) sin(2 chi),
    F_phi = sin(theta)
              + 2 epsilon sin(theta)cos(theta)cos(2 chi).

Check its divergence and regularity explicitly. Vary epsilon, include order-one non-axisymmetric perturbations, and do not confine the campaign to perturbations of an axisymmetric seed.

Compute H = the leading coefficient of curl U_0. Record its zeros and minimum magnitude, but do not impose a nowhere-zero H as an allegedly necessary condition without an independently verified theorem. A nonvanishing leading H would avoid one specific type of tail degeneracy; it would not establish compatibility with every exclusion theorem.

## 4. Primary representation and pressure treatment

Prefer a divergence-free spectral representation

    U = curl(T y) + curl curl(S y).

Use spherical harmonics or regular vector spherical harmonics and radial spectral elements. Remove potential null modes and document their gauges.

Near the origin, use regular solid-harmonic/Taylor-compatible basis functions. For example, scalar harmonic coefficients can be built from r^ell times smooth functions of r^2. Do not represent a nonsmooth Cartesian field by apparently smooth spherical components.

Use a core domain, radial shells, and an explicitly treated exterior, or a carefully factored compactified exterior. Factor the fractional decay powers and logarithmic spiral analytically. Do not demand analyticity of an unfactored compactified endpoint.

A possible smooth angular twist for scalar potentials is

    phi + omega/(2*c_l) * log(1 + r^2/R_0^2).

It is regular at the origin and has the required logarithmic behavior at infinity. Apply this construction to potentials and then take the actual curls, including all radial derivatives of the twist. An r-dependent rotation of an already divergence-free vector field does not automatically preserve divergence-free structure.

Derive separate radial envelopes for the two potentials. Schematically, T of order r^(-alpha) and S of order r^(1-alpha) can both contribute velocity of order r^(-alpha); verify the actual coefficients and exceptional null modes.

When c_l or omega is optimized, differentiate the entire parameter-dependent representation, including the decay exponent, twist, matching, and pressure reconstruction. Freezing these derivatives gives an incorrect Jacobian.

Use a vorticity residual for inexpensive discovery when it benchmarks well. Compare against a primitive-variable or projected-momentum formulation; high derivatives of poloidal potentials may make the vorticity formulation poorly conditioned. Choose based on measured performance and validation, not ideology.

### Full-space pressure is mandatory

Recover pressure from

    -Delta P = sum_{i,j}(partial_i U_j)(partial_j U_i),

with the full-space decaying pressure convention, up to an irrelevant constant. Equivalently, for this decaying class,

    P = sum_{i,j} R_i R_j(U_i U_j).

Do not impose P=0 at a finite outer boundary, and do not add a free harmonic pressure to hide a momentum residual.

A useful radial Green-function implementation is the following. If f is the right-hand side of -Delta P=f and f_{ell,m}(r) is its spherical-harmonic coefficient, then

    P_{ell,m}(r) = 1/(2ell+1) * [
        r^(-ell-1) integral_0^r s^(ell+2) f_{ell,m}(s) ds
        + r^ell integral_r^infinity s^(1-ell) f_{ell,m}(s) ds
    ].

Include the exterior integral using the resolved tail and quantify its uncertainty. Check the formula and normalization convention on manufactured examples. Cross-check selected values with an independent free-space kernel or separately discretized Poisson solver. Do not treat the pressure of the truncated interior source as the pressure of the full field.

## 5. Gauges, nontriviality, and honest residuals

The exact dilation symmetry is

    U_L(y) = L U(y/L),    P_L(y) = L^2 P(y/L).

It leaves c_l and omega unchanged. In particular,

    curl U_L(y) = Omega(y/L).

Thus dilation does not freely adjust vorticity amplitude or DU(0). Do not impose arbitrary gradient/vorticity normalizations and call them gauge fixing.

For the primary nonzero-tail class, a useful single dilation gauge is

    integral_S2 |F|^2 dS = 1.

Under dilation this norm changes by L^(2*(alpha+1)), while the angular pattern changes by a rotation. Verify this covariance. Do not simultaneously fix an independent core radius as a second dilation gauge. An alternative one-gauge formulation is allowed, with its equivalence documented.

Fix the global azimuthal phase with a nonzero Fourier coefficient, including a safe pivot change when that coefficient vanishes. Fix only the additive pressure gauge consistently with the pressure convention.

Track non-axisymmetric content in both the core and tail. Do not declare a candidate genuinely rotating just because omega is nonzero: verify that

    J U - (J y · grad)U

is not identically zero and does not collapse relative to the resolved field under refinement. Do not insist that m=2 remains nonzero if another allowed non-axisymmetric mode carries the solution.

Use absolute residuals, per-term relative residuals, weighted shell diagnostics, and maximum residuals together. Never rank solely by a training average or a residual divided by an adjustable large denominator.

For each spherical shell, report the RMS of the residual and the RMS of every individual term. A relative defect may use their summed RMS as denominator, but near-zero denominators must be flagged rather than silently treated as success.

Use a core ball plus logarithmically spaced shells. Balance shell contributions rather than letting volume or point count dominate. For large r, inspect at least

    r^alpha R_u,    r^(1/c_l) R_Omega,

in a fixed gauge. Also test the next-order coefficient equations and appropriately rescaled remainders: the leading tail solves the linear equation automatically, so a naive far-field loss can become small while the nonlinear matching is wrong.

Build a dilation-invariance regression test for the candidate-ranking diagnostics, rescaling evaluation domains as well as fields. Report fixed-gauge absolute values separately.

Guard against zero-field collapse, almost-axisymmetric collapse, thin spikes, unresolved high-frequency oscillations, a core drifting outside the sampled region, and a nonzero tail whose onset is pushed to arbitrarily large radii. Track core location, width, amplitude, spectral coefficient decay, and tail-onset radius.

A useful necessary check is available: at a nonzero global maximum of |Omega| for a smooth decaying exact profile,

    Omega · sym(DU) Omega = |Omega|^2.

Derive this from the vorticity equation. Consequently the largest strain eigenvalue must reach at least 1 somewhere. Use it to diagnose vanishing-amplitude false solutions, not as an artificial pointwise boundary condition.

First pass the operator, regularity, derivative, covariance, and pressure tests above. Verify the first two far-field orders independently. Check local Taylor residuals near the origin rather than merely omitting r=0 from collocation. In particular, if U(0)=u_0 and DU(0)=M, the constant-order equations include

    trace M = 0,
    (1-c_l)u_0 + M u_0 + omega J u_0 + grad P(0) = 0.

Do not set u_0=0 without justification. Extend the Taylor audit through at least the quadratic residual terms, and higher when the representation supports it.

## I.2. Launch a diverse parameter/initialization search

Start with exploratory values such as

    c_l in {0.41, 0.43, 0.45, 0.47, 0.49},
    |omega| in {0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10}.

Include representative negative omega checks unless an exact symmetry equivalence has been derived and tested. These points are search choices, not predicted solution parameters. Extend ranges when evidence motivates it. Near-boundary branches require extra conditioning checks and must not silently be relabeled as interior solutions.

Begin with fixed-parameter solves; then allow both c_l and omega to move. A smooth bounded parameterization is acceptable, but watch for artificial saturation at imposed bounds.

Use diverse seeds: the explicit tail family, mixed poloidal/toroidal random fields, different core sizes before gauge fixing, distinct helicity distributions, order-one non-axisymmetric fields, and low-residual fields obtained from unrelated initialization methods.

Aim for roughly 100–300 distinct inexpensive starts if measured throughput permits. Diversity and honest refinement matter more than achieving a nominal count. Preserve unsuccessful starts and their failure reasons.

An initial spectral ladder might use maximum spherical degree 6, 10, and 14, with about 16, 24, and 36 radial degrees of freedom per appropriate domain. Adapt these choices to benchmarks. Do not judge existence or nonexistence at a single resolution.

Evaluate nonlinear products with sufficient angular and radial padding. Test aliasing by substantially increasing quadrature resolution, not by trusting a generic padding factor.

Use trust-region/Gauss-Newton, Levenberg-Marquardt, L-BFGS, or a measured combination. For large systems use matrix-free Jacobian actions and preconditioning. Do not construct a dense global Jacobian by default. Compare at least two optimization strategies on representative seeds.

## I.3. Start the independent route now

Do not wait until day six to implement the second method. Reserve resources during Stage I for a genuinely independent Cartesian/vector-potential PINN, smooth spline collocation, or another discretization with separately implemented derivatives.

At least one route should evaluate the primitive momentum equation with an independent pressure treatment rather than reproduce the primary spectral curl residual. Any neural architecture must use sufficiently smooth activations and represent the far-field spiral/decay without introducing an origin singularity.

Conduct independent random starts before transferring primary candidates. Transferring a spectral solution into a neural network is useful cross-validation, but is not an independent discovery.

## I.4. Stage-I gate

Deliver a tested solver, a running independent route, a reproducible sweep, and a ranked shortlist based on holdout diagnostics. Keep several structurally different branches, not merely the lowest training losses.

A Stage-I candidate is only a discovery lead. It must have measurable nonzero vorticity, non-axisymmetric content, and no obvious core/tail escape or singularity. Document its full residual vector and resource cost.

If no candidate is promising, continue rather than stop. Diagnose parameter trapping, normalization errors, pressure truncation, missing modes, derivative conditioning, and representation limitations. Reallocate part of Stage II to corrected search and removal of the two-fold symmetry restriction.

# STAGE II — Refinement, continuation, and full-space matching, hours 48–120

## II.1. Refine several genuinely distinct branches

For the best roughly 3–8 branches, independently increase radial resolution, angular resolution, nonlinear quadrature, and exterior accuracy. Use at least three meaningful refinement levels for each serious candidate.

Possible larger levels include spherical degrees 20, 28, and 40 and radial sizes 48, 72, and 108 per suitable domain, but measured convergence determines the actual choices. Never exceed available memory just to follow these illustrative numbers.

Use damped Newton-Krylov or a robust large-scale least-squares refinement. Precondition the similarity/transport structure and remove gauge null directions correctly. Verify Jacobian actions after each representation change.

Study continuation in c_l, omega, and non-axisymmetric content. Use pseudo-arclength continuation when ordinary parameter continuation encounters a fold. The parameters may belong to a continuous branch; do not assume a uniquely selected pair without evidence.

Record whether a branch approaches c_l=1/2, c_l=2/5, omega=0, large |omega|, an axisymmetric limit, or unbounded derivatives. These are important outcomes, not successful interior profiles.

## II.2. Treat infinity as part of the problem

Compare at least two tail/matching strategies, such as a factored compactified exterior and a finite collection of shells with explicit analytic tail integrals.

Vary matching radii substantially, for example over factors of two. Refine velocity and derivative matching, pressure values/derivatives, and the asymptotic coefficient equations. Do not overdetermine a first-order transport problem by arbitrarily prescribing every velocity and pressure component at an artificial boundary.

Evaluate the logarithmically untwisted leading field on new radii and angles. For spherical components, test convergence of

    r^alpha U_sph(r, theta, chi - (omega/c_l)log r)

toward one nonzero F(theta,chi). Do the corresponding derivative and vorticity tests. Report convergence errors, not only fitted log-log slopes.

Subtract the leading term and test the predicted first correction. Inspect deviations before forcing them into the assumed expansion: they may indicate globally generated pressure terms, resonance, or a failed asymptotic model.

Track both training and untouched holdout grids in the core, axis neighborhoods, matching layers, and far exterior. Periodically create new final-test points that were not used for adaptive sampling or model selection.

## II.3. Recover pressure and refine the original equation

For every candidate surviving initial refinement, reconstruct full-space pressure and evaluate all components of R_u. The pressure method must include the exterior velocity, not merely a boundary fit.

A small curl residual with a large momentum residual is a rejected candidate or a signal to repair pressure/boundary handling. It is not evidence of an exact solution.

Where practical, compare velocity against an independent full-space Biot-Savart reconstruction from Omega, with exterior contributions included. Document convergence and any harmonic ambiguity instead of assuming it away.

## II.4. Stage-II gate

By the end of Stage II, produce either a small set of refinement-stable candidates with independently checked pressure, or a precise failure map identifying which assumptions or numerical mechanisms prevent convergence.

If losses stagnate, test alternative bases, primal versus curl formulations, randomized continuation, and unrestricted azimuthal modes. Do not spend all remaining resources repeatedly restarting the same unsuccessful local optimization.

# STAGE III — Independent replication and adversarial validation, hours 120–168

The purpose of this stage is to disprove apparent successes whenever possible. Preserve enough time for it even when Stage II remains difficult.

## III.1. Independent representation and implementation

Reconstruct serious candidates in the second representation and refine using its own differential operators, pressure treatment, and holdout points. Distinguish independent discovery from independent verification of transferred data.

Evaluate selected points using independent Cartesian derivatives or high-order finite differences with a step-size convergence study. Use increased precision on small representative problems and cancellation-sensitive far-field evaluations.

Compare CPU and GPU results, different quadratures, and different nonlinear dealiasing levels. A shared plotting grid or shared residual routine is not an independent validation.

## III.2. Exact identities and necessary conditions

Derive these identities independently and test them at many radii, accounting for numerical and tail errors.

Energy-flux identity:

    (5*c_l-2) integral_{B_R} |U|^2/2 dy
      = integral_{partial B_R} [
           (|U|^2/2 + P)(U · n) + c_l R |U|^2/2
        ] dS.

For a nonzero leading tail of the specified type, check consistency with

    integral_{B_R} |U|^2 dy ~ R^(5-2/c_l),

including the coefficient predicted by F, not only the exponent.

Helicity identity, when the verified decay makes the integrations legitimate:

    (1-2*c_l) integral_{R^3} U · Omega dy = 0.

Thus the target range requires zero total helicity under these assumptions. Estimate the missing exterior integral; near c_l=1/2 its convergence can be slow. Do not enforce pointwise zero helicity or discard a field because a finite-ball integral is nonzero.

Check the vorticity-maximum strain identity described earlier. Check divergence and flux conservation across spheres. Check the Poisson equation independently of momentum.

Reconstruct

    V(y,s) = exp(omega s J) U(exp(-omega s J)y)

and verify the ordinary time-dependent similarity equation at several s. Its period 2*pi/|omega| provides a further rotation-sign check; a smaller period may follow from symmetry.

## III.3. Robustness and anti-artifact tests

Perturb candidates and re-solve from several nearby starting points. Change matching radii, twist regularization radius R_0, radial maps, gauges, shell weights, and far-field truncation orders. Compare fields after appropriate gauge alignment.

Release optional symmetry constraints and check that the full Euler residual remains small. Do not require dynamical stability: an unstable stationary profile can still be a valid solution. Failure of forward time relaxation is not a nonexistence test.

Look for unresolved axis/origin singularities, narrow sheets, angular oscillations, an escaping core, vanishing non-axisymmetric content, and residual concentrated between collocation nodes. Use targeted maximization of residual and derivative magnitudes to search for hidden defects.

If investigating backward W-trajectories, label it exploratory dynamics. Finite integration time cannot prove convergence, recurrence, or a global dynamical exclusion theorem.

## III.4. Literature audit with explicit hypotheses

Check the exact assumptions of the references below against each candidate. Distinguish physical velocity/vorticity from profile fields, uniform-in-time conditions from fixed-time conditions, and strict decay improvements from a critical power law.

Do not treat any earlier chat claim about a universal nonrotating Liouville theorem, a point-sequence condition, or automatic applicability of Chae's theorem as verified input. Obtain the actual theorem and map its assumptions. If the full text is unavailable, record the limitation rather than reconstructing a theorem from an abstract.

Do not claim that a zero of H automatically triggers a time-uniform physical-space hypothesis. Conversely, do not claim that a nowhere-zero H evades every theorem.

A contradiction with a valid theorem is evidence to audit the numerics or the hypothesis mapping, not to announce a counterexample without a much stronger investigation.

## 7. Acceptance targets and interpretation

Pre-register the exact norms and acceptance thresholds before selecting final candidates. The following are ambitious numerical goals, not existence certificates:

    weighted/per-term relative RMS residuals around 1e-6 or below;
    progressively smaller residuals under at least three refinements;
    no unexplained localized maximum residuals;
    stable core and untwisted tail after gauge alignment;
    agreement of independent momentum and pressure evaluations;
    parameter/field stability within measured discretization uncertainty.

Aim substantially lower when convergence permits. Report maximum defects, individual shell values, conditioning, and observed convergence rates even when they are less flattering than the aggregate loss. Never weaken thresholds after seeing a favorite candidate without prominently recording the change and keeping the original assessment.

Use four outcome labels:

    A. Strong numerical candidate:
       nontriviality, smoothness diagnostics, full-space matching, refinement,
       and independent validation all survive. Not a proof of existence.

    B. Promising but unresolved:
       a structured candidate exists, but specified defects or uncertainties
       remain. State them quantitatively.

    C. Search unsuccessful in a precisely documented class:
       give parameter ranges, representations, symmetry restrictions,
       initializations, budgets, and observed failure modes. Not nonexistence.

    D. Rigorous obstruction:
       use only with a complete argument covering explicit hypotheses,
       or a mathematically certified result whose scope is precisely stated.
       Do not upgrade a local numerical optimization failure to this label.

If an approximate profile is found, preserve what would be needed for later computer-assisted analysis: exact basis conventions, coefficients, tail estimates, linearization actions, gauge conditions, and independent error measurements. Do not claim interval certification unless it was actually performed with valid bounds.
