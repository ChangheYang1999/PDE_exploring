# A Schwartz pressure reference with a degree-two angular component

Status: proved analytic identity. This reference tests a Poisson solver; its source is not asserted to be generated by a velocity field.

Fix any real constant beta. For y=(x_1,x_2,x_3) in R^3 set r=|y|, q(y)=1+beta*x_1*x_2 and

P(y)=exp(-r^2)q(y),
f(y)=exp(-r^2)[6-4r^2+beta*(14-4r^2)*x_1*x_2].

Then P and f belong to the Schwartz space S(R^3), -Delta P=f, and P is the unique C^2(R^3) solution of that equation that tends to zero as |y| tends to infinity. Its gradient is

grad P(y)=exp(-r^2)[beta*(x_2,x_1,0)-2*y*q(y)].

The angular support of f on each sphere is contained in spherical harmonic degrees zero and two. For each r>0, the full-space spherical Green formula applied to these source coefficients gives the coefficients of P. No interior source moment may be deleted from that formula.

## Proof

A polynomial times a Gaussian and every derivative of such a function decay faster than every inverse power, proving the Schwartz claim. Write E=exp(-r^2). Direct differentiation gives grad E=-2yE and Delta E=(4r^2-6)E. The polynomial h=x_1*x_2 is homogeneous of degree two and harmonic, so Delta h=0 and y dot grad h=2h. The product rule gives

Delta(Eh)=h Delta E+2 grad E dot grad h+E Delta h=(4r^2-14)Eh.

Combining this with the formula for Delta E proves -Delta P=f; the ordinary product rule proves the displayed gradient. If P_1 is another decaying C^2 solution, P_1-P is harmonic. For every epsilon>0 its absolute value is less than epsilon outside a sufficiently large ball. The maximum principle on that ball bounds its absolute value by epsilon everywhere inside as well. Sending epsilon to zero proves uniqueness.

The constant is a degree-zero spherical harmonic. The restriction of the harmonic homogeneous polynomial h to the unit sphere is a degree-two spherical harmonic. Radial multiplication preserves these angular degrees, proving the support statement. Separation of the Poisson equation in an orthonormal spherical harmonic basis gives, for degree ell and order m,

P_lm(r) = [r^(-ell-1) integral_0^r s^(ell+2) f_lm(s) ds + r^ell integral_r^infinity s^(1-ell) f_lm(s) ds]/(2ell+1).

Both integrals converge by smoothness at zero and Gaussian decay. Differentiating them verifies the radial equation, regularity, and decay, so the preceding uniqueness identifies this Green solution with P. The two integrals describe the raw source. Any cancellation of a full-space moment must emerge from evaluation, not from a replacement of the finite interior integral by zero.

## Dependencies and scope

Dependencies are the product rule, Euler's identity for homogeneous polynomials, the maximum principle for harmonic functions, and the separated spherical Green formula justified in ../pressure_consistency/PROOF.md. Floating-point evaluations and numerical quadratures of these expressions are observations and are not error enclosures. This example makes no Euler momentum or optimization claim.
