Scope of "must use" statements (A2742-A2745, recorded 2026-11-04T02:07Z). A model that satisfies a specified list of
premises and violates a conclusion shows only that those premises alone do not imply the conclusion; a proof needs
some further property that the model fails. Where an entry below says that a proof or an exclusion must use a named
equation or property, read it in that sense: the named item is one such further property, not the only possible one.
Likewise a formal Lyapunov exclusion means that no nonconstant function on the admissible jet space qualifies;
constants always do.

## O1. Rotation destroys the Bernoulli Lyapunov function
B.grad H = (2c-1)(|X|^2 - omega M), and for the nonrotating function H0 = H + omega M,
B.grad H0 = (2c-1)|X|^2 - omega d_theta P (CO1; sympy check). The defect is the pressure torque. In the
rotating-frame form (B.grad)B + 2 omega J B + (1-2c)B + (1-2c) omega Jy + grad Q = 0 (Q a pressure-like scalar),
the term (1-2c) omega Jy is a non-conservative azimuthal body force, while (1-2c)B acts as linear friction: the
B-dynamics is that of a driven damped fluid, which in general supports periodic and chaotic regimes.
Exact non-decaying examples with circular backward limit sets exist (LIT06: U = diag(-c,-c,2c)y).

## O2. Local spectral transfer from L2 fails
At an irrotational zero, lambda_max(S) >= 1 does not give a contracting direction of DB = cI + S - omega J when
omega != 0 (A017: U = (x,-y,0), omega = 2, eigenvalues c, c +- i sqrt 3).

## O3. Conformally symplectic structure allows strange attractors
Omega x B = -(1-2c)U - grad Htil (CO1, C3), so on the quotient by vortex lines the B-flow is conformally symplectic
with L_B w2 = (2c-1)w2. Conformally symplectic dissipative maps (e.g. dissipative standard maps) have strange
attractors, so this structure alone cannot exclude complicated backward limit sets. This comparison is an
illustration of a gap in the method, not a proof that the global three-dimensional Euler quotient has a strange
attractor.

## O4. Kinematics plus positive divergence plus decay do not suffice
Compactly supported divergence-free perturbations of (cI - omega J)y can have periodic orbits (A007). Stronger
(CO6, submitted; construction in the team records, parameters checked by check_abstract_cex.py (accompanying artifacts)): for
every 0 < c < 1/2 and omega there is a compactly supported smooth divergence-free U and a nonzero C^1 (even C^j)
divergence-free W with [B, W] = -(1+c)W on all of R^3, W decaying like |y|^{-1/c}, W nowhere zero on a
Lorenz-type trapping region of the backward flow and tangent to its strong stable direction. So the package (Cauchy law for a W not
assumed equal to curl U, div B = 3c, these decay and regularity properties) cannot by itself exclude the remaining
case (this concerns that package, not every kinematic argument; A066 asserts no transitive attractor); the coupling W = curl U (equivalently
B - Ay = Biot-Savart of W) must be used. Theory Agent B reached the same conclusion independently (in the team records 4.5, B07).
The regular (non-Lorenz) counterpart is the marked regular model (CONFIRMED A242-A243, the literature agent LIT48; CO19 is a second
proof of its meridian step, CONFIRMED A246): the same package
admits a transitive uniformly hyperbolic regular backward attractor with W tangent to its strong stable line, for
every c in (0, 1/2) and every omega (see O5 (i)). The Lorenz-type counterpart now has an attractor as well: a
parameter variant of CO6 (Theory Agent B B24, CONFIRMED A252-A253) has, for every 2(2 - sqrt 2)/(5 - sqrt 2) < c < 1/2 and every
omega, a transitive singular-hyperbolic backward attractor containing a zero of B that is the backward limit of
Lebesgue-almost every point of a trapping genus-two handlebody, with W nowhere zero there.
Which known identities survive the decoupling (in the team records section 9(c)): circulation law, the
conformally symplectic identity C3 and the helicity law hold kinematically for the primitive beta of i_W vol;
the energy identity div(Bq + PU) = (5c/2-1)|U|^2 does not, because it uses U^T DU U = U.grad q. So the energy
theorem (A040/A052) and the invariant-measure relation int U.grad P dmu = -(1-c) int |U|^2 dmu are the
Euler-specific inputs known so far.
Excluded already: point and single-cycle backward limits of a positive-measure vortical set (LIT03, B03), and
vortical periodic orbits are saddles (A028). The periodic-orbit circulation constraint is a consequence of the Bernoulli/circulation
identity (Theory Agent A C6, in the team records: U.B is a C^1 coboundary along B for every solution), so by itself it is
redundant; it still supplies the recorded retrograde and period conditions and may combine with other geometry.

## O6. Sign-changing helicity on an essential basin (added 2026-11-03 01:16Z)
After smooth coverage (A308), the helicity-sign Liouville theorem (A309), its scope (A317, A318) and the levelwise
refinement (A326-A327), the C-infinity problem is P1: can a nonzero C-infinity decaying profile have an invariant open
essential basin D_nu on which U.Omega takes both signs on the same Bernoulli level H = P + abs(U)^2/2 + (Ay).U, for
every level in an open interval? (At C^2 the coverage step is open, A318.) Necessary conditions known on such a basin:
H0 is not monotone along the vortex line through every recurrent point with U.Omega != 0 (A283); same-level crossings
on one recurrent orbit (A326); equal integrals of (U.Omega)_+ and (U.Omega)_- over every Bernoulli band of every
vortex-invariant set (A328); int_D U.Omega = 0 under (D) (A302); on a regular basin, zero helicity is equivalent to
constancy of H or of U.B along vortex lines, to a carrier period of U.B, and to constancy of H (A320); almost every
vortex line has U.Omega identically zero or both strict signs arbitrarily far in both directions (A348; the
first kind is null for real-analytic profiles, A349). None of these gives a contradiction, for the following
reasons.
 (i) Gauge. The identities Omega.grad H = -(1-2c)U.Omega and B.grad H = -(1-2c)U.B, and the identities derived from
     them with the coefficient R = H - U.B = P - abs(U)^2/2, are preserved by H -> H + F, R -> R + F + B.grad F/(1-2c) for any
     compactly supported F (A322; with the physical R fixed the substitution fails). An argument that treats R as an
     independent scalar cannot see F. In the velocity variables the same freedom is (H0, U) -> (H0 + F,
     U - grad F/(1-2c)) with B and Omega fixed, which breaks only div U = 0 and B = Ay + U (coordinator notes
     coord_math section 25.3; exploration).
 (ii) Reduced scalar countertest. A compact scalar equal to 1 near the regular attractor of the kinematic model
     satisfies every listed reduced scalar identity, the finite band balance and the Bernoulli mean bounds (LIT71,
     A329).
 (iii) Biot-Savart model. The B27 model (Theory Agent B, A331) has a prescribed regular carrier, the Biot-Savart velocity of its
     transported vorticity, a decaying Lamb potential, the subcritical energy bound, L^1 helicity of both signs on the
     regular basin with zero integral, for 2/5 < c < 1/2 and every omega. What fails is the vector equation: the
     momentum residual (U_1 - v) x W is nonzero (A332), the Lamb-derived pressure has a positive Poisson defect at
     infinity (A337, LIT72), and no multiple of the velocity is a profile for any pressure (A338). A338 uses the fixed
     vorticity of the model, so it does not apply when carrier and vorticity change together.
 (iv) Averaging and occupation. The L^2 Bernoulli decay used in A309 is the ball bound
     int_(abs(y)<R) H0^2 <= C R^(7-2/c); on a fixed ball it is weaker than the volume bound for c > 2/7 (coordinator
     coord_math 25.1). Under an extra bounded-density hypothesis, occupation of a fixed ball up to vortex time T is
     O(T^((1-2c)/(1+c))), which against the energy bound gives a contradiction only for c < 2/5 (abs(U)^2) or c < 2/7
     (H0^2) (the external referee (a second model), CONFIRMED in A370 with the bounded density as an explicit extra hypothesis and
     an attractor with a density lower bound for the matching averaged lower bound; without the density hypothesis the
     smear is only L^1; the auditor proposals/srb_vortex_averages for the averaged form, coordinator review
     in the team records; CONFIRMED in A374-A375: nu-typical vortex semiorbits are unbounded and recurrent,
     and supports avoiding {B = 0} and {Omega = 0} give bounded smear density, so the rates then hold without (D)). Under (D), abs(H0) = O((1+abs(y))^(-a/c)) and higher
     powers saturate at the volume rate e^(-3ct) (A371).
     Conservativity and ergodicity alone give no polynomial occupation rate (LIT70, A325); Theory Agent B's pointwise version
     is OPEN (A334).
     (Added 03:47Z from Theory Agent A Note A11; CONDITIONAL on (D) with the normalized pressure.) The fixed-set means have exact
     leading terms: e^(p a t) int_Q abs(H0 o Phi_t)^p -> int_(S(Q minus K)) abs(tau)^p for p < 3c/a, where S is the
     scattering map and tau the Bernoulli tail coefficient, and the same holds for abs(U)^p with rate p(1-c),
     p < 3c/(1-c) (A408). The constants are scattering data. supp nu enters only through the e^(-3ct) core term, whose
     time average is at least sum_nu nu(Q) int_(D_nu) abs(F)^p for every open Q, every observable and every power
     (A409). In 2/5 < c < 1/2 both abs(U)^2 and H0^2 are in the escaping regime (crossovers c = 2/5 and c = 2/7), so the
     rate e^(-2at) is attained and is compatible with the floor. On each basin either the tail coefficient tau o S
     vanishes on D minus K (then the tail satisfies V.curl V = 0 on S(D minus K)) or its square has positive integral on
     every open set in D meeting supp nu (A410). That this closes fixed-set mean estimates as a route is a research
     judgment (A412).
 (v) Local and tail compatibility. An exact local profile on r > 0 near a saddle periodic orbit has helicity 2bz of
     both signs (A335). A twisted-homogeneous field solving the linear tail equation, with the Lamb form of the tail,

Poisson pair. At a point with V = DV = 0 and P = 0, a pointwise lower bound P >= gamma abs(V)^2 on a ball forces
V = P = 0 in these cases: gamma = 1/4 with V of class C2 and P of class C1, sharp in that class (the literature agent LIT315,
A2668-A2670); gamma = 1/4 - delta(abs(x)) with a Dini deficit, while delta -> 0 alone does not suffice (LIT316,
A2692-A2694); gamma > 1/6 with V of class C3 and P of class C4, sharp (LIT318, LIT336, A2700, A2822); gamma > 1/(4 + 2
alpha) under a Hoelder bound of exponent alpha on D2V at the centre, sharp (LIT323, LIT336, A2736, A2737, A2823). The
constant Gamma_2 of the homogeneous quadratic problem lies in [g_star, 3/25), g_star = 0.11964 to five decimals (LIT322,
LIT343, A2718, A2719, A2882, A2883), and there is an exact test for each quadratic field (LIT331-LIT335, A2790, A2791,
A2794, A2805, A2806, A2812-A2814). A quarter bound for centred sphere means alone does not force vanishing (LIT317,
A2696, A2697).
Why the route stops. S < 0 off the orbit gives only the upper bound P < abs(V)^2/2, and no lower bound of these forms is
derived for an actual decaying profile (A2695, A2738, A2847, A2885 and the other OPEN verdicts of the pressure-bound row
of Appendix A). The sharpness pairs are stationary Euler fields or homogeneous polynomial pairs, and none is a decaying
profile for 0 < c < 1/2 and any omega (A2693, A2700, A2709, A2719, A2737); they show the limits of the method, not
counterexamples.

## O15. The energy law and its weighted consequences (added 2026-11-04T09:00Z)
For 2/5 < c < 1/2 a nonzero bounded C^1 profile with U(R.) -> 0 in D' has E(R) = L R^alpha + O(R^(alpha-1)),
alpha = 5 - 2/c in (0, 1), L > 0, about every center (Theorem 1, A3053). The consequences proved from this law are
lower bounds or sign changes. The work w takes both strict signs (A3079), and so does its mean over spheres about the
origin (A3106). The critical positive-source moment int abs(y - a)^(2/c-3) max(q, 0) over abs(y - a) < R is at least
k L log R - C, k = alpha(2 - alpha)(1 - alpha) (the literature agent LIT373 and LIT374, A3094-A3095, A3098-A3099). Radii with a
cumulative source K <= -kappa E have positive logarithmic density (A3087). A local Holder growth bound gives a decay
rate and weak L^(p_V) (LIT375, A3101), and for C^2 profiles with DU = o(abs(y)^(1/2)) the natural rate gives U in weak
L^(3c/(1-c)) and in L^p for p > 3c/(1-c) (A3102), while U = 0 if U lies in L^(3c/(1-c), q) for one finite q.
Why the route stops. A contradiction needs an upper bound or a sign of the same quantity, and none is derived from
decay or from (E): finiteness of the critical moments (A3097, A3100 OPEN), one sign of w or of its spherical means at
large radii (A3081, A3107 OPEN), K(r) -> -infinity (A3089 OPEN), and a finite L^p norm for every bounded Hb or
decaying profile (A3092, A3103 OPEN). The natural bound abs(DU) <= C abs(y)^(-1/c) makes the critical integrand at most
C r^(-1) in r, so the moments grow at most like C log R, the same order as the lower bound; it decides neither
convergence nor divergence (A3096). The kinematic controls of LIT372, LIT373 and LIT375 satisfy the energy law and
solve (E) for no c and omega; they show that the law alone gives no finite L^p or weak L^p norm (A3090), that the
weight 2/c - 3 of the source moment cannot be lowered (A3094), and that the exponent p_V cannot be improved under the
Holder growth bound (A3101). So, in the sense of the scope paragraph above, a proof along this route must use the momentum equation
beyond the energy identity and the Poisson equation for the pressure.
