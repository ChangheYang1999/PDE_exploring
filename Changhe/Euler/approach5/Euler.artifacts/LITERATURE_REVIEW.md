## 1. Conventions and the gap to be closed

Write `c=c_l`, `J(x,y,z)=(-y,x,0)`, `Omega=curl U`, and

\[
 B=(cI-\omega J)y+U,\qquad S=\tfrac12(DU+DU^T).
\]

The target is a whole-space `C^2` divergence-free profile satisfying

\[
 (1-c)U+B\cdot\nabla U+\omega JU+\nabla P=0,\qquad U(y)\longrightarrow0,
 \quad 2/5<c<1/2.
\]

There is no assumed rate of velocity decay, derivative bound at infinity, finite energy, vorticity integrability, symmetry, or restriction on finite `omega`.

Constantin, Ignatova and Vicol use `gamma=c`. Chae and Chae-Shvydkoy use

\[
 \alpha=\frac1c-1,\qquad c=\frac1{1+\alpha}.
\]

Thus the target interval is `1<alpha<3/2`. The formal velocity tail has exponent `alpha`; the formal vorticity tail has exponent `alpha+1=1/c`. These are expected exponents, not consequences established from the target assumptions. The energy-growth exponent is

\[
 3-2\alpha=5-2/c\in(0,1).
\]

Pineau and Vicol instead use `alpha` for their rotation rate. It equals our `omega`, while Navier-Stokes fixes the spatial scaling exponent at `c=1/2`. Those two uses of `alpha` must be kept separate.

Continuous rotation gives an ordinary time-periodic similarity solution. With `R_s=exp(omega J s)`, set

\[
 V(y,s)=R_sU(R_{-s}y),\qquad Q(y,s)=P(R_{-s}y).
\]

Then

\[
 \partial_sV+(1-c)V+(cy+V)\cdot\nabla V+\nabla Q=0,
 \qquad \operatorname{div}V=0.
\]

For nonzero `omega` this is periodic with period `2pi/|omega|`. For zero `omega`, every positive time is a period. Consequently an ordinary discretely self-similar Euler Liouville theorem applies if all its other hypotheses hold. It does not supply those hypotheses. ### 2.1 Finite-energy restriction: Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.2

Let a smooth physical Euler solution start with `omega_0 in C^1(R^3)` and

\[
 u_0=(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\times\omega_0\in L^2(\mathbb R^3).
\]

Suppose it blows up at a finite time `T_*`. If some `gamma>0` satisfies

\[
 \sup_{0\le t<T_*}(T_*-t)^{1+\gamma}
     \|\nabla\omega(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty}<\infty,
\]

then `gamma>=2/5`. If, in addition,

\[
 \sup_{0\le t<T_*}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^p}<\infty
 \quad\text{for some }p\ge2,
\]

Remark 2.2 strengthens this to `gamma>=p/(p+3)`. In particular, a uniform physical `L^3` bound implies `gamma>=1/2`.

These are statements about a physical solution and its uniform norms before blowup. Membership of the profile in `L^p` does not imply the required uniform physical norm. A rotating ansatz preserves spatial norms under its orthogonal factors, so rotation does not spoil the scaling argument. The theorem nevertheless permits the primary interval `2/5<c<1/2`. Nor does `U->0` imply finite profile energy.

### 2.2 Standing profile assumptions in Section 3

The section assumes `U(0)=0` and uniform sublinear growth, equation (3.5). It normalizes a finite nonzero second-derivative norm to

\[
 \|D^2U\|_{L^\infty}=1. \tag{A1}
\]

Most relevant global results also assume a constant `C_flat>0` with

\[
 |U(y)|\le C_{\rm flat}|y|\langle y\rangle^{-1/c},
 \qquad |\Omega(y)|+|DU(y)|\le C_{\rm flat}\langle y\rangle^{-1/c},
 \qquad \langle y\rangle=(1+|y|^2)^{1/2}. \tag{A2}
\]

This is equation (3.8). The preceding paragraph motivates the powers formally and then explicitly assumes the bounds. The paper does not derive (A2) from `U=o(|y|)` or `U->0`. In particular, bounded `D^2U` is not automatic from `U in C^2(R^3)`.

The normalization `U(0)=0` also deserves care when comparing classes. Translation of a similarity profile changes its constant velocity component. For a theorem whose conclusion concerns exactly zero far-field velocity, a normalization must be checked together with that boundary condition. Our translation calculation is in the counterexample audit.

### 2.3 Local outgoing obstruction: Theorem 3.8

For an ordinary stationary profile, let `V=cy+U`. Suppose `p` is a zero of `V`, `Omega(p)!=0`, and some `c_*>=0` and neighborhood of `p` satisfy

\[
 V(y)\cdot(y-p)\ge c_*|y-p|^2.
\]

For a `C^2` profile, Theorem 3.8 concludes

\[
 c\ge\tfrac12+c_*.
\]

This local statement does not need the entire stagnation set to be finite. Its proof combines the vorticity equation at `p`, tracelessness of the strain, and the lower bound `lambda_min(S(p))>=c_*-c`.

The same local conclusion survives rotation if the outgoing inequality is imposed on `B` at a zero of `B`. Indeed,

\[
 (DU(p)-\omega J)\Omega(p)=\Omega(p)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \frac{\Omega(p)\cdot S(p)\Omega(p)}{|\Omega(p)|^2}=1.
\]

Thus `lambda_max(S(p))>=1`; the symmetric part of `DB` is still `cI+S`, and the outgoing inequality again gives `lambda_min(S)>=c_*-c`. Zero trace implies `1<=2(c-c_*)`. This is a direct local calculation, not a deduction that outgoing behavior follows from decay.

### 2.4 Global outgoing theorem: Theorem 3.9

Let `U` be a nontrivial whole-space `C^2` ordinary stationary Euler profile satisfying (A2), normalized by (A1), and subject to the section's conventions. Definition 3.7 requires that the zero set

\[
 N_V=\{p:V(p)=0\}
\]

be finite, and that the same constants `c_*>=0`, `epsilon_*>0` satisfy

\[
 V(y)\cdot(y-p)\ge c_*|y-p|^2
 \quad\text{for every }p\in N_V,\quad |y-p|\le\epsilon_*.
\]

Then Theorem 3.9 gives `c>=1/2`.

The local spectral step above survives rotation. The global proof also uses the ordinary Bernoulli Lyapunov function and capture near stagnation points. Its sign is lost for rotating profiles:

\[
 B\cdot\nabla H=(2c-1)(cy+U)\cdot B.
\]

The last scalar product can be negative, even for exact affine or constant profiles. Therefore replacing `V` by `B` throughout the published proof is invalid. Positive divergence and outward radial behavior outside a ball do not establish its local outgoing hypothesis.

### 2.5 Axisymmetric results: Theorems 4.3 and 4.5

Theorem 4.3 assumes a `C^2` axisymmetric ordinary profile and a fixed point `(r,z)` of the meridional similarity dynamics. If the swirl `U_theta(r,z)` is nonzero there, it concludes `c=1/2`. The proof uses the invariant azimuthal circle and nonzero circulation.

Theorem 4.5 assumes a nontrivial `C^2` axisymmetric profile satisfying (3.5) and (3.8), and concludes `c>=1/2`. It imposes no extra outgoing hypothesis on the meridional dynamics. For `c<1/2`, along backward meridional trajectories,

\[
 \frac{d}{ds}(rU_\theta)=-(1-2c)rU_\theta.
\]

Backward trapping and continuity force swirl to vanish. The remaining equation is

\[
 \frac{d}{ds}\frac{\Omega_\theta}{r}=-(1+c)\frac{\Omega_\theta}{r}.
\]

Whole-space Cartesian `C^2` regularity bounds `Omega_theta/r` on compact meridional sets, including the axis. The same argument then forces all vorticity to vanish. Remark 4.6 treats the swirl-free case for every `c>0` under the stated source assumptions.

Checking these steps shows that uniform sublinear growth suffices for backward trapping; the quantitative bounds (A2) are not needed in those two transport arguments. Harmonic Liouville also works with sublinear velocity. This is the strengthening checked in the earlier approach4 audit. It remains an axisymmetric theorem. For an axisymmetric equivariant vector field, `JU-(Jy)·grad U=0`, so the result applies for every `omega` within that symmetry class. It cannot be presented as the requested unrestricted theorem.

### 2.6 What carries over

The pressure Poisson equation, radial energy-cutoff cancellation, Cauchy transport of the vorticity two-form, and exponential circulation law extend exactly. The ordinary scalar Lyapunov structure and the identification of all relevant backward limit sets with zeros do not. The half-scaling exponent in the circulation law is independent of viscosity and rotation. These distinctions explain both the usefulness and the limit of Reference A.

## 3. Reference B: Pineau and Vicol

Source: *On rotated backwards self-similar solutions of the incompressible 3D Navier-Stokes equations*, arXiv:2607.09619v2, 6 August 2026, archived PDF. The main statements, adjoint-weight construction and identified uses of diffusion have been read directly. This review does not claim an independent verification of every estimate in the manuscript.

### 3.1 Exact scope of Theorem 1.4

Let `u` solve three-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes on `R^3 x [-1,0)` and satisfy, for a fixed constant `C_{U,0}>0`,

\[
 |u(x,t)|\le\frac{C_{U,0}}{|x|+\sqrt{-t}}.
\]

Assume its backward rotated globally self-similar ansatz has profile `U in C^2(R^3)` and rotation parameter `alpha`. Equivalently, the profile obeys

\[
 |U(y)|\le\frac{C_{U,0}}{1+|y|}.
\]

There are thresholds

\[
 0<\alpha_{\rm low}(C_{U,0})\ll1,
 \qquad 1\ll\alpha_{\rm high}(C_{U,0})<\infty
\]

such that `U=0` if `|alpha|<alpha_low` or `|alpha|>alpha_high`. The bound `C_{U,0}` is taken independently of the rotation rate. The result leaves intermediate rates open. It is neither an arbitrary-rotation theorem nor an Euler theorem.

### 3.2 Discrete and rotated discrete statements

Theorem 1.6 assumes the same Navier-Stokes Type I bound and a `C^2` discretely self-similar profile periodic in similarity time with `S=2 log lambda`, `lambda>1`. It gives a threshold `lambda_bar(C_{U,0})>1` such that `1<lambda<lambda_bar` implies triviality.

Theorem 1.7 assumes the same Type I bound and a `C^2` rotated discretely self-similar profile, with rotation `alpha` and period `2 log lambda`. It proves triviality in either of two regimes: bounded sufficiently small `|alpha|` and `1<lambda<lambda_bar`; or sufficiently large `|alpha|` and

\[
 1<\lambda<\lambda_{\rm bar}^{1/(1+\alpha^2)}.
\]

The constants in each regime depend on `C_{U,0}`. The large-rotation statement includes a period restriction that becomes stronger as `|alpha|` grows. It is not a theorem for arbitrary periodic Euler profiles.

### 3.3 Which estimates require viscosity

Lemma 2.1 obtains decay of profile derivatives from the velocity Type I bound by Stokes and parabolic interior regularity. Euler supplies no analogous automatic derivative estimate by this argument.

Proposition 3.1 reduces triviality to a quantitative small-enstrophy condition on a large ball. The global control includes a gradient-of-vorticity term produced by diffusion. Deleting the Laplacian deletes that positive term.

Proposition 5.1 constructs a strictly positive `C^2` adjoint weight for

\[
 L^*=-\Delta-(U+y/2)\cdot\nabla-3/2.
\]

Assuming the source's velocity and derivative bounds, for each `epsilon in (0,1)` there are positive constants `m(epsilon,C_{U,0})` and `M(epsilon,C_{U,0})` with

\[
 L^*w=0,\qquad
 m e^{-(1+\epsilon)|y|^2/4}\le w(y)
 \le M e^{-(1-\epsilon)|y|^2/4}.
\]

This density balances diffusion and the outward linear drift. There is a direct obstruction to transplanting it: if `div B=3c>0`, every backward `B` trajectory is bounded, and `w` is continuous and satisfies `div(Bw)=0`, then along that trajectory

\[
 w(\Phi_{-t}y)=e^{3ct}w(y).
\]

Boundedness on the compact backward orbit forces `w(y)=0`. A nonzero smooth inviscid adjoint density of this form therefore cannot exist. The complete argument, with exact hypotheses, is in the adjoint obstruction, CONFIRMED by the independent auditor as A021. Singular invariant measures are not excluded.

Angular projections, skew cancellation of the rotation generator in radial weighted inner products, and the conversion between rotating and periodic coordinates survive at the algebraic level. They provide identities, not the diffusion-dependent coercivity or a uniform nonlinear Fourier estimate. Reference B should be used to identify structures and missing estimates, not as an inviscid proof.

## 4. Reference C: Chae and Shvydkoy

Source: *On formation of a locally self-similar collapse in the incompressible Euler equations*, arXiv:1201.6009v2, 12 April 2012, archived PDF. The exact theorem statements, pressure convention, vorticity proof, homogeneous-profile proof and explicit examples have been checked. The review below retains the source's general dimension `N` where appropriate.

The paper's profile equation (6) puts the nonlinear term on the opposite side from our ordinary equation. It is converted by `v=-U`, `q=P`, and `alpha=1/c-1`. This sign convention does not change the norm hypotheses.

### 4.1 Associated pressure is part of the velocity results

Unless otherwise specified, the source uses the pressure associated with the velocity:

\[
 q=\sum_{i,j}R_iR_j(v_iv_j),
\]

with the principal-value interpretation in equation (8). For finite `p>2`, this gives the usual `L^{p/2}` pressure when `v in L^p`; for bounded velocity the natural space is BMO modulo constants. One must not add an arbitrary harmonic pressure to these theorems.

A separate campaign argument proves that the target equation with bounded decaying velocity forces the canonical pressure gradient, modulo an additive constant. Its proof is in the auditor's pressure-normalization topic and is CONFIRMED as A031 after an independent challenge in this agent. This does not assert pointwise pressure decay. The source theorem itself does not remove this hypothesis by merely writing the pressure Poisson equation.

### 4.2 Velocity integrability: Theorem 3.2

Suppose `v in L^p(R^N) intersect C^1_loc(R^N)` for some `3<=p<=infinity`, with associated pressure. If

\[
 -1<\alpha\le N/p\qquad\text{or}\qquad\alpha>N/2,
\]

then the archived theorem states `v=0`. Its literal bounded endpoint `p=infinity, alpha=0` needs qualification: a nonzero constant velocity has associated constant BMO pressure and satisfies the complete equation, while the outer normalized energy stays positive. The exact endpoint audit, LIT239, is independently CONFIRMED A1481-A1485. The unqualified bounded statement is therefore not used at that endpoint. The journal revision has not been inspected.

For `N=3` and `1<alpha<3/2`, neither alternative holds for any `p>=3`. In particular, boundedness and decay do not place the primary interval in its exclusion range. Stronger velocity integrability can be useful by other energy arguments, but it must be stated.

Under the same framework, Corollary 3.4 treats

\[
 N/p<\alpha\le N/2
\]

and gives

\[
 \int_{|y|\le R}|v(y)|^2\,dy\le C R^{N-2\alpha}
\]

for large `R`, with a constant depending on the solution and the hypotheses. In the target interval this is `E(R)<=C R^{5-2/c}`. The power is positive, so the estimate permits infinite profile energy. It is not a contradiction with `U->0`.

### 4.3 Energy-conservative exponent: Theorem 3.1

This theorem is in the subsection with `alpha=N/2`. Assume `v in L^2 intersect C^1_loc`, associated pressure, and constants `delta>0`, `C>0`, `c_0>0` such that, at all sufficiently large radii,

\[
 \frac{c_0}{|y|^{N+1-\delta}}\le |v(y)|\le C|y|^{1-\delta}.
\]

Then `v=0`. The lower bound is a substantive two-sided tail assumption. The result cannot be cited as unconditional critical finite-energy nonexistence. In three dimensions its exponent is `c=2/5`, outside the open primary interval.

### 4.4 Vorticity integrability: Theorem 4.1

Let `v in C^1_loc(R^N)` solve the profile equation with `alpha>-1`. Let `varsigma=(Dv+Dv^T)/2` denote strain and `varpi=Dv-Dv^T` denote vorticity as an antisymmetric tensor. Assume

\[
 |\varsigma(y)|=o(1),\qquad
 \varpi\in L^p(\mathbb R^N)
 \quad\text{for some }0<p<\frac{N}{1+\alpha}.
\]

Then `v` is constant. In three dimensions the integrability threshold is `0<p<3c`. Vanishing strain, rather than all of `Dv`, is the precise source assumption. There is no associated-pressure requirement in this vorticity argument.

The proof first shows that the radial velocity is sublinear from the strain hypothesis. An exterior vorticity identity then forces compact vorticity support; a sufficiently small power in a global identity forces vorticity to vanish. This mechanism survives the rotating equation: `Jy` is tangent to spheres and `Omega dot J Omega=0`. A standalone rotating extension gives all boundary and small-power details and is CONFIRMED as A029. Such an extension still assumes vanishing strain and one subcritical vorticity norm. Neither additional hypothesis is derived from profile decay here.

The endpoint `p=3c` is not included. A nonzero angularly nondegenerate vorticity tail of size `|y|^{-1/c}` lies in `L^p` at infinity for `p>3c`, precisely the other side of this threshold.

### 4.5 Exact homogeneity near infinity: Theorem 4.2

The source assumes global `C^1_loc` regularity and the exact identity

\[
 v(y)=|y|^{-\beta}F(y/|y|)
\]

outside a ball, with `F in C^1(S^{N-1};R^N)`. It concludes triviality if any of the following holds:

1. `0<beta<alpha`.
2. `-1<alpha<beta`.
3. `alpha=beta=N/2`.

The stated exception at `beta=0` is a constant field. These alternatives leave `beta=alpha` in the primary interval unexcluded. Exact homogeneity outside a ball is stronger than a leading-order asymptotic expansion, and neither is proved from decay alone. Rotating profiles may also have logarithmically rotating angular tails, which are not of this exact form.

## 5. Relevant Chae Liouville theorems

### 5.1 The 2007 nonexistence result and its quantifier

Source: *Nonexistence of self-similar singularities for the 3D incompressible Euler equations*, archived arXiv:math/0601060v2, 16 January 2006, later published in 2007, PDF.

Theorem 1.1 excludes a finite-time self-similar blowup with `alpha!=-1` if its vorticity profile belongs to `L^p(R^3)` for **every** `p in (0,p_1)`, for some `p_1>0`. This is not the assertion that one unspecified small `L^p` norm suffices. Exponential vorticity decay or compact support satisfies the condition; a nonzero algebraic tail generally does not.

Theorem 1.2 proves a more general statement for a classical physical Euler flow whose vorticity has the representation

\[
 \omega(x,t)=\Psi(t)\Omega(\Phi(t)x),
\]

where `Psi` is positive continuous, `Phi` is continuous and invertible, and `Omega` lies in every sufficiently small positive `L^p`. It concludes that either `det Phi(t)` is constant or `Omega=0`. Its proof compares the vorticity norm under the incompressible flow and lets `p` tend to zero. The characteristic estimates use finite `integral_0^t ||grad v||_infinity` on each pre-blowup time interval. Thus the theorem should not be applied to a merely locally `C^1` velocity with uncontrolled gradient as though this global classical-flow requirement were absent.

For an exact stationary-profile statement with explicit global derivative control, the next theorem is cleaner. The 2007 wording also concerns blowup, not an unconditional statement that every irrotational nondecaying profile vanishes.

### 5.2 Explicit bounded-gradient version: Chae, Theorem 1.2

Source: *On the uniqueness of solution to the steady Euler equations with perturbations*, archived arXiv:1105.3639v5, 18 September 2012, PDF.

For constants `a,b` with `ab!=0`, let a classical divergence-free vector field solve

\[
 (v\cdot\nabla)v=-\nabla p+av+b(x\cdot\nabla)v.
\]

Assume

\[
 \|\nabla v\|_{L^\infty}<\infty,
 \qquad\operatorname{curl}v\in\bigcap_{0<q<q_0}L^q(\mathbb R^3)
\]

for some `q_0>0`. Then `v=grad h` for an entire harmonic scalar `h`. If also `v(y)->0`, then `v=0`.

For ordinary profiles in the primary interval, take `a=-(1-c)` and `b=-c`. The global derivative bound and the interval of small vorticity exponents are genuine extra assumptions. The proof does not require a pressure normalization. The curl argument can be repeated with the skew rotation term, but the published statement is the ordinary equation displayed above.

### 5.3 Time-periodic maximum-principle theorem

Source: *Euler's equations and the maximum principle*, arXiv:1308.1051v3, 19 June 2014, PDF.

Theorem 1.1 assumes a time-periodic solution `V in C_s^1 C_y^2(R^3 x R)` of

\[
 \partial_sV+\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}V
 +\frac1{1+\alpha}(y\cdot\nabla)V+(V\cdot\nabla)V=-\nabla P,
 \qquad\operatorname{div}V=0,
\]

with nonzero period `S_0`, `alpha!=-1`, and `|D_y V(y,s)|=o(1)` as `|y|->infinity` over a period. If `alpha>-1`, it further assumes some `k>alpha+1` with

\[
 |\operatorname{curl}V(y,s)|=O(|y|^{-k})
\]

at infinity over a period. If `alpha<-1`, no additional vorticity-tail assumption is made. The conclusion is `V(y,s)=C(s)`, spatially constant, with `C` periodic.

The source writes the far-field conditions for all `s in [0,S_0]`; its compact-period estimates use uniform far-field control. For the continuously rotating conversion used here, spatial bounds on `U` give uniform bounds automatically, so this interpretation introduces no extra temporal issue.

For the present problem this gives triviality for **every finite omega**, subject to

\[
 DU(y)\to0,\qquad |\Omega(y)|=O(|y|^{-k}),\qquad k>1/c,
\]

and `U->0`. The vorticity exponent is strictly stronger than the expected `|y|^{-1/c}` tail. The proof first obtains compact vorticity support from a far-field transport maximum principle, then uses small positive powers. Its maximum principle is a first-order transport argument, so it is relevant to Euler despite the absence of diffusion.

Theorem 1.2 treats the more general coefficients `a,b`, `b!=0`: with vanishing derivative, no extra vorticity decay is required if `(a+b)b<0`, while if `(a+b)b>0` it requires `k>(a+b)/b`. The zero-product boundary is not covered by those alternatives. The target has `a=1-c`, `b=c`, hence `(a+b)b=c>0` and threshold `k>1/c`.

The source's sentence after (2.21) needs an intermediate exponent: big-O at rate k only bounds the weight r^(2k)|Omega|^2. Choosing 1/c<theta<k makes r^(2theta)|Omega|^2 vanish while preserving the strict transport sign. The standalone boundary clarification supplies a complete stationary rotating verification and the small-power step. A3267 refutes only the literal same-exponent inference; A3268 confirms the strict-weight repair under the time-uniform bounds used in the source and this exact stationary rotating consequence. The same rotating conclusion is already covered by A029, using 3/k<p<3c. No endpoint or weaker-tail conclusion is added.

### 5.4 Chae and Tsai: the 2014 DSS criteria

Source: Dongho Chae and Tai-Peng Tsai, *On discretely self-similar solutions of the Euler equations*, Mathematical Research Letters 21 (2014), 437-447, DOI `10.4310/MRL.2014.v21.n3.a2`, actual journal PDF. The complete eleven-page manuscript has been read. Its equation (1.6) is precisely the ordinary periodic similarity equation in Section 1, with `alpha>-1` and period `S_0>0`.

**Theorem 2.1.** Let `V in C_x^2 C_t^1(R^3 x R)` be periodic in similarity time, have bounded first derivatives, and satisfy `V(y,s)->0` at spatial infinity for each `s` in a period. If, for some `r>0`,

\[
 \operatorname{curl}V\in\bigcap_{0<q<r}L^q(\mathbb R^3\times[0,S_0]),
\]

then `V=0`. The proof uses the bounded spatial gradient and a global small-power vorticity identity. There is no pressure normalization assumption in this theorem.

**Theorem 2.2.** Under the same regularity and bounded-first-derivative wording, assume

\[
 \lim_{|y|\to\infty}\sup_{0<s<S_0}
       (|V(y,s)|+|D_yV(y,s)|)=0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{curl}V\in L^q(\mathbb R^3\times[0,S_0])
\]

for one `0<q<3/(1+alpha)`. Then `V=0`. The exterior flux identity first makes vorticity compactly supported, after which Theorem 2.1 applies. The source's phrase about bounded first derivatives is preserved here; the proof estimates spatial derivatives. For the rotating problem one can avoid any ambiguity about the time derivative by using the independently written stationary rotating proof, A029, which needs only vanishing strain and the same one-exponent vorticity norm.

**Theorem 3.1.** Let `(V,P) in C^1_loc(R^3 x R)` be periodic and let `P` be the associated pressure given in its equation (3.2). For some `3<=r<=9/2`, either of the following implies `V=0`:

1. `alpha>3/2` and `V in L^3(0,S_0;L^r(R^3))`.
2. `-1<alpha<3/2` and `V in L^2(0,S_0;L^2(R^3)) intersect L^3(0,S_0;L^r(R^3))`.

The critical case `alpha=3/2` is absent. For bounded rotating profiles, finite spatial `L^2` implies the additional `L^3` condition by interpolation, and the orthogonal coordinate factors preserve these norms. The pressure hypothesis can be supplied by campaign A031 under the target's bounded decay assumptions. Finite energy is still an extra assumption in the primary interval.

**Theorem 3.2, with an endpoint correction.** The printed statement assumes `(V,P) in C^1_loc(R^3 x R)`, periodicity with period `S_0>0`, the associated pressure(3.2), and `V in L^p(R^3 x [0,S_0])` for `3<=p<=infinity`. It asserts `V=0` when

\[
 -1<\alpha\le3/p\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad\alpha>3/2.
\]

The literal endpoint `p=infinity, alpha=0` is false. For any nonzero constant vector `v_0`, take `V=v_0` and `P=-|v_0|^2/3`. All derivatives in equation(1.6) vanish. The kernel `K_ij(z)=(3z_i z_j-delta_ij|z|^2)/(4pi|z|^5)` has zero spherical mean, so its integral against the constant tensor over every centered annulus is zero. Thus the centered principal-value form of(3.2) gives this same pressure. The pair is periodic for every positive period and belongs to the stated bounded class. Moreover, for any nonzero nonnegative compact smooth `sigma`,

    L^-3 integral_0^S0 integral |V|^2 sigma(y/L) dy ds
       = S0 |v_0|^2 integral sigma > 0.

The normalized outer energy does not vanish. Integrating the Chae-Shvydkoy proof over a period does not repair that endpoint. This exact counterexample is independently CONFIRMED A1769; A1768 and A1770 refute the literal endpoint conclusion and vanishing boundary term. Outside that qualification, the printed exclusion ranges are those of the stationary theorem in Section4.2. For bounded decaying rotating profiles with canonical pressure, the subcritical energy argument excludes `0<c<2/5` for every finite rotation rate. It does not exclude `2/5<c<1/2`. The false bounded endpoint corresponds to `c=1`, and its constant counterexample fails decay. No target theorem changes.
These DSS results are directly relevant because continuous rotation produces an ordinary periodic solution. They do not require rotation to be small. Their limitations come from the norms and exponent ranges, rather than from an inability to represent the rotating solution.

### 5.6 Xue: energy behavior for DSS profiles

Source: Liutang Xue, *Discretely self-similar singular solutions for the incompressible Euler equations*, arXiv:1408.6619v2, 2 March 2015, PDF. The complete extracted primary manuscript has now been read, with the relevant printed displays inspected. LIT392, independently confirmed at the specified scopes in A3197-A3200, records the time-maximum inference issue in Theorem 1.2, the finite-energy input of its pressure lemma, and a repair of the critical fixed-time pressure estimate. Theorem 1.1 concerns the periodic similarity equation in dimension `N>=3`, with `alpha>-1`, `V in C_s^1 C^3_{y,loc}`, a positive period `S_0`, and the associated pressure of its equation (1.13), up to a function of similarity time. If

 V\in L^3(0,S_0;L^p(\mathbb R^N)),\qquad 3\le p<\infty,
\]

 \sup_{s\in[0,S_0]}\int_{|y|\le R}|V(y,s)|^2\,dy

for all sufficiently large `R`. In the strict range `N/p<alpha<N/2`, either `V=0` or its time-averaged ball energy is bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of `R^(N-2alpha)`.

\]

for sufficiently small positive epsilon and all large `R`. This statement does not itself assert unconditional triviality at the critical exponent.

### 7.1 Exact Yomdin curve-growth input

The original source is Y. Yomdin, *Volume growth and entropy*, Israel Journal of Mathematics 57(3) (1987), pages 285-300, DOI 10.1007/BF02766215. Its journal manuscript has not been obtained. The exact theorem has instead been checked in the full contemporaneous exposition by M. Gromov, *Entropy, homology and semialgebraic geometry [after Y. Yomdin]*, Bourbaki exposé 663, Astérisque 145-146 (1987), pages 225-240, archived PDF. Gromov Sections 2.1-2.2 and 3.1-3.8 have been read, and the formula pages 228-229 rendered.

Let f be a Cr self-map of a compact smooth manifold, with positive integer r, and Y a compact Cr submanifold of dimension ell. Gromov's Theorem 2.2 bounds the exponential growth of the volume of the graph of f^n restricted to Y by


the orbit-complexity entropy restricted to Y plus


the correction `(ell/r) log^+(limsup ||D f^n||_infinity^(1/n))`.

The restricted entropy means the complexity of f-orbits starting in Y; it does not require f(Y) to lie in Y. It is bounded by h_top(f). Graph volume handles possible multiplicity when f is not injective. For a diffeomorphism and an embedded closed curve gamma, projection of the graph onto its second component gives the precise consequence

\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\operatorname{length}(f^n\gamma)
 \le h_{\rm top}(f)+\frac{R(f)}r,
 \qquad R(f)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log^+\|Df^n\|_\infty.
\]

For a smooth map and smooth curve the correction vanishes. Yomdin's own later manuscript, *Smooth Parametrizations in Dynamics, Analysis, Diophantine and Computational Geometry*, arXiv:1406.1719v2, Section 5.1 and Theorem 5.1, gives the related local-complexity estimate and its smooth consequences. This is a verification against full mathematical sources, not a claim that the unavailable original journal article was read or that its parametrization theorem has been independently reproved here.

Campaign A055 uses this input with a fixed loop whose nonzero circulation grows at rate `1-2c` under the backward similarity flow. The loop is first moved into the unchanged trapping region before the field is cut off and compactified. It proves

\[
 h_{\rm top}(\Phi_1|_\Lambda)\ge1-2c
\]

for every nonconstant smooth uniformly sublinear profile below one half and every finite omega. This gives a conditional Liouville theorem when the compact core has zero entropy. The smoothness hypothesis is extra regularity beyond the initial C2 target. For finite regularity the source theorem retains the explicit loss R(f)/r. ### 7.3 Ledrappier-Young: exact hypotheses, including zero exponents

The actual Part I manuscript by F. Ledrappier and L.-S. Young has been obtained from Young's public paper archive: *The metric entropy of diffeomorphisms. Part I: Characterization of measures satisfying Pesin's entropy formula*, Annals 122(3) (1985), pages 509-539, DOI 10.2307/1971328, archived PDF.

Let M be a compact smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary, f a C2 diffeomorphism of M, and mu an invariant Borel probability. Theorem A, Section 1.5, states that

\[
 h_\mu(f)=\int\sum_i\max\{\lambda_i(x),0\}\dim E_i(x)\,d\mu(x)
\]

if and only if mu has absolutely continuous conditional measures along its unstable manifolds. The definition uses every measurable partition subordinate to those manifolds and their induced Riemannian volumes. Ergodicity is not assumed. Zero exponents are allowed, and their treatment is explicitly the principal extension proved in Part I. Thus the zero exponent of a nonstationary flow does not prevent application of this theorem.

The conclusion concerns unstable conditional measures. It does not automatically establish a positive-volume statistical basin for a time-one map. Young's own 2002 survey, *What are SRB measures, and which dynamical systems have them?*, Section 2.2, Theorem 3 and the following remarks, retains this distinction in the presence of zero exponents. The constant-roof suspension example in the team records makes the missing implication explicit: fixed-phase torus measures satisfy the entropy formula but have zero-volume time-one basins. They are not invariant under the whole flow.

A flow-ergodic maximizing measure can instead be obtained by flow averaging followed by extremality in the maximizing face. If it sees nonzero B and Omega almost everywhere, campaign A037 gives backward spectrum `{1-2c,0,-(1+c)}`; Ruelle and A055 then force entropy exactly `1-2c`, so Ledrappier-Young supplies unstable absolute continuity. The possibility that a maximizing measure is supported where Omega vanishes remains open. This consequence is independently CONFIRMED as LIT17 in auditor A065; it is separate from A055. No unconditional SRB or physical-measure existence is asserted.

### 7.5 Fast dynamos: prescribed-field growth and entropy

Isaac Klapper and Lai-Sang Young, *Rigorous bounds on the fast dynamo growth rate involving topological entropy*, Communications in Mathematical Physics 173 (1995), pages 623-646, DOI 10.1007/BF02101659, is available as a full author manuscript. Theorem 1 and Section 4 were checked against rendered pages. Its field is denoted M here to avoid confusing it with the similarity carrier B.

Let a prescribed divergence-free steady velocity v of class C^(k+1), k>=2, and a divergence-free initial magnetic field M0 of class C^k be supported in a compact domain D in R^d. Let f be the time-one v-flow. For magnetic induction

\[
 \partial_t M+v\cdot\nabla M=M\cdot\nabla v+R_m^{-1}\Delta M,
 \qquad \operatorname{div}M=0,
\]

Theorem 1 gives

\[
 \limsup_{R_m\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \frac1n\log\int |M(x,n;R_m)|\,dx
 \le h_{\rm top}(f|_D)+\frac{r(f)}k,
 \quad
 r(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\max_D\|Df^n\|.
\]

The same estimate holds at R_m=infinity, the ideal transport equation. At smooth regularity the derivative correction can be removed. Section 4, Claim 2, bounds the growth of lengths uniformly over a normalized C^k family of curves by this entropy and derivative correction. Claim 1 converts field growth into such curve growth. Its decomposition into a large constant field and a remainder avoids assuming that the initial field has no zeros. Section 7 gives a corresponding passive-scalar-gradient estimate through two-dimensional area growth, with correction 2r(f)/k.

The prescribed velocity is divergence-free. The campaign carrier has div B=3c and is not compactly supported, and the field Omega is constrained by Omega=curl U. Thus Theorem 1 is a precedent for the stretching-to-entropy mechanism, not a directly applicable rotating Euler theorem. Its absence of field nonvanishing assumptions also does not remove rank-loss issues from a different proof based on a regular foliation.

### 7.6 Conformally transported forms

Marie-Claude Arnaud and Jacques Fejoz, *Invariant submanifolds of conformal symplectic dynamics*, Journal de l'Ecole polytechnique Mathematiques 11 (2024), pages 159-185, DOI 10.5802/jep.252, is archived as an actual publisher manuscript. Sections 2-3 and Appendix A were read; the principal formula pages were rendered.

For a conformal symplectic diffeomorphism f with f* sigma=a sigma, 0<a<1, Proposition 4 says that a compact invariant smooth submanifold L is isotropic if its restricted entropy is less than -log a. At finite regularity C^r the sufficient inequality is

\[
 h_{\rm top}(f|_L)+\frac2r R(f^{-1}|_L)<-\log a.
\]

Here R is the logarithmic derivative growth on the indicated invariant manifold. The proof follows the area of a small surface on which sigma is nonzero under f^{-n}, then applies Yomdin. Appendix A, Theorem 17, states the dimension-s volume-growth bound with correction s R(f)/r. This is a direct precedent for the campaign's use of exponential circulation or form growth and Yomdin entropy.

Theorem 5 improves the regularity requirement under a foliation hypothesis. Let N be a compact Riemannian C2 manifold of dimension n, let F be a C2 foliation of rank p<=n-1, and let beta be an (n-p)-form that induces a transverse volume. If a C1 diffeomorphism f preserves F and f*beta=b beta with b>1, then

\[
 h_{\rm top}(f)\ge\log b.
\]

Its corollary for a presymplectic form assumes constant rank, so that the characteristic foliation is regular. A vorticity form i_Omega vol loses rank at Omega=0, and the campaign core Lambda need not be a submanifold. Those two hypotheses cannot be dropped when citing this theorem.

LIT18 adapts the transverse-volume proof to a compact trapping region using C1 flow boxes and a minimum Bowen cover. ### 7.7 The active-vector closure is a separate constraint
