# Global affine-group leaves of the carrier and vorticity fields

## Statement

Let 0<c<1/2 and omega be finite. Let U in C2(R3;R3), P in C1(R3) solve the full rotating profile equation, with div U=0 and U(y)=o(|y|) uniformly. Define

    B=(cI-omega J)y+U,    Omega=curl U,    b=1+c,
    Reg={B cross Omega !=0},
    alpha=(B cross Omega).dy.

Every connected leaf of span(B,Omega)=ker alpha in Reg is an orbit of the simply connected affine group. With its intrinsic leaf topology it is either a plane or an open annulus. A plane leaf contains no periodic B orbit. An annulus leaf contains exactly one periodic B orbit. Neither type contains a periodic Omega orbit. The immersion of a leaf into R3 need not be an embedding or a proper map.

The action is at least C1 under the stated regularity. Saying it is smooth in the C-infinity sense would require stronger regularity. All areas below are oriented positive measures on the leaf, not ambient Euclidean surface area.

## Complete flows and the action

A006 gives complete B flow Phi_s, and A048 gives complete Omega flow psi_tau. A002 gives

    Phi_s psi_tau=psi_(exp(bs)tau) Phi_s.

The set Reg is invariant under both flows: Phi transports B into B and Omega into a nonzero scalar multiple of Omega; along psi the bracket identity transports their two-dimensional span with an invertible triangular change of basis. Equivalently the group action below preserves its orbit dimension.

On G=R2 define

    (s1,tau1)*(s2,tau2)
       =(s1+s2, exp(-b s2)tau1+tau2).

This is a Lie group, and (s,tau).z=Phi_s psi_tau z is an action by the displayed conjugacy. Its differential in the two parameter directions spans B and Omega. Thus an orbit through Reg is a two-dimensional immersed integral manifold. Local flow boxes show that each orbit is open in the connected integral leaf; since the orbits partition that leaf, connectedness makes it a single orbit. The stabilizer H is a closed discrete subgroup and the intrinsic orbit is G/H. This gives the usual injective immersion, without asserting that the intrinsic and subspace topologies agree.

## Classification of stabilizers and periodic points

Let N={(0,tau)} and S={(s,0)}. The projection pi:G->R, pi(s,tau)=s, is a group homomorphism with kernel N. A014 excludes every nonconstant periodic Omega orbit, so H intersects N only at the identity. The projection pi is therefore injective on H. Since every commutator lies in N, H is abelian.

If H is nontrivial, choose h0=(s0,tau0) in H; then s0!=0. Conjugation by a suitable translation (0,t) sends h0 to (s0,0). The centralizer of (s0,0) is exactly S: comparing the second coordinates of the two products gives tau(exp(-b s0)-1)=0. Since H is abelian, its conjugate is contained in S. A nontrivial closed discrete subgroup of S=R is generated by (T,0) with T>0. Thus H is either trivial or conjugate to this infinite cyclic group.

For the trivial stabilizer, no point of the orbit has a nontrivial stabilizer under S, so there is no periodic B orbit. For a cyclic stabilizer, change the basepoint to make H=<(T,0)>. A point represented by g=(s,tau) is B-periodic exactly when g H g^-1 meets S nontrivially. For a nonzero power of (T,0), direct multiplication shows this happens exactly when tau=0. Those points form one S orbit. Its least B period is T.

For completeness the quotient topology can be read off explicitly. Right multiplication by (T,0) sends

    (s,tau) -> (s+T, exp(-bT)tau).

The coordinates theta=s mod T and z=exp(bs)tau identify G/H with (R/TZ) times R, an open annulus. When H is trivial the same formula with s in R gives a plane. In either case the fields on the leaf have the simple intrinsic expression

    B=partial_s+b z partial_z,    Omega=partial_z,      (1)

with s understood modulo T in the annulus case. In particular the unique annular periodic orbit is z=0. These are intrinsic coordinates; they place no bound on the embedding's derivatives at infinity.

## Leafwise area

Put n=(B cross Omega)/|B cross Omega|^2 and beta=i_n vol on Reg. Then alpha(n)=1, alpha wedge beta=vol and beta(B,Omega)=1. If another two-form beta' obeys alpha wedge beta'=vol, its restriction to ker alpha equals that of beta. Thus beta_L is intrinsically specified by alpha and the ambient volume form.

The exact transport laws are

    Phi_s^*vol=exp(3cs)vol,
    Phi_s^*alpha=exp((2c-1)s)alpha.

Restricting their quotient to the tangent plane gives

    Phi_s^*beta_L=exp(bs)beta_L.

Under the orbit map (s,tau)->Phi_s psi_tau z0 the pullback is

    beta_L=exp(bs) ds wedge d tau=ds wedge dz.          (2)

This is RIGHT Haar measure on the displayed group. It is not left Haar: left multiplication by (s0,tau0) scales it by exp(b s0), whereas right multiplication preserves it. The unqualified Haar statement in CO11 should therefore be read as right Haar. Formula (2) descends under the cyclic right action, as required.

The leaf potential from A059 says U.dy restricted to a leaf is exact, with potential -Ht/(1-2c). This is compatible with both topologies; on an annulus its period around z=0 vanishes. Neither exactness nor (2) establishes monotonicity of that potential or excludes an annulus leaf.

## Dependencies and scope

This confirms CO11 with C1 action regularity and the right-Haar qualification. It uses the proved complete flows (A006, A048), bracket identity (A002), absence of closed vortex lines (A014), and the exact one-form transport law (A056). It does not use an outgoing hypothesis, an invariant measure, or a trapping neighborhood. No global conclusion about an attractor follows solely from this leaf classification.
