# Pressure Hessian localization and the limits of local sign tests

Date: 2026-11-02T22:10:41Z. A221 CONFIRMED for the bounds and explicitly stated sufficient conditions. A222 leaves the universal sign-impossibility assertion OPEN.

## Setting and exact claims

Let U be a bounded C2 divergence-free field on R3 and let P be its canonical pressure, modulo a constant, so -Delta P=partial_i partial_j(U_i U_j). Let S=(DU+DU^T)/2, Omega=curl U and f=|S|^2-|Omega|^2/2. Then -Delta P=f. For an actual profile the canonical normalization follows from A031 or A219 under Hb. All constants below are absolute except those explicitly depending on the energy exponent.

For a compact set K contained in B_(R0) and R>=2R0, the Hessian contribution generated by U outside B_R obeys

    sup_K |D2 P_far| <= C int_(|z|>R) |U(z)|^2 |z|^-5 dz.

If E(r)=int_(B_r)|U|^2 <= C_E r^alpha for r>=R, with 0<=alpha<5, the right side is at most C C_E R^(alpha-5)/(5-alpha). For the energy exponent alpha=5-2/c this is O(R^(-2/c)). At c=2/5, finite total energy gives the stronger little-o bound o(R^-5).

For every x and r>0, put M_j=sup_(B_(2r)(x)) |D^j U|, j=0,1,2. Then

    |D2 P(x)| <= C [M1^2+r M1 M2+M0^2/r^2
                    +int_(|z-x|>r) |U(z)|^2 |z-x|^-5 dz].

These estimates do not assert a sign. In fact smooth compactly supported divergence-free exterior sources can generate either sign of a prescribed directional pressure Hessian at a point where their velocity and all velocity derivatives vanish.

## Remote contribution

Write Gamma(z)=1/(4 pi |z|), K_ij=partial_i partial_j Gamma off zero. Two further derivatives satisfy |D2 K_ij(z)|<=C|z|^-5. For x in K and |z|>R, |x-z|>=|z|/2. Differentiating the renormalized exterior pressure twice is absolutely justified by boundedness of U and integrability of |z|^-5 at infinity. This gives the first estimate.

In radial Stieltjes form, its scalar integral is int_R^infinity r^-5 dE(r). Integration by parts gives

    -R^-5 E(R)+5 int_R^infinity r^-6 E(r) dr
       <= 5 C_E R^(alpha-5)/(5-alpha).

If E(infinity)<infinity, the same integral is bounded by R^-5 int_(|z|>R)|U|^2=o(R^-5). The estimates also hold after averaging any unit-vector directional Hessian against a probability supported in K. A smooth cutoff between R and 2R gives the same bound.

## Local contribution

Choose a smooth radial cutoff chi about x, equal to one on B_r(x), supported in B_(2r)(x), with derivative bounds C/r and C/r^2. Split P into the canonical pressure of chi U_i U_j and the exterior part. The exterior Hessian is bounded by the displayed tail integral.

Since div U=0,

    partial_i partial_j(chi U_i U_j)
       =chi f+2 grad chi dot ((U dot grad)U)+D2 chi:(U tensor U).

The last two sources are supported in the annulus. Their Newton Hessian at x is bounded by C(M0 M1/r+M0^2/r^2), since the kernel there has magnitude at most C/r^3. Young's inequality absorbs the mixed term into C(M1^2+M0^2/r^2).

For the chi f term, the distributional Hessian of Gamma has its local term -f(x)I/3 plus the principal-value kernel with zero spherical mean. Radiality of chi permits subtraction of f(x) inside the integral. Now |f(x)|<=C M1^2 and |grad f|<=C M1 M2. Integrating |z-x| times |z-x|^-3 over B_(2r) gives C r. Thus this contribution is bounded by C(M1^2+rM1M2). Combining the bounds proves the claim. This argument does not invoke a false L-infinity Calderon-Zygmund bound.

## Explicit opposite signs from exterior sources

Fix R>0 and take a smooth nonconstant radial bump phi_epsilon centered at R e3, supported in B_epsilon(R e3), with epsilon much smaller than R. For a unit constant vector a put V=grad phi_epsilon cross a. Then V is smooth, compactly supported and divergence free. Isotropy of the radial bump gives

    int V_i V_j = m_epsilon (delta_ij-a_i a_j),
    m_epsilon>0.

The canonical pressure Hessian at zero is

    partial_33 P_V(0)
       =-m_epsilon partial_33aa Gamma(R e3)
          +O((epsilon/R)m_epsilon R^-5).

Indeed Taylor-expand the smooth fourth-derivative kernel over the bump and contract its constant term with the covariance above. The trace term vanishes because Gamma is harmonic away from zero. Direct differentiation on the axis gives

    partial_3333 Gamma(R e3)=24/(4 pi R^5),
    partial_3311 Gamma(R e3)=-12/(4 pi R^5).

Hence a=e3 yields a negative Hessian component and a=e1 yields a positive one, for sufficiently small epsilon/R. Both velocities vanish identically near the observation point, so every local velocity jet there is the same. These are genuine canonical pressure sources, not an arbitrary added harmonic pressure. Adding them to a profile generally violates the momentum equation, so they do not prove sign flexibility among actual profiles.

## Conditional averaged tests

Suppose now U is an actual C2 profile with 0<c<1/2, and mu is a compactly supported carrier-invariant probability with mu(Omega!=0)=1 and the moment hypotheses of A119/A212. Put n=Omega/|Omega|, a=n dot S n and b=Sn-a n. Then

    int a dmu=1,
    I_mu:=int n dot D2P n dmu
        =int |b|^2 dmu-2-int (a-1)^2 dmu.

Thus int|b|^2<2 implies I_mu<0; Sn=a n almost everywhere implies I_mu<=-2. Also, with Q0=D2P-(Delta P)I/3,

    I_mu=-(1/3)int f dmu+int n dot Q0 n dmu.

The separate hypotheses |int n dot Q0 n dmu|<=epsilon and int f dmu>3epsilon imply I_mu<0. These are sufficient conditions, not estimates supplied by the original hypotheses. When omega=0, the Bernoulli identity and invariance already force int|B|^2 dmu=0, excluding a nonstationary vortical SRB probability independently of the Hessian route.

A scalar Laplacian sign does not control a directional Hessian: x1^2-x2^2+epsilon|x|^2, 0<epsilon<1, has positive Laplacian and opposite first two diagonal Hessian signs. Unstable conditional absolute continuity supplies integration by parts along unstable leaves, not a density derivative along the stable vortex direction. Any proposed stable-direction integration by parts must supply that extra information.
