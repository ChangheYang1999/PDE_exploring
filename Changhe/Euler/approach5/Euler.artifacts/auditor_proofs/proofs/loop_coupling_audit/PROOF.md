# Loop characterization of the complete Euler coupling

## Statement

Let O be an open subset of R3, 0<c<1/2, omega any finite real number, a=1-2c, A=cI-omega J, and B,W in C1(O;R3). Suppose div B=3c, div W=0 and [B,W]=-(1+c)W. Set U=B-Ay and alpha=(B cross W).dy. Then the following are equivalent:

1. W=curl U and a single-valued C1 pressure P solves the rotating profile equation on O.
2. Every closed piecewise C1 curve gamma in O satisfies

    integral_gamma B.dy + 2 omega A_12(gamma) = a^(-1) integral_gamma alpha,

where A_12(gamma)=one half the integral of y_1 dy_2-y_2 dy_1.

If these conditions hold, define H=P+|U|^2/2+(Ay).U. Every C1 arc I from p to q then satisfies

    integral_I (a U.dy-alpha)=H(p)-H(q).              (1)

If W=curl U is already assumed, it is enough to check the periods of a U.dy-alpha on a basis of H_1(O;R). For a region homotopy equivalent to a knot exterior there is one condition, on a meridian generator. Components of O are treated separately.

## Proof

The divergence of U is zero. Direct vector calculus gives

    curl(B cross W)=-[B,W]-(div B)W+(div W)B=aW.     (2)

Also the integral of cy.dy around a closed curve is zero and the integral of Jy.dy is 2A_12. The proposed loop condition is therefore precisely the vanishing of all periods of the C1 one-form delta=aU.dy-alpha.

If all periods vanish, fix a base point in each component of O and put H(y)=-integral delta along any piecewise C1 path to y. Path independence follows by closing two paths into a loop. Local integration on short straight segments proves H is C2 and dH=-delta. Taking exterior derivatives and using (2) gives a curl U=aW, hence curl U=W since a!=0. The identity aU+grad H=B cross curl U is the Lamb form of the profile equation. It yields the claimed pressure P=H-|U|^2/2-(Ay).U, which is C1.

Conversely the momentum equation and W=curl U give exactly this Lamb identity, so delta=-dH and its periods vanish. Integrating gives (1).

When curl U=W is known, (2) makes delta closed. Its integral depends only on real homology, so checking a basis is equivalent to checking every closed curve. This includes the one-dimensional first homology of a knot exterior, generated by its meridian.

## Unstable-arc consequence and qualifications

If an arc I stays in a fixed region N where |H| is bounded, is everywhere transverse to ker alpha, and is parametrized by positive alpha-length ds, let Z denote its tangent with alpha(Z)=1. Equation (1) yields

    |integral_I (a U.Z-1)ds| <= 2 sup_N |H|.

This is an exact bounded-discrepancy identity, with no assumption on the length of I. Averaging it against an SRB measure still requires the appropriate conditional-measure and integrability hypotheses; this arc identity alone does not supply them.

The equality ker alpha=span(B,W) is valid only where B cross W is nonzero. At a degenerate point alpha=0 and its kernel is all of R3. The loop equivalence and (1) themselves need no nondegeneracy. The period formulation restates the full curl and pressure coupling; it supplies no sign or new nonexistence theorem.
