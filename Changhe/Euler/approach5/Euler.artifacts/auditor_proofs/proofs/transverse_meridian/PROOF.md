# A transverse meridian from a compact hyperbolic boundary leaf

## Exact statement

Let T be a smooth oriented torus with a smooth nonsingular one-dimensional foliation F. Let C be a smooth embedded compact leaf. Suppose the derivative of its holonomy around one circuit is a positive number different from one. Then every sufficiently small tubular neighborhood of C contains a smooth embedded closed curve E transverse to F and isotopic to C in T.

Consequently, in the conditional completion theorem A181, the transverse-meridian hypothesis follows if the boundary foliation T(boundary N) intersect span(B,W) has a compact meridional leaf with nonunit holonomy. This statement assumes the meridional slope of the actual boundary leaf. It does not infer that slope from an unmarked orbit equivalence.

For a smooth regular hyperbolic plug with the exact identities of A181, a compact boundary leaf associated to a free stable separatrix of a periodic orbit has nonunit holonomy. Its absolute logarithmic multiplier is a positive integer multiple of a times the orbit period, where a=1-2c>0. In the orientable cylinder case and for a single circuit the integer is one.

No Euler curl relation is used. No regular plug is constructed here.

## Local proof

Choose smooth tubular coordinates (theta,r), with theta in R/(2pi Z), C={r=0}. After multiplying a smooth oriented generator by a positive scalar, the foliation in a sufficiently narrow annulus is generated by

    V=partial_theta+v(theta,r) partial_r,  v(theta,0)=0.

Write k(theta)=partial_r v(theta,0) and

    mu=(1/(2pi)) integral_0^(2pi) k(theta) dtheta.

The linear variational equation dr/dtheta=k(theta)r gives holonomy derivative exp(2pi mu). By hypothesis mu is nonzero. There is a smooth positive periodic function

    h(theta)=exp(integral_0^theta (k(s)-mu) ds).

For a small nonzero epsilon, define E_epsilon by r=epsilon h(theta). Its tangent is partial_theta+epsilon h'(theta) partial_r. Uniformly in theta,

    v(theta,epsilon h(theta))-epsilon h'(theta)
      =epsilon mu h(theta)+O(epsilon^2).

Since h is positive on a compact circle and mu is nonzero, the right side has one strict sign for sufficiently small nonzero epsilon. The tangent is therefore nowhere parallel to V. The graph is embedded, smooth, and isotopic to C through graphs. It can be placed in any prescribed tubular neighborhood by reducing epsilon.

The argument also works with a C1 generator and a C1 tubular chart: the remainder is o(epsilon) uniformly, and the resulting C1 transverse embedded curve can be approximated in C1 by a smooth curve without losing strict transversality or its isotopy class. The smooth formulation is sufficient for A181.

## Holonomy for the exact regular bracket

Let X=-B. On the plug, W spans the stable line of X, and span(B,W) is the weak stable plane. This follows directly from [B,W]=-bW and the regular hyperbolic splitting. Let theta=i_B i_W m. The divergence and bracket identities give

    L_B theta=-a theta,   theta(B)=theta(W)=0.

At a periodic B-orbit of period S, the normal holonomy of the plane foliation is the derivative induced on the one-dimensional quotient by span(B,W). The displayed identity makes this multiplier exp(-aS). Reversing the circuit takes its reciprocal. Equivalently, the backward-flow transverse exponents are -b and a.

A compact boundary leaf in a free weak stable separatrix and its associated periodic orbit are homotopic inside the same weak stable cylinder. Holonomy along homotopic loops is conjugate by the smooth holonomy of a path joining their base points. Their derivatives at the fixed points therefore agree, up to reversal of the circuit and possible integer covering. In every case the derivative is positive and different from one. In the untwisted cylinder case, a simple boundary leaf makes a single circuit. Global nonzero W supplies the stable orientation and excludes the Mobius alternative in this setting.

The local proof now supplies a nearby transverse curve of exactly the same boundary slope. If that slope is meridional, this is the E required by A181. The construction does not require the curve to cross the original compact leaf: it lies on one side of that leaf.

## Relation to the inspected primary sources

The actual Christy manuscript, in the team records, Section 4 on printed page 773, describes the boundary circle obtained from a boundary periodic orbit along its stable manifold. Section 6, printed pages 776-777, describes two meridional boundary periodic orbits for the standard suspended DA figure-eight example. Its Figure 6.2 embedding has not been independently reconstructed here. The source supports a candidate meridional marking, not the exact bracket field or derivative normalization.

The actual Beguin-Bonatti-Yu manuscript, in the team records, Proposition 3.8 and Remark 3.10, supplies compact leaves and contracting holonomy for attracting-plug boundary foliations. The beginning of the proof of Proposition 7.7 also uses small annuli with transverse boundary curves around these compact leaves. The elementary argument above gives a direct proof under the stronger nonunit derivative supplied by the exact bracket.

Thus compact meridional leaves are not, by themselves, an obstruction to A181's transverse-meridian assumption. A smooth exact plug retaining the standard marked DA weak stable foliation would satisfy that assumption by this lemma. Existence of that exact plug remains open.
