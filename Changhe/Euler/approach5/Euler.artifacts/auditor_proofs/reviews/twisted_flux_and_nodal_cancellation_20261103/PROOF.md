# Twisted tail flux, scalar nodes, and the surviving swirl term

Status: CONFIRMED in A641-A644 after complete source and independent proof postreads.

## 1. Exact kinematic setting

Let 1<p<2, c=1/(p+1), omega be any real number, t=omega/c, lambda=p-1, mu=2-p, a=c lambda, eta=lambda/(2p), and A=cI-omega J, where Jx=e3 cross x. On D=R3 minus {0}, let V be C1, divergence free, with

    V(exp(sA)x)=exp(-(1-c)s)exp(-omega sJ)V(x).

Set W=curl V and k=(Ax).V. Assume the pointwise nodal condition

    k(x)=0 implies W(x)=0.                                      (N)

This is an extra premise for a kinematic field. For an actual tail, A597 supplies it under the exact vortical rank, scalar transport and locally A2 weight hypotheses. It is not inferred from bare velocity decay.

Use rho=log r, z=cos(theta), chi=phi+t rho, w=1-z^2, and the globally defined angular one-form and functions

    V.flat=r^(-lambda)(K d rho+gamma),
    gamma=-(H/w)dz+F dchi, R=K+tF, k=c r^(-lambda)K.

Cartesian C1 regularity at the poles is required; these are intrinsic C1 sphere objects, not charts with independently assigned pole values. Put Z=partial_chi and M=g_S2^(-1)+t^2 Z tensor Z. M is positive definite. The C1 sphere field

    Bq=M gamma+tKZ=-H partial_z+(F/w+tR)partial_chi

satisfies div_S2 Bq=-mu R. This follows by raising V.flat in the metric

    r^2[d rho^2+dz^2/w+w(dchi-t d rho)^2]

and using the volume density r^3. No polar integration boundary is introduced.

The conclusions are

    integral_{K>0} R<=0<=integral_{K<0} R,
    integral |K|<=-t integral sgn(K)F,                           (1)

where all integrals are over the round sphere and sgn(0)=0. The two displayed formulations are equivalent here. Consequently V=0 if t=0. If V is nonzero, K is not identically zero and the right side of (1) is strictly positive. There is no arbitrary-twist rigidity from these statements alone.

## 2. Complete sphere flow and arbitrary nodal sets

Exterior differentiation gives

    d(V.flat)=r^(-lambda)[d gamma-d rho wedge(lambda gamma+dK)].

At W=0, both d gamma=0 and lambda gamma+dK=0. Thus at K=0, premise(N) gives

    Bq=-M dK/lambda,  dK(Bq)=-M(dK,dK)/lambda.

A regular zero is crossed forward strictly from K>0 to K<0. At a critical zero gamma=0 and Bq=0, so uniqueness of the C1 flow excludes reaching it in finite time along a nonconstant orbit. First-contact arguments therefore give

    Phi_(-s){K>0} subset {K>0},
    Phi_s{K<0} subset {K<0},  s>=0.

The C1 sphere field has a complete C1 flow since the sphere is compact. Change of variables and differentiation of its uniformly bounded Jacobian at time zero are valid on any measurable subset. The two set inclusions give

    integral_{K>0} div Bq>=0,  integral_{K<0} div Bq<=0,

hence the first part of (1). No regular boundary, finite-perimeter assertion, or Sard theorem is needed.

For the equivalence in (1), integrate div Bq to get integral R=0. Also dK=0 almost everywhere on {K=0}: its regular zeros lie in countably many C1 hypersurface charts, each of zero area. At its remaining zeros gamma=0 by(N). Hence F=R=0 almost everywhere on {K=0}. It follows that the two signed domain integrals are negatives of one another. Their difference is

    integral sgn(K)R=integral |K|+t integral sgn(K)F.

This proves the equivalence, including positive-area critical zero sets.

If t=0, (1) forces K=0. Then(N) and lambda gamma+dK=0 give gamma=0, hence V=0. The same argument shows that K identically zero forces V=0 for every t.

## 3. Weighted flux and a precise three-dimensional section statement

For any positive C1 sphere function psi, apply the same inclusion to weighted area. Differentiation gives

    integral_{K>0} (mu psi R-Bq psi)<=0,
    integral_{K<0} (mu psi R-Bq psi)>=0.                         (2)

This is the exact weighted inequality; no prescribed sign of R at every point follows.

Let g be any C1 function on the sphere. The graph rho=g(z,chi) defines a complete compact section of the spiral rays generated by Ax, oriented so that Ax points to its outer side. Its portion over {K>0} has flux

    integral_{K>0} exp(mu g)(R-Bq g).

Indeed i_V volume pulls back to exp(mu g)(R-Bq g)dS on that graph. Taking psi=exp(mu g) in (2) proves that this flux is nonpositive. The analogous portion over {K<0} has nonnegative flux. This includes spherical sections and avoids integration over a possibly irregular lateral boundary.

On every regular lateral boundary point, twisted homogeneity gives

    grad k=-aV+(Ax) cross W=-aV,

so V has nonnegative outward flux from {k>0}, whose outward normal is -grad k/|grad k|. At a critical zero k, V=0. This pointwise assertion does not assert regularity of the entire boundary. A partial or oppositely oriented section is not covered by the flux claim.

## 4. The finite defect and the zero-free identity

Define finite quantities

    Dfrak=-lambda mu integral sgn(K)R>=0,
    Tfrak=-lambda mu t integral sgn(K)F.

Then identically

    Dfrak+lambda mu integral |K|=Tfrak.                        (3)

In the target range 1<p<3/2, when the transported scalar S=r^(-2p)h has no zeros and the exact rank hypotheses of A618 hold, let f=|h|^eta, eta=lambda/(2p), g=K/f, and use its global potentials psi0 and Psi. A618 gives

    lambda gamma+dK=f(lambda dpsi0+dg),
    dPsi=sgn(g)dpsi0.

Consequently Xi=|g|+lambda Psi satisfies

    f dXi=sgn(K)(dK+lambda gamma)

almost everywhere. Sphere integration by parts using div Z=0 gives

    t mu integral Xi Zf
      =-t mu integral f ZXi
      =-lambda mu t integral sgn(K)F=Tfrak,

because integral sgn(K)ZK=integral Z|K|=0. Comparison with A618 proves that its nonnegative defect is exactly Dfrak. This identification is for the zero-free case. Formula(3) remains valid under(N), without singular divided potentials.

## 5. A nodal axisymmetric scalar cancellation

In addition to Section1, assume V.W=0, and a C1 scalar S=r^(-2p)h satisfies W.grad S=0. Suppose f=|h|^eta is positive almost everywhere with 1/f locally integrable on the sphere. Retain(N), supplied, for example, by the full A597 hypotheses. If Zh=0, then V=0. No axisymmetry of V is imposed.

The two angular identities, obtained by taking V.flat wedge d(V.flat) and dS wedge d(V.flat), are

    K d gamma=dK wedge gamma,
    2p h d gamma=lambda dh wedge gamma+dh wedge dK.

For the continuous one-form Y=gamma+dK/lambda, with bounded distributional derivative dY=d gamma, they imply

    h dY=eta dh wedge Y,  dK wedge Y=K dY.                     (4)

Let f_delta=(h^2+delta^2)^(eta/2). Direct regularization gives

    d(Y/f_delta)=[delta^2/(h^2+delta^2)] dY/f_delta.

The right side tends to zero in L1, dominated by |dY|/f, and Y/f_delta tends to Y/f in L1. Thus Y/f is distributionally closed. For phi_epsilon(s)=s/sqrt(s^2+epsilon^2), the product rule and (4) give

    d(phi_epsilon(K)Y/f)=K phi_epsilon'(K)dY/f.

Here K phi_epsilon'(K) is uniformly bounded and tends to zero at every K, including K=0. Dominated convergence proves that sgn(K)Y/f is closed. The distributional Poincare lemma on simply connected S2 supplies a global W1,1 scalar A0 with

    dA0=lambda sgn(K)Y/f.                                    (5)

Because f is axisymmetric and bounded, Fubini on angular charts and the periodic W1,1 slice property give integral f ZA0=0. Equation(5) now yields

    0=integral sgn(K)(lambda F+ZK)=lambda integral sgn(K)F.

Insert this into (3) to conclude K=0 and V=0. No integration by parts against a singular Zf is needed. For nonaxisymmetric nodal h, the pairing integral f ZA0 is defined by(5), but replacing it by -integral A0 Zf requires its own justification.

## 6. Why the separated zero-free defect calculation fails at regular nodes

This is a local countertest, not a global tail. Fix t=0 and solve analytically on an equatorial band

    (w K')'=lambda mu K,  K(0)=1, K'(0)=0,
    gamma=-dK/lambda.

Then V.flat=-d(r^(-lambda)K)/lambda is curl free and divergence free. Put

    e=(K^2+w(K')^2/lambda^2)/2.

Let v solve (w v')'+2p(2p-1)v=0, v(0)=1, v'(0)=b!=0, and set u=v-e, h=v-2e. Since r^(-2p)v is harmonic and Delta(|V|^2/2)=tr((DV)^2), P2=r^(-2p)u satisfies the local pressure equation. All vorticity transport/rank requirements are vacuous. We have

    h=bz+O(z^2), f comparable to |z|^eta,
    g=K/f comparable to |z|^(-eta).

The actual asymptotic derivatives give, for every fixed epsilon>0,

    f epsilon^2(g^2+epsilon^2)^(-3/2) M(dg,dg)
       comparable to epsilon^2 |z|^(2eta-2).

Since 0<eta<1/4, the latter is nonintegrable. Thus the separated nonnegative D_epsilon of the zero-free proof is not a finite integral even though f is locally A2. The mixed term has the opposite divergent leading part; cancellation must be performed before integration, using f dg+g df=dK. The original singular potentials separately lose their previous regularity.

A global smooth extension of this curl-free K would satisfy integral |dK|^2+lambda mu integral K^2=0, contradicting K(0)=1. The construction fails global pole regularity and uses a local harmonic pressure addition. It gives no counterexample to(N)-rigidity or the campaign conjecture.

## 7. Exact pressure estimate and limits of the conclusion

Additionally suppose P2=r^(-2p)u is a global C1 pressure coefficient with -Delta P2=tr((DV)^2) in distributions and h=u-e, e=|V|^2 r^(2p)/2. Let

    A0=integral R^2,  B0=integral (H^2+F^2)/w.

The averaged radial acceleration is

    integral r^(2p+1)((V.grad)V).er=-2lambda A0-B0.

Indeed its radial expression is -pR^2+Bq R-(H^2+F^2)/w and integral Bq R=mu integral R^2. Taking divergence makes the averaged source

    integral r^(2p+2)tr((DV)^2)=(2p-1)(2lambda A0+B0).

The constant angular Laplacian mode therefore gives

    integral u=-(2lambda A0+B0)/(2p),
    -integral h=[(3p-2)A0+(p+1)B0]/(2p).

Since integral w=8pi/3, Cauchy-Schwarz in the definition of Tfrak proves

    |Tfrak|<=lambda mu |t|
       sqrt[16pi p (-integral h)/(3(p+1))].                    (6)

For zero-free h this is the same T as A618. Neither (6) nor (1) bounds T by C|t|(Dfrak+integral |K|), so no positive small-twist threshold is established. Zero mean h forces V=0, for example when h is a pure nonzero Fourier mode with no constant mode. Adding a constant mode removes this argument.

Sharpness of (1) at the kinematic level is the A637 field

    V=rho_c^2 r^(-p-3)Jx,

where rho_c is distance to the axis. It has R=H=0, F=w^2, K=-t w^2 and satisfies(N); equality holds in(1) for every t. This field does not satisfy the global pressure and scalar rank equations of an actual tail. No global Euler counterexample is provided.
