# Audit of the P1 scalar identities, boundary formulas and reduced models

Status: exact identities and reduced models CONFIRMED in A524-A527; printed extrema REFUTED in A528; universal method classification OPEN in A529. The exact identities and explicitly reduced models are separated from a universal impossibility assertion about proof methods.

## 1. Setting and the claims actually tested

Fix 2/5<c<1/2 and any finite real omega. Put k=(1-c)/c in(1,3/2), a=1-2c, alpha=3-2k, gamma=c alpha/2, A=cI-omega J and B=Ax+U. Let U in C2(R3;R3), P in C1(R3) be a full incompressible rotating Euler profile. Assume the natural bounds

    |U(x)|<=C(1+|x|)^(-k),
    |W(x)|<=C(1+|x|)^(-k-1), W=curl U,
    ||DU||infinity<infinity,
    |P(x)|<=C(1+|x|)^(-2k)log(2+|x|),                     (1)

with P the canonical pressure, normalized to zero at infinity. For statements about the renormalized energy e_D, impose the A435 Dini-scattering hypotheses explicitly: the volume-preserving scattering map S and its actual tail V exist, the density |U|²-|V composed with S|² is integrable, and A435 gives

    e_D=integral_D (|U|²-|V composed with S|²)
        =gamma^(-1)integral_D U.grad P.                    (2)

This is used only for the energy interpretation of the boundary formulas and the two signed subclasses. No basin-coverage assertion is imported here.

Write

    q=|U|²/2, H=P+q+(Ax).U, h=U.W, g=U.B,
    C=B cross W, f=tr((DU)²).

The exact identities are

    aU+grad H=C,
    BH=-ag, WH=-ah,
    [B,W]=-(1+c)W, div B=3c, div W=0.                    (3)

Here Xf denotes X.grad f. Uniform sublinearity of U gives at most linear growth of B; this growth and boundedness of W give complete flows. A measurable invariant set means invariance under the specified flow, at least modulo null sets, for every fixed time. Let a radial cutoff chi_R equal1 on the ball of radius R, vanish outside radius2R, and have gradient at most C/R. We use the exact invariant-set integration rules from volume change and incompressibility. Every limiting cutoff below is checked explicitly.

The principal distinction is that(3)'s two scalar derivative equations can be considered with H,h,g as independent scalar unknowns on a fixed background B,W. This reduced system omits the definitions of H,h,g in terms of U,P. A countertest for that reduced system is not a full Euler counterexample.

## 2. Vortex-invariant conditional helicity cancellation

Let E be any measurable set invariant under the W-flow. By(1), H=O((1+r)^(1-k)), h=O((1+r)^(-2k-1)), and h belongs to L1 and L2. If phi is a bounded C1 function such that H Wphi is integrable on E, then

    integral_E phi h=(1/a)integral_E H Wphi.              (4)

Indeed the complete W-flow preserves Lebesgue measure and E. Differentiating its change-of-variables identity for compact C1 test functions gives integral_E Wpsi=0. Apply this to psi=chi_R H phi and use WH=-ah. The only extra term is bounded by

    C R^(1-k) R^(-k-1) R^(-1) R³=C R^(2-2k),

which tends to zero because k>1. The two non-error terms converge by their stated integrability. This proves(4).

Let I_W(E) denote the invariant measurable subsets of E, and let A_E be the sigma-algebra generated by those sets and H. Then

    integral_E h Phi=0                                  (5)

for every bounded A_E-measurable function Phi. To prove this rigorously, fix G in I_W(E). For a smooth bounded scalar function psi, its primitive F(s)=integral_0^s psi satisfies |F(H)|<=||psi||infinity|H| and W F(H)=-a psi(H)h. The same cutoff argument on G proves integral_G h psi(H)=0. Define the finite signed Borel measure on the real line

    nu_G=(H)_*(1_G h dx).

It annihilates every compactly supported smooth function, hence is the zero measure. For example, convolution with a smooth approximate identity vanishes, and testing continuous compactly supported functions then gives uniqueness of the finite measure. Consequently integral_G h psi(H)=0 for every bounded Borel psi. This measure argument replaces the source's unsupported claim that every bounded Borel function is a pointwise limit of one sequence of smooth functions.

The sets G intersect H^(-1)(J), where G is invariant and J is Borel, form a pi-system generating A_E. The finite signed measure h dx vanishes on them and has total mass zero on E. The zero-mass class is a lambda-system, so the pi/lambda theorem gives vanishing on A_E. Approximation by bounded simple functions gives(5).

If M=integral_E |h|>0, the probability measure dLambda=1_E|h|dx/M satisfies

    E_Lambda[sgn h | A_E]=0.                              (6)

This follows directly from(5), since its integral against every A_E indicator is zero. The statement is about an integrated conditional expectation, not pointwise helicity cancellation. The probability Lambda is a convenient finite measure. No claim that every vortex-invariant finite-volume E is null is made: the volume-expansion argument concerns B-invariant sets, and E here is required to be invariant only under W.

## 3. The reduced scalar replacement test and its exact scope

Using only the two derivative equations in(3) and the commutator gives

    Bh+(1+c)h=Wg.                                       (7)

For a C1 weight w satisfying Bw=c beta w on its domain,

    div(B w h²/2)=-(1-c(1+beta)/2)w h²+w h Wg.           (8)

At beta=0, integration on a B-invariant measurable D gives

    (1-c/2) integral_D h²=integral_D h Wg,
    (2-5c/2) integral_D h²=-integral_D h W(P-q).          (9)

The outer cutoff for B h² is O(R^(1-4k)), hence vanishes. The source term is absolutely integrable: from the momentum equation and bounded DU, grad P=O(1+r), while grad q is bounded times |U|. Thus W(P-q)=O((1+r)^(-k)); multiplication by h is O((1+r)^(-3k-1)), integrable in dimension3. The second identity follows from P-q=H-g and WH=-ah. Weighted integrated versions require their own inner and outer cutoff bounds; only the pointwise identity(8) is automatic for an arbitrary admissible weight.

Now fix B,W and replace the scalars by any F in C2_c(R3) and

    h_F=-(1/a)WF, g_F=-(1/a)BF.                         (10)

Their two derivative equations hold by definition, so(7),(8),(9) and the vortex-invariant cancellation proof remain valid, with P-q in(9) replaced by F-g_F. All unweighted terms have compact support. If y0 belongs to a vortical open D, choose e=W(y0)/|W(y0)| and a compact cutoff zeta equal1 near y0. For

    F(x)=zeta(x)((x-y0).e)²,

one has h_F=-(2/a)((x-y0).e)W(x).e near y0. By continuity W(x).e>0 on a sufficiently small neighborhood, so h_F takes both signs in every neighborhood of y0.

This proves a precise conditional obstruction: on any fixed background with a vortical open D, the reduced scalar relations and their valid integration rules do not single out one-signed h. They also do not distinguish the physical H,h,g from(10) without an additional coupling statement. This is not a universal impossibility theorem for a proof that separately eliminates the background B,W itself. Such a theorem cannot be obtained from a replacement construction conditioned on a background whose nontrivial Euler existence is the main open question.

For completeness, the residual identity in the source is exact. For any divergence-free C2 U and any P, define

    R=(1-c)U+DU B+omega J U+grad P.

Then

    curl R=BW+(I+omega J-DU)W,
    Bh+(1+c)h-Wg=U.curl R.                              (11)

In the second computation the antisymmetric contribution W.(DU-DU^T)B vanishes, and (1+c)I-A=I+omega J. Thus(7) requires only zero curl of the momentum residual, not the full momentum equation.

The correlation in the second line of(9) is already exactly known. For any eta<2-5c/2, the extra inequality

    integral_D h W(P-q)>=-eta integral_D h²

is equivalent to h=0 almost everywhere on D. This diagnoses that particular proposed coercivity condition; it does not show that an independent proof of the inequality is logically impossible.

## 4. Pressure integrability and the distributional boundary formulas

The following supplies details for OB.3 that are implicit in the source. First DU belongs to L2 and

    integral |DU|²=integral |W|², integral f=0.            (12)

To see this, apply the whole-space identity integral |DF|²=integral(|curl F|²+|div F|²) to F=chi_R U. Expanding the cutoff terms and using Cauchy-Schwarz gives a uniform bound for integral chi_R²|DU|² in terms of integral|W|² and integral|grad chi_R|²|U|². The latter is O(R^(1-2k)), which vanishes. Passage to the limit first proves DU in L2. Returning to the exact cutoff identity, all cross terms vanish by this L2 bound and the same cutoff estimate, proving equality. Pointwise |W|²=|DU|²-tr((DU)²) then gives the last equality. Since DU is also bounded, f belongs to L1 intersect L2 intersect Linfinity. Locally f is C1 because U is C2. Interior Poisson regularity therefore makes P C2, so the compact vector fields in the boundary pairings below are C1.

Let Gamma=1/(4pi|x|). The pressure is P=Gamma*f. Indeed this Newtonian potential solves -Delta P=f, is bounded, and tends to zero: approximate f in L1 by compactly supported functions, and use the bound ||Gamma*g||infinity<=C||g||1^(2/3)||g||infinity^(1/3) on the tail. Its difference from the normalized actual pressure is harmonic and tends to zero, hence vanishes. This argument only fixes the pressure normalization already required in(1).

Set K=grad Gamma. Splitting K into its part on the unit ball and its complement shows grad P belongs to L2: the first part is L1 and convolves with f in L2; the second is L2 and convolves with f in L1. Also

    R^(-1) integral_(R<|x|<2R) |grad P(x)| dx ->0.        (13)

Since integral f=0, write grad P(x)=integral[K(x-z)-K(x)]f(z)dz. For each fixed z, the annular integral of the kernel difference divided by R tends to zero. It is bounded uniformly in R,z by a constant: the integral of |K| on any translated annulus of radius O(R), divided by R, is bounded by scaling and the local integrability of |x|^-2. Dominated convergence against |f| proves(13). The same argument without subtraction gives a uniform O(R) annular L1 bound.

Because U and C=B cross W are O(r^-k), this annular O(R) bound shows U.grad P and C.grad P are absolutely integrable at infinity: their integrals on dyadic annuli are bounded by C R^(1-k), a summable series. The quantities P f and H f are integrable because P,H are bounded and f is L1. These facts justify all the following whole-domain integrals.

For any measurable D let chi=1_D and define the distributional boundary functional by

    <grad chi,X>=-integral_D div X

for compactly supported C1 vector fields X. No bounded-variation or smooth-boundary hypothesis is hidden in this definition. Set

    N_D(w)=lim_(R->infinity)<grad chi,chi_R w grad P>,

for w=P or H. Product differentiation and the just established integrability show these limits exist and give

    integral_D f=lim_R<grad chi,chi_R grad P>,
    integral_D |grad P|²=integral_D P f-N_D(P),
    a integral_D U.grad P
       =integral_D C.grad P-integral_D H f+N_D(H).        (14)

For the first formula the outer error tends to zero by(13). For the other two it is bounded by R^(-1) times an annular integral of |grad P| times sup(|P|+|H|), hence tends to zero by(1),(13). The last formula also uses aU=C-grad H. If D is B-invariant and(2) applies, its left side is a gamma e_D.

On a C1 boundary patch of a set invariant under both flows, B.nu=W.nu=0. Where C is nonzero, therefore nu=epsilon C/|C| with epsilon in{1,-1}, constant on each connected such patch. From(3) and U.nu=-(Ax).nu,

    partial_nu H=epsilon|C|+a(Ax).nu.                   (15)

There is no established sign for epsilon. This is a local patch statement; it neither grants regularity to the entire boundary nor interprets an arbitrary distributional grad chi as a surface measure.

For a B-invariant D, <grad chi,chi_R P B>=0. Since B=Ax+U and div U=0, this gives

    <grad chi,chi_R P Ax>
       =integral_D chi_R U.grad P
          +integral_D P U.grad chi_R.

The last error is O(R^(2-3k)log R), hence vanishes. Under(2),

    gamma e_D=lim_R<grad chi,chi_R P Ax>.                (16)

On a smooth boundary this is -lim integral_boundary chi_R P(Ax).nu, equivalently the pressure work with U.nu. The calculation never separately integrates P div B, which need not be integrable in the target range.

Two subclasses have a determined sign. If D is also invariant under exp(tA), the right side of(16) is zero. If U belongs to L2(D), then T=|V composed with S|² is in L1(D), by the integrable difference assumed in(2). Its exact time scaling and the volume factor give

    integral_D T=exp((5c-2)t)integral_D T.

Since 5c-2>0, this forces T=0 almost everywhere on D. Thus e_D=integral_D |U|²>=0. These are conditional subclasses, not a sign theorem for every basin.

Finally Delta H=div C is exactly the divergence of(3). Integrating this particular equality cannot add information independent of the full Lamb identity. This observation does not classify every possible choice of weighted Poisson test or prove that all such choices fail.

## 5. Exact sign-changing linear tail model

Fix c=9/20, omega=7/3, k=11/9 and a=1/10. On the punctured space let r=|x|, t=x3/r, Jx=(-x2,x1,0), and

    F(t)=t+t²/2, G(t)=1/2+t-2t²,
    V=curl(r^(-k)F(t)Jx)+r^(-k-1)G(t)Jx,
    B=Ax, W=curl V, P=0, H=(Ax).V, g=H, h=V.W.          (17)

Both summands in V are divergence-free. The second has zero divergence because G and r are axisymmetric and Jx is tangent to rotations. The field is smooth off zero, homogeneous of degree-k and axisymmetric, hence twisted homogeneous for this A. Differentiation of that homogeneity gives LV+DV Ax=0. The elementary curl identity then gives

    aV+grad((Ax).V)=Ax cross W.

Taking scalar contractions yields the two reduced derivative equations, and curl differentiation yields [Ax,W]=-(1+c)W. Therefore(7),(8) hold. The energy density q=|V|²/2 satisfies Bq=-2(1-c)q, and its renormalized difference from its identical tail is zero. Its beta=0 pressure work is zero. All compact test integration rules on the punctured space hold.

Here is an exact helicity sign test and integral, replacing sampled extrema. In spherical components, with sin(theta)=sqrt(1-t²), define

    A0(t)=2tF(t)-(1-t²)F'(t)=2t³+3t²-t-1,
    B0(t)=2tG(t)-(1-t²)G'(t)=-8t³+3t²+5t-1.

Then

    V_r=r^(-k)A0,
    V_theta=r^(-k)(k-2)sin(theta)F,
    V_phi=r^(-k)sin(theta)G,
    W_r=r^(-k-1)B0,
    W_theta=r^(-k-1)(k-1)sin(theta)G,
    W_phi=r^(-k-1)sin(theta)[(1-k)(k-2)F+A0'].

Multiplication cancels the two k-dependent terms, giving

    r^(2k+1)h=A0 B0+(1-t²)G A0'.                       (18)

The expressions extend smoothly to the axis. At the north unit pole h=-3; at the south unit pole h=5. Thus the source's printed values min=-2.98 and max=4.99 cannot be exact extrema. They may be sampled values, but the sign change itself is exact and needs no computation.

Put v0=(1-t²)G, so B0=-v0'. Integration by parts, with v0(+-1)=0, gives

    integral_(S²)h=4pi integral_(-1)^1 v0 A0' dt
                  =376pi/105.                          (19)

For completeness the even part of v0 A0' is

    -1/2+(23/2)t²-23t^4+12t^6,

whose integral is94/105. This proves(19) by ordinary polynomial integration. The identity WH=-ah integrated outside the sphere of radius epsilon gives

    integral_(r>epsilon) h
       =(1/a)epsilon^(2-2k) I1,
    I1=integral_(S²)H W_r=141pi/175.                     (20)

Equivalently(19)=(2(k-1)/a)I1. The outer flux vanishes because k>1, while the inner expression diverges as epsilon tends to zero. Thus h is not integrable through the missing core. The critical weighted energy flux of r^(-alpha)q Ax is independent of radius and nonzero, so its inner flux cannot be dropped; the subcritical weighted energy integral likewise diverges at the core.

This model obeys the explicitly listed reduced identities. It is not a full profile: its carrier is Ax rather than Ax+V, its pressure is not the canonical nonlinear pressure, and the nonlinear momentum equation is not imposed. It is singular at zero. Therefore it tests arguments using only the stated reduced tail equations and valid punctured-domain integrations, not every global Euler argument.

## 6. Other exact countertests from the source

The scalar two-copy energy model is also valid at its stated abstract scope. Let E_-=(1+r²)^(-k), T=r^(-2k), and choose a nonnegative smooth bump b0 on1<r<2 with integral r²b0 dr=1. Define

    d=integral_0^infinity r²[T-E_-]dr
      =k B(3/2,k-1/2)/(3-2k)>0,
    E_+=E_-+2d b0,
    F_+-=-(c/2)(r E_+-'+2k E_+-).

The integral for d converges at both ends. Its beta-function evaluation follows by integrating the derivative of r³(1+r²)^(-k)-r^(3-2k); the remaining convergent integral is B(3/2,k-1/2)/2. Direct radial integration gives

    e_+-=integral(E_+--T)dx=+-4pi d,
    integral F_+-=gamma e_+-,
    W_+-(beta)=(c/2)(-alpha-beta)integral r^beta E_+- dx>0
        for -3<beta<-alpha,
    W_+-(-alpha)=2pi c, W_+-(0)=-gamma e_+-.

The boundary terms are zero in the open interval; the outer critical boundary equals1 in radial units and the inner critical boundary is zero. The beta=0 formula uses the renormalized constant after cancellation of the common leading radial term. Thus e_++e_-=0 while the two constants have opposite signs. These are two disjoint copies of scalar data, not a velocity field satisfying the Euler coupling.

The affine local example is exact for omega=0 and every c:

    U=(-x1/2-bx2,bx1-x2/2,x3), b!=0,
    P=(1/8+b²/2)(x1²+x2²)-x3².

Writing U=Mx, its profile residual is Mx+M²x+grad P, which vanishes by direct multiplication. Its curl is(0,0,2b), h=2b x3, and

    P-q=-3x3²/2,
    H=(b²+a/4)(x1²+x2²)-(a/2)x3²,
    U.grad P=-(1/8+b²/2)(x1²+x2²)-2x3².

Thus local exact momentum and signed pressure work do not force local helicity to have one sign. The carrier spectrum is c-1/2+-ib and1+c. This is a saddle, with no positive-volume backward-attracting basin, and the velocity does not decay.

For reflection R=diag(1,1,-1), define U^R(x)=R U(Rx), P^R(x)=P(Rx). Since R commutes with A and J, the reflected pair is a profile at the same c,omega. The curl is -R W(Rx), so h^R(x)=-h(Rx). Orthogonal change of variables preserves scalar energy and pressure-work data on the reflected domain. This reverses overall helicity sign, but by itself says nothing about whether helicity changes sign within one basin.

Finally the source's compatibility observation is exact. If div U=0, B=Ax+U, div W=0, [B,W]=-(1+c)W and aU+grad H=B cross W, then

    curl(B cross W)=-[B,W]-(div B)W=aW.

Since a!=0, taking curls forces curl U=W. The full momentum equation likewise forces its Poisson equation by divergence. A model retaining that full structure cannot violate only the curl or Poisson relation independently.

## 7. Final scope of the method discussion

The exact scalar identities, boundary formulas and reduced models above are suitable checked results. They identify concrete missing uses of the full coupling and concrete nonvanishing boundary terms. The following stronger assertions are not established:

1. That no proof using arbitrary further background information can force one-signed helicity. The replacement construction is conditional on a vortical background; a separate contradiction for that background is not addressed.
2. That the list N1-N3 exhausts every possible future P1 proof. It is a useful set of candidate directions, not an exhaustive classification theorem.
3. That every weighted local Poisson argument fails. The displayed integrations are checked, and their unknown terms remain unknown; all possible test functions and inequalities have not been classified.

A lower bound equivalent, via an exact identity, to h=0 can still be useful if independently proved. What fails in the tested proposal is treating that bound as an already reduced or established condition. None of these countertests refutes P1, constructs a smooth decaying full Euler profile, or resolves the main arbitrary-omega conjecture.
