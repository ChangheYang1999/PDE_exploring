# Exact tangential dual optimum for the solenoidal sphere kernel

## 1. Definitions and conclusions

On the unit sphere S2 with area dA, put M=diag(1,-1,0), k(theta)=theta cross M theta, f(theta)=x²-y², and

    F(M)=inf_(phi in C1(S2)) (3/(4pi))
            integral sqrt(|theta cross M theta-grad_S phi|²+4phi²) dA,
    D1=F(diag(1,-1,0)).

Define the tangential dual value

    T=sup (3/(4pi)) integral k.g dA,

where g is measurable, tangent to S2, |g|<=1 almost everywhere and div_S g=0 in distributions. Then

    D1>=T=V0,
    V0=(36/pi) integral_0^1 s sqrt(1-s²) arcsin(s/sqrt2) ds,
    1.66925<V0<1.66926.

The independently certified primal trial A1233 gives D1<1.720646. Its distance from the unknown sharp D1 is therefore below0.0514 and below3.1 percent of D1. The field attaining T is generated by the negative signed distance to the two great circles f=0, with the orientation specified below.

These are statements about a variational kernel constant, not Euler-profile nonexistence. They hold independently of c and omega. Section8 justifies the resulting limitation on the previously proved Euler method for every Holder exponent.

## 2. Dual lower bound and its sign

For any admissible g and every C1 phi, the pointwise Cauchy inequality gives

    sqrt(|k-grad_S phi|²+4phi²)>=(k-grad_S phi).g.

The weak divergence condition makes the integral of grad_S phi.g zero, so D1>=T. For a smooth scalar psi set g=grad_S psi cross theta. This field is tangent, divergence free and has magnitude |grad_S psi|. Put q0=f/2. The vector triple product and the spherical harmonic equation give

    theta cross k=-grad_S q0, Delta_S q0=-6q0,
    integral k.(grad_S psi cross theta) dA
       =integral psi Delta_S q0 dA=-3 integral psi f dA.       (1)

Thus a positive pairing with psi f uses g=-grad_S psi cross theta. The sign is immaterial to the supremum but matters when naming the attaining field. Consequently

    D1>=(9/(4pi)) integral psi f dA

whenever psi is1-Lipschitz for geodesic distance. To justify this for nonsmooth psi, average psi over smooth rotation kernels near the identity. Each average is smooth, retains Lipschitz constant at most1 because rotations preserve distance, and converges uniformly to psi. The displayed integral then passes to the limit.

## 3. Signed distance is globally1-Lipschitz

Let Z={f=0}, the union of the great circles x=y and x=-y, and let d denote spherical geodesic distance. Set

    psi2(theta)=sign(f(theta)) dist(theta,Z), with psi2=0 on Z.

For two points with the same sign, this is the usual1-Lipschitz property of distance to a closed set. For points of opposite signs, a minimizing geodesic meets Z at a point p, by continuity of f. Then

    |psi2(theta1)-psi2(theta2)|
      =dist(theta1,Z)+dist(theta2,Z)
      <=d(theta1,p)+d(p,theta2)=d(theta1,theta2).

Points on Z follow by the same estimate or continuity. Thus psi2 is globally1-Lipschitz. Its pairing with f is nonnegative, and Section2 supplies a rigorous lower bound from this exact function, without numerical optimization.

## 4. Exact reduction to one integral

The eight coordinate sign changes and the interchange x,y reduce the integral of psi2 f to sixteen copies of the wedge x>y>0,z>0. On this wedge the nearer great circle is x=y and

    dist(theta,Z)=arcsin((x-y)/sqrt2).

Use orthonormal coordinates u=(x-y)/sqrt2, v=(x+y)/sqrt2, z. Then f=2uv. Taking u as polar-axis coordinate, write v=sqrt(1-u²)cos t, z=sqrt(1-u²)sin t; spherical area is du dt. The wedge is

    0<u<1/sqrt2, 0<t<arccos(u/sqrt(1-u²)).

The integral of v over this t interval is exactly sqrt(1-2u²). Hence

    (9/(4pi)) integral psi2 f dA
      =(36/pi) integral_0^(1/sqrt2) 2u sqrt(1-2u²) arcsin u du
      =(36/pi) integral_0^1 s sqrt(1-s²) arcsin(s/sqrt2) ds=V0.

Every domain boundary has zero area, so no convention there changes the integral.

## 6. Every tangential divergence-free field has a Lipschitz stream potential

Here is a direct justification of the source's Hodge step, avoiding an optimal-transport theorem. First let g be smooth. The one-form beta=(theta cross g).dtheta is closed, because its surface curl equals div_S g. On each stereographic chart it has a scalar potential: integrate the closed form on disks exhausting the plane, using independence of paths on each disk and fixing additive constants. The north and south chart potentials differ by a constant on their connected overlap, so they glue to one smooth b on the sphere. Then

    grad_S b=theta cross g, g=grad_S b cross theta.

If |g|<=1, integrating along geodesics proves b is1-Lipschitz.

For a bounded distributionally divergence-free g, smooth it by averaging its rotated push-forwards. The averaged fields g_epsilon are smooth, tangent, divergence free and bounded by1; they converge to g in L1. Their stream potentials b_epsilon, normalized at one fixed point, are1-Lipschitz and bounded by pi. A uniformly convergent subsequence exists; its limit b is1-Lipschitz. Testing the gradient identity against smooth fields and passing to the limit yields grad_S b=theta cross g weakly, hence the required representation almost everywhere. Formula(1) passes to this limit as well.

Conversely, a1-Lipschitz b has a bounded weak gradient obtained from local difference quotients, and grad_S b cross theta is an admissible distributionally divergence-free field. Thus

    T=(9/(4pi)) sup_(b1-Lipschitz) integral b f dA.     (3)

The sign in(1) is absorbed by b->-b.

## 7. Reflection proves exact optimality

For f(theta)>0, reflect theta in the nearer great circle of Z. Explicitly,

    T_ref(x,y,z)=(y,x,z) when xy>=0,
    T_ref(x,y,z)=(-y,-x,z) when xy<0.

This is a measure-preserving involution from {f>0} to {f<0}, with f(T_ref theta)=-f(theta). Each piece is an orthogonal reflection, and

    d(theta,T_ref theta)=2 dist(theta,Z).

Indeed, reflection across a plane with unit normal n has theta.T_ref(theta)=1-2(n.theta)², so the distance is2 arcsin|n.theta|. The nearer of the two planes gives the asserted formula.

For any1-Lipschitz b, change variables on the negative region to obtain

    integral b f dA
      =integral_(f>0) f(theta)[b(theta)-b(T_ref theta)] dA
      <=integral_(f>0) 2f(theta)dist(theta,Z) dA
      =integral psi2 f dA.

Equality holds for b=psi2. Combining with Sections4 and6 proves T=V0. In the original g orientation the attaining field is -grad_S psi2 cross theta. No Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality is needed. A stronger lower bound obtained through admissible dual fields must therefore use a nonzero radial component somewhere; purely tangential admissible fields cannot exceed V0.

## 8. Why another Holder exponent cannot evade this method ceiling

Extend F to every real symmetric matrix by the definition in Section1. It is an even positively homogeneous convex function: scale or negate phi for homogeneity and use phi1+phi2 with the pointwise Euclidean triangle inequality for subadditivity. It is invariant under orthogonal conjugation, since a rotation of the sphere preserves the integrand after rotating phi, with an additional sign for a reflection if needed.

For0<alpha<=1 set M_alpha=diag(1,-alpha,0). A conjugate followed by negation is M_alpha'=diag(alpha,-1,0), so F(M_alpha')=F(M_alpha). Their average is((1+alpha)/2)M. Convexity gives

    F(M_alpha)>=((1+alpha)/2)D1.

The worst-orientation coefficient D_alpha is at least this orthogonal-pair value. Hence

    D_alpha/alpha >= ((1+alpha)/(2alpha))D1 >= D1.     (4)

This fills the all-exponent comparison needed for a limitation on the method, not just its alpha=1 form. The known alpha=1 orientation reduction identifies D1 as the limiting coefficient of this route. Thus a sufficient vorticity bound obtained by that route cannot pass Gamma_a/D1, where Gamma_a=min(c(1+a),1), or1/(2D1) in the older natural-tail SV.1 form.

From the certified lower bound in(2), exact rational comparisons give

    Gamma_a/D1 <0.599072 Gamma_a,
    1/(2D1)<0.29954.

The upper trial1.720646 remains the actual certified sufficient coefficient, giving the known threshold Gamma_a/1.720646. A lower bound on the optimal constant is a limitation on that method; it does not improve a sufficient Euler threshold or construct a profile above it.

## 9. What is and is not optimized

Only the tangential dual problem has been solved exactly. The full D1 may be strictly larger, and its exact value is not determined. The primal numerical trials reported by the source are not used here. The source's advertised3.1 percent statement is certified by(1.720646-V_lower)/V_lower<0.031 using(2), so it is an error bound for a trial relative to an unknown optimum, not a claim that the optimum has been computed.

No arbitrary-omega obstruction beyond the already proved derivative criterion follows. The constants in this sphere calculation have no omega dependence, but the remaining Euler hypotheses still matter.
