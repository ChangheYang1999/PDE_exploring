## 1. Statement, hypotheses and reading scope

Let 0<c<1/2, omega be any real number, J(y1,y2,y3)=(-y2,y1,0), and let U be C-infinity on R3 and P be C1. Assume div U=0, U is uniformly sublinear, and

    (1-c)U + ((cI-omega J)y+U).grad U + omega JU + grad P = 0.

Write B=(cI-omega J)y+U, W=curl U, d=3c, kappa=1+c and a=kappa-d=1-2c. Let Psi be the flow of -B. For an admissible large closed ball Q define K=intersection_(t>=0) Psi_t(Q) and O={W!=0}. The claim being rechecked is that O is covered up to Lebesgue measure zero by the pairwise disjoint positive-volume Borel basins of at most countably many whole-flow-ergodic probabilities on K. Their spectrum is (a,0,-kappa), their center and stable lines are RB and RW, their entropy for Psi_1 is a, and their unstable conditionals are absolutely continuous. The further claims are the conditional density C|alpha(eu)|, alpha=(B cross W).dy, and the disjoint invariant open essential domains of infinite volume.

The corresponding abstract claim assumes smooth complete B, smooth W, div B=d>0, [B,W]=-kappa W with kappa>d, and B(y).y>=b0|y|^2 outside a ball for some b0>0. It asserts the coverage and spectral conclusions, not the special alpha-density formula.

## 2. Complete flow, common cutoff and the incoming construction

Uniform sublinearity gives |B(y)|<=C(1+|y|). The smooth locally Lipschitz field therefore has complete solutions in both directions, by the linear growth estimate. The skew identity Jy.y=0 gives B(y).y>=c|y|^2/2 for all sufficiently large |y|, uniformly in the sense that no restriction on a fixed real omega is required. Every forward Psi orbit enters int Q, and the union of forward orbits of a bounded set is bounded. The compact sets Psi_t(Q) decrease with t; their intersection K is compact and whole-flow invariant. These statements in the abstract theorem use its assumed completeness and radial inequality instead of linear growth.

Choose once a smooth radial nonincreasing cutoff rho, equal to1 near Q and zero outside a larger ball. In the collar with rho>0 the flow of -rho B decreases the radius and does not decrease rho. Its initially positive value supplies a positive lower bound until entry into int Q, which occurs in finite time. Every point of this collar is nonrecurrent. An invariant probability gives that collar mass zero by recurrence. Where rho=0, nonnegativity and smoothness give D rho=0, hence D(rho B)=0; all exponents of these fixed points vanish. The added point in the compactification has the same property. A relevant ergodic probability with a positive exponent is therefore supported on K. The metric and map can be kept Euclidean near Q. Choosing rho before the tree parameters removes any dependence on a later incompatible cutoff.

Transport gives D Psi_t W=e^(-kappa t) W composed with Psi_t, D Psi_t B=B composed with Psi_t, and det D Psi_t=e^(-dt). A positive-volume subset of O yields, by the singular-value determinant bound and coordinate Fubini, an arc with a positive-length subset having upper tangent exponent at least (kappa-d)/2.

The printed tree proof chooses its regularity r before the long integer iterate p, and its spatial scale last. Its bounded affine restrictions keep total distortion below2 without multiplying a new distortion factor at each level. The counted outputs are nonlarge outputs. All outputs are small, so replacing nonlarge by small would be false; the current text does not make that replacement. The hyperbolic-time counting gives selected sets E_n of mass at least1/(2n^2). Thus their entropy cost log(1/m_n) is O(log n). With N_n=floor(sqrt n), both N_n/n and N_n log(1/m_n)/n tend to0.

The entropy-selection lemma then gives absolute continuity of the joint incoming measure over normalized curve arclength. Fixed windows supply a positive lower bound for image length, so constant limit curves cannot carry its mass. Selected parents increase with the window; the parent of a selected atom is selected in the next larger window, using the same future witness. This proves g_*mu>=mu in the increasing limit, and finite equal masses give invariance. Original approximants meet a compact density set and have target fractions tending to1. Their normalized curves converge in C1, their distortion stays below2, and every negative g-iterate contracts in diameter at least100^(-k). These are exactly the interfaces used below.

## 3. Components and the irrotational exclusion

The joint incoming measure is absolutely continuous with respect to its curve marginal times normalized arclength. Two fixed points of B cannot lie on one incoming curve: their negative iterates stay fixed while the curve diameter tends to0. Thus m(B=0)=0. At regular points, Dg^n B(x)=B(g^n x) is bounded for every integer n because the orbit stays in K. The forward rate is the largest exponent present and the backward rate is the negative of the smallest. B consequently belongs to the zero space. The incoming tangent contracts backward, giving a positive exponent. The determinant fixes the remaining exponent, so the spectrum for g=Psi_p is (p chi,0,-p(d+chi)), chi>0.

All generic points on one incoming curve have the same g-ergodic component, because backward distances tend to0 and continuous tests are uniformly continuous on K. A countable dense family makes the component label measurable. Disintegrating the curve marginal over this label retains absolute continuity of incoming conditionals. The incoming curves lie in full unstable leaves, using the exponential-backward-approach characterization at regular points. Leaf-null sets are therefore null for almost every component. The jointly measurable Radon-Nikodym argument in Lemma20 converts this null-set statement into absolute continuity on a subordinate partition; it does not require a measurable choice of singular supports. The lower entropy bound and Ruelle's upper bound give h_mu(g)=p chi.

Suppose m(W=0)>0. A finite-window incoming curve contains a positive-length compact set of regular points in that closed invariant zero set. Choose a density point where its tangent is unstable. On the strong stable plaque through that point, the transported field W vanishes. To check this last assertion pointwise, put lambda=p(d+chi)>0. In tempered two-sided Lyapunov coordinates the least singular value of Dg along the base orbit is at least e^(-lambda-epsilon). Its perturbation along the exponentially approaching plaque is O(e^((-lambda+2epsilon)n)). For sufficiently small epsilon this yields a derivative lower bound c e^((-lambda-3epsilon)n). The C1 vanishing of W at the base orbit gives |W(g^n y)|<=C e^((-lambda+epsilon)n), whereas transport gives the lower bound c e^((p kappa-lambda-3epsilon)n)|W(y)|. Letting n grow proves W(y)=0. The real-time bracket law, not merely the integer law, also makes its flow translates irrotational.

The carrier strip through the incoming curve is transverse to that stable plaque: its two columns span unstable and center directions. Uniformly converging original strips must meet the plaque by the local degree argument. But every original strip starts in O and O is invariant under all carrier times, a contradiction. Hence m(W=0)=0. This step assumes no measure-theoretic absolute continuity of stable holonomy.

## 4. Whole-flow averaging and the revised entropy step

For almost every g-ergodic component mu, both B and W are nonzero almost everywhere. Recurrence to sets on which |W| is bounded below, together with its transport formula, makes its exponent exactly -p kappa. Consequently chi=kappa-d. Define

    nu = (1/p) integral_0^p (Psi_s)_*mu ds.

The family being averaged is p-periodic and consists of g-invariant probabilities. The average is invariant for the whole flow. A whole-flow-invariant Borel set is also g-invariant, has mu measure0 or1, and has the same value under every member of the family. Thus nu is whole-flow ergodic. This argument does not assert that nu is ergodic for Psi_1.

Every Psi_s is a conjugacy commuting with g. Its derivative and inverse are uniformly bounded on K for 0<=s<=p, so the spectrum and g entropy are unchanged under this conjugacy. The proved integral-affinity identity applies to the measurable kernel s -> (Psi_s)_*mu without requiring that this kernel itself be an ergodic decomposition. It gives h_nu(g)=p(kappa-d). Since p is an integer, g=(Psi_1)^p and the entropy iterate identity gives h_nu(Psi_1)=kappa-d. Its proof uses the join over the first p iterates of any finite partition; it needs neither ergodicity nor an Abramov formula. Ledrappier-Young's entropy-equality implication now gives the required unstable absolute continuity for nu.

For each continuous test phi, include phibar=(1/p)integral_0^p phi composed with Psi_s ds in the countable genericity family. Then its g Birkhoff sums are exactly the carrier integral averages at times np. Boundedness handles the leftover time interval. Every selected point generic for these tests lies in Basin(nu). This explains why the averaged tests must be chosen before invoking genericity.

## 5. Original-target capture and countability

The revised Lemma26 applies the incoming selection to an m-full Borel set of two-sided generic regular points whose g-component satisfies the preceding conclusions. Measurability of its component label was established before this selection. A finite-window curve then has a positive-length compact set T of points with one common component label. No preassigned component is claimed to work.

At a density point in T, the map H(s,t,tau)=Gamma_tau(Psi_t gamma(s)) has independent unstable, center and stable columns. It is a local C1 diffeomorphism, and a compact positive-length subset of T times a small rectangle maps into Basin(nu). The carrier and W shift identities preserve basin membership. The construction takes place near K inside int Q, so two-sided small carrier times cause no boundary problem. This product set has positive volume.

For the original curves converging to gamma, the first coordinate of H inverse converges uniformly to s. Its endpoint values bracket a fixed compact subset of T. The intermediate value theorem and the Lipschitz bound of that coordinate force a fixed positive portion of image arclength into the basin product set. Distortion below2 and g invariance pull it back to a fixed positive relative length in the original parent. The parent's target fraction tends to1. For large indices the target and the basin therefore have positive-length intersection.

Consider now all whole-flow-ergodic probabilities on K with nonzero B and W, the stated simple spectrum and absolutely continuous unstable conditionals. For every member of this class, recurrence and transport give its stable line RW, and two-sided bounded transport gives its center line RB. Thus each satisfies the product-set hypotheses and has a positive-volume basin. The entropy lower and upper bounds also give kappa-d for every member, not only for the initially selected component. Bounded continuous tests distinguish distinct probabilities, so distinct basins are disjoint. The basins are Borel by the countable test construction. A disjoint family of positive-volume Borel subsets of R3 is countable: each has volume greater than1/j in an integer-radius ball, and each fixed pair permits only finitely many.

Every such basin is contained in O, since the closed invariant zero set of W gives zero average for min(1,|W|). If the complement of their union in O had positive volume, integer-time entrance into int Q and preservation of null sets by flow diffeomorphisms would provide a positive-volume target there. The preceding capture gives a point in one of the very basins omitted from that target, a contradiction. This proves almost-everywhere coverage.

## 6. Density and pointwise essential-domain invariance

For Euler, curl(B cross W)=aW, alpha(B)=alpha(W)=0, L_W alpha=0 and Psi_t^*alpha=e^(at)alpha. The pressure curl is legitimate with P initially C1 because its gradient, expressed by the equation, is C1. At regular points the stable and center lines are independent and span ker alpha. Put A=|alpha(eu)|. Covariance gives J^u Psi_1=e^a A/(A composed with Psi_1). The unstable conditional product formula telescopes, leaving A(y)/A(z) times a backward-endpoint ratio. Backward returns of a regular base point to a compact Pesin block with A bounded below, together with the common unstable graph's tangent control, make the endpoint ratio tend to1 along a subsequence. The product already has a full limit. Hence the conditional density is C A. Strict positivity on full conditional plaques is supplied by the checked Ledrappier-Young corollaries, not inferred only from positivity at regular points.

Near almost every generic regular point, this positivity and the product chart make the basin have full volume in an open neighborhood. Let D be the union of all open subsets of O on which that basin has full volume. A countable subcover gives Leb(D minus Basin)=0 and nu(D)=1. Carrier shifts preserve it by basin invariance and preservation of null sets.

The revised W argument uses only pointwise forward boundedness. For each y, the closure of its forward Psi orbit is compact; the W flow has a common existence time on a compact neighborhood of this closure. For sufficiently small |s| the bracket identity yields

    Psi_r Gamma_s y = Gamma_(s e^(-kappa r)) Psi_r y,
    distance(Psi_r Gamma_s y,Psi_r y) <= C_y |s|e^(-kappa r),  r>=0.

Basin membership is consequently locally constant on every W orbit, hence constant along each connected W segment. No common bound over all starting points in a neighborhood is assumed. For a fixed short W time the local map is a C1 diffeomorphism, preserves null sets, and preserves basin membership at each point; it therefore preserves D. Every basin point eventually enters D, using a nonnegative continuous test supported in D of positive nu integral, and carrier invariance then places that point in D already. Maximality follows from the definition; overlapping distinct domains would force disjoint basins both to have full volume in a nonempty open set.

Finally, div B=d and whole-flow invariance give Leb(D)=e^(dt)Leb(D). Since D has positive volume, its volume is infinite. This is not an integration by parts over D and has no omitted boundary flux. The irrotational case gives harmonic U; the harmonic derivative estimate and uniform sublinearity force U constant.
