## 4. Spectral scope corrections still needed

The new C66-C70 summaries match their separately checked results, subject to the later status updates. C71-C72 remain submitted in this pin. Their source constructions have been fully read separately, but that does not supply an automatic handoff-wide approval. In item34 the integrability range should be written 0<p<2.

Several unchanged summary sentences exceed their own precise theorems.

First, C.7 literally says that no scalar function of a finite jet of order at most five is nonincreasing along B on all local solutions when omega is nonzero. This literal statement is false. For any fixed constant K, Psi=K is C1 on every jet space and d_B Psi=0 on every solution. The intended classification concerns functions nonconstant on the jets actually attained by local solutions, with the exact admissibility and closure qualifications stated in the detailed theorems. Those qualifications also matter because the source itself gives functions nonconstant away from solution jets which vanish on every local solution. The short sentence should state the precise classification instead.

Second, the omega-blindness lemma classifies a specified family of rotation-equivariant local densities and axisymmetric compactly supported test weights. Its proof does not quantify over every possible integral argument. Therefore the sentence that the integral method reaches c<2/5 only must refer to the particular decay-only criterion cited there. No upper limit on every integral method is established.

Third, C.11(5) and C.12 infer that any arbitrary-omega obstruction must be global and use curl globally. Exclusion of specified local Lyapunov functions through a finite derivative order does not exclude other local identities, higher-order functions, or combinations of local arguments. Kinematic counterexamples show that equations they satisfy cannot by themselves distinguish those examples from Euler solutions. They do not show that every successful proof must use a particular equation globally. The inference is unsupported; the truth of all possible alternative proof mechanisms is not decided here.

Fourth, the existence of leading tails with any selected angular mode does not establish existence of actual profiles with that mode. C.11(1) should say that the leading-tail constraints alone force no such mode to vanish. The unrestricted nonlinear statement remains open. Fifth, C.11(6) points to a nonexistent C.14 in this handoff; it should identify the actual modal-energy result or source. These repairs do not refute the individually qualified Fourier and finite-jet theorems.
