# An axisymmetric transported scalar forces symmetry of the velocity tail

Status: CONFIRMED at A622, A623, A624. Exact hypotheses, proof and limitations are stated below. Informal independent verification; no formal build or numerical proof is claimed.

## 1. Statements and exact assumptions

Let 1/3<c<1/2, omega be any finite real number, Jx=(-x2,x1,0), A=cI-omega J, a=1-2c, p=(1-c)/c and d=p-1=a/c, so 0<d<1. Let D=R3 minus {0}, V in C1(D;R3), W=curl V, k=(Ax).V, and assume

    div V=0,
    V(exp(sA)x)=exp(-(1-c)s)exp(-omega sJ)V(x).

Let sigma in C1(D;R) be axisymmetric about the third axis and have twisted degree -nu, with nu>a. Put gamma=a/nu and w=|sigma|^gamma. Assume 1/w is locally integrable on D, interpreting it as infinity on the zero set, and

    W.grad sigma=0 on D,
    grad k cross grad sigma=0 on {W!=0}.                      (1)

Then V is axisymmetric about the third axis. The reciprocal condition implies w>0 almost everywhere. Neither local A2, a prescribed nodal geometry, vorticity density, a pressure equation, nor a separate helicity assumption is needed.

For the full-profile consequence let 2/5<c<1/2, omega be arbitrary, U in C2(R3;R3) and P in C1(R3) solve

    ((1-c)I+omega J)U+DU(Ax+U)+grad P=0, div U=0,
    U.curl U=0 on R3,
    |U(x)|<=C(1+|x|)^-p,
    |DU(x)|<=C(1+|x|)^(-p-1).                                (2)

Then every low actual pressure multipole Theta_n with (n+5)c<3 is0. At c>=3/7 these indices are0,1; at c<3/7 they are0,1,2. Uniform U->0 and DU=O(sqrt r) are alternative starting assumptions because the confirmed derivative theorems give(2). The actual S need not be A2 and the vorticity need not be dense.

A second full-profile consequence is U0 if the actual S=P2-|V|^2/2 is axisymmetric and |S|^-beta is locally integrable, beta=a/[2(1-c)]. S identically0 is separately excluded for a nonzero tail by the canonical mean identity. These are conditional results; no nonzero low multipole is known to be forced by U!=0.

## 2. A harmonic barrier on an arbitrary open set

Let O be open in D. Suppose f is continuous on D, harmonic on O, zero on D minus O, and |f(x)|<=C|x|^-d with 0<d<1. Then f0.

For epsilon<|x|<R use the positive harmonic barrier

    b(y)=C epsilon^(1-d)/|y|+C R^-d

on O intersect {epsilon<|y|<R}. It dominates |f| on both spherical boundaries. At every remaining boundary point f0 by continuity. The closure of this truncated domain is compact in D, so f-b and -f-b are continuous on it; the elementary maximum principle on arbitrary bounded open sets gives

    |f(x)|<=C epsilon^(1-d)/|x|+C R^-d.

No smooth boundary is needed: any positive maximum above the boundary values would be attained in the interior and contradict the maximum principle on its component. Let epsilon->0 and R->infinity. The strict d<1 and d>0 make both terms vanish. The same result is already true outside O by hypothesis. This is a comparison proof, not analytic continuation across the interface.

## 3. The actual scalar k is axisymmetric

Homogeneity gives the exact vector identity

    grad k=-aV+(Ax) cross W.                                  (3)

Set f=(Jx).grad k. This is continuous and has twisted scalar degree -a, since rotations generated by J commute with A. Its bounded values on the unit sphere therefore give the global bound C r^-d, without any differentiability beyond C1 for k.

Since w>0 almost everywhere, sigma!=0 is dense in every open subset of D. At a point with W!=0 and sigma!=0, homogeneity gives Ax.grad sigma=-nu sigma!=0. The second equation of(1) says grad k is parallel to grad sigma. Axisymmetry of sigma then gives f0. Continuity extends this to the entire open set W!=0 and its closure.

Let O=int_D{W=0}. Its complement is the closure of {W!=0}, so f0 there. On O equation(3) gives V=-grad k/a. Thus Delta k0 distributionally, by div V0, and harmonic regularity makes k smooth. The infinitesimal rotation operator Jx.grad commutes with the Euclidean Laplacian, so f is harmonic on O. Section2 gives f0 everywhere. Integrating this infinitesimal identity along each rotation circle proves that k is globally axisymmetric.

This does not extend the original rank through an irrotational component. It extends the vanishing of a different scalar, the rotational derivative of the actual harmonic k, using its strict global growth bound at0 and decay at infinity.

## 4. The nonsymmetric velocity difference has a global potential

Let R_s=exp(sJ) and define

    Vbar(x)=(1/(2pi))integral_0^(2pi) R_s V(R_s^-1 x)ds,
    Y=V-Vbar, Z=curl Y.

Rotations preserve divergence, commute with A, and preserve sigma. Therefore Vbar and Y are C1 and divergence free, with the original twisted homogeneity, and Z.grad sigma0. Axisymmetry of k gives

    (Ax).Vbar=k, hence (Ax).Y=0.                              (4)

Apply(3) to Y. Since its scalar in(4) is identically0,

    (Ax) cross Z=aY.

Crossing on the left with grad sigma and using Ax.grad sigma=-nu sigma and Z.grad sigma0 yields

    grad sigma cross Y=(sigma/gamma)Z.                       (5)

This has global scope, unlike the original rank premise.

For epsilon>0, ordinary differentiation proves

    curl((sigma^2+epsilon^2)^(-gamma/2)Y)
       =epsilon^2(sigma^2+epsilon^2)^(-gamma/2-1)Z.            (6)

On each compact set, the left field converges to Y/w in L1. The right side converges to0 almost everywhere and is dominated by |Z|/w. The reciprocal hypothesis and bounded Z make this an integrable majorant. Values on the measure-zero scalar zero set do not affect the argument. Thus curl(Y/w)=0 in distributions.

A distributionally closed L1 one-form on simply connected D has a global potential psi in W1,1_loc(D), with

    grad psi=Y/w almost everywhere.                          (7)

For clarity, on balls one obtains a potential by mollifying the field on slightly larger balls, using the classical local Poincare construction and the ordinary L1 Poincare inequality to pass to a W1,1 limit after subtracting its mean. On connected overlaps the potentials differ by constants. Their continuation around loops is consistent because D is simply connected; equivalently its first real cohomology is0. The patched potential is unique up to a constant. No weighted Sobolev completion or weighted elliptic theorem is used.

## 5. An annular energy identity and azimuthal cancellation

Choose any nonnegative smooth radial cutoff chi(r) with compact support in 0<r<infinity. Then chi psi is ordinary W1,1 and compactly supported in D. Smooth approximation on that compact set permits it as a test against the bounded C1 divergence-free field Y. By(7),

    integral chi |Y|^2/w
       =-integral chi'(r)psi(x.Y)/r
       =-(omega/c)integral [chi'(r)/r]psi(Jx).Y.              (8)

The second equality uses c x.Y=omega(Jx).Y from(4). The first integrand is integrable because Y is bounded locally and1/w is locally integrable. The second is integrable because psi is L1 locally and Y, x and cutoff derivatives are bounded on its compact support. There is no boundary at the origin or at infinity in this argument.

The last integral in(8) is0. Use cylindrical coordinates (rho,phi,z) away from the measure-zero axis. Ordinary W1,1 slicing gives, for almost every (rho,z) with rho>0, a periodic W1,1 representative of psi as a function of phi. Axisymmetry and positivity almost everywhere of w imply that on almost every such circle it is a finite positive constant. Equation(7) gives on that circle

    (Jx).Y=w partial_phi psi.

A one-dimensional W1,1 function is bounded and absolutely continuous. Its square is therefore W1,1 on that circle and

    integral_0^(2pi) psi(Jx).Y dphi
       =(w/2)integral_0^(2pi)partial_phi(psi^2)dphi=0.

Fubini is justified by the absolute integrability already proved for the original product psi(Jx).Y. The factors chi'(r)/r and the cylindrical Jacobian are independent of phi. This proves the asserted cancellation. When omega0 the right side of(8) is0 directly.

Thus integral chi |Y|^2/w=0 for every such chi. Nonnegative radial cutoffs positive on successive compact annuli cover D. Positivity of w almost everywhere implies Y0 almost everywhere, and continuity gives Y0 everywhere. Hence V=Vbar is axisymmetric, proving the standalone theorem at C1 regularity.

## 7. Dependencies, countertests and scope

The new argument uses the actual homogeneity identity(3), a harmonic barrier with strict0<d<1, distributional ordinary potentials on simply connected punctured space, and an annular identity with an axisymmetric weight. It has no singular-origin extension, no vorticity-density premise and no weighted regularity import. The barrier would fail at d1; no endpoint conclusion is made.

Local irrotational or matched-interface examples do not contradict this global theorem. Their domains are proper cones and do not supply the global growth comparison, entire rotation averages, or a full sphere with the required scalar. Conversely the proof does not extend original rank across their interfaces and does not assert that every scalar becomes axisymmetric.

The full-profile application separately uses A550/A610 for actual multipoles, A608 for polynomial inverse integrability, and A569-A570 for the canonical axisymmetric-tail exclusion.
