## 3. The independent degree in Example 5

One local notation repair is needed before unqualified cumulative approval. The row first uses k for complex source dimension, but then states that Example5 is not C^(k+1). In the source example the corresponding exponent is an independent positive integer polynomial degree. Write the complex dimension as d_complex>=2 and the degree as m>=1. With t=log(1/|z|) and 1/2<epsilon<(2d_complex-1)/(2d_complex), the example is

    u_m(z)=exp(-t^epsilon) z_1^m,  u_m(0)=0.

It is not C^(m+1). On the positive real first-coordinate axis, u_m(x)=o(x^m), whereas u_m(x)/x^(m+1)=exp(t-t^epsilon) tends to infinity. A C^(m+1) Taylor expansion would have all coefficients through degree m zero and a finite latter quotient, a contradiction.

It is nevertheless C^m. For each fixed derivative order j and t>=1, the product and chain rules give

    |D^j exp(-t^epsilon)| <= C_j |z|^(-j) exp(-t^epsilon),

because the positive-order t derivatives of t^epsilon are bounded there. Differentiating the polynomial and summing Leibniz terms gives, for j<=m,

    |D^j u_m(z)| <= C_(j,m) |z|^(m-j) exp(-t^epsilon).

Every derivative through order m tends to zero at the origin. For the induction identifying the derivative of order j at zero, divide the bound of order j-1 by |z|: the result is bounded by C |z|^(m-j) exp(-t^epsilon), which still tends to zero for j<=m. This proves Frechet differentiability at each induction step and continuity of the new derivative. Hence u_m extends as C^m with zero m-jet.

In particular, in complex dimensiontwo choose m=3 and any allowed epsilon. Then u_m is C^3, contradicting a reading of the report's C^(k+1) as C^(2+1). The correct report sentence should explicitly use an independent m>=1 and say not C^(m+1). This is a notation repair, not a failure of the source example, the confirmed source theorem or the Euler results.
