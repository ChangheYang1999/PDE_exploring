## Statement and notation

    N_j(A)^2 = integral_A [f_j^2+|X|^2|grad f_j|^2] w(|X|) dX,
    w(r)=L^6 r^-6(1+r^2/L^2)^(3/2-k), j=0,1.

Require that these are the same global functions, with their weak gradients, in every regional estimate. In particular they extend across the center and their traces and classical derivatives agree at the annular joins as in the accepted canonical regularity theorem. Independently valid estimates for unrelated fields or piecewise functions with jumps do not satisfy this hypothesis.

Partition the plane into I={r<exp(a)}, M={exp(a)<r<exp(b)} and O={r>exp(b)}. Suppose nonnegative finite exact rational numbers S_(A,j) satisfy N_j(A)^2<=S_(A,j) for A=I,M,O and j=0,1. Then

    N_j(R^2)^2 <= S_(I,j)+S_(M,j)+S_(O,j).                 (1)

The boundary circles and the origin have planar measure zero. The global weak derivative hypothesis rules out extra singular derivative terms supported on a join. Additivity of the nonnegative Lebesgue integral on this partition proves (1). Finiteness of the right side gives the full norm in the stated space.

For each region and norm, if two independently proved bounds S^A_(A,j) and S^B_(A,j) are available, their maximum is also a bound. We deliberately use the maximum, rather than treating interval overlap as proof. Replacing one regional bound by another valid bound for exactly that same region, trial and norm preserves (1). In particular the exterior may be divided at b+17: the finite cells 0<=t-b<=17 and the infinite tail t-b>=17 have only measure-zero overlap. Their squared bounds add.
