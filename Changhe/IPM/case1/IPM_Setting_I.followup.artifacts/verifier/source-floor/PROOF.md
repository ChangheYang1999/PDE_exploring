# Center obstruction, source regularity, and a numerical residual floor

Date: 2026-10-20. Status: complete audit of logical implications. No new coupled profile is constructed.

## Statements and notation

For the classical third axis equation, let A=2c_l-1>1, g=rho_y(x,0), w=W_1(x,0), h=A+1-w', a_2=(g'-w'')/2 and b_3=-a_2'/3. Put F_3=-a_2g'-b_3g. Let H_3 be the positive homogeneous solution of

    w G_3' +(3h-1)G_3=F_3,

normalized by H_3/x^(6+2/A)->1 at the center. Suppose the endpoint limit ell_3=lim G_3/H_3 at the first positive source x* exists and the origin finite part exists with the stated normalization. Define

    B_3=-FP int_0^x* F_3/(wH_3) dx,
    C_0=FP_(x->0) G_3(x)/H_3(x).

Then C_0=ell_3+B_3. Consequently the implication from a nonzero B_3 to failure of source regularity requires an independent condition C_0=0, such as a smooth center. It cannot be obtained from the source endpoint theorem alone for a weaker profile whose center already has a nonzero obstruction coefficient.

The exact axis data below have B_3=-1, ell_3=0, and a third trace analytic at the source. They are a counterexample to that implication for the axis equations used in its proposed proof. They are not asserted to be a canonical full-plane coupled profile, so they are not a counterexample to an independently proved theorem about all such profiles.

The second conclusion is elementary: even an exact approximation error law e_N=C N^(-p) with C>0 and p>0 allows every positive target accuracy at sufficiently large finite N. Algebraic convergence therefore does not imply the impossibility of reaching a fixed target with a global polynomial basis.

## The endpoint identity

Division by wH_3 and use of the homogeneous equation gives

    (G_3/H_3)'=F_3/(wH_3).

Integration from x to x*, with the assumed endpoint limit, gives

    G_3(x)/H_3(x)=ell_3-int_x^x* F_3/(wH_3).

Taking the finite constant at x=0 proves C_0=ell_3+B_3. For an even smooth center, p_3=6+2/A lies strictly between6and8 and is not an even integer. Smooth even Taylor powers divided by x^p_3 have no constant term, so C_0=0. A source theorem that gives ell_3=0 under additional source regularity then forces B_3=0 for such a smooth-center profile. In the weaker class, C_0 can instead equal B_3 while ell_3=0. This argument imposes no upper bound on source regularity in that case.

## Exact axis example

On 0<x<1 set A=2, c_l=3/2, and

    w=-2x(1-x^2)/(1+x^2), g=x^2/(1+x^2), h=3-w',
    H_3=x^7/[(1+x^2)^3(1-x^2)].

These have the normalized axis data at0, w<0 on the segment, w(1)=0, w'(1)=2 and h(1)=1. Direct differentiation gives the first axis transport equation w g'+(h-1)g=0 and

    F_3/(wH_3)=5(3x^2-11)/(6x^6),
    Q(x)=(11-5x^2)/(6x^5), Q'=F_3/(wH_3).

The primitive Q has zero finite constant at0 and Q(1)=1, so B_3=-1. Select

    G_3=H_3[Q(x)-Q(1)]
       =x^2(6x^4+6x^3+6x^2+11x+11)/[6(x+1)(x^2+1)^3].

The second formula is analytic at x=1, with G_3(1)=5/12. Thus ell_3=0. At0, expansion gives

    G_3=11x^2/6-9x^4/2+9x^6-x^7+O(x^8).

Its even extension contains -|x|^7 and is not smooth at0. The term x^7 in H_3 has leading coefficient1, so C_0=-1, as required. The velocity coefficients a_2,b_3 and further coefficients generated by the div/curl recursions are analytic near x=1. This is an exact consistent third-order axis hierarchy, with smooth source traces at that order and a nonzero center coefficient. No assertion of its full two-dimensional realization is needed or made.

## Approximation rate does not select a family or exclude accuracy

If e_N=C N^(-p), then e_N<=epsilon for N>=(C/epsilon)^(1/p). A rate near p=2.8 can make a target expensive, but cannot exclude it. The class C^(2,alpha) also contains analytic functions; membership alone does not specify an exact approximation rate or a nonzero leading singular coefficient. Identifying a sharp rate requires a nonzero singular term, a norm, a stable approximation operator, and control of every other error. Density approximation error is not automatically transport-residual error, since the residual differentiates the density and depends nonlocally and nonlinearly on it.

Two finite residual refinements do not determine the regularity of a limit or the dimension of a continuum kernel. These limitations persist when the sampled errors concentrate near a source. A frozen local homogeneous solution is an example in its own model. It does not force its homogeneity or its coefficient on every nonlinear solution or on the computed branch.

## Numerical audit protocol and scope

The associated code checks the displayed rational identities and the endpoint and center constants independently with exact symbolic algebra. It also reads the published operator-channel JSON as data and recomputes ratios of its reported scalar norms. It does not execute the numerical researcher's residual or claim an independent evaluation of those actions. In particular, a difference of two norms and the norm of a vector difference are distinct quantities; the triangle inequality gives only the former <= the latter.

Dependencies: the explicitly stated first-order equations, elementary finite parts, rational differentiation and Taylor expansion, and elementary inequalities. The nonlinear source endpoint theorem is used only conditionally for explaining its correct logical implication. No numerical result proves a PDE existence or nonexistence endpoint.
