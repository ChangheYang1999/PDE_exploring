**C1 — CORRECT WITH QUALIFICATION.**

Parts (a)–(c) have the correct signs. The recurrence in (d) is correct within the indicated power series, but that series is not generally the complete expansion to arbitrary order.

Write \(A=\Theta _0\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
\partial_x(r^kA)=r^{k-1}(k\cos\theta\,A-\sin\theta\,A'),
\]
and
\[
-\Delta(r^{k+1}F)
=-r^{k-1}\bigl((k+1)^2F+F''\bigr).
\]
Thus (a), including its denominator, is correct.

Since \(\partial_x\rho\) is odd in both Cartesian variables, the canonical streamfunction has those same parities, up to an irrelevant additive constant. Its angular modes are precisely \(\sin(2n\theta)\), \(n\ge1\). There is no mean mode, cosine mode, or odd-frequency sine mode. Added constant velocities would require linear harmonic streamfunctions; canonical inversion and the stated prohibition exclude them.

The leading velocity is
\[
(U_{r,0},U_{\theta,0})=r^k(-F',(k+1)F),
\]
so
\[
U_0\cdot\nabla(r^kA)
=r^{2k-1}\bigl((k+1)FA'-kF'A\bigr).
\]
For nonzero \(A\), the leading drift term gives \(c_lk=1\). At the next order,
\[
(1-c_l)A\Theta _1+(k+1)FA'-kF'A=0.
\]
This proves (c). “\(\Theta _0\) free” means free at this formal leading-order transport calculation; it does not remove the global normalization or matching conditions.

The divisions by \(A\) are harmless in the appropriate axis limits. If
\[
A(\theta)\sim a_x\theta,\qquad
A(\pi/2-t)\sim a_y t^2,
\]
then
\[
\Theta _{1e}(0)=\frac{F'(0)}{c_l-1},\qquad
\Theta _{1e}(\pi/2)=\frac{(k+2)F'(\pi/2)}{c_l-1}.
\]
Indeed, \(F\sim F'(0)\theta\) at the first axis and \(F\sim-F'(\pi/2)t\) at the second. Consequently, the numerator in (c) vanishes to at least the order of \(A\). The fractional angular factor has exponent \(p_3-2>4\) and does not alter these leading vanishing orders.

For (d), set
\[
\alpha_j=k+j(k-1),\qquad \beta_j=\alpha_j+1,
\]
and write the density coefficients as \(r^{\alpha_j}A_j\), with associated streamfunctions \(r^{\beta_j}F_j\). The precise recurrence is
\[
j(1-c_l)A_j+
\sum_{p+q=j-1}
\left[-\alpha_qF_p'A_q+\beta_pF_pA_q'\right]=0.
\]
The sum uses only earlier coefficients, so determining \(A_j\) is algebraic once those are known.

There are two qualifications:

- Elliptic resonance occurs when \(\beta_j=\pm2n\). For \(j=0,1,2,3\), the exponents are approximately \(1.17936,0.35872,-0.46192,-1.28256\); none resonates. The stated absence of logs in the first three orders is therefore correct.
- A compact change in the core produces exterior streamfunction multipoles starting at \(r^{-2}\sin2\theta\), hence velocity \(O(r^{-3})\). These are generated by the source; they are not forbidden added harmonic polynomials. Their interaction with the leading density produces a transport term of order \(r^{k-4}\), requiring, generically, a density term of that order. Here it lies between the \(j=4\) and \(j=5\) density terms.

**Corrected (d):** the recurrence describes the pure-power sector. A complete expansion must also accommodate core multipoles and their interactions.

Yes, one needs control of the trial’s own velocity remainder. For this trial, the required estimate follows from the differentiated expansion and canonical inversion. For an angular source mode \(f_\ell(r)\sin(\ell\theta)\), \(\ell=2,4,\ldots\),
\[
\psi_\ell(r)=\frac1{2\ell}
\left[
r^{-\ell}\int_0^r s^{\ell+1}f_\ell(s)\,ds+
r^\ell\int_r^\infty s^{1-\ell}f_\ell(s)\,ds
\right].

A density remainder \(O(r^{2k-1})\), with controlled derivatives, gives a source remainder \(O(r^{2k-2})\). This formula yields

in the open first quadrant. Exact decay therefore requires that compatibility.
