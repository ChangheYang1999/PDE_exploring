D13 is analytically correct **when the source condition holds**. More generally,
\[
I(P)=\pi\bigl(h(P)-c_l+g(P)/4\bigr).
\]
Indeed, write the shifted density as \(\sum a_n(r)\sin(n\theta)\). The second sine coefficient of \(\rho_x\) is
\[
f_2=\tfrac12(a_1'-a_1/r+a_3'+3a_3/r).
\]
The canonical Green formula and integration by parts give
\[
\psi_{xy}(P)=\tfrac12\int_0^\infty f_2/r
=-g(P)/4+\int_0^\infty a_3/r^2.
\]
Meanwhile \(I(P)=\pi\int a_3/r^2\). The linear Taylor term cancels on circles; local Hölder regularity and sublinear growth justify the limits. Thus replacing \(h(P)\) by \(1\) requires that condition.

The extended circular quadrature gives
\[
I(P)=8.993773430291,\qquad
\pi(1-c_l+g(P)/4)=8.993784114595,
\]
a difference of \(-1.0684304\,10^{-5}\). Source: `basis3_d13_tail_check.json`, command `P d13_tail_check.py basis3`.

The refined velocity gives \(h(P)=0.999996703588\), substantially different from the coarse export’s printed value, and its velocity-based right side differs from the circular integral by only \(-3.28335\,10^{-7}\). Source: `basis3_d13_velocity_refinement.json`, command `P d13_velocity_refinement.py basis3 --axis-check`. This explains most of the apparent D13 inconsistency as velocity discretization error; the approximate source condition also contributes.
