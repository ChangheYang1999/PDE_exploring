# Origin regularity of a separately interpolated stream function

Verifier result 107. Written 2026-10-21 before the supporting code.

## Statement, hypotheses and scope

Write r=sqrt(x^2+y^2), theta=arg(x+iy). A genuine finite Q density, as defined in result106, is smooth in Cartesian coordinates at the origin. Its true canonical full-plane stream function and transport residual are also smooth there. This statement concerns the exact formula with the exact inverse. It does not extend automatically to a separately interpolated numerical stream function.

Let rho be smooth, even in x and odd in y, with rho=a y+rho_3+O(r^5), where a is nonzero and rho_3 is a homogeneous cubic polynomial. Suppose a numerical stream has the expansion

    psi_tilde = c x y + sum_(m even>=2) v_m r^4 sin(m theta) + remainder,       (1)

where the sum is finite and the remainder has Cartesian derivatives of orders j<=5 bounded by C_j r^(6-j) on the punctured disk. The quadratic term in (1) is assumed to have been made exactly polynomial. If the actual interpolant also has nonpolynomial quadratic terms, the conclusions below apply only after their removal; those lower-order artifacts can give a worse defect.

Let R_tilde=(ell X+grad_perp psi_tilde).grad rho-rho with grad_perp psi=(-psi_y,psi_x). Then its homogeneous cubic terms outside the polynomial frequencies1 and3 are

    r^3 sum_(n odd>=5) s_n sin(n theta),
    s_n = (a/2)[(5-n)v_(n-1)+(n+5)v_(n+1)].              (2)

If any v_m for m>=6 is nonzero, at least one s_n for n>=5 is nonzero. The residual is then not C^3 at the origin. Put

    K = pi sum_(n odd>=5) [(n^2-9)(n^2-1)]^2 s_n^2.       (3)

For fixed sufficiently small r0>0,

    int_(epsilon<r<r0) |D^4 R_tilde|_ordered^2 dX
          = K log(r0/epsilon) + O(1),                    (4)

as epsilon decreases to zero. In particular K>0 and the full-plane fourth derivative norm diverges. These are assertions about the interpolated stream and its residual, not about a density with its true inverse.

A second statement is a concrete error example. There are smooth, nondegenerate, center-normalized densities and perturbations of their stream functions, with the required parities and sublinear velocity gauge, whose pointwise stream, velocity, Poisson-defect and residual errors tend to zero but whose residual difference has infinite fourth-derivative norm for every nonzero perturbation. This example violates the Poisson equation and is not a profile or a candidate for one.

## 1. Why the exact Q density and its true inverse are smooth

The Q map satisfies

    r^2 = L^2 s [2(1-s)]^(-2 kappa),  L>0, kappa>1.

Near s=0 the right side is real analytic with positive derivative L^2 2^(-2 kappa). The analytic inverse function theorem makes s a real analytic function of r^2 there, with s/r^2 nonzero and analytic. Each factor

    s^j cos(2j theta) = (s/r^2)^j Re(x+iy)^(2j)

is therefore real analytic. Multiplication by y(1-s) and a finite radial Chebyshev polynomial preserves that property. Away from the origin the map and formula are smooth. At infinity the formula and all its Cartesian derivatives have symbol bounds O(r^(p-j)), with p=1-1/kappa in (0,1): solve the map for 1-s=r^(-1/kappa) times a smooth function of r^(-1/kappa), and differentiate its finite formula.

For a direct local inverse check, expand the analytic source rho_x in angular sine modes. Its even mode m is a convergent sum of terms f_(m,h) r^(m+2h), h>=0. The regular solution of -Delta psi=rho_x has the particular terms

    - f_(m,h) r^(m+2h+2) sin(m theta)
        / [(m+2h+2)^2-m^2],

and a regular harmonic term H_m r^m sin(m theta). These are all Cartesian polynomials at each order; the series converges on any sufficiently smaller disk. The canonical exterior data determine H_m and remove the growing full-plane harmonic freedom. Equivalently the local difference from this analytic particular solution is harmonic. Thus there is no r^4 sin(m theta) term for m>=6 in the true stream. Its residual is smooth.

The same finite-mode Green formula and the sublinear symbol bounds give D^j U=O(r^(p-j)) at infinity. One can see this without a local elliptic estimate by differentiating the two radial Green integrals: their nondecaying parts have order r^(p+1-j) for psi, and the decaying interior harmonic parts satisfy a stronger bound. Consequently D^k R=O(r^(p-k))+O(r^(2p-1-k)) at infinity. For k>=2 these are square integrable there. The exact finite Q density and its canonical residual therefore have finite full-plane ordered derivative norms at every integer order k>=2. Large values and numerical difficulty are separate matters.

## 2. The nonpolynomial cubic coefficients

Polar differentiation gives the exact identity on r>0

    partial_x [r^d sin(m theta)]
      = (r^(d-1)/2)[(d-m)sin((m+1)theta)+(d+m)sin((m-1)theta)].              (5)

In the cubic part of R_tilde, the terms generated by ell X+c(-x,y), rho_3, and -rho are homogeneous cubic polynomials. The remaining contribution is a partial_x of the quartic stream terms. Taking d=4 in (5) gives (2).

The polynomial quartic terms have m2 andm4. They generate only cubic frequencies1 and3, since the coefficient of frequency5 from m4 is zero. Conversely if some v_m with m>=6 is nonzero, take its largest such index m*. Then s_(m*+1)=a(4-m*)v_m*/2 is nonzero. Thus cancellation among finitely many high modes cannot remove every nonpolynomial cubic coefficient. For example s5=5a v6 and s7=a(-v6+6v8).

A homogeneous cubic function that is C^3 at the origin equals its homogeneous cubic Taylor polynomial. This follows by applying the order3 Peano expansion on each ray and using homogeneity. A cubic polynomial restricted to a circle contains only angular frequencies1 and3 in the stated parities. Formula (2), with a nonzero higher frequency, therefore excludes C^3.

The leading Poisson defect is visible separately. Since

    -Delta[r^4 sin(m theta)] = (m^2-16)r^2 sin(m theta),                    (6)

the higher quartic stream frequencies give nonpolynomial quadratic source errors, although the density source is smooth. Their pointwise size tends to zero quadratically at the origin; small pointwise Poisson error does not repair their regularity.

## 3. The exact logarithmic divergence constant

For F=r^3 sin(n theta), write D_+=partial_x+i partial_y and D_-=partial_x-i partial_y. Acting on r^d exp(i n theta), these derivatives multiply by d-n and d+n and shift the angular frequency by +1 and -1 respectively. Four such derivatives of F have radial order r^-1. For each j=0,...,4 the product coefficient for j operators D_+ and4-j operators D_- at d=3 is, up to sign,

    Q_n = (n^2-9)(n^2-1).

The complex change of orthonormal coordinates in the full ordered derivative tensor contributes the factor2^-4. Integrating the squares over a full circle removes distinct angular frequencies. The binomial sum of the16 tensor components cancels this factor. Hence, exactly,

    int_0^(2pi) |D^4 [r^3 sum_n s_n sin(n theta)]|_ordered^2 dtheta
       = pi r^-2 sum_n Q_n^2 s_n^2.                     (7)

This is also the k4 homogeneous specialization of result102, which proves the positive full tensor formula without an integration by parts. Terms n1 andn3 have Q_n=0, as required for cubic polynomials.

The remainder of the residual has fourth derivatives O(r), because rho is smooth, the stream remainder in (1) has five controlled derivatives, and all omitted products have degree at least5. Its square is integrable near zero; its product with the r^-1 leading fourth derivative is also integrable. Integrating (7) with area element r dr dtheta proves (4). The O(1) term need not have a particular sign.

## 4. A small pointwise error with an infinite derivative norm

Fix ell>1, a=4(ell-1), and rho=a y exp(-r^2). Set

    J(r)=r^-4 int_0^r s^3 exp(-s^2) ds,
    psi=-a x y J(r).

The smooth extension J(0)=1/4 gives psi_xy(0)=1-ell. Direct differentiation proves -Delta psi=rho_x. The stream is canonical: its velocity is divergence free, has the stated parities, and decays at infinity. These facts do not make this density solve the transport equation.

Choose a smooth radial cutoff chi(r^2), equal to1 near zero and0 outside a fixed disk, and perturb the stream by

    delta_psi = tau chi(r^2) r^4 sin(6 theta),  tau != 0.                    (8)

It has the stream parities, is C^3 at the origin, and changes neither psi_xy(0) nor the behavior at infinity. Its velocity perturbation is C^2. The sup norms of delta_psi, its first derivatives, its Poisson defect and its resulting transport-residual difference are all O(|tau|); the derivatives of the cutoff contribute only on a fixed annulus. On the inner disk the residual difference is

    delta_R = a tau r^3[5 sin(5 theta)-sin(7 theta)] + O(r^5),              (9)

with the differentiated remainder bounds used above. Equation (3) gives the strictly positive logarithmic coefficient

    K = 7372800 pi a^2 tau^2.                             (10)

Thus D^4 delta_R is not square integrable for any tau != 0, despite arbitrarily small pointwise errors and an exact center strain. The construction has an explicit nonzero Poisson defect20 tau r^2 sin(6 theta) near zero; it must never be called a canonical inverse of rho.

## 5. Relation to a saved interpolant

If a separately interpolated stream mode is written as r^2 w(s) P_m(2s-1), where w(0)=1 and P_m(-1)=0 for a high mode, its quartic coefficient is

    v_m = [2^(2 kappa+1)/L^2] P_m'(-1).                  (11)

This follows from s=2^(2 kappa)r^2/L^2+O(r^4). Cartesian smoothness for mode m requires the first m/2-1 Taylor coefficients of w(s)P_m(2s-1) to vanish, equivalently P_m^j(-1)=0 for0<=j<m/2-1 when w is nonzero at0. A center value row alone does not impose these higher constraints.

Reported values of P_m'(-1) from the producer are not independently confirmed by this proof. To confirm them one must obtain the actual interpolated stream coefficients and their nodes, preserve their source hashes, and differentiate them independently. The original density coefficient data alone determine the true canonical inverse, which has no such defect, and do not specify the producer's separate interpolation error.

## Dependencies and verification

The map and exact Q formula are in result106; the local canonical inverse is derived again above. The norm identity is derived in Section3 and cross-referenced to result102. Supporting code must independently differentiate rational Cartesian representatives of r^4 sin(m theta) and r^3 sin(n theta), integrate their angular polynomial squares exactly, and check (2), (6), (7), (10) and the map factor. Reported stream-coefficient values remain OPEN until data are supplied and evaluated independently. This is neither a YES nor a principal NO endpoint.
