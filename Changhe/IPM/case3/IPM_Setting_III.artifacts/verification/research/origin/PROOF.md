# Origin compatibility and the inner boundary sign

Status: independently derived and symbolically checked. These are necessary conditions, not an existence or nonexistence theorem.

## Statement and hypotheses

Assume rho is smooth, even in x and odd in y, with |rho|+(1+r)|grad rho|<=C(1+r)^gamma for some 0<gamma<1, and use the canonical inverse from ../inverse/PROOF.md, together with the exact profile equation

    (c_l X+U).grad rho=rho,  c_l>1,  a0=rho_y(0)!=0.

Write A=2c_l-1 and c=psi_xy(0). The following identities hold:

    c=1-c_l,  DW(0)=diag(A,1),  W=c_l X+U;
    c=-a0/4 + integral_0^infinity b3(r)r^-2 dr.

Here f4 is the sin(4 theta) coefficient of rho_x, with -Delta psi=rho_x and U=(-psi_y,psi_x). The coefficient bk(r) is the sin(k theta) Fourier coefficient of rho, normalized by bk=pi^-1 integral_0^(2pi) rho(r,theta)sin(k theta)dtheta. If

    rho=a0 y+b x^2y+e y^3+O(r^5),
    psi=cxy+q1 x^3y+q2 xy^3+O(r^6),

then

    q1=-2Ab/(3a0), q2=-2e/a0,
    e=(a0-2A)b/6,
    q1-q2=integral_0^infinity f4(r)r^-3 dr=(a0-4A)b/(3a0).

For phi=r^-3 sin(3 theta), with angular integration performed first at the origin,

    (1-c_l)pi integral_0^infinity b3(r)r^-2 dr
      + integral_R2 rho U.grad phi dX = pi a0(1-c_l)/2.

The last RHS has the opposite sign to SPEC.md v0, Section 7 (I5).

## Proof of the center and strain identities

Parity gives U(0)=0, grad rho(0)=(0,a0), and DU(0)=diag(-c,c). Differentiate W.grad rho=rho in y at zero. Since W(0)=0, the equation is (c_l+c)a0=a0. Division by the nonzero a0 gives c=1-c_l and the source eigenvalues A and 1.

In the derivative-free angular inverse, the m=2 mode is

    h2(r) = [r^2 integral_r^infinity s^-2 b3(s)ds
             -r^-2 integral_0^r s^2 b1(s)ds]/2.

Smoothness gives b1(r)=a0 r+O(r^3), b3(r)=O(r^3). Consequently h2(r)/r^2 tends to integral_0^infinity b3(s)s^-2 ds/2-a0/8. Since xy=r^2 sin(2 theta)/2, this limit is c/2, which proves the formula. The b3 integral is absolutely convergent: its integrand is O(r) at zero and O(r^(gamma-2)) at infinity.

This does not justify writing an absolutely convergent two-dimensional integral of rho sin(3 theta)/r^3 near zero. Its leading absolute radial majorant is dr/r. Angular cancellation and the local -a0/4 term are essential.

## Cubic compatibility

Poisson gives q1+q2=-b/3. At degree one, W=(Ax,y)+O(r^3). The degree-three terms of W.grad rho-rho give

    2Ab+3a0 q1=0,   2e+a0 q2=0.

Combining these equations gives the displayed q1, q2 and e. For the nonlocal relation, the sin(4 theta) coefficient of the quartic part of psi is (q1-q2)r^4/8. In the Green formula its coefficient is integral_0^infinity f4(s)s^-3 ds/8. The source f4 is O(r^4) at zero because the cubic density produces only a sin(2 theta) source. At infinity f4=O(r^(gamma-1)). Both endpoints are integrable. Equating coefficients proves the last compatibility condition.

## The weighted identity

On an annulus epsilon<r<R, the exact transport equation, div W=2c_l, and X.grad phi=-3phi imply

    div(rho phi W)=(1-c_l)rho phi+rho U.grad phi.

The outer flux tends to zero. Its c_l X term is O(R^(gamma-1)), and its U term is O(R^(2gamma-2)). The integral of rho phi is pi integral b3(r)r^-2 dr. The U integral converges at infinity since its absolute radial majorant is O(r^(2gamma-3))dr.

At zero, U=(-cx,cy)+O(r^3) and rho=a0 r sin(theta)+O(r^3). One computes

    U.grad phi=3c r^-3 sin(5 theta)+O(r^-1).

The leading angular integral of rho U.grad phi vanishes because sin(theta) and sin(5 theta) are orthogonal. The remaining angular integral is locally integrable. This is an angularly regularized improper integral; its unregularized absolute integral need not converge.

The small-circle flux with radial normal +e_r has limit

    a0 integral_0^(2pi) sin(theta)sin(3 theta)(c_l-c cos(2 theta))dtheta
    =-pi a0 c/2.

The outward normal of the annulus on its inner boundary is -e_r. The divergence theorem therefore supplies +pi a0 c/2. Substituting c=1-c_l proves the stated sign.
