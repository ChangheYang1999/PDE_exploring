3.74 The hydrostatic problem: isopycnal form, leading far field, no regular expansion at c = 1, and formal far-field terms ((a)-(c) and the compactness statement of (e) proved, (d) and the other statements of (e) formal; derived in an external analysis; hand-checked in the main analysis; AUDITPLACEHOLDER). Let c, p, D, g, G, v and K be as in 3.73, A = 2c - 1, and h = V_1, a = c xi + h and b = c y + V_2 as in 3.73(c), so that h_y = g_xi, b_y = c - h_xi and a g_xi + b g_y = g; let k(xi) = g_y(xi, 0), let r, th be polar coordinates of X = (xi, y), and write V[u] = (V_1[u], V_2[u]) for the formulas (2) of 3.72 applied to a function u. (a) Boundary law and isopycnal form. If k(xi) > 0 for all xi, then, with a_0(xi) = a(xi, 0),
@@    a_0 k' = (a_0' - A) k,   a_0(xi) = A k(xi) int_0^xi dt/k(t),   k(xi) = L |xi|^{p - 1} (1 + O(|xi|^{gamma - 1}))  (|xi| -> inf)   (1)
with a constant 0 < L < inf. If g_y > 0 on {y >= 0}, then for each xi the map y -> g(xi, y) is an increasing bijection of [0, inf) onto itself; with y = Y(xi, sigma) its inverse, Z = Y_sigma and H(xi, sigma) = h(xi, Y(xi, sigma)),
@@    d_xi((c xi + H) Z) + sigma Z_sigma = A Z,   H_sigma = -Y_xi,   H(xi, sigma) = int_sigma^inf Y_xi(xi, s) ds,   (2)
with Y(xi, 0) = 0, Z(xi, 0) = 1/k(xi), Z(0, 0) = 1 and H_sigma(xi, 0) = Z_sigma(xi, 0) = 0, and at fixed xi, as sigma -> inf, Y ~ (sigma/K)^c, Z ~ c K^{-c} sigma^{c - 1} and H -> 0. Moreover, for sigma_1 > 0,
@@    int_0^inf Y_xixi(0, sigma) dsigma = D,   d_xi int_0^{sigma_1} (c xi + H) Z dsigma + sigma_1 Z(xi, sigma_1) = 2c Y(xi, sigma_1),   (3)
and, without a sign condition on g_y, M(xi) = int_0^inf (G(y) - g(xi, y)) dy converges and is C^2, with M' = h(xi, 0) and M''(0) = D. These identities give no sign of Z_xi and no maximum principle (by (2), a Z_xi + sigma Z_sigma = (D - H_xi) Z, and the coefficient H_xi has no known sign); they exclude no solution with g_y > 0 and constrain c only through 3.73(a). (b) Leading far field. There is a continuous function Theta of the angle, with Theta(pi - th) = Theta(th) = -Theta(-th), Theta(pi/2) = K and |Theta(th)| <= C'' |sin th|, such that
@@    g = r^p Theta(th) + o(r^p)   as r -> inf, uniformly in th;   (4)
in particular |g| <= C' (1 + r)^p. If g_y > 0 on {y >= 0}, then Theta > 0 on (0, pi) and Theta(th) = L th + o(th) as th -> 0+, with L of (1). The proof gives no convergence of derivatives of r^{-p} g and no asymptotics of V; on a fixed annulus the second xi-derivatives of R^{-p} g(R X) are only O(R^{gamma - p}). Since p < 1, the bound |Theta(th)| <= C'' |sin th| excludes the trace K |sin th|^p as a global trace; near th = pi/2 such a trace is not excluded by (4). (c) No regular expansion at c = 1. If solutions g of (3) of 3.72 with c = 1 + D had the form g = y + D u + O(D^2), with V[g] = D V[u] + O(D^2), then u would solve (5) below. Theorem: there is no C^2 function u on R^2, even in xi and odd in y, with |u(X)| <= C (1 + r)^q for some 1 < q < 2, such that int_y^inf u_xixi(xi, t) dt converges for all xi and all y >= 0 and
@@    (X . grad - 1) u + V_2[u] = -y   on {y >= 0}.   (5)
In particular no such first-order coefficient of size O(r log r) exists. This is not a nonexistence statement for small D > 0: it excludes only an expansion of this regular form. By 3.73(a), a family of solutions with c -> 1+ also needs gamma >= 1/c, so gamma -> 1. For comparison, the functions K_D y^{1/(1 + D)} on y > 0, with K_D = 1 + kappa_1 D + O(D^2), do not depend on xi, have zero velocity and solve the transport equation of (3) of 3.72 on y > 0; their first-order coefficient y (kappa_1 - log y) is not C^1 at y = 0. (d) Formal far-field terms (not proved: they assume an expansion g = f_p + f_{2p-1} + ..., f_p = r^p Theta(th) with Theta smooth, that may be differentiated and inserted in the formulas (2) of 3.72 term by term). The terms of degree 2p - 1 in (3) of 3.72 give
@@    f_{2p-1} = (V[f_p] . grad f_p)/D,   f_{2p-1}(0, y) = K Q y^{2p - 1}/((1 - p) D),   Q = p K + Theta''(pi/2),   (6)
since (f_p)_xixi(0, y) = Q y^{p - 2} and V_2[f_p](0, y) = Q y^p/(p (1 - p)). The degrees d_n = n p - (n - 1) generated by the nonlinearity have c d_n - 1 = -(n - 1) D != 0 for n >= 2, so no transport resonance occurs among them; a logarithm can enter the velocity where d_n = 0, that is at c = n/(n - 1). If int_0^inf t |g_xixi(0, t) - Q t^{p - 2}| dt < inf for some constant Q, then exactly V_2(0, y) = Q y^p/(p (1 - p)) + m_0 + o(1) with m_0 = int_0^inf t (g_xixi(0, t) - Q t^{p - 2}) dt, and formally m_0 contributes K m_0 c^{-2} y^{p - 1} to G. Near the vertical axis, with F(s) = f_p(1, s), q(s) = p (p - 1) F(s) - 2 (p - 1) s F'(s) + s^2 F''(s) and the finite number C_p(Theta) = int_0^inf s (q(s) - Q s^{p - 2}) ds, V_2[f_p](xi, y) = Q y^p/(p (1 - p)) + C_p(Theta) |xi|^p + O(xi^2 y^{p - 2}) as xi/y -> 0, so f_{2p-1} contains p K C_p(Theta) |xi|^p y^{p - 1}/D, which is not differentiable in xi at xi = 0; an expansion of this kind with coefficients that are C^1 in xi near the vertical axis therefore requires C_p(Theta) = 0. (e) Selection of c and compactness. At fixed c the dilations g -> l g(X/l) map solutions to solutions (3.73(d)), so the function g - X . grad g, which is not zero by 3.73(d)(iii), lies in the kernel of the formal linearization L_g u = (c X + V[g]) . grad u - u + V[u] . grad g; a variation c_1 of c enters as L_g u + c_1 X . grad g = 0 and, at fixed Theta, changes the leading term of (4) by -c_1 p^2 r^p Theta(th) log r, so it is not contained in spaces with the fixed weight r^p. The results of 3.73 and (a)-(d) give no count of solutions and no rule selecting c: the free axis data of 3.73(b) and the trace Theta are functions, not finitely many parameters, and the frozen estimate of 3.73(c) concerns a Cauchy problem, not a boundary problem. Compactness (proved): let c_n -> c > 1 and let g_n be C^3 functions, even in xi and odd in y, with d_y g_n(0) = 1, the three bounds of 3.72(d) with one C and one gamma, |D^3 g_n| <= C_R on each disc of radius R, and (c_n xi + V_1[g_n]) d_xi g_n + (c_n y + V_2[g_n]) d_y g_n - g_n -> 0 uniformly on compact subsets of {y >= 0}; then a subsequence converges in C^2 on compact sets to a solution of (3) of 3.72 with c_lim = c in the class of 3.73. Scope: (a)-(c) and the compactness statement are proved for solutions of (3) of 3.72 in the class of 3.73 (and (c) for the stated functions u); they establish neither existence nor nonexistence for (3), select no c and give no uniform estimates for the exact profiles of 3.72(e); (d) and the statements on selection in (e) are formal.
