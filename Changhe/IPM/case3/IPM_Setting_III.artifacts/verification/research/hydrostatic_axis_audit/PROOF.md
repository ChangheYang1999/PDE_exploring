# Hydrostatic axis, formal continuation and excluded forms

Statement and hypotheses. Assume c>1, 0<gamma<1 and an even-in-x, odd-in-y density g in C^2(R^2), with g_y(0)=1 and the three common power bounds |g|<=C(1+r)^gamma, |grad g|<=C(1+r)^(gamma-1), |g_xx|<=C(1+r)^(gamma-2). Use the canonical hydrostatic velocity
 h=V1=-int_y^infinity g_x(x,t)dt,
 V2=int_0^y t g_xx(x,t)dt+y int_y^infinity g_xx(x,t)dt
on y>=0 and parity extension. Suppose (c x+h)g_x+(c y+V2)g_y=g. These are hypotheses about the limiting problem, not about a proved limit of original IPM profiles. The inverse and its regularity are proved in result175. For statements involving arbitrarily high formal derivatives we explicitly assume smooth axis data. This qualification is not implied by C^2 alone.

1. Axis identities and positivity. Put G(y)=g(0,y), v(y)=c y+V2(0,y), D=c-1. Oddness of g_x in x makes h(0,y)=0. Transport gives vG'=G. Since the g_xx bound is integrable in y, differentiation of the inverse gives v'=c+int_y^infinity g_xx(0,t)dt and v''=-g_xx(0,y). Also v(0)=0 and V2=O(y^gamma), so v/y tends to c. Divide vG'=G by y at zero: G/y and G' tend to1, hence v'(0)=1. All three functions v,G,G' are positive near zero. If v had a first positive zero y1, the ODE G'=G/v would make G positive and increasing up to y1, bounded below by a positive value, contradicting the continuous identity v(y1)G'(y1)=G(y1). Thus v,G,G'>0 on the positive axis.

An integration by parts, justified by v in C^2 on [0,R], gives
 int_0^R (1-t/R)g_xx(0,t)dt = v'(0)-[v(R)-v(0)]/R=1-v(R)/R.
It tends to1-c<0. In particular g_xx cannot be nonnegative on the whole positive axis. The center integral is v'(0)-c=1-c. Since v=c y+O(y^gamma) already follows from the assumed three bounds, 1/v-1/(c y)=O(y^(gamma-2)) is integrable at infinity. Integrating G'/G=1/v gives G~K y^(1/c) with K strictly positive and finite. Merely assuming v/y tends to c would give only G=y^(1/c+o(1)), but the stronger tail is automatic in the present hypotheses. If g is C^3 at zero with g=y+B x^2y+E y^3+o(r^3), then v''=-2By+o(y) and v=G/G'=y-2Ey^3+o(y^3), whence B=6E.

2. Compatible axis data. For any c>1 set v=c y-D arctan y, and G=y exp(int_0^y [1/v(t)-1/t]dt). Then v'=c-D/(1+y^2)>=1, v-y=D(y-arctan y)>0 and v''=2Dy/(1+y^2)^2>0 for y>0. The integrand is odd and has a removable real-analytic singularity at zero. Therefore G is odd and real analytic on the real line, G'(0)=1, vG'=G, G'>0, G<=y, and G~K y^(1/c). The local expansion v=y+D y^3/3+O(y^5) yields G=y-D y^3/6+O(y^5).

Fix 0<y1<y2 and let m be the positive minimum of v-y and v'' on [y1,y2]. For any smooth odd phi supported in the reflected two intervals and with max_{j<=2}sup|phi^(j)|<m, v_phi=v+phi stays above y and has v_phi''>0 on the positive axis. Outside the support it agrees with v. The associated G_phi has the same normalization, sign properties, cubic coefficient and power exponent, though its positive tail constant K may change. There are infinitely many independent smooth compactly supported perturbations. The perturbed pair is generally C-infinity, not real analytic. Indeed a nonzero compactly supported real-analytic function on the connected real line cannot exist: it vanishes on an open exterior interval, so its Taylor series and analytic continuation force it to vanish everywhere. Thus 'preserves all these properties' must exclude real analyticity. These are axis data, not constructed global densities. Even their exact finite-radius curvature identity defines only the required value g_xx(0,y)=-v_phi''(y).

3. Formal recurrence with smooth data. Put a=c x+h, b=c y+V2, and suppose formal series with smooth y-dependent coefficients are being sought:
 g=G+sum_{k>=1}x^(2k)g_{2k}, h=sum_{k>=0}x^(2k+1)h_{2k+1}, b=v+sum_{k>=1}x^(2k)b_{2k}.
The exact identities are h_y=g_x, b_y=c-h_x, ag_x+bg_y=g. Their first orders give h1=c-v', g2=-v''/2, and
 b2=[(4c-1-2v')v''+v v''']/(2G'), h3=-b2'/3, g4=-b2''/12.
For the general order x^(2k), k>=1, the only new b coefficient in transport is G'b_{2k}; every other coefficient is determined already. Since G'>0, solve for b_{2k}, then h_{2k+1}=-b_{2k}'/(2k+1), g_{2k+2}=h_{2k+1}'/(2k+2). This proves formal uniqueness at fixed smooth G. It proves neither convergence of the series nor existence of a smooth or analytic germ. In particular the displayed b2 needs v''' and higher orders need further derivatives; the original C^2 density hypothesis by itself does not supply an arbitrary-order formal expansion. Global vertical inverse identities and growth constraints still need to hold at every order.

4. Frozen symbol and its precise scope. Where q=g_y>0, eliminate b=(g-a h_y)/q. Then g_x=h_y and h_x=c-d_y[(g-a h_y)/q]. Linearization at fixed c in directions (u,z) is
 u_x=z_y,
 z_x=-d_y[(u-h_y z-a z_y-b u_y)/q].
At every axis point (0,y0), y0>0, a=h_y=a_y=h_yy=0 by parity, q=G'>0 and b=v>0. Expanding the y-derivative before freezing gives
 z_x=(v/q)u_yy+((v/q)'-1/q)u_y+(q'/q^2)u.
There is no z,z_y or z_yy term there. For constant coefficients and Fourier frequency eta, lambda^2=-i(v/q)eta^3-[(v/q)'-1/q]eta^2+i(q'/q^2)eta. Thus one square root has real part sqrt(v/(2q))|eta|^(3/2)+o(|eta|^(3/2)), the other its negative. Even the axis-parity condition z(0)=0 gives u(x)=u(0)cosh(lambda x). For any x>0 its amplification exceeds every fixed power of |eta|. Frequency-localized smooth packets give failure of a continuous solution map between any fixed pair of Sobolev spaces for this frozen problem.

This does not prove nonlinear or variable-coefficient ill-posedness. It does not prove that a vanishes only on the axis: no global sign theorem for a has been established. Nor does failure of a general frozen Sobolev Cauchy estimate rule out specially chosen analytic or faster-decaying data or a construction imposing global compatibility. The justified conclusion is that the proposed simple forward marching argument has no supplied well-posedness justification, and the global constraints remain unsolved. A statement that all solutions must be computed in one particular way is not a mathematical consequence.

5. Elliptical inputs. Suppose g=yF(alpha x^2+beta y^2), alpha,beta>0. The C^2 density determines a C^2 F on (0,infinity), with limit F(0+)=1; redefine the irrelevant value F(0) to1 if necessary. The axis growth gives F(s) tending to0. Since g_x=(alpha/beta)x d_y F(alpha x^2+beta y^2), the convergent canonical integral gives h=(alpha/beta)xF(s). The center relation h_x(0)=c-1 forces alpha/beta=D. Differentiating transport once in y on y=0 gives exactly 2c sF'=D F(F-1), s=alpha x^2>0. Near zero put Z=(F-1)/F. The ODE is s Z'=nu Z, nu=D/(2c) in(0,1/2), so Z=k s^nu. If k were nonzero then g_y(x,0)=1/[1-k alpha^nu |x|^(2nu)] would not be C^1 at x=0, contradicting g in C^2. Hence F=1 near zero, and ordinary uniqueness at every positive s forces F=1 everywhere, contradicting its decay. No assumed differentiability of F at s=0 is needed.

6. Products and discrete scaling. If g=f(x)G0(y), normalization ensures f(0)G0'(0)=1. The axis G=f(0)G0 is unbounded by paragraph1. For fixed x, the gradient bound applied to f'(x)G0(y) as y tends to infinity forces f'(x)=0. Then for fixed y, the same bound on f(0)G0'(y) as x tends to infinity forces G0'(y)=0, contradiction. The differentiability of the factors can be recovered from g using slices with nonzero factors, if it was not given initially.

If g(X)=l g(X/l) for a positive l different from1, iterating both ways gives g(X)=l^(-n)g(l^n X) for integers n. Along n with l^n tending to0, differentiability at zero yields g(X)=grad g(0).X=y, contradicting sublinear growth. For positive lambda,mu the general hydrostatic scaling is g_new(x,y)=(lambda^2/mu)g(x/lambda,y/mu), with Phi_new=lambda mu Phi(x/lambda,y/mu), V_new=(lambda V1,mu V2) at those arguments. Both equations are preserved; the new slope is(lambda/mu)^2, so normalization forces lambda=mu. Thus the discrete exclusion includes invariance under any nontrivial normalized positive scaling.

Verdict. CONFIRMED, with the listed regularity and scope qualifications: the axis identities, explicit axis family and perturbation constant, formal recurrence, exact frozen symbol, and all three ansatz exclusions. REFUTED if read literally: preservation of real analyticity by every smooth supported perturbation. OPEN/unproved: arbitrary-order recurrence for only C^2 data, the assertion that a vanishes only on the axis, any nonlinear ill-posedness or universal construction-method conclusion. The stronger axis tail is already a consequence of the stated hypotheses. Nothing establishes an original IPM endpoint.
