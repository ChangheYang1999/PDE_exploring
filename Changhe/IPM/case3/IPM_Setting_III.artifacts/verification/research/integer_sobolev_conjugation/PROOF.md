# Weighted integer Sobolev conjugation and the transport graph core

Status: independently derived; this is a domain lemma, not a completed IPM Fredholm estimate. Date: 2026-10-20.

## Hypotheses and conclusion

Let W=ell X+U be a smooth parity-compatible field on R^2, ell>1. Suppose U and a smooth parity-compatible density rho satisfy the stronger symbol bounds

    |partial^alpha U(X)|+|partial^alpha rho(X)|
           <=C_alpha (1+|X|)^(p-|alpha|), 0<p<1,

outside a fixed ball, for every multi-index alpha. These bounds are additional hypotheses; the primary prompt's density and first-gradient bound does not supply them.

Let V[h] be the canonical full-plane IPM velocity generated by an even-x/odd-y density h. Define

    N h=V[h].grad rho, P h=W.grad h-h+N h.

Fix 0<nu<1 and an even integer s=2m>=4. Put on the plane a smooth radial metric g=b(r)^2 (dx^2+dy^2), with b=1 for r<=2 and b=1/r for r>=4. It is Euclidean near zero and a flat half-cylinder dt^2+dtheta^2 at infinity, t=log r. Let dmu_g be its volume measure. Choose a positive smooth even-even weight w which is r^(-nu) times a bounded smooth factor, bounded away from zero after dividing by r^(-nu), at infinity. Every covariant derivative of log w is required bounded. Let

    A_m=(1-Delta_g)^m, J h=A_m(w h),
    E={h: J h in L2(dmu_g)}, ||h||_E=||J h||_2.

All spaces are restricted to the prescribed density parity. The Laplacian is its self-adjoint nonpositive realization; equivalently A_m is the positive integer Sobolev operator on this complete metric.

Then:

1. E has the same elements and an equivalent norm to the dyadic H^s space with annular weights 2^(-nu*j) used in the lead's conditional formulation. In particular N:E->E is bounded.
2. The maximal distributional realization of P has domain {h in E: P h in E}. Under the unitary map J:E->L2, it becomes

       B=T+C, T=W.grad+(div_g W)/2, C bounded on L2,
       D(B)=D(T), B*= -T+C*, D(B*)=D(T).         (1)

3. C_c^infinity functions of the prescribed parity are a graph core for P in E. Thus a compact-error energy estimate proved on the original smooth compactly supported density perturbations extends to the maximal graph domain and supplies the corresponding adjoint estimate through (1).

Specifically, if a compact K:E->H0 and epsilon>0 satisfy

    Re <P h,h>_E >= epsilon ||h||_E^2-C0||K h||_H0^2            (2)

on those tests, then P is Fredholm. The proof of (2) for the actual IPM coefficients is not supplied here. There is no claim that this holds under the primary admissible class, or at all Sobolev orders. Source thresholds, an escape weight and end estimates belong to that further step.

## Sobolev realization on the cylindrical metric

The stated metric is complete, has positive injectivity radius and bounded curvature derivatives: its end is a fixed flat cylinder and the remaining region is compact and smooth. Uniform coordinate patches, a locally finite partition of unity with bounded derivatives, and the Euclidean Fourier estimate give

    ||v||_(H^(2m)(g)) equivalent to ||(1-Delta_g)^m v||_2.

For completeness, the upper estimate follows by expanding the differential operator in those charts. For the reverse estimate, choose patches small enough that the highest coefficients differ from their value at the patch center by a uniformly small amount. The constant-coefficient elliptic Fourier estimate controls all derivatives of order 2m there. Absorb the coefficient error; partition commutators have order at most 2m-1 and are absorbed by interpolation with the L2 term. Summing patches has bounded overlap. The spectral inequality 1-Delta_g>=1 absorbs that L2 term. The same estimate follows for the operator domain by completion; weak local elliptic regularity identifies it with the distributional H^(2m) domain. Functional calculus gives A_m^-1:L2->H^(2m), bounded and onto. Smooth right-hand sides give smooth solutions by applying the local estimates at every order.

On a dyadic annulus, the cylinder chart is equivalent with uniform constants to physical rescaling X=2^j Z on a fixed annulus. Its measure is equivalent to dZ. Derivatives of w/r^(-nu) and their inverses are bounded in these charts. The product rule in integer H^s therefore proves the claimed weighted dyadic norm equivalence, including a fixed Euclidean core norm.

The symbol hypotheses imply W and all its covariant derivatives are bounded in this metric. Its flow is complete because |W(X)|<=C(1+|X|). The function div_g W and all its derivatives are bounded. The latter follows directly from

    div_g W=div_euclidean W+2 W.grad log b;

no constant-divergence hypothesis is needed for the domain lemma.

## Boundedness of the nonlocal term

Use a smooth radial dyadic partition on the input h and the output. Each input piece remains centrally odd. The velocity kernel and contact term are those in ../inverse/PROOF.md. On neighboring annuli, rescale to a fixed annulus. The order-zero Fourier multipliers are bounded on every integer H^s by Plancherel; derivatives of the cutoffs produce bounded lower-order terms. This yields a uniform near-diagonal H^s bound.

For an input annulus indexed by k and output annulus j with k>=j+3, subtract K(-Y) before integration. It integrates to zero against the odd input piece. The kernel difference and its scaled output derivatives have size at most C*2^j*2^(-3k). The input volume and weighted amplitude then give the operator bound

    C*2^(-(1-nu)(k-j)).                        (3)

For j>=k+3, the input's integral is zero. Subtract K(X) instead; its first-order remainder and scaled output derivatives are bounded by C*2^(-3j)*2^k. The resulting bound is

    C*2^(-(3+nu)(j-k)).                        (4)

Input derivatives are not needed in these separated estimates; the L2 input controls its L1 norm after rescaling. The same reasoning applies when one annulus is the fixed core; near the origin the canceled kernel is smooth, and its derivatives obey the fixed-coordinate version of the estimates. Both geometric series sum because 0<nu<1. The elementary l2 convolution estimate on the dyadic block norms proves V:E->E for the vector component parity spaces.

All scaled derivatives of grad rho are bounded; on annulus j they are O(2^((p-1)j)). Multiplication by grad rho is consequently bounded in weighted integer H^s, including the compact core. This proves N:E->E. The canonical integral itself exists for h in E: Sobolev embedding for s>=4 gives |h|+r|grad h|=O(r^nu) on annuli, and the same cancellation gives the infinite tail. Distributional definitions agree with limits from smooth input.

## Exact bounded conjugation

Write D=W.grad and b0=-1-D log w. Weight conjugation gives

    w P w^-1=D+b0+N_w, N_w=w N w^-1.

Here b0 and all needed derivatives are bounded. Therefore

    B=A_m w P w^-1 A_m^-1
      =D+[A_m,D]A_m^-1+A_m b0 A_m^-1+A_m N_w A_m^-1.

The commutator [A_m,D] is a differential operator of order at most 2m with bounded coefficients. Its order-(2m+1) terms cancel. The preceding elliptic estimate shows [A_m,D]A_m^-1 is bounded on L2. Multiplication by b0 is bounded on H^(2m), so its conjugate is bounded. The N conjugate is bounded by the preceding section and the E norm definition. Separating D=T-(div_g W)/2 gives exactly B=T+C with bounded C.

The identities initially hold for smooth inputs and then distributionally. Hence P h belongs to E exactly when T(J h) belongs to L2. Completeness of W and the unitary half-density transport group imply T is skew-adjoint on its maximal distributional domain; a full proof, including its compactly supported smooth core, is in ../transport_graph_domains/PROOF.md. Bounded perturbation gives (1). In particular both B and B* have the same transport domain. An adjoint energy estimate cannot be dismissed by an unverified change of domain here.

## The original density tests form a graph core

The preceding transport result first gives a compactly supported smooth core in the variable v=J h. For such a v, h=w^-1 A_m^-1 v is smooth on the entire plane, though usually not compactly supported, and belongs to D(P).

Choose radial cutoffs chi_R which equal one on increasing compact sets, vanish outside a larger compact set, and have uniformly bounded covariant derivatives of every order. Their transition regions escape to infinity. For every f in E,

    chi_R f -> f in E.

This follows from the integer product rule and the Sobolev tail norm. Also W chi_R and all needed derivatives are uniformly bounded and supported in the escaping transition, so (W chi_R)h ->0 in E. The exact commutator identity is

    P(chi_R h)-P h=(chi_R-1)P h+(W chi_R)h
                              +N(chi_R h)-chi_R N h.

Its first two terms tend to zero as just shown. The last two do too, because N is bounded and the cutoffs converge strongly on E. Thus chi_R h ->h in the P graph norm. These are compactly supported smooth density functions of the required parity. Apply this construction to a transport-core approximation v_n and take a diagonal sequence. This proves statement3.

## Conditional Fredholm consequence and the remaining gap

By the core result, (2) extends to D(P). Transfer it by J to B and the compact operator KJ^-1. Since D(B*)=D(B)=D(T) and T is skew-adjoint, the same real-part inequality holds for B*. The compact-error Fredholm lemma proved in ../transport_graph_domains/PROOF.md gives finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel and closed range, proving the stated conditional consequence.

This settles only the bounded-conjugation and graph-domain step at even integer Sobolev order under the explicit symbol bounds. It does not prove the global inequality (2), compute an index, establish those symbol bounds from the original equation, or provide a nonlinear inverse. Those remain separate audit items. The proof uses no numerical data and no formal proof-assistant build.
