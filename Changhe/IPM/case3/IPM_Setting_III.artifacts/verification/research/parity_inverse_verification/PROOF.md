# A parity-based check of the canonical inverse near an axis

Date: 2026-10-20. Numerical verification method. No profile or uniform error bound is asserted.

## Setting and problem

Let rho be a smooth full-plane density, even in x and odd in y, with sublinear density and first-derivative growth as in PROMPT.md. Its canonical IPM velocity U is smooth locally, is generated by -Delta psi=rho_x and U=(-psi_y,psi_x), and satisfies U=o(r). It has U1 odd in x and even in y, and U2 even in x and odd in y. The canonical inverse and its distributional contact term are proved in the earlier inverse results.

At a fixed positive x, direct numerical integration of U2(x,y) near y=0 can lose relative accuracy: U2 is odd and of order y, whereas individual quadrature terms need not be that small. A tiny y does not make the actual transport quotient undefined when its denominator is positive. This note justifies a distinct numerical evaluation using direct canonical inverse samples at larger, still nearby ordinates.

Define, for z>=0,

    g_x(z)=U2(x,sqrt(z))/sqrt(z),
    g_x(0)=partial_y U2(x,0).

Local smoothness and oddness imply that g_x is smooth on the closed half-line locally. One way to see the endpoint regularity is to divide the odd Taylor expansion of U2 by y. For any required k, Taylor expansion with the differentiated remainder through order2k+1 gives a polynomial in y^2 and a remainder with continuous derivatives through orderk in z=y^2. Taking arbitrarily large k proves smoothness.

## Interpolation statement and its precise limit

Choose four distinct positive ordinates h_i, i=1,...,4, and let L_i(z) be the degree-three Lagrange polynomials for nodes z_i=h_i^2. Then for0<=y^2<=max z_i,

    U2(x,y)=y*sum_i L_i(y^2)*U2(x,h_i)/h_i + E,
    |E|<=|y|*M4/24*product_i |y^2-h_i^2|,

where M4=sup_(0<=z<=max z_i) |g_x''''(z)|. The standard interpolation remainder proves this because g_x is C4 on that interval. The same formula holds for negative y by parity. If the four h_i are all O(h) and |y|<=min h_i, the interpolation error in U2/y is O(h^8), with the displayed derivative bound. The bound is not claimed computable for the candidate data in this run.

If each sampled U2(x,h_i)/h_i has absolute numerical error at most epsilon_i, interpolation adds at most

    sum_i |L_i(y^2)|*epsilon_i

to the error in U2/y. This is why one must balance smaller ordinates against cancellation in the sampled canonical inverse. Agreement of successive stencils is supporting evidence, not a certified estimate of M4 or of quadrature error.

The implementation uses h_i=h*(1,1/2,1/4,1/8), normalized nodes(1,1/4,1/16,1/64), and target(y/h)^2. It averages U2(x,h_i) and -U2(x,-h_i) before dividing by h_i. This enforces the known parity at the sample level and does not prescribe a strain. U1 is evaluated by the original direct inverse at the target. The direct inverse does not use angular Poisson modes or producer velocity coefficients. Comparisons with the independent angular inverse are performed only after the interpolated velocity and residual have been computed.

For target points with positive density-residual denominator, Cartesian FD8 supplies density derivatives. We report the original direct value, each stencil, inverse refinements, and both normalizations. A discrepancy is retained as OPEN until the supporting comparisons justify an interpretation. No denominator is replaced by a floor, no arbitrary harmonic term is inserted, and the true canonical inverse is not replaced by a boundary condition.

## Manufactured whole-plane test

Take p=1/2 and define

    psi=x*y*(1+r^2)^(-1/4),
    rho=-y*[4+(7/2)r^2]*(1+r^2)^(-5/4).

Differentiation gives -Delta psi=rho_x. Both fields have the required parities. The velocity is O(r^(1/2)) and hence canonical by uniqueness; rho and its gradient have the required growth. The exact quotient is

    U2(x,y)/y=(1+r^2)^(-1/4)-(x^2/2)*(1+r^2)^(-5/4).

This field is not claimed to satisfy the profile transport equation. It tests the inverse independently of any solver or origin constraint. The test covers multiple x, small y/x, several h, and two quadrature resolutions. It also checks exact interpolation of polynomials of degree at most3 in z using rational arithmetic. The measured inverse errors are numerical diagnostics, while the displayed Poisson identity and interpolation remainder are analytical statements.

## Scope

This method addresses cancellation in evaluating a known smooth odd velocity, not existence or smoothness of a limit of optimized densities. Even successful manufactured tests and candidate agreement would establish a finite-point cross-check only. The numerical archive must retain any disagreement and may not present stencil convergence as an interval certificate.
