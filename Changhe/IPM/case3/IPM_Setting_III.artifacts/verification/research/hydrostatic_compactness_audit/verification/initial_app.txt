## A.3.72 Exact anisotropic rescaling and the large-slope limit of the inverse

(a) With x = h xi, d_x = h^{-1} d_xi, so rho_x = (a0/h) g_xi, rho_y = a0 g_y, U_1 = -psi_y = h V_1 and U_2 = psi_x = V_2. The transport operator becomes (c_l h xi + h V_1)(a0/h) g_xi + (c_l y + V_2) a0 g_y = a0 [(c_l xi + V_1) g_xi + (c_l y + V_2) g_y] and rho = a0 g. The Poisson equation -psi_xx - psi_yy = rho_x becomes -h^{-1} Phi_xixi - h Phi_yy = (a0/h) g_xi; division by h and a0/h^2 = s give the second equation of (1). For fixed a0 the norms |(x, y)| and |(xi, y)| are comparable, so smoothness, the parities, the growth bounds and the condition o(r) correspond (with constants depending on a0). Uniqueness: a difference of two velocities comes from an odd potential with Phi_yy + eps Phi_xixi = 0; in z = xi/sqrt(eps) it is harmonic, so its velocity components are harmonic functions of sublinear growth, hence constants (Liouville), and the parities make them zero. The centre: psi_xy = h^{-1} h Phi_xiy. The jets: g = rho(h xi, y)/a0 = y + (b h^2/a0) xi^2 y + (e/a0) y^3 + ..., and h^2/a0 = s. By (T1), 6E = 6e/a0 = (1 - 2A/a0) b = (1 - 2 A s eps) s B = (s - 2A eps) B. The axis statement is 3.6 divided by a0, and the representative with L_K = 1 is that of 3.6.

(b) For y > 0, c_l y d_y(K y^p) = c_l p K y^p = K y^p, and the same holds for y < 0. Since rho is continuous and absolutely continuous in y, its distributional derivative is K p |y|^{p - 1}, which is locally integrable, so y rho_y = rho/c_l holds in distributions and there is no singular part on y = 0. Since rho_x = 0, the canonical velocity is 0. At fixed y != 0, |grad rho| = |K| p |y|^{p - 1} does not tend to 0 as x -> inf, while the class requires |grad rho| <= C (1 + r)^{gamma - 1}.

(c) Partial Fourier transform in xi: for q = sqrt(eps) |k| > 0 the odd-in-y solution of -Phi_yy + eps k^2 Phi = (ik) ghat is Phihat(k, y) = ik int_0^inf D_q(y, t) ghat(k, t) dt with D_q(y, t) = [exp(-q |y - t|) - exp(-q (y + t))]/(2q) (the odd reflection of the Green function of -d_y^2 + q^2), and d_y D_q(0, t) = exp(-q t). By the uniqueness in (a) this is the canonical inverse. For g = H(xi) f(y) this gives Phi_xiy(0) = -(2 pi)^{-1} int k^2 Hhat(k) L(sqrt(eps) |k|) dk with L(q) = int_0^inf exp(-q t) f(t) dt. For H = exp(-xi^2) and f_tau(y) = y exp(-tau y^2) (tau > 0) the integral is elementary and Phi_xiy(0) = -(sqrt(tau) + sqrt(eps))^{-2}. With nu = 1 - gamma/2, f(y) = y (1 + y^2)^{-nu} = Gamma(nu)^{-1} int_0^inf tau^{nu - 1} exp(-tau) f_tau(y) dtau, and Tonelli (all integrands have one sign) gives Phi_xiy(0) = -Gamma(nu)^{-1} int_0^inf tau^{nu - 1} exp(-tau) (sqrt(tau) + sqrt(eps))^{-2} dtau. The substitution tau = eps v^2 gives eps^{gamma/2} Phi_xiy(0) = -(2/Gamma(nu)) int_0^inf v^{1 - gamma} exp(-eps v^2) (1 + v)^{-2} dv, which tends by monotone convergence to -(2/Gamma(nu)) B(2 - gamma, gamma) = -2 Gamma(2 - gamma) Gamma(gamma)/Gamma(1 - gamma/2). Numerical check (main analysis): at gamma = 1/2 the closed form and direct quadrature of the Fourier formula agree to 1e-11 at eps = 1e-2 and 1e-3, and the scaled strain is -2.17, -2.44, -2.52, -2.55 at eps = 1e-4, 1e-6, 1e-8, 1e-10, against the limit -2.5637; the Gaussian formula was checked at (tau, eps) = (1, 0.3) and (2, 0.05) to 1e-15. Bounds of the class: H and its derivatives decay faster than any power, |f| <= C (1 + |y|)^{gamma - 1} and |f'| <= C (1 + |y|)^{gamma - 2}, and 1 + r <= (1 + |xi|)(1 + |y|). Hydrostatic velocity: every potential odd in y with -Phi_yy = H'(xi) f(y) is y beta(xi) - H'(xi) int_0^y (y - t) f(t) dt, so V_2(0, y) = y beta'(0) + 2 int_0^y (y - t) f(t) dt (H''(0) = -2), and the integral is asymptotic to y^{gamma + 1}/(gamma (gamma + 1)), which no term y beta'(0) cancels.

(d) For y, t >= 0, D_q(y, t) = (1/2) int_{|y - t|}^{y + t} exp(-q tau) dtau, and exp(-sqrt(eps) |k| tau) is the Fourier multiplier of convolution with the Poisson kernel P_{sqrt(eps) tau}, a positive even kernel of mass one with P_a * P_b = P_{a + b}. Hence, first for compactly supported g and then for g by cutoff approximation (cutoffs at radius R preserve the three bounds with constants independent of R), V_eps,1(xi, y) = -(1/2) int_0^inf [sgn(t - y) (P_{sqrt(eps) |y - t|} * f_t)(xi) + (P_{sqrt(eps) (y + t)} * f_t)(xi)] dt and V_eps,2(xi, y) = (1/2) int_0^inf int_{|y - t|}^{y + t} (P_{sqrt(eps) tau} * f_t')(xi) dtau dt, where f_t = g_xi(., t), which is odd, with |f_t| <= C (1 + t)^{gamma - 1} and |f_t'| <= C (1 + t)^{gamma - 2}. Convolution with P_a preserves these sup bounds and oddness, so |P_a * f_t (xi)| <= C min((1 + t)^{gamma - 1}, |xi| (1 + t)^{gamma - 2}) and |P_a * f_t'| <= C (1 + t)^{gamma - 2}. Splitting the t integral at t = |xi| gives |V_eps,1| <= C' (1 + |xi|)^gamma, and int_0^inf min(y, t) (1 + t)^{gamma - 2} dt <= C' (1 + y)^gamma gives the bound on V_eps,2; these majorants do not depend on eps. As eps -> 0, P_{sqrt(eps) tau} * F -> F locally uniformly for bounded continuous F, and dominated convergence gives (2), locally uniformly. Uniqueness of the hydrostatic velocity: two odd potentials with the same -Phi_yy differ by y beta(xi), whose velocity (-beta, y beta') is sublinear on each vertical line only if beta' = 0, and beta is odd, so beta = 0. Setting y = 0 in V_eps,1 and differentiating once in xi (majorant C (1 + t)^{gamma - 2}) gives the strain formula and its limit. For varying inputs the same majorants hold, and C^2 convergence on compact sets with the uniform sup bounds controls the Poisson tails, so every step is unchanged.

(e) For each n, g_n solves (1) with eps_n = 1/a0,n -> 0, s = 1 and c_l,n. By (d) for varying inputs, V_n -> V_0[g_lim] locally uniformly, and g_n -> g_lim in C^1 on compact sets, so the transport equation, g_y(0) = 1 and the three bounds pass to the limit, and Phi_n,xiy(0) = 1 - c_l,n converges to int_0^inf g_lim,xixi(0, t) dt. For the second statement, suppose profiles with c_l,n in J, a0,n -> inf and representatives obeying the bounds with C, gamma and (C_R) exist; a subsequence has c_l,n -> c_lim in J and, by Arzela-Ascoli with the bounds on D^3 g_n, g_n -> g_lim in C^2 on compact sets; then g_lim solves (3) with the three bounds, which was excluded. For a0 < 0, (1) has s = -1, and the same argument applies to -g_xi.
