# Frozen linear growth and nonlinear trajectory sensitivity

Statement. Let F be a C^2 vector field on a finite-dimensional real normed space, and let B(t) solve B'=F(B), B(0)=B0, on a fixed interval where the nearby solutions exist. For a direction h define d_e(t)=[Phi_t(B0+e h)-Phi_t(B0)]/e. Then as e tends to zero, d_e converges to the variational solution q'=DF(B(t))q, q(0)=h, not generally to z'=DF(B0)z. A finite interval of norm amplification in either linear system does not imply an eigenvalue with positive real part. These exact statements audit the interpretation of the producer's frozen-J versus nonlinear-difference experiment. They do not independently validate the producer's trajectories.

Dependencies and notation. Phi_t is the local ODE flow. For the fixed linear reconstruction and projection in dynamic rescaling, the finite-dimensional field is quadratic: F(u)=P[w(u)rho_u-(x(u)X+U_u).grad rho_u], where w,x,U and rho depend linearly on u, and P is a fixed linear map. Thus its Hessian D^2F is constant and the formulas below apply without a remainder from higher derivatives. This is the finite equation, not a well-posedness assertion for the continuum PDE. The canonical directional formula is independently proved in eigenvector_canonical_audit/PROOF.md.

1. Variational equation. Subtract the two nonlinear equations and use the fundamental theorem of calculus:
 d_e'=int_0^1 DF(B(t)+theta e d_e(t))d_e(t) dtheta.
On a compact neighborhood of the reference trajectory, DF is locally Lipschitz. Gronwall first bounds d_e uniformly and then bounds its difference from q by O(|e|) on the fixed time interval. This proves the convergence. When F is quadratic the identity sharpens to d_e'=DF(B(t))d_e+(e/2)D^2F[d_e,d_e].

2. Difference from a frozen Jacobian. Put J0=DF(B0). At zero q'=J0 h and q''=J0^2 h+D^2F[F(B0),h], whereas z''=J0^2 h. For smooth F, q(t)-z(t)=(t^2/2)D^2F[F(B0),h]+O(t^3). Thus a nonzero residual F(B0) generically produces an error independent of e. Small drift in one scalar diagnostic w(B(t)) does not bound the change of the full operator DF(B(t)); a bound for the whole background in a norm controlling that derivative is required.

An exact counterexample uses F(x)=x^2, B0=1 and h=1. For t<1, B(t)=1/(1-t), q(t)=1/(1-t)^2 and z(t)=exp(2t). More precisely, d_e(t)=1/[(1-t)(1-t-e t)], which tends to q, not z. The difference begins at t^2, in agreement with D^2F[F(B0),h]=2. This example is solely about an ODE inference and is not an IPM profile.

3. Frozen finite-time growth does not determine eigenvalue signs. Take A=[[-1,K],[0,-1]], h=(0,1). Both eigenvalues equal -1, but exp(tA)h=exp(-t)(Kt,1), with Euclidean norm exp(-t)sqrt(1+K^2 t^2). For any fixed t>0 this norm can exceed any prescribed factor by choosing K. The asymptotic growth is negative. Consequently direct finite-window amplification supports a sensitivity observation, not the sign of a continuum eigenvalue or even of an unrelated compressed matrix. The computed positive eigenvalues of a specified finite matrix are separate numerical evidence; their large canonical lift residuals prevent the proposed continuum inference.

5. Report scope. The proposed report should say 'finite amplification is seen in direct linear integration' rather than 'the sign is supported only by direct linear integration.' Angular and size comparisons at finitely many grids are not convergence to a continuum spectrum. Projection-defect percentages from the older tau approximately20.04 checkpoints must retain those source times when quoted beside the newer exact-tau20 comparisons. Conditional convergence only on a stable set does not exclude trajectories in that set.
