3.80 Finite polynomial axis data do not solve the axis equation of the hydrostatic problem (proved by the checker, with exact supporting checks; hand-checked in the main analysis; confirmed in two external reviews). Let C > 0 and 1 < P <= 2 be real, and let H and k be real polynomials with H(0) = 0 and H'(0) != 0. For 0 < t < 1 put w = 1 - t^2 and y = C t w^{-P}; since dy/dt = C Q w^{-P - 1} with Q = 1 + (2P - 1) t^2, the map t -> y is an increasing bijection of (0, 1) onto (0, inf), and t and w are regarded as functions of y. Let G(y) = y H(w), and let V be a function with V'(y) = k(w) (for instance V(y) = int_0^y k(w(s)) ds). Then for no real c does (c y + V) G' = G hold on a nonempty open interval of y > 0. Application to 4.10. On the vertical axis V_1 = 0 by evenness, so the hydrostatic equation (3) of 3.72 reads (c y + V_2(0, y)) G' = G there, with G(y) = g(0, y), and (2) of 3.72 gives d/dy V_2(0, y) = int_y^inf g_xixi(0, s) ds (an integral of the horizontal curvature on the axis, not g_xixi itself). For the saved trial densities of 4.10 the radial map r = L ((1 + t')/2)^{1/2}/(1 - t')^P of 4.7 and 4.10 (t' the compactified radial variable there, L > 0) is, on the axis, the map of this result with w = (1 - t')/2, t = (1 - w)^{1/2} and C = 2^{-P} L. For nine of them, the checker's exact reconstruction of the axis data from the saved coefficient vectors gives g(0, y) = y H(w) and d/dy V_2(0, y) = k(w) with polynomials H and k of degree N + 1 (N the radial degree), where V_2 is the velocity (2) of 3.72 of the trial density, obtained through an exact polynomial primitive of the axis integral and not through the quadrature of 4.10; the checker verified in exact rational arithmetic that H(0) = k(0) = 0, H'(0) != 0 and C > 0 for the stored map exponent P (P = 2 at c = 2, and at c = 3 the stored binary value 6755399441055743/2^52, slightly below 3/2): the three states at c = 3 with the logarithmic-panel rule of 4.10 (64 x 16, 96 x 24, 128 x 32), two states at c = 3 with the same rule and rows at 3M angles (64 x 16, 96 x 24), and four states at c = 2 with the same rule (96 x 24; 128 x 32 at the iteration cap and continued past it; 160 x 40), the last six not otherwise reported here. So none of the nine satisfies the axis equation on a nonempty open interval of y > 0. Scope. This concerns exact solutions within the stated class of polynomial axis data: it gives no lower bound on the residual uniform in the degrees, does not exclude limits of such densities as the degrees tend to infinity, densities of other forms or profiles at finite slope, and is not a nonexistence result for the hydrostatic problem.
