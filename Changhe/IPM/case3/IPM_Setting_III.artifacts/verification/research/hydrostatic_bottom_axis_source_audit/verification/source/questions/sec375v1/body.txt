3.75 The hydrostatic problem: local analytic solutions from the bottom, divergence of the horizontal series from the axis, a representation at the source, and no sign of the cusp coefficient ((a)-(c) and (e) proved, (d) formal; (a)-(d) derived in an external analysis and (e) obtained by the checker; hand-checked in the main analysis; AUDITPLACEHOLDER). Let c > 1, p = 1/c, D = c - 1 and A = 2c - 1. By the local system we mean the equations
@@    h_y = g_xi,   b_y = c - h_xi,   (c xi + h) g_xi + b g_y = g   (1)
for a triple (g, h, b) of functions with g even in xi and odd in y, h odd in xi and even in y, and b even in xi and odd in y. (a) Data on the bottom. (i) Let g be C^2 on {y >= 0} with these parities, g_y(0) = 1 and the three bounds of 3.72(d), and let h and b be C^1 functions on {y >= 0} such that (1) holds, b(xi, 0) = 0 and h(xi, y) -> 0 as y -> inf for every xi. Then h = V_1 and b = c y + V_2 with V given by (2) of 3.72, so g solves (3) of 3.72 with c_lim = c; conversely every solution of (3) of 3.72 in the class of 3.73 gives such a triple, with h = V_1 and b = c y + V_2. (ii) Let k be an even real-analytic function on R with k > 0 and k(0) = 1, and put zeta(xi) = int_0^xi dt/k(t), a_0 = A k zeta and h_0 = a_0 - c xi, so that a_0 k' = (a_0' - A) k as in (1) of 3.74. For every R > 0 there is a real-analytic triple (g, h, b) solving (1) on a neighbourhood of [-R, R] x {0} with g(xi, 0) = 0, g_y(xi, 0) = k(xi), h(xi, 0) = h_0(xi) and b(xi, 0) = 0, and any two real-analytic triples solving (1) with g(xi, 0) = 0, g_y(xi, 0) = k(xi) and b(xi, 0) = 0 on connected neighbourhoods of [-R, R] x {0} agree on the component of their common domain that contains [-R, R] x {0}. The height of the neighbourhood is bounded below in terms of the complex extension of k (A.3.75); no common positive height on the whole line is claimed. In g = sum_n g_{2n+1}(xi) y^{2n+1} the coefficients are determined by k: g_1 = k and, for n >= 1, a_0 g_{2n+1}' + ((2n + 1) b_1 - 1) g_{2n+1} = F_n with b_1 = 1 - a_0 k'/k and F_n a polynomial in g_1, ..., g_{2n-1} and their derivatives (F_1 = -k'^2/2 + k k''/6), and each of these equations has exactly one solution that is bounded near xi = 0. So a solution of (3) of 3.72 in the class of 3.73 with g_y(xi, 0) = k(xi) has these coefficients in its Taylor expansion in y at the bottom if it is C^inf near the bottom, and coincides with the triple of (ii) near [-R, R] x {0} if it is real analytic there; for such solutions the remaining requirements are global: continuation to {y >= 0}, the three bounds and h(xi, y) -> 0 as y -> inf. Whether a C^inf or C^2 solution is determined by k is not known. (b) Divergence of the horizontal series from the axis. For odd smooth axis data G with G' > 0, put v = G/G' and Q_G = G/G'^2; then the coefficients of 3.73(c) satisfy g_{2n} = Q_G^n G^{(3n)}/(2n)! + R_n for n >= 1, where R_n depends only on G, G', ..., G^{(3n - 1)}. Fix y_0 > 0, let G_* be the axis function of 3.73(b) for this c, and for delta > 0 and alpha > 0 let B be the real Banach space of odd holomorphic functions phi on the strip |Im z| < delta, real on the real axis, with phi'(0) = 0 and finite norm sup |exp(alpha z^2) phi(z)| over the strip. For delta small and a ball N about 0 in B small enough, every G = G_* + phi with phi in N has G'(0) = 1 and G' > 0 on R, and v = G/G' has v'' > 0 on (0, inf) and v(y) - c y = O(1), so that G(y) ~ K_G y^p with 0 < K_G < inf; and the set of such G for which limsup_n |g_{2n}(y_0)|^{1/(2n)} = inf contains a countable intersection of dense open subsets of G_* + N. For such G the series sum_n g_{2n}(y_0) xi^{2n} has radius of convergence zero, so no triple solving (1) that is real analytic near (0, y_0) has g(0, y) = G(y) near y_0. Whether the series for G_* itself diverges is not decided, and divergence excludes only real analyticity near the axis point, not C^inf solutions with these axis data. So, unlike the bottom data k in (a), analytic axis data near G_* do not in general generate real-analytic local solutions. (c) A representation at the source. Let g be a solution of (3) of 3.72 in the class of 3.73 with g_y > 0 on {y >= 0}, and let Y, Z and H be as in 3.74(a). Then, for a constant C', |h_xi(xi, y)| <= C' (1 + |xi| + y)^{gamma - 1} and |h(xi, y)| <= C' ((1 + |xi|)^gamma - 1) on {y >= 0}; H is C^1 on {sigma >= 0}; every backward trajectory of the vector field T = (c xi + H) d_xi + sigma d_sigma in {sigma >= 0} tends to (0, 0); and
@@    Z(P) = exp( int_{-inf}^0 (D - H_xi)(P_tau) dtau ),   (2)
where tau -> P_tau is the trajectory of T with P_0 = P, H_xi is taken at fixed sigma (H_xi = h_xi - g_xi^2/g_y at y = Y), and the integral converges absolutely. So Z is determined by H, and H is a fixed point of the map that takes H to Z by (2), then to Y = int_0^sigma Z, then to int_sigma^inf d_xi Y(xi, s) ds; the dilations g -> l g(X/l) act by H -> l H(xi/l, sigma/l). The domain of this map, its differentiability and any contraction property are not established. (d) Formal statements. For (1) with coefficients frozen at a point where b != 0, perturbations proportional to exp(lambda y + i eta xi) require
@@    b lambda^3 + (i a eta - 1) lambda^2 + i g_xi eta lambda + g_y eta^2 = 0,   a = c xi + h.   (3)
On the vertical axis (a = g_xi = 0, b = v, g_y = G') the roots are (G'/v)^{1/3} |eta|^{2/3} omega + O(1) with omega^3 = -1, and the largest real part is (1/2) (G'/v)^{1/3} |eta|^{2/3} + O(1); where a != 0 and g_y != 0 the roots are +-(i g_y eta/a)^{1/2} + O(1) and -i a eta/b + O(1). This suggests, and does not prove, that the evolution of (1) in y can be controlled in horizontal Gevrey classes of order at most 3/2 near the vertical axis and at most 2 away from it; it concerns frozen coefficients at interior points, not the bottom, where b = 0. The evolution in xi of 3.73(c) instead has roots with real part of order |eta|^{3/2} at vertical frequency eta. Counting: one free function k by (a), one functional condition h(xi, y) -> 0, the parameter c and the one-parameter dilation give a formal count of zero, which would mean isolated solutions modulo dilation, with isolated values of c, if a suitable fixed-c defect map were Fredholm of index zero and a transversality condition held; neither is known, and the count is heuristic. (e) No sign of the cusp coefficient. For 0 < p < 1 put C_0 = 1/(p + 2) and, on R^2 minus the origin,
@@    f = C_0 y r^{p - 3} (3 xi^2 + (p + 2) y^2),   V_1[f] = C_0 xi r^{p - 3} (xi^2 + p y^2),   V_2[f] = -C_0 y r^{p - 3} (p xi^2 + y^2).   (4)
The formulas (2) of 3.72 applied to f give exactly these functions on {y >= 0} minus the origin. The trace Theta(th) = f(cos th, sin th) = C_0 sin th (3 cos^2 th + (p + 2) sin^2 th), a positive multiple of sin th + ((1 - p)/(3 (p + 3))) sin 3th, is positive on (0, pi) and increasing on (0, pi/2) with Theta(pi/2) = 1; f_y > 0 on R^2 minus the origin, f_xi < 0 where xi > 0 and y > 0, and C_p(Theta) = 0 for the number C_p of 3.74(d). If chi >= 0 is smooth, not identically zero and supported in (1, 2), then replacing F(s) = f(1, s) by F + eps chi for s > 0 (and extending by homogeneity of degree p and the parities) gives, for |eps| small, a smooth trace with the same jets at both axes and the same strict sign properties, and changes C_p by eps (p + 1)(p + 2) int_0^inf s chi(s) ds. So positivity of the trace, its monotonicity on (0, pi/2) and these signs of f_y and f_xi do not determine the sign of C_p: C_p = 0, C_p > 0 and C_p < 0 all occur. The function f is not a solution: (c X . grad - 1) f = 0, while V[f] . grad f = -C_0^2 y r^{2p - 6} (3 xi^4 + 2p (p + 2) xi^2 y^2 + p (p + 2) y^4) < 0 on y > 0; and f is not C^1 at 0. Scope: none of (a)-(e) establishes existence or nonexistence for (3) of 3.72 or selects c; (a)(ii) concerns the local system near the bottom and imposes neither the bounds nor the condition at infinity; (b) concerns generic analytic axis data; (d) is formal.
