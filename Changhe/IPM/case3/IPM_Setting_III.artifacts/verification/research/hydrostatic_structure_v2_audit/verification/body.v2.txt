3.74 The hydrostatic problem: isopycnal form, leading far field, no regular expansion at c = 1, and formal far-field terms ((a)-(c), the conditional moment identity in (d) and the compactness statement of (e) proved under their stated hypotheses, the far-field expansion of (d) and the linearization and selection statements of (e) formal; derived in an external analysis; hand-checked in the main analysis; AUDITPLACEHOLDER). Let c, p, D, g, G, v and K be as in 3.73, A = 2c - 1, and h = V_1, a = c xi + h and b = c y + V_2 as in 3.73(c), so that h_y = g_xi, b_y = c - h_xi and a g_xi + b g_y = g; let k(xi) = g_y(xi, 0), let r, th be polar coordinates of X = (xi, y), and write V[u] = (V_1[u], V_2[u]) for the formulas (2) of 3.72 applied to a function u. (a) Boundary law and isopycnal form. If k(xi) > 0 for all xi, then, with a_0(xi) = a(xi, 0),
@@    a_0 k' = (a_0' - A) k,   a_0(xi) = A k(xi) int_0^xi dt/k(t),   k(xi) = L |xi|^{p - 1} (1 + O(|xi|^{gamma - 1}))  (|xi| -> inf)   (1)
with a constant 0 < L < inf. If g_y > 0 on {y >= 0}, then for each xi the map y -> g(xi, y) is an increasing bijection of [0, inf) onto itself; with y = Y(xi, sigma) its inverse, Z = Y_sigma and H(xi, sigma) = h(xi, Y(xi, sigma)),
@@    d_xi((c xi + H) Z) + sigma Z_sigma = A Z,   H_sigma = -Y_xi,   H(xi, sigma) = int_sigma^inf Y_xi(xi, s) ds,   (2)
with Y(xi, 0) = 0, Z(xi, 0) = 1/k(xi), Z(0, 0) = 1 and H_sigma(xi, 0) = Z_sigma(xi, 0) = 0, and at fixed xi, as sigma -> inf, Y ~ (sigma/K)^c, Z ~ c K^{-c} sigma^{c - 1} and H -> 0. Moreover, for sigma_1 > 0,
@@    int_0^inf Y_xixi(0, sigma) dsigma = D,   d_xi int_0^{sigma_1} (c xi + H) Z dsigma + sigma_1 Z(xi, sigma_1) = 2c Y(xi, sigma_1),   (3)
and, without a sign condition on g_y, M(xi) = int_0^inf (G(y) - g(xi, y)) dy converges and is C^2, with M' = h(xi, 0) and M''(0) = D. These identities give no sign estimate for Z_xi and establish no maximum-principle argument excluding g_y > 0: by (2), (c xi + H) Z_xi + sigma Z_sigma = (D - H_xi) Z, where H_xi is taken at fixed sigma and has no known sign. No restriction on c beyond 3.73(a) is obtained here. (b) Leading far field. There is a continuous function Theta of the angle, with Theta(pi - th) = Theta(th) = -Theta(-th), Theta(pi/2) = K and |Theta(th)| <= C'' |sin th|, such that
@@    g = r^p Theta(th) + o(r^p)   as r -> inf, uniformly in th;   (4)
in particular |g| <= C' (1 + r)^p. If g_y > 0 on {y >= 0}, then Theta > 0 on (0, pi) and Theta(th) = L th + o(th) as th -> 0+, with L of (1). The proof gives no convergence of derivatives of r^{-p} g and no asymptotics of V; on a fixed annulus the second xi-derivatives of R^{-p} g(R X) are only O(R^{gamma - p}). Since p < 1, the bound |Theta(th)| <= C'' |sin th| excludes K sgn(sin th) |sin th|^p as the entire trace; it does not exclude agreement with that function near th = pi/2. (c) No regular expansion at c = 1. If a family of solutions g_D of (3) of 3.72 with c = 1 + D satisfied g_D = y + D u + O(D^2) in C^1 on compact sets and V[g_D] = D V[u] + O(D^2) uniformly on compact sets, with V[u] defined and u satisfying the conditions of the following theorem, then u would solve (5) below. Theorem: there is no C^2 function u on R^2, even in xi and odd in y, with |u(X)| <= C (1 + r)^q for some 1 < q < 2, such that int_y^inf u_xixi(xi, t) dt converges for all xi and all y >= 0 and
@@    (X . grad - 1) u + V_2[u] = -y   on {y >= 0}.   (5)
In particular no such first-order coefficient of size O(r log r) exists. This is not a nonexistence statement for small D > 0: it excludes only an expansion of this regular form. By 3.73(a), a family of solutions with c -> 1+ also needs gamma >= 1/c, so gamma -> 1. For comparison, the functions K_D y^{1/(1 + D)} on y > 0, with K_D = 1 + kappa_1 D + O(D^2), do not depend on xi, have zero velocity and solve the transport equation of (3) of 3.72 on y > 0; their first-order coefficient y (kappa_1 - log y) is not C^1 at y = 0. (d) Formal far-field terms (formal, except the moment identity below, which is proved under its hypothesis: they assume an expansion g = f_p + f_{2p-1} + ..., f_p = r^p Theta(th) with Theta smooth, that may be differentiated and inserted in the formulas (2) of 3.72 term by term, the velocity formulas being used termwise only where the integrals converge). The terms of degree 2p - 1 in (3) of 3.72 give
@@    f_{2p-1} = (V[f_p] . grad f_p)/D,   f_{2p-1}(0, y) = K Q y^{2p - 1}/((1 - p) D),   Q = p K + Theta''(pi/2),   (6)
since (f_p)_xixi(0, y) = Q y^{p - 2} and V_2[f_p](0, y) = Q y^p/(p (1 - p)). The degrees d_n = n p - (n - 1) generated by the nonlinearity have c d_n - 1 = -(n - 1) D != 0 for n >= 2, so no transport resonance occurs among them. For a homogeneous term of degree d <= 0 the axis integral int_0^y t^{d - 1} dt diverges, so its velocity requires matching to a regular core; at d = 0 a nonzero axis curvature Q_0 y^{-2} gives Q_0 log y in V_2 after this matching. Hence the values c = n/(n - 1), where d_n = 0, are possible logarithmic cases; whether a logarithm occurs is not determined here. If int_0^inf t |g_xixi(0, t) - Q t^{p - 2}| dt < inf for some constant Q, then V_2(0, y) = Q y^p/(p (1 - p)) + m_0 + o(1) as y -> inf (a proved statement), with m_0 = int_0^inf t (g_xixi(0, t) - Q t^{p - 2}) dt, and formally m_0 contributes K m_0 c^{-2} y^{p - 1} to G. Near the vertical axis, with F(s) = f_p(1, s), q(s) = p (p - 1) F(s) - 2 (p - 1) s F'(s) + s^2 F''(s) and the finite number C_p(Theta) = int_0^inf s (q(s) - Q s^{p - 2}) ds, V_2[f_p](xi, y) = Q y^p/(p (1 - p)) + C_p(Theta) |xi|^p + O(xi^2 y^{p - 2}) as xi/y -> 0, so f_{2p-1} contains p K C_p(Theta) |xi|^p y^{p - 1}/D, which is not differentiable in xi at xi = 0; an expansion of this kind with coefficients that are C^1 in xi near the vertical axis therefore requires C_p(Theta) = 0. (e) Selection of c and compactness. At fixed c the dilations g -> l g(X/l) map solutions to solutions (3.73(d)), so the function g - X . grad g, which is not zero by 3.73(d)(iii), lies formally in the kernel of the linearization L_g u = (c X + V[g]) . grad u - u + V[u] . grad g (for a C^2 solution, g - X . grad g is only C^1 and its velocity is not automatically defined classically); a variation c_1 of c enters as L_g u + c_1 X . grad g = 0 and, at fixed Theta, changes the leading term of (4) by -c_1 p^2 r^p Theta(th) log r, so for c_1 != 0 this formal leading variation is not O(r^p). The results of 3.73 and (a)-(d) do not determine existence or the number of solution classes modulo dilation, and give no rule selecting c: the axis-data families of 3.73(b) and the trace Theta are function-valued data whose compatibility with a global solution has not been established, and the frozen estimate of 3.73(c) concerns a Cauchy problem, not a boundary problem. Compactness (proved): let c_n -> c > 1 and let g_n be C^3 functions, even in xi and odd in y, with d_y g_n(0) = 1, the three bounds of 3.72(d) with one C and one gamma, |D^3 g_n| <= C_R on each disc of radius R, and (c_n xi + V_1[g_n]) d_xi g_n + (c_n y + V_2[g_n]) d_y g_n - g_n -> 0 uniformly on compact subsets of {y >= 0}; then a subsequence converges in C^2 on compact sets to a solution of (3) of 3.72 with c_lim = c in the class of 3.73. Scope: (a)-(c), the conditional moment identity in (d) and the compactness statement in (e) are proved under their stated hypotheses, for solutions of (3) of 3.72 in the class of 3.73 (and (c) for the stated functions u); they establish neither existence nor nonexistence for (3), select no c and give no uniform estimates for the exact profiles of 3.72(e); the far-field expansion of (d) and the linearization and selection statements of (e) are formal.
