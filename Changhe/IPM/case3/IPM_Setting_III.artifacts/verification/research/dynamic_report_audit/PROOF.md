# Audit of the dynamical rescaling report paragraph

Statement and scope. This audit concerns the precise assertions in the frozen proposed Section4.9, not existence of a self-similar profile. The canonical IPM operator U is linear, divergence free, has curl -rho_x, the stated parities, and sublinear growth. The density is smooth and even in x, odd in y. Let a=rho_y(0), c=psi_xy(0), C>1. The rescaled evolution is
 rho_tau = F(rho) = c_w rho - (c_x X + U[rho]).grad rho,
 c_x=C c_w, c_w=c/(1-C).
Where c_w>0, a steady state gives the normalized profile rho*=rho/c_w with exponent C. Under an asymptotic expansion with exponent p=1/C and differentiable lower order remainder, the far trace is constant in pseudo-time. The logged scalar, spectral and perturbation diagnostics are finite calculations; their interpretation must retain the state, time, sample norm and projection used. No endpoint follows from the facts audited here.

Dependencies. Exact canonical inverse, origin and scaling identities are rederived in the earlier topic proofs listed in INDEX. The independently implemented full-plane density, velocity, derivative and finite matrix checks used here are dynamic_local_reconstruction (173), dynamic_projection_audit (167), independent_dynamic_spectrum (176) and eigenvector_canonical_audit (180). These are computational dependencies with frozen data, not hypotheses of an existence theorem. Frozen_variation_scope (183) contains a standalone proof of the ODE distinctions recalled below. 1. Normalization and tail. Differentiating the transport equation at zero gives a_tau=(c_w-c_x-c)a=0. At a steady state, linearity gives U[rho*]=U[rho]/c_w. Dividing the zero evolution equation by c_w squared gives (C X+U[rho*]).grad rho*=rho*. If c_w is nonpositive this is not the asserted positive normalization, and the case c_w=0 cannot be divided out. If rho=r^p Theta(theta)+o(r^p), with the corresponding differentiated expansion, sublinearity of U makes its transport term lower order; comparing the leading term gives Theta_tau=(c_w-p c_x)Theta=0 for p=1/C. Thus a and the axis trace coefficient K are fixed. The genuine spatial scaling lambda rho(X/lambda) fixes a and changes K by lambda^(1-p). At each fixed C it gives a unique representative on any prescribed positive K/a slice when K/a>0, because lambda maps the positive half-line bijectively to itself. This does not cover zero or negative K/a and does not make different C equivalent. If a start has a prescribed angular trace, convergence in a norm preserving that trace cannot reach a different one; compact convergence alone can.

2. Rates. If q(t)>0, its logarithmic secant rate over [t1,t2] is (log q(t2)-log q(t1))/(t2-t1). Its ordinary secant rate is (q(t2)-q(t1))/(t2-t1). They have different units and differ by the scale of q. For departures q is the logged sampled core maximum of |rho_tau|; for the C-scan q=c_w. The raw logs supply the actual endpoints; replacing them by nominal .1 and .5 changes the calculated departure rate. A rate divided by the initial slope uses a=c_w(0)a_star(0), not a_star at later times. The decimal output limits the arithmetic accuracy. A negative-c_w stop gives a crossing bracket between the last positive and first nonpositive recorded steps. It does not compute an exact zero time.

3. Sample norms and projection. For a finite set S define ||f||_S=max_{X in S}|f(X)|. A comparison ||f-g||_S/||f||_S is not max_{X in S}|f-g|/|f|, and neither is a uniform whole-plane bound. A vertical velocity comparison only controls U2 on those samples. States at distinct times test a mixture of evolution and resolution differences. Similarly ||P F-F||_S/||F||_S must identify whether S consists of fitting nodes or independent points. The older projection snapshots have tau about20.04 and cannot silently be relabeled as matched tau20 states. A single sign-changing derivative discrepancy cannot be summarized as a bounded multiplicative relative error.

4. Spectral and time evolution claims. A finite compressed Jacobian J=P DF Q depends on the background, the coefficient norm, the projection cutoff and the reconstruction. Transferring one background to finer grids is distinct from testing separately refined backgrounds. A lift h=Qv of Jv=lambda v has canonical residual DF h-lambda h; unless controlled in a suitable norm relative to the real rate, it is not an eigenpair certificate for the continuum operator. Finite norm amplification does not identify an eigenvalue sign: for A=[[-1,K],[0,-1]], exp(tA)(0,1)=exp(-t)(Kt,1), which can be arbitrarily large at fixed t even though both eigenvalues are -1. Thus positive spectral abscissae of the specified finite matrices and finite amplification are separate observations.

5. Nonlinear perturbations. Let B'=F(B) in a finite-dimensional discretization, F twice continuously differentiable. Differences [Phi_t(B0+epsilon h)-Phi_t(B0)]/epsilon tend to q'=DF(B(t))q, q(0)=h, by the mean-value identity followed by Gronwall on a compact trajectory neighborhood. A frozen calculation solves z'=DF(B0)z instead. At zero, q''-z''=D^2F(B0)[F(B0),h], so their difference starts with one half t squared times this term. For F(x)=x squared, B0=h=1, q=(1-t)^(-2) but z=exp(2t). Refining epsilon cannot remove this generic difference. The reported nonzero small-epsilon plateaus are therefore compatible with background drift, but neither prove that drift accounts for all the error nor validate the along-trajectory variational flow. The fixed time step gives a further numerical error, unbounded by this arithmetic audit.

6. Physical interpretation. On a constant vertical density gradient a>0, the feedback term -U[h].grad rho has Fourier multiplier a k_x squared/|k| squared, since U2[h] has multiplier -k_x squared/|k| squared. This local constant-gradient calculation is nonnegative. It does not prove a continuum unstable eigenvalue at a nonconstant profile, and the observed ablations change the normalization as well as the velocity feedback. The interpretation that feedback contributes to the observed departures is consistent with those experiments, not a theorem excluding profiles or every use of forward evolution. If an exact profile has an unstable direction in a well-defined dynamical setting, convergence can still occur from its stable set. Failure of the tested starts at the tested C does not decide existence.
