# Mixed-cell Lipschitz bound and additional partial-cell refinement

Use the witness, spaces, kernels, principal values, decomposition and parameter hypotheses of ../PROOF.md, together with the corrected interval rules of ../repairs/PROOF.md. This note proves two refinements used by the source snapshot ../sources_refined_0732. It does not certify any unfinished integration or external decimal.

On a convex ball B(zc,rQ) lying strictly in one half-plane, suppose the signed polygon branch g+c and the projection branch -1/2+lambda P are both certified smooth. Their derivative bounds are Lg and LP from the parent proof. The clamp functions h and -h have gradient norm bounded by Lh because Xmin=distance(zc,midline)-rQ>0. Integration along a segment gives the corresponding Lipschitz bounds on the entire ball.

The minimum and maximum of finitely many real L-Lipschitz functions are L-Lipschitz: for each pair p,q, every entry at p is at most the corresponding entry at q plus L|p-q|, and taking the indicated extrema preserves this comparison. Reverse p,q for the other direction. Thus the complete clamped witness has Lipschitz constant at most Lmix=max(Lg,LP,Lh), even when its active branch changes inside the ball. Reflection to the other half-plane is an isometry. Balls meeting the midline decline this refinement.

The independent global Holder bound remains available. Consequently |f(z)-f(zc)|<=min(rQ^alpha,Lmix rQ) on the ball. Replacing each occurrence of rQ^alpha by this minimum in the first-order product error is valid, both for the smooth increment density and for the two separately compensated near-pole products. No smoothness of the combined clamped function is needed for this first-order estimate. The existing second-order rule is still used only when one branch is certified throughout the ball.

A partial cell is a polar cell intersecting the boundary of the two deleted radius-1/2 disks. The exterior part of any such cell is the disjoint union, up to area-zero boundaries, of the exterior parts of its four radial/angular children. Each leaf is either omitted because it lies inside, integrated by a full-cell enclosure because it lies outside, or enclosed by the existing symmetric absolute bound on its exterior part. The validity of all three leaf rules is independent of refinement depth. Refining partial cells two levels beyond the full-cell depth therefore preserves the enclosure. A descendant found to be full may stop without further subdivision, even if its depth already exceeds the full-cell threshold.
