# A sufficient criterion localized to small divisors and large log degree

Status: PROVED. The theory part independently reviewed and adopted this proof; the final transcription was checked in V207 and V208. The estimates below are conditional convergence results. No required all-order bound has been established for the generated rational sequence.

## Setting and exact statement

Fix a reduced rational delta=a/b in (0,1), and set c=(1-delta)^(-1), g=gcd(a,2), q_0=2b/g and d_*=g/b. Let the nonzero leading density be a finite real odd sine polynomial with highest frequency L and plain coefficient norm F. Use the canonical stationary polynomial-log recurrence, with the entire constant coefficient of each resonant stream mode set to zero.

Write t=log r, P_n=2-n delta, and

\[
 F_n(t,\theta)=\sum_{m\text{ positive odd},\,k\ge0}
 a_{n,m,k}t^k\sin(m\theta),\qquad
 M_n=\sum_{m,k}|a_{n,m,k}|,\qquad N_n=\|G_n\|_*.
\]

Here the plain norm of a sine polynomial in t is the sum of the absolute values of all its coefficients. Each density frequency m has two possible stream incidences:

\[
 (q,s)=(m+1,P_n+m+1),\qquad
 (q,s)=(m-1,P_n-m+1)\quad(m>1).
\]

The second incidence is absent at m=1. For k>=0 define

\[
 W_k(s)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\sum_{h=0}^k\frac{k!}{(k-h)!|s|^{h+1}},&s\ne0,\\
 1/(k+1),&s=0.
 \end{cases}
\]

Define the nonnegative number

\[
 E_n=\frac12\sum_{\substack{\text{density coefficients and their incidences}\\
                          0<|s|<2k}}
             |a_{n,m,k}|W_k(s).
 \tag{1}
\]

Then every finite density input, whether generated by the stationary recurrence or prescribed independently, satisfies

\[
 N_n\le d_*^{-1}M_n+E_n.                       \tag{2}
\]

In particular the constant is 3/2 at delta=2/3. This improves the constant 3 in the closing_loop response without changing the definition of E_n.

For the generated stationary sequence, either of the following additional hypotheses implies geometric bounds on both M_n and N_n, and hence convergence of the full canonical exterior series with all naturally weighted derivatives:

1. E_n<=B M_n for all n, with a fixed finite B>=0;
2. E_n<=B A^n for all n, with fixed finite A>0 and B>=0.

Neither hypothesis is asserted for the actual rational sequence.

## Polynomial inversion and resonant normalization

Put D=partial_t. For nonzero s the unique polynomial inverse of D+s is

\[
 J_sH=\sum_{h\ge0}(-1)^h s^{-h-1}D^hH,
\]

where the sum terminates. For s=0 use J_0H(t)=integral_0^t H(v)dv. Direct substitution proves (D+s)J_sH=H, and therefore

\[
 \|J_st^k\|_*=W_k(s).
 \tag{3}
\]

For each stream frequency q the preliminary factored expression is

\[
 \widetilde B_{n,q}
 =-\tfrac12 J_{P_n+q}H_{q-1}
  -\tfrac12 J_{P_n-q}H_{q+1},
 \tag{4}
\]

where H_m is the density polynomial at frequency m and absent modes are zero. Away from resonance this is the stream coefficient. When P_n=-q, the canonical coefficient is instead

\[
 B_{n,q}(t)=\widetilde B_{n,q}(t)-\widetilde B_{n,q}(0).
 \tag{5}
\]

Removing one coefficient contracts the plain norm. Thus (3), the triangle inequality, and (5) give

\[
 N_n\le\tfrac12\sum_{\text{all incidences}}|a_{n,m,k}|W_k(s).
 \tag{6}
\]

No factor proportional to the number of frequencies is needed.

The entire constant in (5) must be removed. Removing only the integration constant of J_0 leaves an unwanted harmonic contribution from the other branch. For example, take delta=2/3, n=6, and the arbitrary input F_6=sin(3 theta). Then P_6=-2. Formula (4) gives an unwanted +(1/8)sin(2 theta) together with -(1/4)sin(4 theta). The canonical stream is exactly -(1/4)sin(4 theta). This is an input-level normalization example, not a generated stationary coefficient.

## The localized bound

Every nonzero s in an incidence has magnitude at least d_*. Indeed, b s=2b plus or minus an even integer multiple of b, minus na, and hence is a nonzero multiple of gcd(a,2b)=g.

Consider a summand excluded from E_n. If s=0, W_k(s)<=1. If k=0 and s is nonzero, W_0(s)<=d_*^(-1). Finally suppose k>=1 and |s|>=2k. Successive terms of the finite positive sum defining W_k have ratio at most k/|s|<=1/2. Consequently

\[
 W_k(s)\le2/|s|\le1/k\le1.
\]

Since d_*<=1, all excluded weights are at most d_*^(-1). Each density coefficient has at most two incidences, each carrying the factor 1/2. Splitting (6) into included and excluded incidences proves (2). The resonant normalization can only reduce the left side of (6).

## Closing the nonlinear recurrence under either hypothesis

The canonical degree bounds imply

\[
 \deg F_n\le\delta(n-2)/2\quad(n\ge2),\qquad
 \deg G_i\le i/q_0.
\]

The density equation is

\[
 ((n-1)(c-1)-cD)F_n
 =\sum_{i+j=n}\bigl[(P_i+D)G_i\,\partial_\theta F_j
                   -\partial_\theta G_i\,(1-j\delta+D)F_j\bigr].
\]

On the finite polynomial space the norm of D is at most its degree. The inverse has norm at most 2/((c-1)n). The angular frequencies are at most (L+1)n, and the plain polynomial Wiener product has norm at most the product of norms. Therefore, as proved in detail in topic88,

\[
 nM_n\le\kappa\sum_{i+j=n}ijN_iM_j,\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{2(L+1)}{c-1}\left(3-\delta+\frac2{q_0}\right).
 \tag{7}
\]

This restatement also identifies the exact assumptions behind the nonlinear estimate.

For hypothesis 1, (2) gives N_n<=(d_*^(-1)+B)M_n. Set x_n=nM_n. Then x_1=F and

\[
 x_n\le\kappa(d_*^{-1}+B)\sum_{i+j=n}x_ix_j.
\]

The Catalan recurrence gives

\[
 M_n\le\frac F n
 [4\kappa(d_*^{-1}+B)F]^{n-1}.
\]

This also bounds N_n geometrically.

For hypothesis 2, put e_n=nE_n and e(z)=sum_(n>=1)e_n z^n. The hypothesis makes e holomorphic near zero, with nonnegative coefficients and e(0)=0. Equations (2) and (7) imply

\[
 x_n\le\kappa d_*^{-1}\sum_{i+j=n}x_ix_j
       +\kappa\sum_{i+j=n}e_ix_j.
\]

Let X be the holomorphic solution near zero of

\[
 X=Fz+\kappa d_*^{-1}X^2+\kappa e(z)X,\qquad X(0)=0.
 \tag{8}
\]

The derivative with respect to X of the equation moved to the left is one at (z,X)=(0,0), so the analytic implicit-function theorem applies. Its coefficient recursion has nonnegative coefficients and dominates x_n by induction. Cauchy's estimate on any smaller disk bounds x_n geometrically. Equation (2) then bounds N_n geometrically. At delta=2/3 and F_n seeded by sin(theta), (8) becomes X=z+9X^2+6e(z)X.

The joint density/stream reconstruction theorem in topics41 and93-95 now gives geometric potential coefficients and convergence in a sufficiently distant exterior with every fixed naturally weighted derivative. The strict geometric margin also supplies local complex neighborhoods, so the sums are real analytic and satisfy the original stationary potential equation.

## Signed moments and their precise scope

For s nonzero define

\[
 \mu_s(H)=\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k k!h_k/s^{k+1},
 \qquad H(t)=\sum_k h_kt^k.
\]

At a nonresonant stream mode,

\[
 B_{n,q}(0)=-\tfrac12
 [\mu_{P_n+q}(H_{q-1})+\mu_{P_n-q}(H_{q+1})].
 \tag{9}
\]

At a resonant mode the canonical constant is zero, by (5). For every nonzero shift,

\[
 [t^\ell]J_sH=\mu_s(H^{(\ell)})/\ell!.
\]

For the zero shift the coefficient is zero at ell=0 and h_(ell-1)/ell at ell>=1. Combining these formulas with (5) evaluates all stream coefficients. In particular a lower bound on an individual moment contribution proves a stream lower bound only after controlling cancellation, and a constant-coefficient argument must use a nonresonant output mode.

## Dependencies, evidence and limits

The recurrence and canonical normalization are from topics01,10 and17. The nonlinear norm estimate is topic88; the geometric convergence implication is topics41 and93-95. This topic independently checks the proposed localized criterion in the theory part's closing_loop response, corrects its resonant constant formula, and sharpens its harmless-divisor constant. Exact Cartesian checks and finite interval measurements are stored in verification, with executable source copies in sources.

The definition E_n uses absolute values before adding branch contributions. It can therefore overestimate N_n severely. Failure of a geometric bound on E_n would not prove divergence of the generated stream. Finite measurements of E_n, however accurate, do not prove either all-order hypothesis.
