# Repeated small divisors near delta = 2/5

Status: PROVED through order20 after independent theory-part review, V444. The constants are independently checked by two exact implementations.

## Statement and notation

Set delta = 2/5 + epsilon, c = 1/(1-delta) and f_1 = sin(theta). For each fixed finite order, define the pure-power density and stream coefficients at nonresonant epsilon by the stationary recursion. They are meromorphic functions of epsilon near zero. Let R be the sin(theta) coefficient of f_10 at delta = 2/5, computed before the first stream inverse fails. Let sigma_k be the sin((2k-1)theta) coefficient of the density response at order 5(k+1) to a unit free stream harmonic sin((2k-2)theta) at order 5k, with the lower coefficients evaluated at delta = 2/5. Only k = 2 and k = 3 are used below.

If R, sigma_2 and sigma_3 are nonzero, the resonant coefficients obey

    [sin(2 theta)] g_10 = (R/20) epsilon^(-1) + O(1),
    [sin(4 theta)] g_15 = (R sigma_2/600) epsilon^(-2) + O(epsilon^(-1)),
    [sin(6 theta)] g_20 = (R sigma_2 sigma_3/24000) epsilon^(-3) + O(epsilon^(-2)).

Consequently the phrase a factor 1/epsilon at each resonant inverse is correct, while a common overall 1/epsilon size for all later coefficients is not. At delta = 2/5 the successive stream resonances are 10,15,20,..., not only the multiples of 10. This statement concerns a fixed leading profile near an incompatible rational; it does not concern the tuned compatible profiles.

## Local form of the stream inverse

Write p_n = 1-n delta and P_n = p_n+1. The coefficient of sin(2j theta) in g_n is

    ((P_n-2j)a_(n,j-1) + (P_n+2j)a_(n,j)) / (2((2j)^2-P_n^2)).

At a resonance n = 5k, k >= 2, j = k-1 and P_n = -2j-n epsilon. Thus

    2((2j)^2-P_n^2) = -8jn epsilon - 2n^2 epsilon^2.

If the coefficient a_(n,j-1) has leading term A epsilon^(-d), and all density coefficients have pole order at most d, then the stream's resonant coefficient has leading term

    A/(2n) epsilon^(-d-1).

The other numerator term has the additional factor P_n+2j = -n epsilon and cannot affect this leading term. Nonresonant stream coordinates do not increase the pole order.

## Propagation to the next two resonances

All densities through order 10 and streams through order 9 are analytic at epsilon = 0. At order 10 the preceding inverse calculation gives the first formula.

The first singular stream is g_10, and the first singular density is f_11. A nonlinear product with two singular factors therefore has total order at least 10+11 = 21. Through order 20 every singular contribution is consequently linear in one singular factor. Multiplication by analytic parameter-dependent coefficients and division by the nonzero density divisor preserve this pole-order statement.

At orders 11 through 15, the leading simple-pole density contribution is exactly R/20 times the response to the unit harmonic at order 10. This follows by collecting the coefficient of epsilon^(-1) in the stationary recursion. The lower fields occurring in the response have orders at most 5; none has encountered a resonance. The obstruction at order 15 therefore has leading coefficient R sigma_2/20. Applying the stream inverse adds its factor 1/(30 epsilon), giving the second formula.

At orders 16 through 20, the leading double-pole density contribution is generated by the leading harmonic of g_15. The older simple-pole terms are lower order, and there is still no product of two singular factors. Collecting epsilon^(-2) thus gives R sigma_2/600 times the response to a unit harmonic at order 15. The order-20 obstruction has leading coefficient R sigma_2 sigma_3/600; the stream inverse adds 1/(40 epsilon), giving the third formula.

These are finite-order Laurent expansions of rational functions. The O terms are local meromorphic remainder statements and do not assume an infinite convergent expansion.

## Exact finite verification plan

Recompute the density through order 10 in exact rational signed-Laurent Cartesian arithmetic and extract R. Independently propagate the two five-step responses and extract sigma_2 and sigma_3. Check all sine symmetries and all nonresonant Poisson equations. Compare R with the already retained exact obstruction polynomial at b = 0. Record the three nonzero rational values and the resulting leading pole coefficients with hashes. The general argument above supplies the analytic interpretation; a small-epsilon numerical fit alone would not prove the pole orders.
