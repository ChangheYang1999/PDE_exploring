                    + sum_(j odd, 5 <= j <= 19) t_j sin(j theta).

Let O9(b) be the sin(theta) coefficient of f_9 generated by the pure nonresonant recursion through the first compatibility. Then O9 is a rational polynomial of degree exactly9 and has at least one real root. At each such root, the first positive stream resonance at order9, frequency2, is compatible. Multiplication by a_1 restores the original first and all higher harmonics, changing only the coefficient of sin(3 theta).

This statement fixes the higher coefficients exactly. It does not claim that Candidate 007's original sin(3 theta) coefficient already satisfies the compatibility or that any resulting profile has all later responses nonzero.

## Proof

At delta = 4/9, the first positive stream resonance occurs at order9. Before that order all inverses in the S1 sine sector have nonzero rational denominators. Each f_n is a homogeneous polynomial of degree n in the leading coefficients: f_1 has degree1, the inverse is linear, and every ordered product with i+j=n has degree n. Therefore O9 is polynomial of degree at most9 in b.

The coefficient of b^9 comes exclusively from nine copies of the sin(3 theta) input. It is independent of the fixed coefficient of sin(theta) and of all higher harmonics. It therefore equals the nonzero b^9 coefficient of the two-mode obstruction in146. This proves degree exactly9. A real polynomial of odd degree has opposite signs as b tends to the two infinities, so it has a real root. The vanishing of O9 is precisely the first compatibility because at P_9 = -2 the resonant forcing is -2 times that density coefficient.

For a root b, homogeneity shows that multiplying the normalized input by a_1 multiplies the order9 obstruction by a_1^9 and hence preserves its zero. The first and higher coefficients then equal a_j exactly. The change in the original leading profile has Fourier l1 norm |a_1 b-a_3|.
