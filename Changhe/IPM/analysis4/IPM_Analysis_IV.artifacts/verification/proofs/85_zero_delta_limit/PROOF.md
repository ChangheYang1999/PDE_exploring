# An exact zero-delta limit for the sin(3 theta) profile

Status: PROVED fixed-order Laurent limit, independently reviewed in V312. This is not a uniform endpoint asymptotic for delta=4/p, n=p.

## Exact statement

Let f_n(delta,theta) be the pure stationary recursion generated by
f_1=sin(3 theta), with c=(1-delta)^(-1), and let g_n be its Poisson stream coefficient. At every fixed order n, these are rational functions of delta with finite angular support. Their Laurent expansions at delta=0 satisfy

\[
 f_n=\delta^{-2n+2}H_n+O(\delta^{-2n+3}),\qquad
 g_n=\delta^{-2n+1}\frac{h_{n,3}}{2n}\sin(2\theta)
       +O(\delta^{-2n+2}),
 \tag{1}
\]

where h_(n,m) denotes the sine-frequency m coefficient of H_n. Big O is coefficientwise at fixed n and is allowed to have constants depending on n. In particular, the leading stream term may vanish when h_(n,3)=0.

The formal generating function H(x,theta)=sum_(n>=1) H_n(theta)x^(n-1) is given near x=0 by

\[
 H(x,\theta)=e^u\sin\theta\left[-1+
 \frac{4\cos^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta+e^{4u}\sin^2\theta}\right],
 \qquad u=\operatorname{arsinh}x.
 \tag{2}
\]

Its frequency3 and frequency1 coefficients are

\[
 [\sin(3\theta)]H=(1+x^2)^{-3/2},
 \tag{3}
\]

\[
 [\sin\theta]H=-\frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}
                -\frac{x(2+x^2)}{1+x^2}.
 \tag{4}
\]

Thus, for every odd n=2k+1>=3,

\[
 \lim_{\delta\to0}\delta^{2n-2}[\sin\theta]f_n(\delta)
 =(-1)^k\frac{\binom{2k-2}{k-1}}{4^{k-1}}\ne0.
 \tag{5}
\]

The first-harmonic coefficient has a pole of exactly order2n-2 at delta=0 for every such n. This describes an endpoint singularity of the finite-order rational recursion. It does not determine the size of the first-resonance compatibility coefficient at delta=4/n when n also tends to infinity.

## Fixed-order Laurent induction

The factored pure Poisson formula at stream frequency m is

\[
 b_{n,m}=-\frac{a_{n,m-1}}{2(P_n+m)}
          +\frac{a_{n,m+1}}{2(m-P_n)},\qquad P_n=2-n\delta.
\]

At delta=0, its only singular denominator is m-P_n=n delta for m=2. Therefore, assuming the density bound in(1), its leading stream term is exactly h_(n,3) sin(2theta)/(2n), with the stated delta power. Every other stream channel has one less possible pole. The stationary inverse is
(1-delta)/[(n-1)delta]. With p_j tending to1 and P_i tending to2, induction in the quadratic stationary recurrence gives

\[
 (n-1)H_n=
 \sum_{i+j=n}\frac{h_{i,3}}{i}
 \left(\sin(2\theta)\,\partial_\theta H_j
                         -\cos(2\theta)H_j\right).
 \tag{6}
\]

The powers are delta^(-2i+1) from g_i, delta^(-2j+2) from f_j, and one further inverse delta, totaling delta^(-2n+2). The leading profile H_1=sin(3theta) starts the induction. This proves existence of the fixed-order Laurent limit and its recursion.

## Solving the leading recursion

Set T=sin(2theta)partial_theta-cos(2theta) and
A(x)=sum_(i>=1) h_(i,3)x^i/i. Summing(6) gives

\[
 xH_x=A(x)TH,\qquad A'(x)=[\sin(3\theta)]H,
 \qquad H(0,\theta)=\sin(3\theta).
 \tag{7}
\]

Write u(0)=0 and xu'(x)=A(x). Then H=exp(uT)sin(3theta). To compute the operator flow, put w=tan(theta). Away from cos(theta)=0,
T=2w partial_w-(1-w^2)/(1+w^2). Its characteristic formula gives

\[
 e^{uT}f(\theta)
 =e^{-u}\sqrt{\frac{1+e^{4u}w^2}{1+w^2}}
       f(\arctan(e^{2u}w)).
\]

For f=sin(3theta), simplification gives precisely the right side of(2), with u still undetermined. This final expression extends across cos(theta)=0 and is analytic in u near0 uniformly on the real circle, so the temporary chart causes no loss.

For completeness, its needed Fourier coefficients can be obtained without guessing. Put v=tanh u. The identity

\[
 \frac1{\cosh(2u)-\sinh(2u)\cos(2\theta)}
 =1+2\sum_{j\ge1}v^j\cos(2j\theta)
 \tag{8}
\]

holds for u sufficiently small; it is the geometric-series identity with
v=tanh u. Formula(2) becomes

\[
 e^u\left[-\sin\theta+
 e^{-2u}(\sin\theta+\sin(3\theta))
 \left(1+2\sum_{j\ge1}v^j\cos(2j\theta)\right)\right].
\]

Its frequency3 coefficient is
 e^(-u)(1+v-v^2-v^3)=sech^3 u,
and its frequency1 coefficient is e^(-u)(1-v^2)-e^u.
Equation(7) therefore reduces to

\[
 (xu')'=\operatorname{sech}^3u,\qquad u(0)=0,\quad u'(0)=1.
 \tag{9}
\]

There is a unique formal power-series solution: at degree k-1 the new coefficient of x^k in u appears on the left with factor k^2, while the right depends only on lower coefficients. The series u=arsinh x satisfies(9), because
(x/sqrt(1+x^2))'=(1+x^2)^(-3/2)=sech^3(arsinh x).
This proves(2) and(3). Substituting e^u=sqrt(1+x^2)+x and e^(-u)=sqrt(1+x^2)-x in the frequency1 expression gives(4). Finally the binomial expansion of (1+x^2)^(-1/2) gives(5).

## Scope and endpoint interpretation

At an odd prime p and delta=4/p, the first compatibility coefficient is the frequency1 coefficient of f_p. Formula(5) shows that this rational function has an exact pole of order2p-2 at the other endpoint delta=0, and its leading Laurent coefficient is only polynomially small in p. This explains why a uniform compact-center bound cannot simply be continued to delta=0.

However, the remainder in(1) has no uniform-in-n estimate here. Substituting delta=4/p into a fixed-order asymptotic and then sending p to infinity would be an invalid exchange of limits. In particular, this proof does not establish |kappa_(p,4)| >= p^((1+eta)p), does not refute the unlocalized infinite divergence statement by itself, and does not prove convergence or divergence of the rational logarithmic series.

## Dependencies and verification

Only the independently derived pure recurrence, its factored Poisson inverse, formal power-series algebra and the geometric Fourier identity are used. Exact finite checks evaluate(6) by Cartesian Laurent differentiation, then compare its frequency1 and frequency3 coefficients with(3)-(4). A separate independent review is required before the complete theorem is marked PROVED.
