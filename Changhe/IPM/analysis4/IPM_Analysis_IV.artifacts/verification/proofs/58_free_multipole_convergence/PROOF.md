# Convergence with prescribed harmonic stream multipoles

Status: CONFIRMED after the independent theory-part review at04:54Z and the verifier closure at2026-10-31T04:56:42Z. Written on 2026-10-31. The statement concerns a local exterior stationary solution. Prescribing multipoles also generates density corrections; it does not leave the exterior density fixed.

## Statement

Fix an irrational delta in (0,1), let c=1/(1-delta), gamma=1-delta, and assume that alpha=delta/2 has finite Brjuno sum

    Bcal = sum_(j>=0) log(q_(j+1))/q_j,

where q_j are its continued-fraction denominators and q_0=1. Let f be a nonzero real S1 sine polynomial with largest frequency L, put B=L+1 and F=||f||_A0. Prescribe real numbers h_k, k>=2, with at most geometric growth. Thus for some Y_0>0,

    H(Y_0) = sum_(k>=2) k |h_k| Y_0^k < infinity.

Then there are uniquely defined real sine polynomials F_(n,k), G_(n,k), n>=1, k>=0, with F_(1,0)=f, such that the series

    rho = sum_(n>=1,k>=0) r^(1-n delta-2k) F_(n,k)(theta),

    psi = sum_(n>=1,k>=0) r^(2-n delta-2k) G_(n,k)(theta)
           + sum_(k>=2) h_k r^(2-2k) sin((2k-2)theta)

converge on some r>=R with all spatial derivatives after their leading radial weights are removed. Their sums satisfy

    -Delta psi = partial_x rho,
    (cX + (-psi_y,psi_x)) dot grad rho - rho = 0.

They come from one even potential

    chi = sum_(n>=1,k>=0) r^(4-n delta-2k) C_(n,k)(theta)
          - h_2 log r
          + sum_(k>=3) h_k r^(4-2k) cos((2k-4)theta)
                          / ((2k-4)(2k-3)),

whose appropriately weighted derivative series also converge absolutely and uniformly. In particular rho=-partial_y Delta chi and psi=chi_xy. The sums are real analytic in the exterior and rho=r^gamma(f+o(1)).

The density and generated stream, with their leading powers removed, are analytic in two independent variables x=r^(-delta), y=r^(-2) near (0,0). The first independent harmonic stream term needs the displayed logarithmic potential. There are no density or stream logarithms at irrational delta.

An explicit sufficient polydisc is given below. It depends on the Brjuno sum, the leading norm and bandwidth, and the generating function of the prescribed multipoles. This theorem makes no claim that arbitrary prescribed multipoles match a given interior density or extend to a whole-plane stationary solution.

## 1. Two-index recursion

For I=(n,k), put mu_I=n delta+2k, p_I=1-mu_I, P_I=2-mu_I and w_I=n+k. Density indices always have n>=1. Define the independent stream entries

    G_(0,k)=h_k sin((2k-2)theta), k>=2.

No density entries with n=0 are introduced. For every density index I, determine G_I by

    [-(partial_theta)^2-P_I^2]G_I
      = p_I cos(theta)F_I - sin(theta)F_I'.

In physical sine coefficients a_m at odd frequency m, its even-frequency coefficients are

    b_(2j) = -a_(2j-1)/(2(2j+2-mu_I))
              +a_(2j+1)/(2(2j-2+mu_I)).                         (1)

All denominators are nonzero because n>=1 and delta is irrational. Except at I=(1,0), set

    c(mu_I-delta) F_I
       = sum_(A+D=I) [P_A G_A F_D' - p_D G_A' F_D],             (2)

where D is a density index and A is either a density index or an independent stream index (0,k), k>=2. The denominator is positive:

    c(mu_I-delta)=c((n-1)delta+2k) >= (c-1)(w_I-1)>0.

Every child has smaller w than its parent, so (1)-(2) recursively determine all coefficients by increasing w. At fixed w only finitely many indices and products occur. In particular F_(n,1)=G_(n,1)=0: no input carries k=1.

The derivation is directly from homogeneous Cartesian degrees. The velocity generated by a stream term of degree P_A has degree P_A-1. Multiplying by a density gradient of degree p_D-1 gives degree P_A+p_D-2=1-mu_(A+D). The linear stationary coefficient is cp_I-1=-c(mu_I-delta). The signs in (2) follow from u=(-psi_y,psi_x).

Support induction gives

    deg F_(n,k) <= Bn+2k-1,
    deg G_(n,k) <= Bn+2k,
    deg C_(n,k) <= Bn+2k-2.                                    (3)

For the independent stream index (0,k), its frequency 2k-2 is also below Bw_(0,k). Formula (1) raises density bandwidth by at most one; a product in (2) adds the two bandwidths. These facts prove (3).

For example, when f=sin(theta), the first correction produced by h_2 is exactly

    F_(1,2) = -h_2 [delta sin(theta)+(1+gamma)sin(3theta)]/(4c).

Thus an independently added harmonic velocity requires a density correction even though its own potential has zero density. This coefficient is checked by the separate Cartesian implementation.

## 2. The scalar estimate with the correct placement of divisors

Put d_n=dist(n delta,2Z), so 0<d_n<=1, and

    C_P=(1+delta^(-1))/2,    K=14B/(c-1).

Formula (1) implies, for every density index I=(n,k),

    ||G_I||_A0 <= C_P ||F_I||_A0/d_n.                           (4)

Indeed the first denominator is at least 2d_n in modulus, and the second is at least 2n delta. Neither estimate depends on k.

Let Z_I=w_I||F_I||_A0 and U_I=w_I||G_I||_A0. For an independent stream entry let U_(0,k)=k|h_k|. Since mu_I<=2w_I,

    |P_A| <= 4w_A,       |p_D| <= 3w_D,
    deg G_A <= Bw_A,     deg F_D <= Bw_D.

The Wiener algebra inequality and (2) therefore give

    Z_I <= K sum_(A+D=I) U_A Z_D.                              (5)

Here the factor 14 is 7 from the two products, multiplied by w_I/(w_I-1)<=2. For a generated stream, substitute U_A<=C_P Z_A/d_(n_A). For an independent stream, use the prescribed U_(0,k).

The divisor in (5) belongs only to a generated velocity child. Adding a divisor at every density node would give a different and unnecessarily large majorant, especially along repeated interactions with independent multipoles.

## 3. A marked-tree counting lemma

Expand (5) into plane full binary trees. Each internal node is a density interaction; its right child is a density subtree, and its left child is either a density subtree used as a generated stream or one independent multipole. A density leaf is the leading input f and has mass1. A multipole leaf has density mass0 and index k>=2. The density mass n_v of a subtree counts only its leading-density leaves.

Mark the root of each left child that is a generated density subtree. A factor 1/d_(n_v) occurs exactly at these marked nodes. Every marked node has positive density mass. Moreover, if one marked node is a proper ancestor of another, its density mass is strictly larger. To see this, the descendant marked node is the left child of an interaction whose right density child has at least one leading-density leaf. Those right-child leaves lie below the ancestor and outside the marked descendant.

There are exactly n-1 marked nodes in a density tree with n leading-density leaves. Contract the interactions whose left child is an independent multipole. The remaining tree has n leaves and n-1 binary interactions, each corresponding to one mark. This identity is not needed in the strongest form, but gives a convenient prefactor.

The same strict ancestry property holds after adding one further mark at the root of the whole tree. This extra mark will bound Z_I/d_n and U_I. There are then n marked nodes.

For each continued-fraction denominator q_j, define

    S_j={m>=1: ||m alpha|| < 1/(4q_j)}.

The arithmetic lemma proved in topic13 says that every element of S_j is at least q_j, and two distinct elements differ by at least q_j. In the forest of marked nodes whose masses belong to S_j, assign to each node the density leaves not lying below a maximal marked proper descendant. Nodes with zero marked children use at least q_j such leaves; nodes with one marked child also use at least q_j, by the strict ancestry property and arithmetic separation. These assigned leaf sets are disjoint. A finite rooted forest has no more branching vertices than leaves. Consequently the number N_j of these marks satisfies

    N_j <= 2n/q_j.

This argument counts density leaves only. Arbitrarily many zero-density-mass multipoles do not alter it. The product estimate from topic13 now applies to this marked forest:

    product_(marked v) 1/d_(n_v) <= 2^M exp(2n Bcal),            (6)

where M is its number of marks, either n-1 or n. The proof partitions masses into S_j minus S_(j+1) and sums N_j log(q_(j+1)), exactly as in the already verified Brjuno argument.

## 4. A convergent positive majorant

Let

    F_* = 2 C_P exp(2 Bcal) F,
    H(y) = sum_(k>=2) k|h_k| y^k,

and define the analytic branch

    W(x,y) = [1-sqrt(1-4K(F_* x+H(y)))]/(2K).                   (7)

Its power series has nonnegative coefficients and satisfies

    W = F_* x+H(y)+K W^2.

A density tree with n density leaves and s multipole leaves has n+s-1 interactions. Its weight in (5), after (4), is bounded by

    K^(n+s-1) C_P^(n-1)
      product_(marks) d_(n_v)^(-1) F^n product_(multipoles k) k|h_k|.

Using (6), this is no larger than the weight of the same tree in (7). The majorant W allows additional tree shapes, including ones that do not describe a density interaction; their coefficients are nonnegative and only enlarge the bound.

Adding a root divisor gives n marks and at most n factors C_P, still bounded by F_*^n after (6). Hence for every n>=1,k>=0,

    w_I||F_I||_A0 <= [x^n y^k]W,
    w_I||F_I||_A0/d_n <= [x^n y^k]W,
    w_I||G_I||_A0 <= [x^n y^k]W.                               (8)

The extra-root estimate includes the leading input. No independent harmonic leaf is assigned an inverse divisor d_0.

Choose 0<Y<Y_0 so that H(Y)<1/(16K), and put

    X = 1/(16K F_*).

Then F_*X+H(Y)<1/(8K), and W is analytic on a neighborhood of the closed positive polydisc |x|<=X, |y|<=Y. In particular W(X,Y)<infinity. Equation (8) proves absolute summability of all three coefficient arrays there.

## 5. Potential reconstruction with a uniform constant

For a density index I let mu=mu_I and q=4-mu. Irrationality ensures that mu is not an even integer. The finite potential inverse in topic01 is therefore invertible, and its stream must equal G_I by uniqueness of the nonresonant Poisson inverse.

For mu>=3, set a=(mu-2)/2>=1/2. The downward triangular proof of the potential bound in topic13 applies with n delta replaced by mu. Every factor measuring distance from mu to an even integer is at least d_n because 2k is even. It gives

    ||C_I||_A0 <= (3/|4-mu|+1) ||F_I||_A0/d_n.                 (9)

Only finitely many pairs n>=1,k>=0 have mu<3 or |4-mu|<1. Each corresponding finite inverse has finite norm, and none of the exceptional denominators vanishes. Thus there is a finite constant C_chi, depending only on delta and B, such that

    ||C_I||_A0 <= C_chi ||F_I||_A0/d_n

at every density index. Together with (8) this proves potential convergence on the same polydisc.

The independent stream h_2 r^(-2)sin(2theta) is generated by -h_2 log r, since partial_xy log r=-r^(-2)sin(2theta). For k>=3 the displayed harmonic potential is checked by differentiating Re(z^(-(2k-4))). All these potentials have zero exterior density. Their convergence with derivatives follows from H(Y)<infinity. These are the same multipole-to-potential identities independently derived in topic08.

## 6. Exterior convergence and stationarity

Choose R>1 with

    R^(-delta)<X,     R^(-2)<Y.

The weighted density and generated stream terms are respectively

    r^(-gamma) rho_I = x^(n-1)y^k F_I,
    r^(-gamma-1) psi_I = x^(n-1)y^k G_I.

The potential has the analogous weight r^(-gamma-3). Their coefficient norm sums at r>=R are bounded by (8)-(9) evaluated at x=R^(-delta), y=R^(-2), divided by x. Since every n>=1, termwise radial weights decrease for r>=R, so this division is evaluated at R rather than incorrectly bounded by 1/x over an unbounded domain.

Every fixed Cartesian derivative introduces a polynomial in w_I of its order and the corresponding inverse power of r, by the radial degrees and bandwidth (3). A strict margin from (X,Y) absorbs all these polynomial factors. For the independent harmonic terms the same statement follows from the geometric margin in y; the single log r potential and its derivatives satisfy the required weighted bounds for R>1.

A small positive angular strip is available by choosing sigma>0 with

    e^(B sigma) R^(-delta)<X,   e^(2 sigma) R^(-2)<Y.

The bandwidth estimate then proves absolute analytic Fourier convergence. It also gives real analyticity in the physical exterior variables. The only explicit logarithm, log r, is real analytic away from r=0.

All potential identities pass to the sums. The stationary quadratic series converges absolutely with the required weights. Regrouping it by (n,k) is valid; each grouped coefficient vanishes by (2). Distinct pairs give distinct exponents because delta is irrational. The density leading limit is f, since all other weighted terms tend to zero and the series is uniformly dominated.

The result establishes a local exterior solution with the prescribed independent multipoles. The cutoff-potential construction from topic20 can give a smooth whole-plane canonical extension whose stationary residual is compactly supported. It does not show that this residual vanishes or that a specified interior density realizes these multipoles.
