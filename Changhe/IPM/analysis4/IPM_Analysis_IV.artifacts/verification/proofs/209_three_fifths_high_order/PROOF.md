## Independent identities

For z=exp(i theta), let F(z)=sum_m a_m(z^m-z^(-m))/2 represent the physical sine polynomial -iF. Define Cartesian angular operators on a homogeneous term of radial degree q by

    X_q z^m = ((q+m)z^(m-1)+(q-m)z^(m+1))/2,
    Y_q z^m = ((q+m)z^(m-1)-(q-m)z^(m+1))/2.

They follow by substituting the exponential formulas for sine and cosine in partial_x=cos(theta)partial_r-sin(theta)partial_theta/r and partial_y=sin(theta)partial_r+cos(theta)partial_theta/r. The physical y derivative is iY_q, and each derivative lowers q by one.

Let F_n and G_n be the Laurent representatives of density and stream, of degrees p_n and p_n+1. The Poisson residual is

    -X_(p_n)X_(p_n+1)G_n + Y_(p_n)Y_(p_n+1)G_n - X_(p_n)F_n.

The density residual is

    sum_(i+j=n) [X_(p_i+1)G_i Y_(p_j)F_j
                 -Y_(p_i+1)G_i X_(p_j)F_j]
                 - 3(n-1)F_n/2.

Both must vanish coefficient by coefficient. The products include every ordered pair once. This calculation uses no Fourier product routine from the numerical producer.
