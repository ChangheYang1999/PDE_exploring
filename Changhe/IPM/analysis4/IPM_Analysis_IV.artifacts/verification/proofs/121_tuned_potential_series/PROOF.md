# A convergent potential series for the tuned rational solution

Status: PROVED. The theory part independently confirmed the proof at 2026-10-31T09:56Z; the verifier records that review in V254.

## Statement

Let c=3, p_n=1-2n/3, P_n=p_n+1 and S_n=p_n+3=4-2n/3. Suppose pure periodic S1 density and stream coefficients f_n,g_n satisfy -Delta(r^(P_n)g_n)=partial_x(r^(p_n)f_n) at every order. Assume their sine frequencies are bounded by Bn-1 and Bn, respectively, for a fixed even B>=2, and

    ||f_n||_1+||g_n||_1 <= C A^n.

The stream is odd under each coordinate reflection, so it contains only positive even sine frequencies. In particular the compatibility coefficient of f_(3k) at frequency2k-3 is zero for k>=2.

There are finite even-cosine polynomials C_n, and fixed real constants alpha,beta,lambda, such that

    chi(r,theta)=sum_(n>=1) r^(S_n) C_n(theta)
             +r^2 log(r)[alpha+beta cos(2theta)]+lambda log(r),     (1)

with deg C_n<=Bn-2 and ||C_n||_1<=C' A^n, after increasing C'. The logarithmic terms have orders3and6 only. For every R>A^(3/2), the series and each Cartesian derivative converge absolutely and uniformly with the potential weight r^(h-10/3), for derivative order h. The unweighted tails also converge uniformly after finitely many growing terms are removed. The sum is even in both coordinates and satisfies

    chi_xy=psi,  -partial_y Delta chi=rho,
    (-chi_xyy,chi_xxy)=(-psi_y,psi_x).

Thus any stationary density/stream solution under these hypotheses solves the original potential equation by termwise differentiation of (1). This applies to TheoremT and its two explicit examples, on the same strict exterior radius. The weighted generating function in z has only the two indicated logarithmic corrections and is not claimed holomorphic at z=0.

## Density primitive and potential inversion at large orders

Fix n>=12 and put x=(2n/3-2)/2=n/3-1>=3. Let K=(Bn-2)/2. Write the density coefficients a_m at frequencies2m+1, for0<=m<=K. Seek

    Delta chi_n=r^(-2x) sum_(m=0)^K d_m cos(2m theta).

The equation -partial_y Delta chi_n=r^(p_n)f_n is exactly

    a_0=2x d_0+(1-x)d_1,
    a_m=(m+x)d_m+(m+1-x)d_(m+1),  1<=m<=K,

with d_(K+1)=0. Descending substitution is always possible because the diagonal factors are positive. For m>=1,

    |m+1-x|/(m+x)<=1,

and |1-x|/(2x)<=1. Consequently |d_m|<=x^(-1)sum_(j>=m)|a_j|, also for m0. Summation gives

    ||d||_1 <= (K+1)/x ||f_n||_1
             = Bn/(2n/3-2) ||f_n||_1 <=2B||f_n||_1.              (2)

We now solve Delta chi_n=r^(S_n-2)d. On cosine mode2m the multiplier is S_n^2-4m^2. If n is not a multiple of3 it never vanishes. If n=3k>=12, write j=k-1=x, so S_n=-2(j-1). The only zero multiplier is m=j-1. The density relation there is

    a_(j-1)=(2j-1)d_(j-1).

Compatibility of the given pure stream gives a_(j-1)=0, hence d_(j-1)=0. Set the corresponding initial potential coefficient to zero and divide all other d_m by their nonzero Laplace multipliers. Call this potential C_n^0.

For every nonzero multiplier, ||S_n|-2m|>=2/3 and |S_n|+2m>=|S_n|>=n/3. Thus

    |S_n^2-4m^2| >=2n/9,
    ||C_n^0||_1 <=(9B/n)||f_n||_1.                              (3)

## Matching the prescribed harmonic stream

The stream g_n^0 generated by C_n^0 satisfies the same Poisson equation as g_n. At a nonresonant order, the periodic even-sine Poisson kernel is zero, so g_n^0=g_n.

At n=3k>=12, the difference is a multiple of sin(qtheta), q=2k-2, with prescribed whole coefficient H_n minus the generated coefficient H_n^0. Put ell=q-2=2k-4=-S_n. The harmonic potential

    r^(-ell) cos(ell theta)

has zero density and stream ell(ell+1) r^(-q)sin(qtheta). Therefore add

    (H_n-H_n^0)/[ell(ell+1)] cos(ell theta)

to C_n^0. This matches the entire stream coefficient, not just one Poisson branch, while preserving the density.

A Cartesian derivative of a homogeneous coefficient of radial exponent s and frequency at most J has l1 norm at most (|s|+J) times the original norm and increases frequency by at most one. Two derivatives therefore give

    ||g_n^0||_1 <= (B+1)^2 n^2 ||C_n^0||_1,  n>=12.

Indeed |S_n|<=2n/3, J<=Bn-2, and after the first derivative the sum |s|+J increases by at most two. Also ell>=n/3, so ell(ell+1)>=n^2/9. We obtain

    ||C_n||_1 <= [1+9(B+1)^2]||C_n^0||_1+9||g_n||_1/n^2
       <= 9B[1+9(B+1)^2]||f_n||_1/n+9||g_n||_1/n^2.             (4)

This is a polynomially bounded inverse and hence preserves the geometric base A. The added frequency ell is below Bn-2.

## The finitely many small orders

At a nonexceptional n<12, the finite density-to-potential matrix is invertible as in topic01. At n9, the same resonant argument applies with x2 and ell2; all denominators used are nonzero, so it gives a finite linear map from the compatible density and prescribed harmonic stream.

At n3, S_n=2. Use the pure columns cos(ell theta) with ell>=4 to remove all density modes at least5 by descending substitution. The remaining density is a sin(theta)+b sin(3theta). The exact identities

    chi=r^2 log r:
        rho=-4r^(-1)sin(theta), psi=sin(2theta),
    chi=r^2 log r cos(2theta):
        rho=4r^(-1)[sin(theta)+sin(3theta)], psi=-(1/2)sin(4theta)

supply this residual with alpha=(b-a)/4 and beta=b/4. Neither identity has logarithms in density or stream. The stream then agrees with the prescribed g3 because P3=0 has no positive even-sine kernel. The pure potential columns1andcos(2theta) at this degree are gauges and may be set to zero.

At n6, S_n=0. Compatibility gives a_0=0. The pure potential columns at frequencies2,4,... cover exactly the remaining density modes, and the constant potential column can be set to zero. They generate a stream coefficient H6^0 at the resonant frequency2. Since

    Delta log r=0,  partial_x partial_y log r=-r^(-2)sin(2theta),

choose lambda=H6^0-H6 to obtain the prescribed whole coefficient H6. This correction produces no density. There are no other exceptional logarithms: at every later resonance the required harmonic stream comes from the pure harmonic potential already constructed.

All maps in this finite collection are linear on finite-dimensional coefficient spaces. The given finite coefficients therefore have finite potential norms and finite alpha,beta,lambda. Enlarging C' covers them as well as (4).

## Convergence and the original potential equation

The potential exponent is S_n=10/3-(2/3)(n-1), and its bandwidth is linear in n. Every fixed Cartesian derivative costs a polynomial in n and lowers the radial exponent by its order. After the weight r^(h-10/3), the series is dominated by a polynomial times (A R^(-2/3))^n. This converges for R>A^(3/2), and remains convergent in a sufficiently small angular strip. The two logarithmic corrections are finite smooth terms; after the same weights all their derivatives are bounded on r>=R because their radial orders are strictly below10/3.

Consequently at least four derivatives can be taken termwise, recovering density, velocity and the density gradient. Since each coefficient has the prescribed common density and stream, the sums have them too. If these satisfy stationary transport, substitution into the potential formulation is exact. The logarithm is real analytic at every r>0, so the potential is locally real analytic in the exterior as well.

Dependencies: the elementary Cartesian and triangular identities in topic01, the convergence hypotheses in the statement, and finite-dimensional inversion at the specified small orders. No all-order Diophantine estimate, global extension, or unidentified smooth primitive is used.
