# Quantitative divergence for almost every first sine amplitude

Status: CONFIRMED after independent theory-part review at 2026-10-31T04:36Z and verifier recheck. This strengthens the input-data conclusion of the arithmetic divergence theorem. It does not establish necessity of the Brjuno condition.

## Statement

Let h be a fixed real S1-symmetric angular function in a Wiener strip space A_sigma, sigma>0, with zero sin(theta) amplitude. Thus h is a convergent sum of odd sine modes of frequency at least3, with exponentially summable amplitudes. For a real number x set

\[
 f_1^x(\theta)=x\sin\theta+h(\theta).
\]

There is a set E_h of real numbers with Hausdorff dimension zero such that the following holds simultaneously for every x outside E_h and every irrational delta in (0,1): if there exist tau>pi^2/3 and infinitely many primes p and even integers a, 4<=a<p, satisfying

\[
 0<|\delta-a/p|<p^{-\tau p^2},
 \tag{1}
\]

then the pure density series generated by f_1^x has radius zero in the uniform angular norm, and hence also in every angular Wiener norm in which its coefficients are defined.

The set E_h is independent of delta and tau. In particular, it has Lebesgue measure zero. The result applies to finite real sine profiles h without an algebraicity condition on their coefficients. It does not assign a measure to an infinite-dimensional function space.

## 1. A polynomial in the first amplitude

At the prime center delta_0=a/p, all stream inverses below order p are nonresonant. The density coefficient f_p therefore exists and is a homogeneous polynomial of degree p in its leading input. This assertion follows by induction from the bilinear stationary forcing and the input-independent linear Poisson and stationary inverses. For analytic inputs, the finite number of angular derivatives can be taken on a smaller strip, so each coefficient and each selected Fourier amplitude is well defined.

Consequently

\[
 P_{p,a}(x)=[\sin((a-3)\theta)]f_p[f_1^x](a/p)
\]

is a real polynomial in x of degree at most p. Its coefficient of x^p is exactly the compatibility coefficient kappa^0_(p,a) generated by sin(theta) alone. The confirmed prime theorem makes this coefficient nonzero for every admissible p,a. Hence the polynomial has degree exactly p.

The rational denominator estimate in topic46, applied only to the single-sine input, gives the uniform estimate

\[
 |\kappa^0_{p,a}|\ge p^{-(C_0+\eta)p^2},\qquad C_0=\pi^2/3,
 \tag{2}
\]

for every eta>0 and all sufficiently large admissible p, uniformly in a. The higher profile h does not enter this leading coefficient.

## 2. Small-value sets have dimension zero

Factor a real polynomial P of degree p over the complex numbers as b product_(j=1)^p (x-z_j). If |P(x)|<|b|r^p at a real x, at least one factor obeys |x-z_j|<r. Thus the real small-value set is covered by at most p intervals, each of radius r, centered at the real parts of the roots. This conclusion requires no bound on the roots or on the other coefficients.

Fix eta>0. By (2), for sufficiently large p the set

\[
 U_{p,a,\eta}=\{x\in\mathbb R:
 |P_{p,a}(x)|<p^{-(C_0+2\eta)p^2}\}
\]

is covered by at most p intervals of radius p^(-eta p). There are fewer than p admissible numerators. The limsup over all admissible pairs p,a is therefore covered, for every cutoff M, by at most p^2 such intervals at each p>M. For every s>0 their total s-power diameter sum is bounded by

\[
 \sum_{p>M}p^2\big(2p^{-\eta p}\big)^s,
 \tag{3}
\]

where summing over all integers instead of primes only enlarges the bound. This tends to zero as M tends to infinity, and the covering diameters tend uniformly to zero. By the definition of Hausdorff outer measure, the limsup set has zero s-dimensional measure for every s>0, hence dimension zero.

Let E_h be the union of {0} and these limsup sets for eta=1,1/2,1/3,... . Countable subadditivity at each s>0 preserves zero Hausdorff dimension. For x outside E_h and every such eta, all sufficiently large admissible centers satisfy

\[
 |P_{p,a}(x)|\ge p^{-(C_0+2\eta)p^2}.
 \tag{4}
\]

The cutoff may depend on x,h,eta. This dependence is harmless because only infinitely large prime approximants are used.

## 3. Uniform analytic remainder for the infinite higher profile

Fix x outside E_h and put f=f_1^x. Choose a strip width sigma_0>0 with ||f||_(A_sigma_0)<infinity. Restrict the centers to [epsilon,1-epsilon], where epsilon>0 is fixed. Set R_p=1/(4p^2) and

\[
 \sigma_n=\sigma_0-(n-1)\frac{\sigma_0}{2p},
 \qquad 1\le n\le p+1.
\]

The exact same divisor separation as in topic46 holds for every angular frequency, not just a finite band. In a weighted Fourier norm, the stream inverse shifts density frequencies by one; therefore

\[
 \|g_i(z)\|_{A_{\sigma_i}}
 \le 4p e^{\sigma_0}\|f_i(z)\|_{A_{\sigma_i}}
 \tag{5}
\]

for i<p on |z-a/p|<=R_p and for i=p on the boundary. Here z denotes the complex parameter delta, not the far-field series variable. The constant4p is deliberately coarse and covers both channels.

For i+j=n, both sigma_i and sigma_j exceed sigma_n by at least sigma_0/(2p). The elementary Fourier estimate |k|exp(-s|k|)<=1/s gives

\[
 \|\partial_\theta H\|_{A_{\sigma_n}}
 \le\frac{2p}{\sigma_0}\|H\|_{A_{\sigma_i}}
\]

when H belongs to the i-th strip, and similarly for j. Use |P_i|<=4p, |p_j|<=3p and |1/(c-1)|<=2/epsilon on the parameter disk. If Y_n bounds ||f_n|| on its strip, the bilinear recurrence gives

\[
 Y_n\le\frac{112e^{\sigma_0}}{\epsilon\sigma_0}
       \frac{p^3}{n-1}\sum_{i+j=n}Y_iY_j
 \le Kp^3\sum_{i+j=n}Y_iY_j.
 \tag{6}
\]

For n<=p the bound is on the whole disk; for n=p+1 it is on the boundary. Dropping n-1 and using the Catalan estimate gives a constant C depending only on f,sigma_0,epsilon such that

\[
 \|f_n(z)\|_{A_{\sigma_n}}\le(Cp^3)^n
\]

on the applicable disk or boundary. All Banach norms may then be weakened to A_(sigma_0/2).

Only the resonant mode of g_p has a pole at the center. Thus H(w)=w f_(p+1)(a/p+w) is holomorphic in that fixed Banach space on a neighborhood of |w|<=R_p. The same Cauchy argument as in topic46 yields

\[
 \left\|f_{p+1}(a/p+w)-\frac{\operatorname{Res}_{p,a}}w\right\|_{A_{\sigma_0/2}}
 \le2(Cp^3)^{p+1}\quad (|w|\le R_p/2).
 \tag{7}
\]

This is exp(O(p log p)), still negligible compared with p^(eta p^2). It does not assert a geometric bound on the full sequence at a fixed parameter.

## 4. A nonzero pole without a highest input frequency

Let j=a/2-1 and gamma_0=1-a/p. Write alpha_m for the real sine amplitudes of f, so alpha_1=x. The residue calculation from topic15, without any truncation of f, gives

\[
 \operatorname{Res}_{p,a}(\theta)
 =-\frac{P_{p,a}(x)j}{p^2(c_0-1)}
 \big(\sin(2j\theta)f'(\theta)+\gamma_0\cos(2j\theta)f(\theta)\big).
 \tag{8}
\]

The sine amplitude at frequency 2j+1 of the bracket is exactly

\[
 \frac12\left((1+\gamma_0)x-(4j+1-\gamma_0)\alpha_{4j+1}\right).
 \tag{9}
\]

These are the only contributing input frequencies, by the sine product identities. Since f is analytic, (4j+1)|alpha_(4j+1)| tends to zero. Since x!=0 and gamma_0>=epsilon, the modulus of (9) is at least |x|/4 for all sufficiently large j, uniformly over these centers. Also j>=epsilon p/4 and 1/(c_0-1)>=epsilon. Therefore the chosen residue amplitude has modulus at least

\[
 \frac{\epsilon^2|x|}{16p}|P_{p,a}(x)|.
 \tag{10}
\]

This replaces the highest-frequency argument for finite polynomials.

## 5. The divergent subsequence

Choose eta=1/m so small that C_0+2eta<tau. Along the infinitely many approximants in (1), combine (4), (7) and (10). The modulus of the selected sine amplitude in f_(p+1)(delta) is at least

\[
 \frac{\epsilon^2|x|}{16}
 p^{(\tau-C_0-2\eta)p^2-1}
 -2(Cp^3)^{p+1}.
\]

The first term dominates and its (p+1)-st root tends to infinity. A real sine amplitude is at most twice the uniform angular norm, so the uniform-norm power-series radius is zero. E_h was constructed using all admissible centers and a countable list of eta values, so it is independent of delta and tau, as stated.

## Dependencies and finite checks

The proof uses the confirmed single-sine prime nonvanishing and denominator bound from topics31 and46, the exact Poisson and stationary recurrences, the elementary root-factor covering argument, and Cauchy's parameter estimate. No measure theorem for infinite-dimensional data is invoked. Exact Q[u] calculations with leading profile u sin(theta)+sin(3theta) verify that the degree is p and its leading coefficient equals the independently computed single-sine compatibility coefficient at the tested centers. A Cartesian calculation also verifies (9) for finite higher-mode test profiles; the analytic case follows from absolute convergence of the differentiated Fourier series.
