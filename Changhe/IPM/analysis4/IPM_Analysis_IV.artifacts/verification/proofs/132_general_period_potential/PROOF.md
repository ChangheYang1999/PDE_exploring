# A common convergent potential for every integral resonance period

Status: PROVED under the stated geometric density and stream bounds, with independent theory-part confirmation dated2026-10-31T10:38Z and audit V273. Topic131 supplies a sufficient condition. No parity restriction on Q is needed.

## Statement and notation

Let Q>=3 be an integer, delta=2/Q, p_n=1-2n/Q, P_n=p_n+1 and S_n=p_n+3. Let B>=2 be even. Suppose real finite sine polynomials f_n and g_n have respectively positive odd frequencies at most Bn-1 and positive even frequencies at most Bn. Assume

    -[partial_theta^2+P_n^2]g_n
        =p_n cos(theta)f_n-sin(theta)f_n',
    ||f_n||_1+||g_n||_1 <= C A^n

for every n>=1, with C,A>0. The norm is the sum of absolute Fourier sine coefficients. Then there are even-cosine polynomials C_n of degree at most Bn-2 and fixed real numbers alpha,beta,lambda such that

    chi=sum_(n>=1) r^(S_n)C_n(theta)
          +r^2 log(r)[alpha+beta cos(2theta)]+lambda log(r),       (1)
    ||C_n||_1 <= C' A^n,
    chi_xy=sum_(n>=1)r^(P_n)g_n(theta),
    -partial_y Delta chi=sum_(n>=1)r^(p_n)f_n(theta).

The two logarithmic corrections occupy only orders Q and2Q. For each R>A^(Q/2), the series in(1) and every fixed Cartesian derivative converge absolutely and uniformly on r>=R after multiplication by r^(h-S_1), where h is the derivative order and S_1=4-2/Q. All sufficiently late tails also converge without a radial weight. The finite logarithmic terms are smooth and have bounded weighted derivatives there.

The potential is even under both coordinate reflections. If the density and stream satisfy stationary transport for c=Q/(Q-2), the same functions solve the original potential formulation. The reconstruction preserves the strict geometric convergence radius. It does not assert a whole-plane stationary extension or analyticity of the weighted potential at z=r^(-2/Q)=0, because of the explicitly displayed logarithms.

## 1. The density primitive

Fix n>=4Q, set x=n/Q-1>=3 and K=(Bn-2)/2, and write

    f_n=sum_(m=0)^K a_m sin((2m+1)theta).

Seek a cosine coefficient d with

    Delta chi_n=r^(-2x)sum_(m=0)^K d_m cos(2m theta).

Direct polar differentiation of -partial_y gives exactly

    a_0=2x d_0+(1-x)d_1,
    a_m=(m+x)d_m+(m+1-x)d_(m+1),   1<=m<=K,
    d_(K+1)=0.                                                   (2)

For m>=1 and x>=3, |m+1-x|<=m+x. Descending substitution therefore gives

    |d_m| <= x^(-1)sum_(j>=m)|a_j|.

The equation for d_0 gives the same bound, since |1-x|/(2x)<=1 and its direct diagonal bound is even smaller. Summing over m yields

    ||d||_1 <= (K+1)/x ||f_n||_1
       = Bn/[2(n/Q-1)] ||f_n||_1
       <= (2BQ/3)||f_n||_1.                                    (3)

All these are bounds in finite coefficient spaces and require no angular regularity estimate.

## 2. Laplace inversion and compatibility

On the cosine mode2m the Laplace multiplier for radial exponent S_n is S_n^2-4m^2. It can vanish at positive n only when n is a multiple of Q. If n=kQ>=4Q, put j=k-1=x. Then S_n=-2(j-1), and the only zero multiplier is m=j-1. At this index(2) reduces to

    a_(j-1)=(2j-1)d_(j-1).

The assumed pure Poisson equation at stream frequency2j has forcing -2j a_(j-1). Its multiplier is zero, so compatibility forces a_(j-1)=0 and hence d_(j-1)=0. Set the corresponding initial potential coefficient to zero and divide all other d_m by their nonzero multipliers. Denote the result C_n^0.

Because Q is integral, every nonzero distance ||S_n|-2m| is at least2/Q. Also n>=4Q implies |S_n|=2n/Q-4>=n/Q. Thus

    |S_n^2-4m^2| >= 2n/Q^2,
    ||C_n^0||_1 <= [BQ^3/(3n)]||f_n||_1.                        (4)

## 3. The prescribed harmonic stream

Let g_n^0 be the stream generated by r^(S_n)C_n^0. It satisfies the same Poisson equation as g_n. At n not divisible by Q there is no positive even sine kernel, so g_n^0=g_n. At n=kQ>=4Q the difference is a multiple of sin(q theta), q=2k-2. Put ell=q-2=2k-4=-S_n. The exact harmonic identity is

    Delta[r^(-ell)cos(ell theta)]=0,
    partial_x partial_y[r^(-ell)cos(ell theta)]
        =ell(ell+1)r^(-ell-2)sin((ell+2)theta).

Adding (H_n-H_n^0)/[ell(ell+1)] times this harmonic matches the entire prescribed stream coefficient and changes no density. Here H_n and H_n^0 mean the whole coefficients of the resonant sine mode.

One Cartesian derivative increases the angular degree by at most one and costs at most the sum of the absolute radial exponent and angular degree. At n>=4Q both successive derivative factors are at most (B+1)n, because Q>=3. Hence

    ||g_n^0||_1 <= (B+1)^2 n^2 ||C_n^0||_1.

Also ell>=n/Q. The corrected coefficient obeys

    ||C_n||_1 <= [1+Q^2(B+1)^2]||C_n^0||_1
                       +Q^2||g_n||_1/n^2
       <= [BQ^3/(3n)][1+Q^2(B+1)^2]||f_n||_1
                       +Q^2||g_n||_1/n^2.                     (5)

Its added harmonic degree ell is below Bn-2. In particular this reconstruction preserves the base A, rather than merely yielding a larger unspecified exponential bound.

## 4. The finite exceptional prefix

For n<4Q not divisible by Q, the descending density primitive and Laplace inverse are finite invertible matrices. Indeed their possible zero diagonal factors require n=Q or a multiple of Q. Their norms are finite and can be included in C'. At n=3Q, the construction in Sections1to3 works directly with x=2 and ell=2; no asymptotic bound is needed for this single order.

At n=Q the potential exponent is2. Pure cosine columns with degree at least4 eliminate the density modes at least5 by descending substitution: each highest multiplier is nonzero, since the coefficient of sin((ell+1)theta) is exactly(ell/2)(4-ell^2), nonzero for ell>=4. The remaining density is a sin(theta)+b sin(3theta). Direct Cartesian differentiation gives

    chi=r^2 log r:
        rho=-4r^(-1)sin(theta), psi=sin(2theta),
    chi=r^2 log r cos(2theta):
        rho=4r^(-1)[sin(theta)+sin(3theta)], psi=-(1/2)sin(4theta).

Thus alpha=(b-a)/4 and beta=b/4 match the remaining density without introducing a logarithm in density or stream. At this order P_n=0, and the even sine Poisson kernel is zero, so the stream agrees automatically. The pure potential columns1 and cos(2theta) are density and stream gauges and may be set to zero.

At n=2Q the potential exponent is0. Pure-stream compatibility gives the sin(theta) density coefficient a_0=0. The pure cosine columns of positive even degree produce exactly the remaining density modes. The constant column is a gauge. If their generated resonant stream coefficient is H^0, use

    Delta log r=0,
    partial_x partial_y log r=-r^(-2)sin(2theta)

to set lambda=H^0-H, where H is the prescribed coefficient. This changes no density and supplies precisely the missing stream. These are the only logarithmic corrections.

## 5. Convergence and scope

The radial exponent is S_n=S_1-(2/Q)(n-1). The geometric bound supplied by(5), together with the finite prefix, shows that each weighted derivative is bounded by a fixed polynomial in n times (A R^(-2/Q))^n. This is summable exactly under the stated strict condition R>A^(Q/2). A sufficiently small complex angular strip is obtained by absorbing its factor exp(Bn tau) into the strict margin. For the unweighted tails, S_n eventually becomes negative and the maximum over r>=R is attained at r=R, so the same summability applies after finitely many terms.

The logarithmic terms are finite, smooth and even. Their weighted derivatives are bounded since2<S_1 and0<S_1. Four derivatives of the potential suffice for density, velocity and the density gradient, and all orders are available. Absolute convergence therefore justifies each common-potential identity and the stationary equation by differentiation and multiplication. Every term is locally real analytic for r>0, and the local derivative bounds give a locally real analytic potential on the exterior.

Dependencies: the elementary polar derivative identities, the assumed Poisson equation, and geometric coefficient bounds. This argument generalizes the already independently reviewed topic121; it does not depend on the smooth-primitive theorem of109.
