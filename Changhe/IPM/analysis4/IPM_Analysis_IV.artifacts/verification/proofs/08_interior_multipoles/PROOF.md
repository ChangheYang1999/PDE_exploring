# The harmonic multipoles produced by an interior density change

## Statement

Let eta be a real integrable density supported in the disk |y|<=a, with a>0. Suppose eta is even in y_1 and odd in y_2. Write z=x_1+i x_2 and w=y_1+i y_2. Define the exterior stream associated with this compact density by

\[
\psi_\eta(x)=\partial_{x_1}\left[-\frac1{2\pi}
\int\log|x-y|\,\eta(y)\,dy\right].
\]

For |x|>a this is a smooth harmonic function and -Delta psi_eta=partial_(x_1) eta distributionally. It has the convergent S1 multipole expansion

\[
\boxed{\psi_\eta(r,\theta)=\sum_{j\ge1} h_j r^{-2j}\sin(2j\theta),\qquad
h_j=-\frac1{2\pi}\int\operatorname{Im}(w^{2j-1})\eta(y)\,dy.}
\]

On every r>=R>a the expansion and each fixed derivative converge uniformly after the usual radial scaling. It is the change in stream induced by the compact density eta, not a prescription for the canonical inverse of an arbitrary growing density.

## Green kernel and expansion

The two-dimensional kernel -log|x|/(2pi) satisfies -Delta G=delta_0. For |w|<|z|,

\[
\log|z-w|=\log|z|-\operatorname{Re}\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{w^m}{m z^m}.
\]

On |z|>=R>a, the ratio |w/z|<=a/R<1 justifies integration and every fixed exterior derivative term by term. Put M_m=int w^m eta(y)dy. Symmetry gives M_0=0, so differentiation in x_1 yields

\[
\psi_\eta=-\frac1{2\pi}\operatorname{Re}\sum_{m\ge1}M_m z^{-(m+1)}.
\]

Reflection in y_2 replaces w by conjugate(w) and eta by -eta. Hence M_m=-conjugate(M_m), so every moment is purely imaginary. Reflection in y_1 replaces w by -conjugate(w) without changing eta. Therefore M_m=(-1)^m conjugate(M_m), and M_m=0 for even m. For odd m=2j-1 write M_m=i mu_m with mu_m real. Then

\[
\operatorname{Re}(i\mu_m z^{-2j})=\mu_m r^{-2j}\sin(2j\theta),
\]

which proves the coefficient formula and its sign.

Also |M_m|<=a^m||eta||_1. Each fixed derivative of z^(-m-1) introduces only a polynomial factor in m and an additional inverse power of r. The resulting geometric series converges uniformly on every r>=R>a, proving the stated regularity.

## The first multipole and its potential

The first coefficient is

\[
h_1=-\frac1{2\pi}\int y_2\eta(y)\,dy.
\]

If eta is nonnegative on the upper half-plane and is nonzero on a set of positive measure there, h_1<0. The corresponding stream term is h_1 r^(-2) sin(2theta). Since partial_(xy) log r=-r^(-2) sin(2theta), it can be represented by the harmonic exterior potential -h_1 log r.

For j>=2, the harmonic potential

\[
\chi_j=\frac{h_j}{(2j-2)(2j-1)}
 r^{-2j+2}\cos((2j-2)\theta)
\]

has zero density and stream h_j r^(-2j) sin(2jtheta). This follows from the homogeneous potential formula: with s=-2j+2 and l=2j-2, the surviving stream multiplier is (2j-2)(2j-1). Thus a density-free exterior potential can change the velocity, and the first multipole naturally requires a logarithmic potential.

## An explicit sign check

Take eta(y)=y_2 on the unit disk and zero outside. Then M_1=i int_(disk) y_2^2 dy=i pi/4. Angular orthogonality gives M_m=0 for every other positive m. Hence

\[
\psi_\eta=-\tfrac18 r^{-2}\sin(2\theta),\qquad r>1.
\]

This exact example checks the sign and normalization. It is an interior perturbation example, not a stationary density.

## Comparison with the pure stationary exponent sequence

The pure stream sequence has exponents P_n=2-n delta. The harmonic exponent -2j belongs to that sequence exactly when

\[
n\delta=2(j+1).
\]

No harmonic multipole lies in the sequence for irrational delta. For delta=A/B in lowest terms, the allowed harmonic indices satisfy A divides 2(j+1). This is a restriction on which independent multipoles the pure ansatz can contain; it is not a failure of the local exterior equation.

A compact change to the interior density can therefore change infinitely many exterior harmonic coefficients without changing the exterior density. To identify a local exterior solution with a whole-plane canonical velocity, the corresponding multipole data must match. The local convergence theorem alone supplies no such global matching result. Conversely, this missing global condition does not invalidate an exterior solution generated by its convergent potential.

## Dependencies and scope

The proof uses the explicit logarithmic Green kernel, its convergent geometric expansion away from compact support, parity, and the potential differentiation identities in `../01_recurrence/PROOF.md`. It does not assign finite moments to the growing far-field density, does not assume a canonical inverse for such a density without a normalization, and does not claim an obstruction to every possible global extension.
