# Quantitative divergence for algebraic leading profiles

Status: CONFIRMED after the theory part's independent adversarial review at2026-10-31T04:14Z, together with topic46 and the retained exact algebraic checks.

## Statement

Let K be a number field contained in the real numbers, of degree d over Q. Let

\[
 f_1(\theta)=\sum_{\substack{1\le m\le L\\m\text{ odd}}}
 \alpha_m\sin(m\theta),\qquad \alpha_m\in K,
 \quad \alpha_1\alpha_L\ne0,
 \quad B=L+1.
\]

If an irrational delta in (0,1) has infinitely many distinct admissible prime centers a/p, where p is prime, a is even and 4<=a<p, with

\[
 0<|\delta-a/p|<p^{-\tau p^2},\qquad
 \tau>\frac{dB\pi^2}{6},
\]

then the pure stationary density series for this leading profile has radius zero in both the angular Wiener and uniform norms. A nonzero real scalar multiple of such a profile has the same conclusion.

The explicit arithmetic set U from topic46, defined by infinitely many approximations |delta-a/p|<p^{-p^3}, therefore forces zero radius simultaneously for every finite algebraic leading profile with nonzero first harmonic, and for their nonzero real multiples.

## 1. Clear algebraic denominators

Choose a positive integer Q such that each Q alpha_m is an algebraic integer. Such a Q exists: if alpha satisfies an integer polynomial with leading coefficient q, multiplying its equation by q^{degree-1} gives a monic integer polynomial for q alpha, and a common multiple handles finitely many alpha_m.

Use exactly the integer D_p in topic46, formula(5), with this Q and B. The binary-tree proof there shows

\[
 \beta_p=D_p\kappa_p\in\mathcal O_K,
\]

where kappa_p is the first compatibility amplitude at delta_0=a/p. Each tree term becomes an algebraic integer after multiplication by D_p, and algebraic integers form a ring. By the confirmed nonvanishing theorem in topic42, beta_p is nonzero outside a fixed finite set of primes. The same height estimate holds:

\[
 \log D_p\le\left(\frac{B\pi^2}{6}+o(1)\right)p^2\log p.
 \tag{1}
\]

## 2. Use all conjugates

The field norm of the nonzero algebraic integer beta_p is a nonzero integer. Thus, taking absolute values over all d embeddings sigma:K->C,

\[
 1\le|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta_p)|
 =D_p^d\prod_\sigma|\sigma(\kappa_p)|.
 \tag{2}
\]

The integer-norm fact follows, for example, because the conjugates of an algebraic integer satisfy a monic integer minimal polynomial and their product is its constant term up to sign; the field norm is an integer power of that product.

The recurrence has rational coefficients at the center. Applying sigma to its input coefficients commutes with every addition, multiplication and division by its nonzero rational denominators. Hence sigma(kappa_p) is exactly the compatibility amplitude generated by the conjugate leading profile.

Restrict the centers to [epsilon,1-epsilon], which contains all sufficiently close approximants to a fixed delta. The analytic estimate in topic46, Section3, applies also to these complex leading amplitudes. There are only d embeddings and their input norms are fixed. A single constant C therefore gives

\[
 |\sigma(\kappa_p)|\le(Cp)^p
 \quad\hbox{for every sigma and all sufficiently large p}.
 \tag{3}
\]

Use (3) for all embeddings except the chosen real one in (2). We obtain

\[
 |\kappa_p|\ge D_p^{-d}(Cp)^{-p(d-1)}.
\]

By (1), this yields

\[
 \log\frac1{|\kappa_p|}
 \le\left(\frac{dB\pi^2}{6}+o(1)\right)p^2\log p.
 \tag{4}
\]

This is the only step in topic46 that used rational rather than algebraic input.

## 3. Finish with the pole estimate

Choose beta strictly between dB pi^2/6 and tau. Equation(4) gives |kappa_p|>=p^{-beta p^2} for all sufficiently large relevant primes. The highest-frequency pole has residue

\[
 -\frac{\kappa_pj_0(L+\gamma_0)\alpha_L}
 {2p^2(c_0-1)},\qquad j_0=a/2-1.
\]

Its modulus is at least c_*|kappa_p|/p with c_*>0 fixed on the compact interval of centers. The remainder of the order-p+1 density coefficient is at most2(C'p)^{p+1} for |delta-delta_0|<=1/(8p^2), by the same holomorphic-disk proof as in topic46. Thus the selected density amplitude is at least

\[
 c_*p^{(\tau-\beta)p^2-1}-2(C'p)^{p+1}.
\]

Its (p+1)-st root tends to infinity. A real sine amplitude is bounded by twice the uniform angular norm, completing both radius-zero conclusions. The scaling identity f_n(lambda f_1)=lambda^n f_n(f_1) proves the final scalar assertion.

## Dependencies and scope

This proof depends on the complete denominator and holomorphic-remainder argument in topic46, and on topic42's confirmed nonvanishing for finite algebraic profiles. It gives a sufficient arithmetic condition, not a necessary one. It does not supply a height bound for arbitrary transcendental coefficient vectors, and does not say that all Liouville parameters diverge. No rational logarithmic continuation is addressed.
