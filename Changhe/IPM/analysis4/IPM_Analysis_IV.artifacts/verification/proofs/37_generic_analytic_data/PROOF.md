# Generic analytic data at divergent parameters

Status: CONFIRMED after the theory part's independent adversarial review, 2026-10-31T03:56:16Z. Exact hypotheses and limitations below remain in force.

## Function space and finite coefficient maps

Fix sigma>0. Let X_sigma be the real Banach space of odd sine series

\[
 f(\theta)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_k\sin((2k+1)\theta),\qquad
 \|f\|_\sigma=\sum_{k\ge0}|a_k|e^{\sigma(2k+1)}<\infty.
\]

This is a space of analytic S1 density profiles. At a fixed irrational delta in (0,1), write P_n(f;delta) for the nth density coefficient generated by the pure recursion. For each fixed n, the map

\[
 P_n(\cdot;\delta):X_\sigma\longrightarrow C^0(S^1)
\]

is a continuous homogeneous polynomial of degree n. The same statement holds for P_n at a rational parameter if no earlier stream resonance occurs.

To see continuity without assuming infinite-order convergence, reserve a finite decreasing sequence of positive angular strip widths for the finitely many operations through order n. Angular differentiation is bounded from a wider Wiener strip to a strictly narrower one. Products are bounded on each strip. Every nonresonant Poisson inverse is a bounded Fourier multiplier at fixed radial exponent; its denominator grows quadratically at high angular frequencies, and the finitely many low denominators are nonzero. A finite induction gives a continuous n-linear representation for P_n, followed by diagonal evaluation. Only finitely many strip losses are used. The coefficients are polynomial and homogeneous because every recursion combines two lower orders bilinearly.

## 1. Generic analytic profiles have densely obstructed rational parameters

Assume the prime-denominator result of topic 31 for the single-sine input. Let T be the countable set of parameters a/p with p>=5 prime and a even, 4<=a<p. For t=a/p, let K_t(f) be the coefficient of sin((a-3)theta) in P_p(f;t). This is a continuous real homogeneous polynomial of degree p on X_sigma. Topic 31 states that K_t(sin theta) is nonzero.

For any fixed f, the scalar polynomial

\[
 h\longmapsto K_t(f+h\sin\theta)
\]

has leading coefficient K_t(sin theta), hence has at most p real roots. Thus the zero set of K_t is closed and has empty interior. Its complement V_t is open and dense. The intersection

\[
 \mathcal R_\sigma=\bigcap_{t\in T}V_t
\]

is a dense residual subset of X_sigma. For every f in this subset, all prime-denominator centers in T have a nonzero first compatibility coefficient. These centers are dense in (0,1).

At each such center, the first-pole identity from topic 15 holds for analytic inputs as well:

\[
 \mathop{\rm Res}_{\delta=t}P_{p+1}(f;\delta)
 =-\frac{K_t(f)j}{p^2(c_*-1)}
 [\sin(2j\theta)f'(\theta)+\gamma_*\cos(2j\theta)f(\theta)],
\]

where j=a/2-1 and gamma_*=1-t. The bracket cannot vanish for a nonzero analytic S1 profile. At zero, if f has odd vanishing order v>=1, its leading coefficient in this bracket is multiplied by 2jv+gamma_*>0. The residue is therefore a nonzero continuous function. The finite coefficient maps depend analytically on delta away from earlier resonances: high-frequency Fourier multipliers are uniformly bounded on a small parameter neighborhood, and there are only finitely many low-frequency denominators to exclude. Consequently this is a genuine simple pole in any smaller strip and in C^0.

The Baire argument in topic 15 can now be applied using C^0 norms directly. For each f in R_sigma, a dense residual set of irrational delta satisfies

\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\|P_n(f;\delta)\|_\infty^{1/n}=\infty.
 \tag{1}
\]

Its pure density terms do not tend to zero uniformly in angle at any fixed positive radius.

On a finite-dimensional coefficient space containing sin(theta), the same exceptional zero sets are proper real algebraic sets. They have Lebesgue measure zero. Thus the corresponding generic-profile statement holds on a full-measure residual subset of each such finite-dimensional space.

## 2. One divergent datum implies generic divergent data at that parameter

Fix an irrational delta. Suppose g in X_sigma satisfies

\[
 \limsup_n\|P_n(g;\delta)\|_\infty^{1/n}=\infty.
 \tag{2}
\]

Then the set of f in X_sigma satisfying (1) at this same fixed delta is a dense G_delta subset of X_sigma.

For positive integers M,N define the open set

\[
 U_{M,N}=\bigcup_{n\ge N}
 \{f:\|P_n(f;\delta)\|_\infty>M^n\}.
\]

Suppose this set is not dense. There is an open ball B(f_0,r) on which all n>=N obey ||P_n(f;delta)||_infinity<=M^n. Choose h>0 such that h||g||_sigma<r. The C^0-valued polynomial

\[
 Q_n(t)=P_n(f_0+t g;\delta),\qquad -h\le t\le h,
\]

has degree at most n, is bounded by M^n, and has leading coefficient P_n(g;delta).

A Banach-valued polynomial Q of degree at most n, bounded by B on [-h,h], has leading coefficient norm at most 2^n B/h^n for n>=1. Indeed, after rescaling to [-1,1], its nth Chebyshev coefficient is

\[
 \frac2\pi\int_0^\pi Q(h\cos\theta)\cos(n\theta)\,d\theta,
\]

whose norm is at most 2B. The leading coefficient of the nth Chebyshev polynomial is 2^(n-1), so the claimed bound follows. The integral is an ordinary continuous Banach-valued integral.

Applying this to Q_n gives

\[
 \|P_n(g;\delta)\|_\infty\le(2M/h)^n\qquad(n\ge N),
\]

contradicting (2). Therefore every U_(M,N) is dense. Baire's theorem makes their countable intersection a dense G_delta, and that intersection is exactly the set in (1).

The same argument works in every finite-dimensional leading-data subspace containing g. No assertion of full Lebesgue measure in this fixed-parameter result follows from this proof.

## Consequences and limits

Topics 31 and 32 supply irrational parameters where g=sin(theta) obeys (2), including a deterministically specified computable Liouville parameter in every prescribed interval. At each such parameter, divergence is residual in the analytic leading-data space. Separately, the Brjuno convergence theorem still gives convergence for every analytic profile at almost every parameter. These statements concern different quantifiers and are consistent.

No assertion is made that every non-Brjuno parameter diverges, that every analytic profile diverges at a fixed bad parameter, or that divergence is open in the leading-data norm. The analytic Banach space and its topology are explicit because a residual-set statement without a topology would be incomplete.
