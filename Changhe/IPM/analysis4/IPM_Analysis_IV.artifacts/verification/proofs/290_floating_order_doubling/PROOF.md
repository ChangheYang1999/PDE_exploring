## Argument

Let y_n be a printed base-ten norm logarithm. For every full block jQ<n<=(j+1)Q, let m_j be its largest y_n and let x_j=(j+1)Q. Density and stream channels are treated separately. Omit incomplete blocks and explicitly record the last printed order; a completion banner can report one order beyond the last norm row.

On a specified set of block endpoints x_j>=L, the least-squares affine fit has

    a=(m sum(x_j m_j)-sum(x_j)sum(m_j))
       /(m sum(x_j^2)-(sum(x_j))^2),
    b=(sum(m_j)-a sum(x_j))/m.

The denominator is positive when at least two distinct block endpoints occur. Computing these expressions with exact rational printed inputs yields the exact minimizer for that finite dataset. Its squared RMS residual is sum((a x_j+b-m_j)^2)/m. Directed evaluation encloses A=10^a and the apparent physical radius A^(Q/2). Baseline windows start at100,150,200; extended windows start at100,150,200,300,400,500 when enough blocks exist.

The producer divides each quantity by its exact homogeneous power of its internal amplitude. Two positive amplitudes that are integer powers of two therefore target the same unit-amplitude values, although their rounded logarithms can differ. Record both amplitudes and every overlap discrepancy. Identical overlapping printed norm rows establish reproducibility to the printed precision for those rows. They do not bound unprinted differences. Comparing A values quantifies finite fit drift. Neither small drift nor agreement of two finite runs controls the uncomputed tail. Approximate-root input error persists even in an extended-precision rerun. No percentage variation is an error bar for an infinite-order limit.
