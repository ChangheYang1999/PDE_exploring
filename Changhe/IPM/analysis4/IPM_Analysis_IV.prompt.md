# 24-Hour Research Campaign: Convergence of the Infinite Far-Field Series for Self-Similar IPM

## Mission

Investigate whether there exist nontrivial leading angular data \(f_1(\theta)\) and a similarity exponent \(c_l\) such that the infinite stationary far-field series generated by the self-similar IPM angular recursion **actually converges**, rather than merely defining a formal asymptotic expansion.

The strongest desired result is absolute and uniform convergence, together with enough uniform convergence of derivatives to obtain a genuine smooth stationary solution in an exterior domain \(r\ge R\).

Use our previous numerical IPM results to select promising parameter values and leading angular data, but do not assume that numerical evidence proves convergence.

**Research duration: 24 hours of actual wall-clock work, including numerical exploration, theoretical analysis, independent verification, and the final report.**

The main objective is to find and rigorously justify at least one nontrivial convergent infinite series, or identify a precise mathematical obstruction.

Do not conclude convergence merely because the first several coefficients decrease.

# Part I. Mathematical formulation

## 1. Original potential formulation

Start from the potential \(\chi\):

\[
\rho=-\partial_y\Delta\chi,
\qquad
u=(-\chi_{xyy},\chi_{xxy}).
\]

The stationary self-similar IPM equation is

\[
\boxed{
(c_lX+u)\cdot\nabla\rho-\rho=0.
}
\]

Impose S1 symmetry:

\[
\rho(-x,y)=\rho(x,y),
\qquad
\rho(x,-y)=-\rho(x,y).
\]

The potential \(\chi\) is even in both variables, modulo irrelevant gauge freedoms.

Work only with the far-field expansion. Do not develop a Taylor expansion at the origin unless needed to determine a possible global compatibility obstruction.

## 2. Infinite far-field series

Let

\[
\gamma=\frac1{c_l},
\qquad
\delta=1-\gamma,
\]

and

\[
p_n=1-n\delta.
\]

Consider

\[
\boxed{
\rho(r,\theta)
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{p_n}f_n(\theta).
}
\tag{1}
\]

The leading term is

\[
r^\gamma f_1(\theta).
\]

Write the associated stream function as

\[
\psi(r,\theta)
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{p_n+1}g_n(\theta),
\]

with

\[
-\Delta\psi=\partial_x\rho,
\qquad
u=(-\psi_y,\psi_x).
\]

Equivalently, the original potential should have the expansion

\[
\boxed{
\chi(r,\theta)
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
r^{p_n+3}C_n(\theta).
}
\tag{2}
\]

The functions \(f_n\) and \(g_n\) must be derived consistently from the same potential \(C_n\).

Do not introduce independently chosen stream-function coefficients that violate the original potential formulation.

Establish the precise correspondence among \(C_n,f_n,g_n\), including its gauge freedoms and possible resonances.

## 3. Angular Poisson inverse

For a single density term \(r^pf(\theta)\), the stream-function coefficient satisfies

\[
\boxed{
-\left(\partial_\theta^2+(p+1)^2\right)g
=
p\cos\theta\,f-\sin\theta\,f'.
}
\tag{3}
\]

With S1 symmetry,

\[
f_n(\theta)
=
\sum_{k\ge0}a_{n,k}\sin((2k+1)\theta),
\]

\[
g_n(\theta)
=
\sum_{j\ge1}b_{n,j}\sin(2j\theta).
\]

The proposed explicit Fourier formula is

\[
\boxed{
b_{n,j}
=
\frac{
(p_n-2j+1)a_{n,j-1}
+
(p_n+2j+1)a_{n,j}
}{
2[4j^2-(p_n+1)^2]
}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Independently derive and verify this formula, including signs, Fourier conventions, and any exceptional cases.

## 4. Nonlinear angular recursion

The proposed stationary recursion is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
f_n
=
\frac1{(n-1)(c_l-1)}
\sum_{\substack{i+j=n\\i,j\ge1}}
\bigg[
&-p_jg_i'f_j\\
&+(p_i+1)g_if_j'
\bigg],
\qquad n\ge2.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{5}
\]

Here \(g_i\) is determined from \(f_i\) by (3)–(4).

Thus, formally,

\[
\boxed{
(c_l,f_1)\longmapsto f_2,f_3,f_4,\ldots
}
\]

without independently choosing the higher-order coefficients.

Check this recurrence directly against the full stationary equation expressed in \(\chi\), not only against a previously written recurrence.

Explain whether the recurrence remains valid at every order or must be supplemented by harmonic multipole or logarithmic channels.

# Part III. What notion of convergence do we seek?

## 6. Uniform convergence on an exterior region

The primary question is whether there exists some finite \(R>0\) such that

\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{p_n}f_n(\theta)
\]

converges for all \(r\ge R\).

Ideally prove absolute, uniform convergence in \(\theta\) and in the radial variable after factoring out the prescribed leading growth.

For example, study

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{r\ge R}
r^{-\gamma}
\left|
\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}r^{p_n}f_n(\theta)
\right|\longrightarrow0
}
\tag{6}
\]

uniformly in \(\theta\) as \(N\to\infty\).

Because

\[
r^{p_n}=r^\gamma(r^{-\delta})^{n-1},
\]

the problem can be regarded as convergence of a power series in

\[
z=r^{-\delta}
\]

with angular-function coefficients:

\[
\boxed{
r^{-\gamma}\rho
=
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n-1}f_n(\theta).
}
\tag{7}
\]

Investigate whether this series defines an analytic function of \(z\), taking values in a suitable Banach space of angular functions.

Do not confuse uniform convergence of the weighted expansion with uniform boundedness of \(\rho\) itself, since \(\rho\) grows like \(r^\gamma\).

## 7. Convergence of derivatives

Convergence of \(\rho\) alone is insufficient to solve the PDE.

Aim to establish uniform convergence of the appropriately weighted derivatives of:

\[
\rho,\qquad u,\qquad\chi.
\]

For example, obtain convergence in norms controlling

\[
r^{j-\gamma}D^j\rho,
\]

and the corresponding scaled derivatives of \(u\) and \(\chi\).

Determine the precise derivative orders needed to justify:

\[
\rho=-\partial_y\Delta\chi,
\]

\[
u=(-\chi_{xyy},\chi_{xxy}),
\]

and

\[
(c_lX+u)\cdot\nabla\rho-\rho=0
\]

by termwise differentiation and multiplication.

Ideally prove locally uniform convergence of every derivative in the exterior domain, or convergence in an analytic angular norm with sufficient radial regularity.

State clearly which derivative convergence has actually been proved.

## 8. Stronger possible results

Investigate whether one can establish:

1. Absolute and uniform convergence in \(r\ge R,\theta\).
2. Uniform convergence of the differentiated series.
3. Analyticity of the resulting exterior profile.
4. A positive, explicitly bounded convergence radius in \(z=r^{-\delta}\).
5. Stability of convergence under small perturbations of \(c_l\) and \(f_1\).

The fifth question is especially interesting: if one exact convergent series exists, does it belong to a locally stable family of convergent series?

Do not assume such stability; investigate it.

# Part IV. Resonances and small divisors

## 9. This is a central mathematical issue

The denominator in the Poisson inversion is

\[
\boxed{
4j^2-(p_n+1)^2
=
4j^2-(2-n\delta)^2.
}
\tag{8}
\]

At low orders it is nonzero in our parameter range.

However, as \(n\to\infty\), this denominator may approach zero or vanish exactly.

The exact resonance condition is

\[
\boxed{
n\delta=2(j+1),\qquad j\ge1.
}
\tag{9}
\]

This issue cannot be ignored when studying an infinite series.

Investigate it systematically.

### A. Rational versus irrational \(\delta\)

If \(\delta\) is rational, exact resonances may occur at sufficiently high orders.

The numerical reference parameter is stored as a rational number, so the consequences of using it as an **exact** \(c_l\) must be examined.

A resonance does not automatically imply that the series diverges: the corresponding forcing coefficient may vanish, or a logarithmic/harmonic correction may resolve the equation.

Determine the precise compatibility condition.

### B. Irrational and Diophantine \(\delta\)

If \(\delta\) is irrational, exact resonances of this type disappear, but small divisors may remain.

Analyze whether a Diophantine condition on \(\delta\) gives sufficient bounds.

Investigate explicit algebraic irrational choices of \(c_l\) close to the numerical reference.

For example, one mathematically convenient test is

\[
\delta=\frac1{\sqrt5},
\qquad
c_l=\frac{5+\sqrt5}{4}\approx1.809017.
\]

This is close to the numerical reference, is irrational, and may permit explicit Diophantine lower bounds.

This is only a suggested test parameter, not an assumed solution.

### C. High angular frequencies

Even if \(f_1\) has finite Fourier support, the nonlinear recursion generates higher angular frequencies.

Track their growth with \(n\).

Determine whether the potentially resonant angular modes are actually populated by the recursion.

Do not infer a small-divisor obstruction from denominators corresponding to modes whose coefficients vanish identically.

### D. Possible logarithmic terms

If resonance occurs, investigate whether the correct expansion must include

\[
r^{p_n}(\log r)^k f_{n,k}(\theta)
\]

or additional harmonic stream-function modes.

If logarithmic terms are necessary, derive the modified recurrence and determine whether a convergent generalized expansion remains possible.

# Part V. Growth of the recursive coefficients

## 10. The key quantitative problem

Determine the asymptotic growth of

\[
\|f_n\|,\qquad \|g_n\|
\]

as \(n\to\infty\).

Use norms appropriate for analytic or smooth angular functions.

Possible choices include

\[
\|f\|_{\mathcal A_\sigma}
=
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
|\widehat f(k)|e^{\sigma|k|},
\]

and Fourier–Sobolev norms.

Investigate whether there exist constants \(C,A>0\) such that

\[
\boxed{
\|f_n\|_{\mathcal A_\sigma}\le CA^n.
}
\tag{10}
\]

If so, the power series has a positive convergence radius.

However, the nonlinear recursion includes angular derivatives and a Poisson inverse, so derivative loss and small divisors must be handled together.

Investigate majorant-series arguments, analytic Banach algebras, Cauchy estimates, and shrinking analytic-strip methods.

A useful alternative is to study whether

\[
\|f_n\|\sim A^n(n!)^\sigma.
\]

If factorial or Gevrey growth appears, the ordinary infinite series may diverge even though it is an excellent asymptotic expansion.

Determine whether such growth is rigorous or only observed numerically.

Also explore whether a special leading profile can eliminate the mechanism producing factorial growth.

# Part VI. Numerical exploration of the recursion

## 11. Implement a dedicated high-order recursion

Create a new research workspace under `Changhe/IPM`, leaving the existing projects unchanged.

Implement the angular Fourier recursion using sparse coefficient arrays.

Use:

- Exact rational or algebraic arithmetic where practical.
- Arbitrary precision arithmetic for delicate denominators.
- Adaptive angular bandwidth.
- Stable evaluation of the quadratic nonlinear convolution.
- Multicore CPU parallelism when beneficial.
- Checkpoints for long experiments.

Do not use double precision alone when cancellation or small divisors make it unreliable.

Avoid allocating unnecessarily large dense matrices.

The numerical implementation should independently verify the first few recursion orders against direct substitution into the \(\chi\) equation.

Use a second independent implementation for low orders to check Fourier signs, indexing, and convolution factors.

## 12. Investigate multiple families of \(f_1\) and \(c_l\)

Run systematic experiments for:

1. The leading profile extracted from the existing numerical solution.
2. Perturbations of its Fourier coefficients.
3. Low-mode polynomial leading profiles.
4. Nearby \(c_l\) values.
5. Exact irrational parameter choices when analytically useful.

For each experiment, record:

\[
\|f_n\|_{L^2_\theta},
\qquad
\|f_n\|_{L^\infty_\theta},
\qquad
\|f_n\|_{\mathcal A_\sigma},
\]

where computationally feasible.

Also record:

- The largest Fourier frequency.
- The smallest active Poisson denominator.
- The ratio \(\|f_{n+1}\|/\|f_n\|\).
- The root-test quantity \(\|f_n\|^{1/n}\).
- The largest \(n\) computed reliably.
- The dependence on arithmetic precision.

Do not interpret early root-test stabilization as proof of an infinite convergence radius.

## 13. Direct series evaluation

For promising parameters, compute the truncated series

\[
\rho_N(r,\theta)
=
\sum_{n=1}^{N}r^{p_n}f_n(\theta).
\]

Evaluate it for different exterior radii \(R\).

Track the differences

\[
\rho_{N+1}-\rho_N
\]

in weighted uniform norms.

Also evaluate the full stationary residual of the truncated series using the stream function generated from the same coefficients.

Determine whether the residual decreases with \(N\), and whether that decrease is consistent with a provable geometric bound.

Include derivative residual tests, not just function-value tests.

Check for catastrophic cancellation or failure of precision as \(N\) increases.

## 14. A special success criterion

If a candidate appears convergent, independently check the corresponding coefficient recursion and the reconstruction of \(\chi\).

Then attempt to obtain a rigorous majorant estimate that establishes convergence for all orders.

An experiment showing convergence through 50, 100, or 500 terms is not a proof.

The goal is to turn a promising numerical pattern into a theorem.

# Part VII. Relation to the canonical whole-plane velocity

## 15. Exterior versus global solutions

The radial/angular recursion solves the stationary equations formally in the exterior.

But the canonical IPM velocity is nonlocal.

A convergent exterior series need not automatically be the restriction of a smooth whole-plane solution with canonical Biot–Savart velocity.

Investigate carefully:

1. Whether the convergent \(\chi\) series gives a legitimate exterior stream function.
2. Whether its associated velocity agrees with the canonical inverse of the corresponding density.
3. Whether compactly supported interior density can introduce independent harmonic multipole terms in the far field.
4. Whether those multipoles are compatible with the pure \(p_n\) exponent sequence.
5. Whether the required additional channels affect the convergence proof.

Do not allow the canonical-velocity issue to invalidate an otherwise valid **local exterior solution theorem**.

Separate the two claims:

**Exterior theorem:** The convergent series solves the PDE in \(r>R\) with the velocity generated by its convergent potential.

**Global theorem:** That exterior solution extends to a smooth whole-plane canonical IPM solution.

The primary goal of this campaign is the exterior theorem. The global extension is a secondary question.

# Part VIII. Theoretical targets

## 16. Desired Theorem A — Convergent infinite angular recursion

Try to prove a theorem of the form:

There exist

\[
c_l>1,\qquad
f_1\not\equiv0,\qquad R<\infty
\]

such that the recursively defined sequence \(f_n\) yields an absolutely and uniformly convergent exterior expansion

\[
\rho(r,\theta)
=
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{p_n}f_n(\theta),
\qquad r\ge R,
\]

with enough derivative convergence for the stationary IPM equation to hold exactly.

Provide explicit assumptions on \(c_l\) and \(f_1\), and ideally a quantitative convergence radius.

If the theorem can hold for an open family of \(f_1\), identify that family.

## 17. Desired Theorem B — Stability of convergence

Assuming one convergent series exists, investigate whether sufficiently small perturbations of \(c_l\) and \(f_1\) preserve convergence.

If only Diophantine parameter sets are admissible, formulate stability in the correct topology or parameter class rather than claiming an open interval automatically.

Provide a complete proof if possible.

## 18. Desired Theorem C — Obstruction or divergence

If no nontrivial convergent series is found, investigate whether one can prove divergence for a natural class of initial profiles.

Possible mechanisms include:

- Exact resonance with a failed compatibility condition.
- Small divisors.
- Factorial growth from angular derivatives.
- Proliferation of angular frequencies.
- Incompatibility with the potential representation.
- Missing harmonic multipole channels.

A rigorous divergence result is valuable even if the original convergence goal fails.

Do not claim universal divergence merely because a few tested initial profiles diverge numerically.

## 19. Fallback — Gevrey asymptotics

If ordinary convergence is not provable, determine whether the recursion at least defines a controlled Gevrey asymptotic expansion.

Investigate optimal truncation, residual decay, and the possibility of Borel-type summation.

This is secondary to proving ordinary convergence.
