## Checks

1. Weighted membership of the inputs and images. PASS.
The fixed part d = -Delta P is smooth and compact. P is even in x and odd in y, and the Laplacian preserves both parities, so d has density parity. The strips k_n^0 = n^(-alpha) Psi(nz) eta(s) have sup O(n^(-alpha)) and Lipschitz constant O(n^(1-alpha)). Hence |f(P)-f(P')| <= C n^(-alpha) min(1, n|P-P'|) <= C|P-P'|^alpha, which gives (4) for k_n^0. The same split works for V_delta.grad k_n^0 = n^(-alpha) Psi(nz) eta'(s), which has the same two bounds. The compensator term c[k_n^0] zeta is O(n^(-1-alpha)) times a fixed smooth function. Every function involved is supported in the fixed K. Let d0 > 0 be the distance from K to {x=0}. Then h(0,y) = 0. The weighted y quotient vanishes identically for |x| < d0 and is at most d0^(-2)[h]_alpha for |x| >= d0. The x quotient is an ordinary Holder quotient. So ||h||_E2 <= ||h||_inf + (1 + d0^(-2))[h]_alpha, and the weight is harmless exactly as claimed. Separation from {y=0} plays no role in the weight. It is needed only so that the odd reflection is smooth and the four boxes are disjoint. This gives (6) for h_n and for V_delta.grad h_n - h_n. The nonlocal coupling B_delta h_n is not supported in K and is nonzero near both axes. The proof correctly does not use support there. It uses the global E_2 bound (2) of proof 203, which assumes nothing about the support of the input. So L_delta h_n is in E_2 with bounded norm, and h_n lies in D_delta, defined by the transport alone. Since B_delta is bounded, this is the domain of L_delta. Thus (7) holds. N(h_n) is smooth because h_n is in C_c^infinity, so U[h_n] is C^infinity with bounded derivatives. Since grad h_n is supported in K, N(h_n) is compactly supported in K. Its parity is right: U1 is odd-even and h_x is odd-odd, U2 is even-odd and h_y is even-even, and both products are even in x and odd in y. Hence N(h_n) is in E_2. Since chi_delta = 1 on K, it is unchanged by localization. L_delta^(-1) N(h_n) lies in D_delta by the surjectivity in proof 203.

2. The characteristic identity with the actual variable field. PASS.
The coordinates invert (s,z) -> Phi_s(Q + z nu), where Phi is the flow of V_delta itself. By the group property, z(Phi_t X) = z(X) and s(Phi_t X) = s(X) + t. Differentiating at t = 0 gives (2) exactly: V_delta.grad z = 0 and V_delta.grad s = 1. This uses no frozen approximation. In the variable case K lies in B_(delta/2), where V_delta is the actual field V = ell X + U[rho0] (proof 202). In the frozen case V_delta is the saddle (-A0 x, D0 y). Either way the field is smooth and nonzero near Q. By the chain rule, V_delta.grad k_n^0 = n^(-alpha)[n Psi'(nz) eta (V.grad z) + Psi(nz) eta'(s) (V.grad s)] = n^(-alpha) Psi(nz) eta'(s). So the O(n) transverse factor n^(1-alpha) Psi'(nz) grad z is annihilated exactly by the transport. That is why the graph norm stays bounded while the transverse derivative of h_n grows like n^(1-alpha). On the three reflected boxes the same identity needs V_delta to be equivariant under the two reflections, that is V1 odd in x and even in y, and V2 even in x and odd in y. This holds for the saddle and for the actual field. It is a stated hypothesis of proof 202, and the cutoff there is reflection invariant. The proof uses it implicitly (Remark 1).

3. The lower bound ||N(h_n)||_E2 >= c1 n. PASS.
Symbol check. With d^ = |xi|^2 P^, the multiplier gives m1 d^ = xi eta P^ = -(P_xy)^ and m2 d^ = -xi^2 P^ = (P_xx)^. So U[d] = (-P_xy, P_xx) holds pointwise. The identity is local in P, so the reflected copies do not affect the first box. On the tube P is (w2/2)(x-Qx)^2 - w1(x-Qx)(y-Qy), so P_xx = w2, P_xy = -w1 and P_yy = 0. Hence U[d] = w, d = -w2 and grad d = 0, all exactly. The canonical velocity agrees with the Fourier one here: the far subtraction at 0 is zero for compact class-P data by parity (Remark 2). Next, c[d] = 0, because U[d] vanishes near 0 and proof 201 gives DU(0) = diag(-c, c). Equivalently, r^(-3) sin(3 theta) is harmonic off 0 and P has compact support away from 0. The compensator works because the integrand zeta sin(3 theta) r^(-3) is invariant under both reflections, so a nonnegative first-quadrant bump at a point with sin(3 theta) != 0 has nonzero strain after reflection. So c[h_n] = 0 and J(h_n) = 0 exactly. On the inner tube eta' = 0, zeta and its derivatives vanish, and grad d = 0. So grad h_n = n^(1-alpha) Psi'(nz) grad z and U[h_n] = w + U[k_n], which gives (9). The sup bound on U[k_n] is correct by the near, intermediate and far split, and it tends to 0 (the log factor can even be dropped, Remark 5). At z = 0, N = 0 because Psi'(0) = 0. At z = 1/n, Psi'(1) = 2, so N >= 2 n^(1-alpha)(b_* - |U[k_n]||grad z|) >= b_* n^(1-alpha) for large n. The two points are O(1/n) apart, so the C^alpha quotient is at least c n. For the comparison with E_2, go from P = (x,y) to (x',y) and then to P' = (x',y'). This gives |f(P)-f(P')| <= ([f]_x + R^2 [f]_(y,2)) * 2|P-P'|^alpha, where R = sup |x| on K. So the E_2 norm controls the local C^alpha seminorm, which is the direction needed, and (10) follows.

4. The two-trajectory lower bound for L_delta^(-1) N(h_n). PASS.
g = L_delta^(-1) N(h_n) exists and lies in D_delta by proof 203. Then V_delta.grad g = g + (V_delta.grad g - g) is continuous, because g is in D_delta. Near the inner tube chi_delta = 1, so the stated local equation holds distributionally. In flow coordinates, d_s g~ = f~ with f~ continuous. So g~ minus the s-primitive of f~ is a continuous distribution with zero s-derivative, hence a continuous function of z alone. This gives (11) pointwise for every starting point, with the correct sign: d/ds[e^(-s) g(Phi_s X)] = e^(-s)(V.grad g - g)(Phi_s X). The local bounds are justified. g is controlled in C^alpha(K) by Check 3. U[g] is controlled in C^alpha(B_1) by proof 199 (11), whose proof uses only ||chi_x chi_y g||_(C^alpha) <= C M and so holds at p = 2. |c[g]| <= (4/pi)||g||_E2 by proof 200 at p = 2, where p + alpha - 1 = 1 + alpha > 1. grad rho0 and X.grad rho0 are smooth on K. Both trajectories stay in the tube for s in [0, tau] and stay C/n apart. So the endpoint differences, the U[g].grad rho0 differences and the strain-column difference are bounded by C||g||_E2 (n^(-alpha) + n^(-1)). The nonlinear integrals give 0 on z = 0 and at least b_*(1 - e^(-tau)) n^(1-alpha) on z = 1/n. Hence ||g||_E2 >= c2 n with c2 = b_*(1 - e^(-tau))/C, independent of n. Uniqueness is used only to identify this g with L_delta^(-1) N(h_n). The bound holds for every E_2 solution near K whose transport derivative is continuous, and the inverse norm of L_delta is never used (it could only give an upper bound). The global V207 image bound is used only for the graph membership (7) of the inputs, which is legitimate because it is an E_2 to E_2 bound with no support hypothesis. The lower bound for the inverse uses only local estimates. This lower bound does not follow from (10) alone, so the separate argument is needed and it is correct.

5. Rescaling and discontinuity at 0. PASS.
U and c are linear, so N(lambda h) = lambda^2 N(h) exactly, and L_delta^(-1) is linear. With a_n = n^(-1/2) h_n we get ||a_n||_graph <= C n^(-1/2) -> 0, ||N(a_n)||_E2 = n^(-1)||N(h_n)||_E2 >= c1 and ||L_delta^(-1) N(a_n)||_E2 >= c2, while N(0) = 0. The inputs keep J = c = 0 and the support K. The graph norm dominates the E_2 norm, so the same sequence shows discontinuity from E_2 to E_2 on the smooth core. The graph norm of L_delta^(-1) N(a_n) is at least its E_2 norm, so there is also discontinuity from graph to graph. Scaling h_n by any fixed epsilon gives ||L_delta^(-1) N(epsilon h_n)||_E2 >= c2 epsilon^2 n. So h -> -L_delta^(-1)(F + N(h)) is unbounded on every graph ball about 0 intersected with the smooth core. The exponent 1/2 could be any exponent in (0,1).

6. Scope. PASS, no overreach in the proof or in the V211 verdict.
The proof says explicitly that it does not imply that every stronger norm must control h_x, that stronger norms may make the sequence unbounded and are outside the conclusion, and that no nonexistence theorem follows. The V211 verdict repeats these limits. The maps are defined only on the smooth core, which is correctly stated. The theory author's integration in S2_CALPHA_THEORY.md (Lemma N'', sections 0, 4 and 8) stays within these limits. One phrase deserves a qualifier (Remark 7).

## Remarks

1. Reflection equivariance. The C^alpha bound for V_delta.grad k_n^0 on the three reflected boxes, and the statement that the reflected strips are characteristic, use the fact that V_delta is equivariant under both reflections. This is true (proof 202's hypotheses V1 odd-even and V2 even-odd, a reflection-invariant cutoff, and the saddle) but is not stated in the proof. One sentence would close it.

2. Canonical velocity. The canonical U subtracts the far kernel at the origin. For compact class-P data the subtracted constants vanish by parity: K1 is odd-odd against an even-odd density, and K2 is even-even against an odd-y density. So identity (3) is the canonical velocity. The proof's reason, that both sides vanish at infinity, identifies the L^2 multiplier velocity. Parity identifies that with the canonical convention, as already noted in the V207 audit (item A3).

3. Regularity actually used. The proof asserts C^infinity coefficients near Q. For the velocity of the actual trial this uses pseudo-locality of the order-zero multiplier, since rho0 is smooth near Q in the inner modal region and its far part is smooth, not only the modal form of the density. The C^3 regularity of V from proof 202 and proof 33 would already suffice for every step: C^3 coordinates, h_n in C^2 with compact support, and N(h_n) in C^1 with compact support. So this is not a gap.

4. Dependency record. Proof 200 (V204, SHA 91aea5b2...) supplies |c[g]| <= (4/pi)||g||_E2. It is cited in the text but is missing from the dependencies_sha256 list in evidence/check.json. I suggest adding it.

5. Unused or loose bounds. The second bound in (8), the local C^alpha bound for U[k_n], is never used. The first bound holds without the log factor: the strip has width 1/n, so the annular contributions sum to O(n^(-alpha)). Only the sup on the inner tube is needed.

6. Order of choices. The text chooses eta and tau after the inner tube and P0. The intended reading is: first the tube, on which w.grad z >= b_*, P0 is quadratic and zeta vanishes, with z in [-z0, z0] and s in an interval containing [0, tau]; then tau smaller than the tube length; then n large enough that 1/n < z0 and the strips stay inside the boxes. With this reading every constant is independent of n.

7. Meaning of "does not close". What is proved is discontinuity at 0, and unboundedness on graph balls about 0, of N and of L_delta^(-1) N on the smooth core. This rules out the semilinear perturbative map h -> -L_delta^(-1)(F + N(h)) on any E_2 or graph ball about 0. It says nothing about a quasilinear scheme in which U[h].grad is absorbed into the transport, as in section 5 of S2_CALPHA_THEORY.md, or about a change of unknown. Proof 27 says this explicitly. Proof 207 does not, and the theory author's section 10 phrase "so it does not close a nonlinear iteration either" is best read as "a perturbative (semilinear) iteration". I recommend adding that qualifier. It is not a mathematical error.

8. Dependence on the construction. The sequence h_n depends on which localized operator is used (frozen or variable), because z is a first integral of that operator's field. K, c1 and c2 depend on the fixed delta and on the construction. The proof claims no uniformity in delta and needs none.

9. Where the obstruction lives. The weight is harmless only because K is a fixed set at positive distance from x = 0, and the constants degrade like d0^(-2). The result shows a failure in the interior of the first quadrant. It does not claim, and does not need, a separate mechanism near the axes or the origin. The nonlocal image near the axes is handled entirely by the proof 203 bound, as Check 1 confirms.
