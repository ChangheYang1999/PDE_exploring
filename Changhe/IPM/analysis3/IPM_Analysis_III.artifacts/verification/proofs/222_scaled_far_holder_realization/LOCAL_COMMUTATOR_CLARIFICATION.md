# Distributional local terms in the differentiated commutator

When differentiating the locally integrable order-minus1 commutator kernel, the principal-value derivative may include a diagonal term depending on the truncation convention. It is a smooth coefficient, formed from derivatives of chi and an angular kernel moment, multiplied by h(X). This term is C^alpha on each fixed disk and is bounded by the local C^alpha estimate in the proof. It does not change the C^(1,alpha) commutator bound.

Equivalently, retain the inner-boundary term while differentiating a truncated integral, take its limit using h(X-z)-h(X)=O(|z|^alpha), and bound its surviving smooth multiple of h(X). The remaining homogeneous principal kernels z_m partial_l K_i have zero angular mean by the annular flux calculation in the proof. The local multiplier in U2 itself commutes exactly with chi. Thus no derivative of h or unproved global E2 norm is hidden in the localization argument.
