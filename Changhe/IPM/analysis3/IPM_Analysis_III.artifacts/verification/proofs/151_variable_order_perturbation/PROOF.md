# The missing logarithm in a variable-order transport commutator

## Statement

Let s be a nonconstant real smooth bounded order on the cylinder C=R times S1, with all derivatives bounded and inf s>2. Let A=Op(e^(N Lambda)<xi>^s(X)), with Lambda smooth and bounded as in proof 144. There is a fixed smooth compactly supported vector field B and a sequence of smooth compactly supported v_n such that

 ||v_n||H^s<=C,       ||grad v_n||infinity -> 0,
 ||[A,B.grad]v_n||2 >= c log n-C,                       (1)

where c>0. Consequently the estimate

 ||[A,B.grad]v||2
 <=C_s[||grad B||infinity ||v||H^s
                    +||B||H^s ||grad v||infinity]      (2)

is false for a variable order, even with a fixed smooth B. Adding ||B||infinity times ||v||H^s does not repair it. The coefficient of the missing term is B.grad s. This refutes the unmodified full-transport commutator proposed in the theory author's note33. It does not refute a bound for sum_j[A,B_j]partial_j, nor a proof which isolates the logarithm and uses its sign for the total field.

## A localized high-frequency construction

Choose a point X_0 where grad s is nonzero. Choose a constant vector b and a small coordinate ball Q on which b.grad s>=c_0>0. Let chi be a nonzero smooth real function supported strictly inside Q. Choose B smooth with compact radial support, equal to b on a neighborhood of Q. For the periodic coordinate choose an integer angular frequency, or simply use a nonzero radial frequency; write the resulting phase as n nu.X.

Use a properly supported local elliptic parametrix P of A on a slightly larger ball. Its principal symbol is e^(-N Lambda)<xi>^(-s(X)). Multiply its output by a cutoff equal to one on a neighborhood of Q, and put

 w_n=chi exp(i n nu.X),        v_n=P w_n.

The cutoff and the parametrix can be chosen with support in the region where B=b. Every off-diagonal cutoff error is O(n^(-K)) in any prescribed fixed Sobolev norm, by integration by parts against the linear phase. The parametrix identities of proof 144 therefore give

 A v_n=w_n+O(n^(-K)) in L2,
 ||v_n||H^s<=C,
 ||grad v_n||infinity<=C_epsilon n^(1-inf_Q s+epsilon).  (3)

The last estimate follows directly by applying the smooth symbol expansion to the localized plane wave. Each X derivative of <n nu>^(-s(X)) introduces at most one log n, absorbed in n^epsilon. Choose epsilon<inf_Q s-1. Thus the last quantity tends to zero. The same expansion applies to the errors after an arbitrarily long finite parametrix construction; no inverse of the bare A at low frequency is assumed.

On this support B is constant. The exact differentiation identity is

 [A,b.grad]v=-Op(b.grad a)v,
 b.grad a=a[N b.grad Lambda+(b.grad s)log<xi>].          (4)

The smooth composition formula, or proof 147 with a smooth B, gives on Q

 [A,B.grad]v_n
 =-(B.grad s)log<D>w_n-N(B.grad Lambda)w_n+O(1) in L2.   (5)

The terms containing derivatives of B have principal order zero after reduction and are uniformly bounded; their support is separated from that of chi, so they can also be put in the rapidly decreasing error. The relative negative-order composition errors tend to zero, allowing harmless powers of log n.

For the fixed smooth chi,

 log<D>w_n=(log n)w_n+O(1) in L2.                      (6)

This follows from the Fourier multiplier formula: on frequencies within n|nu|/2 of n nu, the difference of the logarithms is bounded by C(1+|eta|)/n plus the constant log|nu|, while the remaining frequencies are controlled by the rapid Fourier decay of chi. Equations(5)-(6) imply

 ||[A,B.grad]v_n||2 >= c_0||chi||2 log n-C.

The right side of (2) is bounded by (3), because B is fixed and smooth. This proves the contradiction. Taking real and imaginary parts gives a real sequence if desired, after choosing a subsequence or taking the larger of their commutator norms. Reflected packets on disjoint parity-related balls give the same contradiction in the even-x, odd-y density class.

## Why the IPM velocity restriction does not remove the term

At any physical point away from the two axes, the values of canonical IPM velocities generated by smooth parity-P densities supported away from the axes span the two-dimensional velocity plane. Here is a direct local argument. Put a smooth density bump in a ball of radius o(epsilon) about X_0-epsilon e, where e is a chosen unit direction, and reflect it with the prescribed density parity. At X_0 the principal bump contributes the kernel vector

 (-sin(2 theta),cos(2 theta))/(2 pi epsilon^2)

times its mass, up to an arbitrarily small relative error. Its reflected bumps are a fixed positive distance from X_0, so their contributions are O(mass). Choose two directions for which these kernel vectors are independent. For sufficiently small epsilon the two full velocity vectors remain independent. The normalization field c[g]X/3 contributes only O(mass), since the bumps remain away from the origin, and does not change this conclusion. These densities vanish in neighborhoods of both axes and the origin, so they satisfy the high-space trace and cubic-normalization conditions and have finite high norms.

The physical-to-cylinder velocity transformation is invertible at X_0. Thus one can choose a fixed such density g for which the cylinder perturbation B[g].grad s is nonzero at a point of nonconstant s; scale g to make its high norm arbitrarily small. The argument above with that smooth B[g] works locally, with bounded order-zero terms involving DB[g]. The leading logarithm remains. The velocity need not have compact support in this version; its smooth local behavior suffices for the lower bound. This paragraph asserts the local obstruction, not a global norm estimate for every velocity coefficient.

## Correct scope for a perturbation proof

The exact identity separates

 [A,B.grad]=sum_j[A,B_j]partial_j-sum_j B_j[partial_j,A].

Only the first sum has the usual coefficient-commutator principal part. The second contains -(B.grad s)log<D> after order reduction. For the full perturbed transport W+B, one must keep (W+B).grad s as a single coefficient and establish its nonnegativity, or impose and pay a positive logarithmic loss. Proof 152 supplies robust nonnegativity for one explicit choice of the order. A tame estimate for the remaining rough-coefficient terms is a separate obligation.
