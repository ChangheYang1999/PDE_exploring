The high input dyadic sum is finite; alpha<1/mu makes its largest term dominate with the required power. The low input sum converges for all alpha, and alpha<1/mu permits absorption into lambda^(-alpha). The low output block and its gradient have L2 kernels, so that block is Lipschitz with norm bounded by C||f||2. At the modal source both a mu>1 and mu<1 hold; the second fact supplies the whole range alpha<1. The upper condition a mu<A+1 from proof 26 is unnecessary throughout this low-order proof.

U in the whole-space source theorem is the L2 Fourier multiplier velocity. Its equality with the global-origin canonical velocity holds on globally even-x, odd-y inputs, since the far-subtraction constant then vanishes by parity. On arbitrary source-centered X_alpha inputs the theorem uses the multiplier convention explicitly.

The strain counterexample concentrates at the PHYSICAL ORIGIN, which in source coordinates is (-p,0). It proves unboundedness on X_alpha on the natural smooth domain of c. It does not prove unboundedness relative to the frozen-source graph norm, nor any obstruction caused by translation-invariant regularity in a fixed neighborhood of P. A cutoff supported away from the physical origin makes the strain kernel smooth and its functional bounded there.
