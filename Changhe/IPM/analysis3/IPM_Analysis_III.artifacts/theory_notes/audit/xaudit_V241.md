## VERDICT: PASS WITH REMARKS

I found no mathematical gap and no exponent error. All four mechanisms check out. The logarithmic velocity bound and the full E2 product bound for bounded E2 inputs are proved in the body of proof 214 and use no weighted L2 input. The pointwise envelope |U[h].grad rho0| <= CM r^(k-1)(1+log r) gives a weighted L2 tail O(R^(k-gamma/2)(1+log R)) for every gamma>2k. The M0 column bookkeeping keeps the E2 tail R^p(1+log R), which dominates for gamma>=2. The range argument of proof 220 uses the E2 norm only. ## Findings

1. REMARK (Exact statement, last paragraph, "The restriction gamma<2 was needed for the weighted velocity theorem used there"). The attribution is incomplete. In proof 214, gamma<2 entered in the annular block bound (8) and in the far integral of r^(-4) w_gamma^(-1) used to identify the L2 extension with the canonical velocity. In proof 217 it entered a second time. The E2 column tail R^(2k-1)(1+log R) and the proof 214 tail R^(k-1)(1+log R) were absorbed into the single exponent 2k-gamma/2 ("Because gamma<2, the exponent in (7) is larger than 2k-1", and "k+1-gamma/2>0"). V241 handles this second use correctly in its body: it retains R^p and says so after (7). Only the summary sentence is incomplete. Suggested repair: "gamma<2 entered through the weighted velocity theorem of proof 214 and through the absorption of the E2 column tail into the weighted exponent in proof 217; neither is needed here."

2. REMARK (Section "The E2 bounds", display (2), "canonical velocity convention"). V241 does not restate how U[h] is defined for an E2 input that lies in no L2(w_gamma) with gamma<2. Exactly such inputs matter for gamma>=2. The definition exists. It is the far-subtracted kernel integral, equivalently the pointwise limit of U[chi(X/R)h] (proof 214, paragraph "The same estimates apply to general E2 inputs"). It converges absolutely at infinity for bounded, locally Holder h, and its subtraction constant vanishes by parity for every compact truncation. For h in Y_gamma with gamma<2 it equals the L2(w_gamma) extension (proof 214), so the new realizations restrict to the old ones on the old spaces. The convention involves no hidden choice. Take any other velocity with the same divergence and curl, the IPM parities (U1 odd in x, U2 odd in y) and o(r) growth. It differs from U[h] by a harmonic field of sublinear growth, which is constant by Liouville and therefore zero by parity. Also note that E2 to E2 boundedness of U[.].grad rho0 is proved in the body of proof 214 ("Thus the complete unnormalized coupling is bounded E2->E2 globally"; "This proves (1) for its E2 part"), not in its formal statement, which is phrased on Y with gamma<2. V241 correctly cites the body. Suggested repair: one sentence giving the definition, the identification with the earlier realizations, and the uniqueness argument.

3. REMARK (display (1)). The four-term bound is correct, and every exponent is negative for gamma>4k. It simplifies to C R^(max(2k-1, 2k-gamma/2))(1+log R) for C0 and C R^(max(k-1, k-gamma/2))(1+log R) for Cren, because 2k-1>k-1 and 2k-gamma/2>k-gamma/2. For 4k<gamma<2 the dominant term is the weighted column term, which reproduces proof 217 (1). For gamma>=2 it is the E2 column term R^(2k-1)(1+log R), as V241 says. For 4k<gamma<2 the new E2-input coupling tail R^(k-gamma/2)(1+log R) is weaker than the R^(k-1) of proof 214, which used the input weighted norm. This is consistent, not an error, since the column term dominates both. The constants C_gamma blow up as gamma decreases to 4k (from (4)), and in (3) as gamma decreases to 2k. This is qualitative and acceptable.

## What I verified independently, and how

- Hashes. I ran sha256sum on PROOF.md and on the five dependency PROOF.md files. All match check.json.
- Logarithmic velocity bound for bounded E2 inputs. I re-derived the proof 214 argument by hand. The local Holder constant is CM(1+|X|^2) on a unit disk, and the cancellation disk has radius (1+|X|^2)^(-1/alpha). The bounded-kernel shell out to 3(1+|X|) costs C_alpha M log(2+|X|), and so does the subtraction annulus. The far kernel difference O(|X||Y|^(-3)) contributes CM. Only ||h||_infinity, [h]_x and [h]_(y,2) enter, uniformly in h. The truncation limit uses dominated convergence with the far-subtracted kernel and needs no weighted input.
- E2 product bound at all radii, including the axis neighborhood. The divided coupling is a1(U1/x) + a2 U2, with |a1|<=Cr^(k-2) and |a2|<=Cr^(k-3). The growth factors in proof 214 (4) are (1+|x|) and (1+x^2), and they give |a1|(1+|x|) + |a2|(1+x^2) <= C r^(k-1). The coefficient-difference terms are r^(k-3)M and r^(k-4)M log r. The tail version on r>=R gives R^(k-1)(1+log R), and the cutoff derivative gives R^(k-3)M|d|. None of this depends on gamma. The previous V224 audit reached the same conclusion.
- Weighted coupling tail (3). The exponent is 2(k-1) - gamma + 1 = 2k-1-gamma, and the integral converges exactly when gamma>2k. With sympy I checked the closed form of the integral of exp(-lam s)(A+s)^2, namely (A^2 lam^2 + 2A lam + 2)/lam^3. With mpmath I checked numerically that the ratio of the tail integral to R^(2k-gamma)(1+log R)^2 stays bounded for gamma = 2k+0.05, 4k+0.01, 1, 2 and 3.5 at R = 1e2, 1e4, 1e6.
- Column tail (4). The exponent is 2p+1-gamma = 4k-1-gamma, and the square root gives R^(2k-gamma/2)(1+log R), since p+1 = 2k exactly (sympy). The numerical ratios are bounded for gamma = 4k+0.02, 1, 2 and 3.
- Column E2 tail (7). I proved the interpolation inequality [f]_alpha <= 2||f||_infinity^(1-alpha)||Df||_infinity^alpha by splitting at ||f||/||Df||. The resulting exponents are p-alpha (horizontal), p-2-alpha (divided vertical) and p (supremum). r^p(1+log r) and r^(p-2)(1+log r) are eventually decreasing, so the suprema over r>=R are attained near R. The bounds in (6) agree with proof 217 (5) and (6), with M0 = m0 outside the support of H0.
- Dominant exponents. For gamma = 0.72, 1, 1.9 the dominant term is 2k-gamma/2. For gamma = 2, 2.5, 4 it is p. All four exponents are negative for every gamma>4k tested.
- Weighted volume. The integral of r(1+r^2)^(-gamma/2) grows like log at gamma=2 and converges at gamma=2.1. This confirms that Y_gamma = E2 for gamma>2 and that Y_2 is a proper subspace.
- Orbit estimates of proof 220. Exact identities from sympy: p ell = 2-ell, ell(k-1) = 1-ell and ell k = 1. The fitted exponent of the forcing integral up to tau_x matches k+b to seven digits for b = 0.55, 0.75 and 0.95. The ratio of the integral of min(x^2, (1+t)exp(-ell t)) to x^2(1+log(1/x)) stays at about 0.367 for x = 1e-3, 1e-6 and 1e-12. The M0 orbit integrand (1+t)exp((1-ell)t) is integrable.
- E2-only character of the range argument. I traced each occurrence of M and of the weighted norm in proof 220 (Finding 7). I checked the characteristic formula and its sign. I checked that D_(Y_gamma) is contained in D_(E2). I checked that the forcing lies in E2 for every b>0 and in L2(w_gamma) whenever 2b+gamma>1.
- Closedness. Both norms control local uniform convergence. V is C^1 with div V = 2 ell, so V.grad h is a well-defined distribution and passes to limits. Bounded perturbations preserve closedness and the maximal domain. F0 is in E2, and it lies in L2(w_gamma) exactly when gamma>4k (proof 213, with the leading term r^(2k-1)S_0 of proof 117).

## Scope I consider proved

Fix 0<alpha<1 and the canonical velocity (the far-subtracted, parity-compatible velocity, unique among those of sublinear growth). Take the dependencies as given: topics 117, 199, 201, 203, 204, 213, 214, 217 and 220, all previously audited as passing with remarks. Then the following hold.

1. U[.].grad rho0 is bounded E2 to E2. It is also bounded E2 to L2(w_gamma) for every gamma>2k, with weighted tail O(R^(k-gamma/2)(1+log R)) and E2 tail O(R^(k-1)(1+log R)).
2. C0 = U[.].grad rho0 + c[.]M0 and Cren = U[.].grad rho0 + c[.]H0 are bounded on E2 and on Y_gamma for every gamma>4k, with no upper bound on gamma. Their output tails obey (1), equivalently O(R^(max(2k-1, 2k-gamma/2))(1+log R)) for C0 and O(R^(max(k-1, k-gamma/2))(1+log R)) for Cren. All of these exponents are negative.
3. T, Lren and A0 are closed on their maximal transport domains in E2 and in each Y_gamma, and these domains are the same for all three operators.
4. F0 lies in E2 and in every Y_gamma with gamma>4k.
5. On E2 and on every Y_gamma with gamma>4k, T, Lren and A0 have infinite-dimensional algebraic cokernel. The cosets of f_b, b in (1/2,1), are linearly independent. Hence these operators are not onto and not Fredholm, and no restriction of the domain restores surjectivity.

For gamma>2 these Y_gamma statements coincide with the E2 statements. Not proved and not claimed: anything about whether F0 lies in the range, injectivity, inverses, closed range, the scaled space Z of proof 222, or existence or nonexistence of a profile.
