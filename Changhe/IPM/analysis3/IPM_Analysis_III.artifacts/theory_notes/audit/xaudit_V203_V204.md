## Numbered issues

### proof 199 (V203)

199-1. Lines 11-14: "For bounded data its far kernel is subtracted at X=0. Thus U(0)=0 and its distributional Fourier symbols away from frequency0 are ...". For bounded, non-decaying h, the symbol determines U only up to the frequency-0 ambiguity. The proof never uses Fourier analysis on h itself: it uses U[g] for the compact g and the subtracted kernel for h-g. Why it matters: a reader might think (6) is being applied to non-L^2 data. *Cosmetic.* Suggested wording: "U[h]:=U[g]+U_sub[h-g]. For compact g the subtracted and unsubtracted definitions agree, by parity."

199-2. Line 41, estimate (3), "<=C ||h||_(E_p)". The proof of (3) uses only ||h||_inf+[h]_(y,p). The x-seminorm [h]_x enters only through (11) and the x-seminorm of B_delta h. Also, (3) holds for every p>=1, not only p<=2, and p>=1 is **sharp** for (3). Take h_eps=eps^(p+alpha)H(X/eps) with H a fixed parity bump. Its E_p norms are bounded, but [V_1[h_eps](eps x0,.)]_alpha scales like eps^(p-1). Why it matters: this confirms that the lower limit p>=1 in the statement is genuine, not a technical artifact. *Cosmetic (strengthening plus sharpness note).*

199-3. Line 64: "A slightly larger frequency cutoff gives ||partial_y f_j||<=C 2^j||f_j|| ...", applied at line 101 to the quotient blocks f_j=U_1[g_j]/x. Bernstein's inequality needs f_j(x,.) to be y-band-limited. This is true: U_1 commutes with D_y multipliers, division by x acts in the other variable, and the x=0 limit d_xU_1[g_j](0,.) has the same spectrum. The proof does not say so. *Cosmetic.* Add one sentence.

199-4. Line 77: "Two integrations in omega give an integrable factor(1+lambda|y|)^(-2)". Here "y" means the y-displacement w, not the point y. *Cosmetic (notation).*

199-5. Line 105: "with |z|<=5, have uniformly bounded L2 norms and first weak derivative L2 norms". The bounds are in fact uniform in every z in R, so the restriction is harmless. The step that really uses compact x-support is the finite length (8) of the x'-integral. That holds because g_0 vanishes for |x'|>4, since Delta_0 acts only in y. *Cosmetic.*

199-6. Line 107: "For compact g this follows directly from its mean-zero order-zero kernel". U_2=T_xx-I/2 also contains the local term -g/2, which is trivially C^alpha. *Cosmetic.*

199-7. Lines 131-135: "(11) bounds U_2 by C delta^alpha M on the cutoff support. Its product difference ..." and "Pairs across the support boundary follow from the same product differences". The argument is correct, but only with the right choice of product split. When exactly one of y,y' lies in supp chi_delta(x,.), write f(y)U(y)-f(y')U(y') with U evaluated at the in-support point. Then only sup_{B_delta}|U_2|<=C delta^alpha M and the V_1 bound on [-1,1] are used. *Cosmetic.* State the choice explicitly.

199-8. Line 151: "For the sum norm used here the displayed maximum therefore applies exactly." This is an upper bound, ||R0||<=max(1,1/kappa_p), not an equality. Line 26 also uses D for both D=2A+1 and the domain D(T0). *Cosmetic.*

199-9. Lines 161-173, the counterexample. It is correct, but it is "soft": the singularity of d_xU_1 sits off the axes, where the weight |x|^p is bounded below, so B_delta h in E_p is immediate. It refutes the literal wording of S2_CALPHA 2(d), "does not map into C^alpha_p for p>1 ... unless d_x U_1[h] is bounded, that is, unless d_x h is controlled" (h here also has unbounded d_x h). The substantive refutation of the 0.5227 barrier and of "an x-derivative of h must enter the norm" is the operator bound (1) together with (2). Why it matters: when the theory author integrates this result, cite (1)+(2) as the refutation and the example only as an illustration. *Cosmetic.*
