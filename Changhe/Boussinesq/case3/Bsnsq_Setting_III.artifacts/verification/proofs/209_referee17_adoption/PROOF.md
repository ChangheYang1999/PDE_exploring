# Endpoint continuation and quantifier audit of the referee17 edits

Written 2026-10-21 before the text checker. This is a conditional corollary and edit audit, not a new full-class nonexistence theorem.

## Statement and dependencies

Take a smooth beta=2 B2 profile in the lead's setting4.7(iii). Let p be an endpoint of its maximal connected zero arc gamma. In the orthonormal physical coordinates (s,z) at p, write the strong unstable curve as C={s=sigma(z)}, sigma(0)=sigma'(0)=0, and its exterior side as O'={s>sigma(z)}. Assume one of: p is outgoing; p is incoming and every zero of W off gamma is isolated; p is accumulating and some neighbourhood of p contains no arc of zeros of W off gamma. Under these assumptions, P203, P204 and P206 verified respectively that Omega=0 on O' near p and that no real analytic vector field on a neighbourhood of p agrees with W on a measurable subset of O' having positive area in every neighbourhood of p. The latter assertion is4.7(xix)(a), transferred by(e) and(xx)(d). The following harmonic-extension exclusion is a corollary. This list gives established sufficient hypotheses, not a theorem that all other endpoint types admit continuation.

## 1. Harmonic continuation of velocity is impossible through p

Suppose a vector field U_tilde on a neighbourhood D of p has harmonic components and equals the given U on D intersect O' near p. Harmonic functions are real analytic on their open domain. Thus W_tilde=2X+U_tilde-U(0) is real analytic on D and equals W wherever the velocities agree. For every sufficiently small disk about p, its intersection with O' is a nonempty open set: sigma(0)=0 and (s,0) is in O' for every small positive s. It therefore has positive area. This contradicts the verified continuation assertion. The same argument works when agreement is merely on a measurable set of positive area in every neighbourhood of p. No assertion at a different point of C follows from this endpoint argument, because the flat reduced zero-curve jet used in the continuation theorem is at p. In particular, continuation through another point by a different harmonic field remains separate from the already proved fact that the actual U is not harmonic on a whole neighbourhood of that point.

## 2. Common analytic interval versus variable zero intervals

The transfer of4.7(xiv)(d) at an accumulating endpoint assumes, for contradiction, a common delta>0 such that z->U(0,z) is real analytic on(-delta,delta) and z->Omega(s,z) is real analytic there for every s in(0,delta). The side theorem supplies an open neighbourhood N of p on whose intersection with O' the vorticity is zero. Choose a in(0,delta) so that (s,0) belongs to N for every s in(0,a). Such points belong to O' because sigma(0)=0. For each such s, openness supplies an epsilon_s>0 such that Omega(s,z)=0 for |z|<epsilon_s. No uniform positive lower bound on epsilon_s is required or claimed. The identity theorem for the one-variable analytic function z->Omega(s,z) makes it zero on the entire connected interval(-delta,delta). Thus Omega=0 on(0,a)x(-delta,delta). The already verified proof of4.7(xii)(c), as used in(xiv)(d), then constructs the real analytic continuation and its zero arc beyond p, contradicting maximality of gamma. This establishes the precise replacement R4; the original wording could misleadingly suggest a uniform zero interval before analytic continuation.

## 3. Summary and scope equivalences

R3 adds exactly the positive-area-in-every-neighbourhood qualification of4.7(xix)(a). Agreement on an unspecified part of O' is too vague to encode that theorem; agreement on a zero-area set, or on a set separated from p, is insufficient for its proof. R2a lists the outgoing, isolated incoming, and no-extra-zero-arc accumulating hypotheses above; R2b operates within the already incoming-and-isolated scope of4.7(xiv).

R5a separates three statements without merging their hypotheses: the necessary failure of analytic U near any endpoint (xii)(a); the failure of a constant-temperature neighbourhood at any endpoint (xxi); and the outgoing-only transverse-analyticity and finite-separated-representation exclusions (xii)(c),(d). R5b and R5c delete special cases of the second statement, already present for all endpoints. They retain the incoming isolation and accumulating no-arc hypotheses for the whole-side, transverse-analyticity and nonanalytic-curve claims. R5d lists the same three sufficient endpoint hypotheses as Section1 and distinguishes directional bounds for the velocity on O' uniformly up to p from directional bounds for vorticity on a full neighbourhood of p. Bounds for W and U are equivalent here: W-U is affine, so derivatives of order>=2 are identical and the order0,1 bounds can be absorbed by changing constants on a bounded neighbourhood. Gevrey order greater than2 is still not excluded. If Z=gamma, the prior type theorem makes both endpoints outgoing, so the stated both-endpoints consequence is valid.
