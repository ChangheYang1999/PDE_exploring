# Exact angular blind directions in the signed-source scan

## Statement, hypotheses and scope

The frozen scan uses the radial map r=L((1-t)^(-b)-1), L=3/2 and b=3, degree-five clamped B-splines on 40 intervals, K=16 cosine modes, and 12 midpoint angular samples phi_j=(j+1/2)pi/12. Here x=r sin(phi), y=-r cos(phi), 0<phi<pi, so no y reflection or wall is imposed. Write

    F(r,phi)=(1-t)^(b+2) sum_(k=0)^15 sum_(ell>=min(k,2)) c_(k,ell) B_ell(t) cos(k phi),
    Theta=x^2 F.

There are Nb=45 radial splines and 691 active coefficients. The fit samples seven Gauss points in each radial interval, hence 280 radii. Its positive row weight is (1-t)^(-(b+2)). Let A be the resulting weighted value sampling map from the active coefficients to R^(280*12).

Theorem. A has rank 519 and nullity 172 in exact arithmetic. Its kernel is generated by the following four families, one vector per ell=2,...,44:

    c_(12,ell)=1;
    c_(9,ell)=c_(15,ell)=1;
    c_(10,ell)=c_(14,ell)=1;
    c_(11,ell)=c_(13,ell)=1.

The coefficient vectors not listed are zero. There are smooth, compactly supported, x-even temperature perturbations with zero values at every sample point and zero jets at the center, but nonzero Theta_x at those sample points. Consequently a small sampled value-fit residual alone does not control the forcing derivative used in the vorticity transport equation. The analogous G fit has the same kernel, but its canonical center-strain functional vanishes on that kernel. This last fact does not remove temperature-gradient errors in the transported G values.

This theorem concerns the literal scan representation and nodes in the frozen JSON and source text. It is not nonexistence for B2, not proof that every reported negative value is false, and not a rigorous error bound for a stored transported field. Attribution of the three negative witnesses to this aliasing needs a separate controlled computation.

## 1. Angular identities and full kernel

For phi_j=(j+1/2)pi/12, cos(12 phi_j)=0. Since 24 phi_j=(2j+1)pi,

    cos((24-k)phi_j)=-cos(k phi_j), k=9,10,11.

The twelve vectors cos(k phi_j), k=0,...,11, are linearly independent. To prove this, write z_j=cos(phi_j), which are twelve distinct real numbers. The polynomial cos(k phi)=T_k(cos(phi)) has degree k with nonzero leading coefficient. A combination through degree11 vanishing at all twelve z_j is the zero polynomial, and all its coefficients in this basis vanish.

The sixteen-mode value map therefore observes exactly the combinations of modes0,...,8 unchanged and modes9-11 minus their aliased partners15-13. Mode12 is invisible. All four affected families share the same radial mask ell>=2. There is no cancellation between different observable angular modes because the twelve vectors are independent.

The univariate B-spline collocation matrix on these radial nodes has full column rank, as does each restricted set ell>=1 or ell>=2. Indeed, a degree-five spline vanishing at seven distinct interior points in every knot interval is identically zero on every interval. B-spline linear independence then forces its coefficient vector to vanish. This also proves independence of the restricted columns. Multiplication by the strictly positive radial weight and factor is invertible on rows.

Hence the observable dimension is 45 for mode0, 44 for mode1 and 43 each for modes2,...,11: 45+44+10*43=519. The total coefficient dimension is 45+44+14*43=691. The four listed independent families span the remaining172 dimensions. This proves both the exact rank and the entire kernel, not merely an upper bound on the rank.

## 2. Smooth invisible perturbations alter the forcing derivative

Choose an interior radial index ell whose B-spline support lies in a compact subinterval of (0,1). The spline itself has only finite regularity at knots. For a smooth counterexample to derivative control independent of the spline regularity, choose instead any nonzero C-infinity function q(r) supported in an annulus 0<r0<r<r1 and positive at some sampled radius. Put

    deltaTheta(x,y)=x^2 q(r) cos(12 phi).

The function is smooth on R^2 because its support avoids the origin, and it extends by zero across the annulus boundaries. The angular factor is smooth for r>0, even in x, and defined on the full circle. It also vanishes on x=0 because of the x^2 factor. All center jets vanish. Every angular value sample of deltaTheta is zero. At such a point the terms obtained by differentiating x^2 and q(r) vanish, leaving

    partial_x deltaTheta = -12 x^2 q(r) (cos(phi)/r) sin(12 phi).

Here partial_x phi=cos(phi)/r, sin(12 phi_j)=(-1)^j, x>0, and cos(phi_j) is never zero because none of the twelve midpoint angles equals pi/2. The derivative is therefore nonzero at every such point where q(r)>0. Scaling q by any real constant leaves all value samples zero and makes the derivative arbitrarily large. This exact smooth construction is not asserted to lie in the finite B-spline space. The actual finite-dimensional kernel contains the same angular defect, with q=(1-t)^(b+2)B_ell(t); it is C4 and has the same first-derivative formula.

A positive radial row weight cannot reveal these directions. The Moore-Penrose inverse used for the F value fit selects the coefficient vector perpendicular to this nullspace. That is an algebraic choice, not a consequence of temperature transport or a control on its derivatives between angular samples.

## 3. Why the direct G-fit strain is different

For G=sum g_k(r)cos(k phi), Omega=r sin(phi)G. Product-to-sum gives its second sine mode

    Omega_2(r)=r(g_1(r)-g_3(r))/2.

The canonical center strain, when the integral converges, equals

    c=-(1/2) integral_0^infinity Omega_2(r) dr/r
     =-(1/4) integral_0^infinity (g_1(r)-g_3(r)) dr.

This follows directly from c=(1/pi) integral_R2 x y Omega/|X|^4 dX, x reflection, and sine orthogonality on(0,pi). In the stated radial basis the high-mode kernel affects neither g1 nor g3. Thus this functional annihilates all172 null directions of the G value-fit map, whenever defined. Its uniqueness on a given sampled G vector does not validate that vector, which in the scan is reconstructed by integrating the derivative of the fitted temperature along trajectories.

## 4. Independent checks and next experiment

The proof above precedes new code. Freeze the scan JSON, relevant source text and published witness values. Check the kernel with exact polynomial remainder arithmetic using the roots of T12 and independently evaluate numerical matrices. A standalone synthetic smooth or polynomial angular control should exhibit the derivative defect. The planned numerical experiment holds the literal Gaussian velocity fixed, reconstructs temperature values by adaptive characteristics, compares 12-angle and resolved fits of the same values, and integrates each fitted Theta_x independently along the same backward paths. Direct gradient transport from P236 supplies a separate reference. That experiment can identify the effect for the chosen source, but cannot certify whole-plane profile existence.

## Dependencies

Only the elementary root bound for polynomials, B-spline linear independence, the product rule and sine orthogonality are used in the theorem. The data provenance uses the frozen NUM scan settings and static inspection of its fit, not execution or trust in its numerical conclusions. The prior P236 direct-gradient witness computations are a separate numerical dependency for the planned experiment. No Lean build or formal proof assistant is used.
