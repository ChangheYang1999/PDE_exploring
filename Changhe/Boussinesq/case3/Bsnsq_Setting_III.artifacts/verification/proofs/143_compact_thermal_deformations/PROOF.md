# Compact changes preserving the temperature equation

Written 2026-10-21T08:10Z. The arguments below concern necessary constraints and an exact linearized parametrization. They do not construct a B2 profile, solve an annular repair problem, or identify a full cokernel.

## 1. Statements and notation

Let beta >= 2, k = beta - 2. For j=0,1 let W_j be smooth vector fields on R^2 with div W_j = 2 beta, and let Theta_j be smooth real functions satisfying W_j . grad Theta_j = k Theta_j. Suppose W_1-W_0 and Theta_1-Theta_0 have compact support. Choose any compact disk K whose interior contains both supports.

**Theorem A.** For every continuous function F on an interval containing the ranges of both temperatures on K,

    integral_K F(Theta_1) = integral_K F(Theta_0).

In particular, the two finite measures obtained by pushing forward area on K under Theta_0 and Theta_1 are equal. No integral of either full-plane temperature power is assumed finite. The conclusion only compares changes in a common compact set.

The residual convention is f=(W.grad+1)Omega-Theta_x and t=W.grad Theta-kTheta.

For the remaining statements set a != 0, q = 4a/beta, lambda1 = (beta-2)/2, lambda2 = 1+3beta/2, and use the exact polynomial configuration

    W = (lambda1 x, lambda2 y - q x^2/2),
    Theta = a x^2, Omega = q x.

This solves both profile transport equations; div W = 2beta and -curl(W-beta X)=Omega. It is used as a local polynomial patch, not as an admissible full-plane profile. Write grad^perp h=(-h_y,h_x).

Let chi and tau be smooth functions with common compact support separated from x=0. They represent a stream perturbation and a temperature perturbation. Their linearized thermal residual is

    t_dot = W . grad tau - k tau - 2a x chi_y.

**Theorem B.** The identity t_dot=0 is equivalent to the existence of a unique smooth compactly supported eta, also separated from x=0, such that

    tau = 2a x eta_y,
    chi = D eta,             D = W . grad - 2beta.

If chi is odd and tau is even in x, then eta is odd in x. The vector perturbation grad^perp chi is the derivative at zero of the pushforward of W by the area-preserving flow generated by grad^perp eta; tau is the corresponding derivative of the pullback of Theta. The support of eta can fill vertical gaps in the support of tau. It is contained in its vertical convex hull, not necessarily in the original support set.

**Corollary C.** Every perturbation in Theorem B has linearized vorticity residual L eta, where

    L eta = -(W . grad + 1) Delta D eta - q partial_y D eta
            - 2a partial_x(x eta_y).

For any smooth test weight p on a neighborhood of the support, integration by parts gives integral p L eta = integral eta L^*p, where

    L^*p = (W . grad + 4beta) Delta[-W . grad p + (1-2beta)p]
           - q (W . grad + 4beta)p_y - 2a x p_xy.

In particular L^*1=L^*x=L^*y=0. This is not an assertion that these three weights span its nullspace or that their vanishing moments suffice to solve L eta=f.

## 2. Proof of Theorem A

For every integer n>=1 the product rule gives

    div(W_j Theta_j^n) = [2beta + n k] Theta_j^n.

Subtract the two identities. The vector field W_1 Theta_1^n-W_0 Theta_0^n has compact support in the interior of K. Its divergence integrates to zero, so

    [2beta+n k] integral_K(Theta_1^n-Theta_0^n)=0.

The coefficient is positive when beta>=2. All polynomial moments agree; the constant moments agree because the same set K is used. This argument integrates compact differences only.

For completeness, polynomials approximate any continuous F uniformly on the compact interval [-M,M] containing both ranges. After a linear change of variable to [0,1], the Bernstein polynomials are the expectations E[F(B_n/n)] with B_n binomial(n,t). Uniform continuity gives an arbitrarily small error on |B_n/n-t|<delta, and the error on its complement is at most 2||F||_infinity/(4n delta^2), by the variance bound. This proves uniform approximation without differentiating F. Passing to the limit in the polynomial integral identities proves the displayed identity.

The measures are equal: continuous functions determine finite measures on a compact interval. Explicitly, continuous piecewise-linear approximations from above and below to interval indicators show equality at every common continuity point of the two distribution functions. Each has at most countably many discontinuities, and right continuity then gives equality at every point. Half-open intervals determine the measures. This proves Theorem A.

The same calculation at one exact configuration gives a useful linearized necessary identity. If delta W is divergence free and delta W,tau are compact, then

    integral Theta^(n-1) t_dot
       = -[2beta+n k] integral Theta^(n-1) tau,   n>=1.

Indeed, integrate the W.grad tau term by parts and use W.grad Theta=kTheta. The remaining term integral Theta^(n-1) delta W.grad Theta is (1/n) integral delta W.grad(Theta^n)=0. This identity is meaningful even when Theta or its powers grow at infinity because every integrand containing a perturbation is compactly supported.

## 3. Proof of Theorem B

Assume t_dot=0 and put T(x)=integral_R tau(x,y)dy. Compact support justifies differentiation and integration by parts in y. Integrating the equation gives

    lambda1 x T'(x) - (lambda2+k)T(x)=0.

For beta>2, lambda1>0. Uniqueness for this regular linear ODE on either open half-axis, together with T=0 for large |x|, gives T=0 on both half-axes. Its support stays away from the axis, so T=0 everywhere. For beta=2 the equation is -4T=0, with the same conclusion.

Define

    eta(x,y) = (1/(2a x)) integral_{-infinity}^y tau(x,z) dz

on x!=0 and set eta=0 in a neighborhood of the axis. This is smooth because the support stays away from x=0. The vanishing vertical integral makes eta zero both below and above a fixed bounding rectangle of the support. Hence eta is compactly supported. Its x support is contained in that of tau; its y support may fill gaps. The displayed relation tau=2ax eta_y holds. For even tau, the formula makes eta odd in x.

Use partial_y W_1=0, partial_y W_2=lambda2 and lambda1-lambda2-k=-2beta. Direct differentiation gives

    W . grad(2ax eta_y) - k(2ax eta_y)
       = 2ax partial_y[(W . grad-2beta)eta].

Thus t_dot=0 implies partial_y(chi-D eta)=0 for x!=0. Both chi and D eta have compact support. A function independent of y and compactly supported must vanish, proving chi=D eta. The same calculation proves the converse. Uniqueness follows from eta_y=tau/(2ax) and compact support in y.

Let h=grad^perp eta and Phi_epsilon its flow. Since div h=0, this flow preserves area. Differentiating the pushforward and pullback gives

    d/d epsilon [(Phi_epsilon)_*W] at0 = Dh W-DW h,
    d/d epsilon [Theta o Phi_epsilon^(-1)] at0 = -h.grad Theta.

The identity div W=2beta implies

    Dh W-DW h = grad^perp[(W.grad-2beta)eta].

For this polynomial W the identity can also be checked component by component. The second derivative equals 2ax eta_y. These are exactly grad^perp chi and tau. This proves Theorem B. It describes the infinitesimal perturbation; it does not claim that an arbitrary finite perturbation is the time-one map of this particular generator.

## 4. Proof of Corollary C and relation to residual moments

The linearized vorticity is -Delta chi. Differentiating the vorticity residual at the polynomial configuration gives

    f_dot = -(W.grad+1)Delta chi - q chi_y - partial_x tau.

Substitute Theorem B to obtain L. Compact support removes all boundary terms. The formal adjoints of W.grad+1 and D are -W.grad+1-2beta and -W.grad-4beta. Reversing the compositions, with Delta self-adjoint, gives the stated L^*. The adjoint of partial_x x partial_y is partial_y x partial_x=x partial_xy, which gives its last term.

For p=1 or x all terms vanish. For p=y the bracket inside the Laplacian is qx^2/2+(1-2beta-lambda2)y, whose Laplacian is q. The first term is 4beta q and the second is -4beta q. Hence L^*y=0. This also agrees with the affine compact-residual moment identities in packet142.

The weight p0=xy/(x^2+y^2)^2 is smooth on any support separated from the origin. Direct differentiation gives

    L^*p0(x,0) = -3q(17beta+28)/(2x^3),   x!=0.

This is the same nonzero variation calculated in packet140. Theorem B now shows that conjugacy generators describe every compact infinitesimal thermal-preserving perturbation on the polynomial patch, under the support hypotheses here. It still supplies no full nullspace calculation and no existence or nonexistence theorem for annular residual removal.

## 5. Scope, dependencies, and verification

Theorem A uses only constant divergence, the exact thermal equation, common compact supports, and beta>=2. It does not assume B2 parity or positivity. It preserves the distribution of temperature values on K, not their spatial arrangement. In a B2 application, both relative fields must use the same center gauge; a change that adds an exterior constant velocity is outside its compact-difference hypothesis.

Theorems B and C are local statements on a polynomial patch, with perturbations supported away from the axis. A general thermal-preserving perturbation on a curved non-polynomial configuration need not have this formula. No polynomial background is claimed to have sublinear full-plane velocity. Packet142 is only a comparison for the affine moments, and packet140 only a comparison for p0; all identities needed here are derived above.
