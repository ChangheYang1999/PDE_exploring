A prescribed history is a different experiment from a standard-start game. The original engine executes the prescribed initial actions. The wrapper loads each underlying entry before those rounds and skips its decisions until the prescribed history ends. A factory entry constructs its instance when loaded; the installed direct entry constructs its instance lazily on its first actual decision. The wrapper does not reset either policy at the boundary. Their decision states have not advanced through the prescribed history. That checks continuation behavior on a supplied position, including the installed bot's foreign-map branch. It does not show that the bot would choose those opening actions itself. For this reason each start has its own result row rather than being pooled into a standard-start win rate.

The earlier reserves also have separate meanings. Pool A tested best_v9a, with 10W/10D/0L from the standard start and 6W/12D/2L on each prescribed-history start. Its two initial 300-second timeouts were retained; later 500-second runs completed those conditions as draws. Pool B tested best_v9b and gave 20 wins on each of its three starts. Each reserve had 60 completed conditions and 30 distinct normalized histories. These are [A's](../../../runs/validation/final-best-v9a-poolA-final-audit-001.json) and [B's](../../../runs/validation/final-best-v9b-poolB-strict-001.json) exact finite tables. Different candidate versions and different opponent parameters prevent reading the two totals as a matched estimate of improvement. C tested best_v9e and the prespecified P5TNUBM comparison before its later reuse; its original [result](final_reserved_validation/RESULTS.md) remains a dated account, not a claim that later selection never happened.

Two physical assignments expose implementation asymmetry, but they do not automatically double independent evidence. The [assignment-symmetry proof](assignment_symmetry/PROOF.md) requires private policy state, invariance under renaming player identifiers, preserved random streams, no external timing dependence and the standard symmetric engine state, with balanced rules and identical private initialization. The policies must not depend on shared process state or external files. Under those hypotheses the original engine swaps player-indexed outcomes when the policies swap seats. The proof excludes asymmetric prescribed openings and policies with wall-clock cutoffs. Empirically equal complete histories are therefore reported separately from the theorem's scope.

Pool C gives a concrete warning about counting. Its 72 nominal conditions produced only 33 distinct complete normalized histories for best_v9e: twelve from the standard start, eleven from one continuation start and ten from the other. Two guard policies differ only in opening actions that those continuation tests replace, so some nominally different opponents become the same continuation. Another pair happens to give the same full recorded history. The [diversity check](effective_pool_c_diversity/RESULTS.md) retains actions, events and states while removing names, seat labels and global interleaving annotations. These are observed equality classes, not independent statistical samples or proof that the underlying policies agree everywhere.

The final validation separates three checks. An original-engine replay takes the recorded action lists and reconstructs every state, combat result and terminal outcome. Fresh entry-point executions reconstruct the recorded public observations through the engine and independently regenerate the installed policy's decisions, checking exact actions and observation preservation. Source receipts bind the declared and observed source bytes at the recorded checks; they do not prove continuous historical identity between checks. None alone establishes the other two. For D, the original recorded direct-entry history is compared with two fresh executions, one direct decide and one default factory instance. That is three histories and two fresh regenerations, not three new strength trials. The [completed supplementary independent review](../reviews/terminal_final_independent_001/completed-review-001/REVIEW.md) and its [dated JSON](../reviews/terminal_final_independent_001/completed-review-001/REVIEW.json) authenticate the complete evidence without executing policies or the engine themselves.

Call accounting matters for runtime as well. C's best_v9e logs contained 1,224 timed wrapper calls, including 120 turns that only copied prescribed actions, and 1,104 actual policy calls. The [accounting correction](decision_timing_accounting/RESULTS.md) preserves those three quantities instead of relabeling wrapper time as isolated policy time. The same distinction is explicit in D. Times are measured on the shared machine at low priority; they are not portable latency bounds. Several tested opponents have wall-clock planning cutoffs, so observed reproduction does not establish independence from machine load.

Exact parameter separation also has a limited meaning. These reserves use new parameter combinations and prescribed histories from known implementation families. An audit of retained earlier records can find exact overlap, and its unresolved or undated inputs must remain visible. It cannot establish that every idea in a fixture was uninformed by development, that an opponent family is new, or that the selected conditions represent a random population of future opponents. The [adaptation examples](ADAPTATION_FINDINGS.md) show why this matters: a public feature can predict behavior on one catalogue and be imitated by a deliberately constructed counter.

The resulting claim is finite. The frozen installed policy has the reported outcomes, action validity and reproduction evidence on D's declared conditions, subject to the completed checks linked above. It does not dominate all tested development policies: the final round robins and the economy lane contain complementary strengths, and the [main report](../../../reports/REPORT.md) retains those comparisons. A broader strength claim needs an explicit opponent distribution or a wider, separately specified game-theoretic objective.
