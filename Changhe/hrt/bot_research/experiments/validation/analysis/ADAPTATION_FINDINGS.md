# What the checked adaptation examples establish

Written UTC: 2026-10-31T22:42:34.944405+00:00. Coverage revision UTC: 2026-10-31T22:45:03.073264+00:00. This reading route uses completed development evidence available before reserved D. No D outcomes or parameters enter it.

A public signal can support a useful response choice while remaining easy to imitate. The examples below make that distinction concrete: selectors improve on earlier finite policy sets, opponents delay the distinguishing action, and later selectors change when or how they commit. The evidence is a finite sequence of checked responses and counter-responses. It supplies no infinite-cycle theorem or universally strongest bot.

[WallPrefixSelector O](wall_prefix_selector/RESULTS.md) provides the first useful example. G is the guard00 early-wall policy; L is WallSignalRepair, whose persistent opponent model is the factory-growing HPHandoff H. G and L share the same first ordered list: twenty walls, then four factories. O advances both on that actual observation. At decision two it keeps L if the opponent has no building or roster, otherwise G. The selected instance retains its private history. O avoided losses against all ten prior policies in both physical seats; all 22 games, including self-play, passed the declared audits. The [source argument](wall_prefix_selector/PROOF.md) explains the shared opening and conditional transfer to the selected member. An empty map is a branch condition, not proof of opponent identity.

[DelayedWallGuard P](delayed_wall_counter/RESULTS.md) moves G's wall construction from round one to round two. O must choose before those new walls are public, so it selects L. P beat O 800:-55 on round 11 in both seats, with complete histories matching its P/L controls, but lost both seats against G, frozen best_v9e and AS1C. The [branch proof](delayed_wall_counter/PROOF.md) proves selection, not victory; moving walls also changes income.

[ReservedWallGuard Q](reserved_wall_counter/RESULTS.md) retains P's round-two walls but withholds two round-three factories, reaching 264 gold and 11 factories for a round-four tier4. Q beats O 800:-326 at round 14, draws G, and beats frozen best_v9e and AS1C, both seats. [TwoRoundWallSelector R](two_round_wall_selector/RESULTS.md) waits until decision three against P and beats it 800:740 in both seats. [LateReservedWallGuard S](late_reserved_wall_counter/RESULTS.md) then stays empty through two, builds walls and splash at three, and withholds two factories to start four with 261 gold for its tier4 purchase. S beats R 800:-326 at round 14 in both seats, while drawing G. These controls expose the cost and benefit of particular openings rather than establish a general rule that waiting wins.

[Protocol 160](wall_timing_transfer/RESULTS.md) directly compares Q/S on eight fixed opponents: 24 new and eight retained records give 16 matched conditions. Their WDL agrees in every condition, including losses to H, where Q finishes 240:500 and S 100:500. This finite timing comparison supports neither general dominance nor full-game equivalence.

[Protocol 159's proof](delayed_signal_ambiguity/PROOF.md) states the narrow obstruction behind H/S. Their normalized public observations agree through decision three. A selector restricted to correctly advanced G or L, their common opening, and irrevocable commitment by that decision has the same probability p of choosing G against either opponent. With win/draw/loss utilities 1/0/-1, G has payoffs (-1,0) against (H,S), and L has (+1,-1). Its worst expected payoff is min(1-2p,p-1), maximized at p=2/3 with value -1/3. The [retained-game application](delayed_signal_ambiguity/RESULTS.md) verifies those entries in both seats and the normalized prefixes. The theorem additionally assumes the selected full continuation reproduces its baseline payoff. Names and identity-bearing timing/process information are excluded; private selection randomness is independent of the opponent choice. Different openings, new responses, later commitment or reselection escape this class.

[T, ThirdRoundWait](third_round_wait/RESULTS.md), changes both the commitment deadline and L's attack timing. Its L4 member attacks at four. Against H and S, T advances G and L4 through three on actual observations, submitting G, then chooses L4 against still-empty H or G against now-visible S. T beats H 800:656 and draws S 740:740, both seats at round 50. [U, BlindFourthRound](blind_fourth_round/RESULTS.md), keeps the same three-round G opening and advanced L4 but commits to L4 unconditionally at four. U/H and T/H whole canonical histories agree; U loses to S -246:800 at round 12 in both seats. This ablation supports the value of T's visible signal on S within these four matched settings. T falls outside protocol 159's fixed G/L, decision-three class, so its success is compatible with that bound.

[V's worked explanation](fourth_round_wall_counter/EXPLANATION.md) shows why correct model chronology does not ensure a correct forecast. V imitates H's first three factory rounds, causing both T and U to commit to L4 before decision-four walls are visible. Their persistent H model sees the actual 291 gold and 17 factories and forecasts a lone splash plus factories. V instead builds guard00 walls and splash. Six tier2 combat copies that would survive the forecast route die on the actual route; their purchased roster entries remain. V buys one tier4 at five and first calls its retained cold default Fortress38 at six. The [audited ten-game result](fourth_round_wall_counter/RESULTS.md) is 4W/0D/6L: V beats T and U 800:-546 at round 12 in both seats, but loses to G 680:740 and to frozen best_v9e and AS1C 755:800 at round 50 in both seats. AS1C uses attack_share=1.0 and catch_up_share=0.5. Those six losses are part of the finding.

[Protocol 164](fourth_round_ambiguity/PROOF.md) moves the restricted question to decision four and the two responses G/U against H/V. The common first three public rounds again conceal the pending distinguishing actions. The [eight retained conditions](fourth_round_ambiguity/RESULTS.md) give G/H=-1, U/H=+1, G/V=+1 and U/V=-1 in each physical selector seat. Under the proof's correctly advanced state, normalized information and full-continuation coupling hypotheses, the restricted worst payoff is min(1-2p,2p-1): value 0 at p=1/2 of G, while either pure choice has worst payoff -1. This is an exact application to two fixed responses, not an impossibility theorem for other policies. Its checker replayed retained actions and authenticated earlier audits without new policy calls or games.

Read the verification receipts alongside these results because three different checks answer different questions:

- An unchanged-engine action replay checks that the recorded lists produce the recorded states, combat, income and terminal outcome. It does not establish that the named policy generated those lists.
- Fresh policy regeneration reconstructs persistent policies from the beginning and checks every returned list on the recorded actual observations. These repeated decisions audit reproducibility; they are not additional strength games.
- A private-state audit separately checks the retained members, discarded proposals, model chronology and relevant private values against independently advanced references. Its scope must be read literally: V's additional audit covers only five rounds of four T/U histories, with four cold F38 references and zero reference F38 decisions. The ordinary fresh audit covers all ten complete games.

Later Fortress34/Fortress38 planning has wall-clock cutoffs. Matching source and early state cannot guarantee matching later decisions under changed shadow work or load. Protocols 159/164 therefore retain full-continuation coupling as a hypothesis; observed complete-history equality supports particular executions. All examples here were selected after disclosed development findings and used the unchanged standard start with both physical assignments. Their proofs and audit receipts provide reusable mechanisms and precise restricted bounds. Strength on held-out D requires its separate evidence.
