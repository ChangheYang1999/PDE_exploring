G denotes `EarlyWallGuard(witness='guard00')`; L denotes `WallSignalRepair()`; R denotes the frozen `TwoRoundWallSelector`. The proposed S, `LateReservedWallGuard`, follows a fresh G whose private witness has `wall_round=3`, retaining the guard00 splash in round three, tier4 purchase in round four, and frozen defender from round five. Only the trailing factory purchases in round three are reduced. After applying its full non-factory round-three prefix to a private copy of its public state, it chooses the largest affordable prefix of the proposed factory list for which projected gold at the start of round four is at least the tier4 cost. The projection includes unspent gold, exact own path income and factory income, with zero outgoing-hit bounty. If no prefix, including zero factories, meets the target, choose zero and record infeasibility. Resume the parent policy unchanged thereafter. The new implementation must change only its instance's witness, not shared data, and must report the actual wall round correctly.

Use the unchanged bundled standard start and default rules: 100 gold, 800 HP, empty buildings/factories/roster, a 12 by 12 grid from (0,0) to (11,11), wall cost 1, splash cost 60, factory cost 20, factory income 15, path income 1 per edge, and tier4 cost 260. Observations are valid consecutive engine snapshots of one player from round one; no external code changes the owned policy state. Claims about a reached round are conditional on that round being reached. In particular, a round-four purchase is not asserted after earlier game termination. The proposed opening has no outgoing roster through round three. Incoming combat can change HP but does not remove buildings, factories or gold under these rules.

## First two rounds

Changing G's wall round to three means that its round-one and round-two branches have no building or minion purchases. G's factory loop submits as many 20-gold factories as are affordable. An empty map has shortest route length 22 edges: Manhattan distance gives a lower bound of 11+11, attained by any monotone route. With no outgoing roster, outgoing-hit bounty is zero.

Round one buys five factories with all 100 gold. Settlement yields 22+5*15=97 gold and five factories. Round two buys four factories for 80, retains 17, and receives 22+9*15=157 income. Thus it enters round three with 174 gold, nine factories, no buildings and no roster. The arithmetic is independent of incoming damage while the relevant decisions remain reachable. Both completed early snapshots have the empty presence signal used by R.

## Legal construction and exact path income

The guard00 wall list consists of (0,1), (1,1), then (x,1),(x,3) for x=2 through 9 in increasing order, followed by (10,3),(11,3). Its only turret is a splash at (1,3). S's round-three non-factory prefix therefore proposes these 20 walls in that order, followed by the splash. All cells are distinct, inside the board and different from spawn and base. Every prefix is affordable from 174 gold.

The axis-aligned route through (0,0), (10,0), (10,2), (0,2), (0,4), (11,4), (11,11) avoids every final wall and the splash. It therefore also survives every construction prefix, proving legality under `apply_action` without executing the proposals. The walls alone require any route to cross row one at x>=10 and subsequently row three at x<=1. Horizontal travel is at least 10+9+10=29, and vertical travel at least 11, giving 40 edges; the route through the row-three gap x=1 attains this.

After the splash occupies (1,3), row three can only be crossed at x=0. Any complete path first crosses row one at some x>=10 and later first crosses row three at x=0, before reaching base x=11. Hence its horizontal travel is at least 10+10+11=31 and vertical travel at least 11. The displayed final route attains 42 edges. Backtracking cannot reduce either lower bound. Thus shortest-path length is exactly 42, independently of tie breaking between shortest routes. The bundled breadth-first search uses right/down/left/up neighbor order, but this argument needs only its shortest-path property.

## Reservation calculation and optimality within factory prefixes

The 20 walls and one splash cost 80 and leave 94 gold before factories. Therefore the unreserved parent would buy four factories. Let k be the retained number, an integer in {0,1,2,3,4}. The map does not change when factories are bought. The projected and actual start-of-round-four gold, whenever that decision is reached, is

    g4(k) = 94 - 20k + 42 + 15(9+k) = 271 - 5k.

This equality includes all gold sources: the opening has no outgoing minions, so the hit-bounty term is exactly zero. The engine applies both submissions, completes both combat maps, and then credits path, factory and outgoing-hit income before checking the outcome. Even incoming attacks cannot change the 42-edge own path or factory count.

The condition g4(k)>=260 is equivalent to 5k<=11, hence k<=2 for the permitted integers. The largest feasible prefix is exactly two factories. It gives 261 gold and eleven factories. Three factories would leave 256, four would leave 251, while two clear the target by one gold. Thus withholding two of the parent's four trailing factory purchases is both sufficient and necessary within these fixed construction-and-factory-prefix choices. This does not claim optimality among other maps or spending sequences.

At a reached round-four decision, G's retained branch buys exactly one tier4 for 260 before attempting factories. S then has one gold, so its factory loop buys none. In round three, replacing the trailing factory count need not repair hidden economic state: `EarlyWallGuard.decide` reconstructs its local spending state from the observation each call. Its persistent defender has not yet been called. Any reused reservation code must likewise keep the real next observation authoritative.

## R selects L and preserves its state

In R/S, S's completed round-one snapshot has neither buildings nor roster, so R defers at round two. S's completed round-two snapshot is also empty in both lists, so R selects L at round three, before observing S's simultaneous round-three walls and splash. The new construction cannot retroactively change that decision. R never reselects.

R's source and shared-prefix proof establish that it has already advanced the very same private L object on copied public observations in rounds one and two, returning actions identical to L's in both rounds. In particular, L's private persistent H model has consumed both rounds and retains its correct player assignment. Consequently the selected L is not a newly initialized substitute lacking its earlier private history.

Under the explicit environmental coupling hypotheses of the R proof, an R/S game therefore has the same actions and engine history as a standalone L/S game in the same physical assignment and with consistent public names. The induction matches the private L state through the shared rounds and through all later permanently delegated decisions, and matches S's corresponding transitions. It requires any decision-relevant global, random or wall-clock branches to agree for both policies. G's and H's frozen planners eventually use time cutoffs; equal source and early observations do not remove that limitation. Changing names in retained controls requires justified normalization. The source establishes the branch and conditional history transfer, not that either game is won by S.

## Optional conditional observation and mixture result

Against the same shared R opening, H and proposed S both submit exactly five factories in round one and four in round two. For H, empty layout and bait, guard rounds four/six, gun extension nine, lead trigger eight, round deadline twelve and disabled panic exclude every competing early action. R submits its shared 20 walls plus four factories, then five factories. Neither side has a roster in either round, so no combat damage or bounty occurs. With identical physical assignments and consistently relabeled player names, the complete public actions and state histories through the end of round two agree between R/H and R/S.

This is not equality of all raw observations with arbitrary names. Engine observations expose player names, which may encode specifications. The conclusion applies to state/action-based selectors that ignore those labels or use the same consistent relabeling, not to selectors allowed to distinguish H and S by name or some other external identity signal. It also does not say that later observations are identical: S's round-three construction supplies new information after R's commitment.

Assume, additionally and conditionally, that a fixed finite payoff experiment establishes the following values, with compatible outcomes in both physical assignments: G/H=-1, L/H=+1, G/S=0 and L/S=-1. No S entry is asserted as measured here. A blind mixture that commits to G with probability p and L with probability 1-p, for 0<=p<=1, then has payoff 1-2p against H and p-1 against S. Its worst payoff over those two opponents is min(1-2p,p-1). The increasing and decreasing affine functions intersect only at p=2/3, where both are -1/3. For p<2/3 the second is below -1/3; for p>2/3 the first is below -1/3. Thus the maximal worst payoff among such mixtures is exactly -1/3, uniquely at G weight 2/3.

The same two-column calculation applies to a randomized state/action-based selector forced to commit to one of its persistent G/L members by the third decision, if the equal public prefixes and decision-relevant environmental coupling make its choice distribution the same against H and S. It does not cover arbitrary policies, later adaptive switching, name-based identification, other histories or unrestricted opponents. The algebra is proved conditional on the four payoff premises; empirical application remains OPEN until all needed entries are validated.
