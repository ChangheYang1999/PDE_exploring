# Transfer of the public roster selector to existing archetypes

## Statement and hypotheses

The object is the unchanged PublicRosterSelector declared in public-selector-a001/b001/c001, with threshold3, and both forced branch controls. Evaluate every one of the twelve named PROFILES in counters/archetypes.py on the bundled standard empty start, both physical assignments. These archetypes are existing development baselines. Their names and parameters have been seen previously; this is not a held-out evaluation.

## Argument and dependencies

The common first-round opening and conditional selected-branch equality are proved in ../public_roster_selector/PROOF.md. No policy changes are made for this expansion. A later observation may reveal behavior inconsistent with the first-round roster, but the selector deliberately never changes branches again. Thus transfer is an empirical question, not a consequence of the finite eight-target result.

There are twelve targets times three modes times two assignments, hence72 declared games. Each game must pass strict attempt accounting, independent unchanged-engine replay, source and data checks, fresh selector decision reproduction, and observation and action legality checks. All modes receive the same standard initial state. Check adaptive histories against the forced branch it actually selects. Report wins, draws and losses for each mode, with target-normalized HP and final round, including every failure. Paired seats and repeated branch histories do not supply independent samples.

## Scope

The claim under test is that selecting once from the first-round roster retains competitive performance outside the eight targets used to choose its threshold. A positive finding would cover only these twelve known archetypes. Neither general opponent identification nor a population win probability follows. The growth branch retains a wall-clock planner, so source identity alone is insufficient for exact reproducibility; regenerate and compare actual decisions. Pool C remains unused.
